# Recursive Method Project Map

This map is evidence for `PR-027`. It makes Jamie Burkart's recurring method
inspectable across three projects without promoting prototypes into production
systems or collective results into individual accomplishments.

| Project | Observe relationships | Model or prototype | Build and expose to use | Response and revision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open House | Considered how social software represents people in relation. | Modeled shared responsibility in an open communal gallery. | Initiated and tended a 10-day public experiment with collective decisions and distributed documentation. | The group met with staff, cleaned the space, and marked a new boundary around technical equipment. |
| CallNYC | Found noisy records and learned that some residents were wary of calling Council offices. | Organized district-and-issue guidance around residents' paths to action. | Built the independent web application and a small JSON interface. | Filtered out-of-city and spam records, added Council-member Twitter contacts, and added borough filtering. |
| NYC Artist Coalition | Listened through recurring meetings in different small cultural spaces. | Developed a convene-listen-translate-route cycle between artists' experience and civic pathways. | Helped establish public events, practical safety sessions, campaigns, hearings, testimony, and relief convenings. | The coalition's later iterations took up issues artists surfaced, including Cabaret Law, M.A.R.C.H. raids, commercial rent, and emergency relief. |

## Technical Specificity

The map names technical structures only where they explain an affordance: a
JSON interface and filters made civic records easier to route; event and
testimony infrastructure carried lived experience into public processes; a
shared operating boundary allowed an inhabitable experiment to continue after
feedback.

## Originality, Deployment, And Credit Audit

- Warren Sack's protected historical evaluations support an early lineage of
  social-network analysis, structural-equivalence reasoning, working software
  prototypes, and embodied interface design.
- Jamie is not described as inventing structural equivalence as a field-wide
  concept.
- The Flickr, dictionary, Jitter, and physical-photo-browsing work remains
  described as classroom analysis, prototypes, and collaborative design, not
  deployed production systems.
- Open House decisions and repair remain collectively credited to the
  housemates.
- CallNYC is framed as an independent project; informal conversations are not
  promoted into representative user research.
- NYC Artist Coalition systems and outcomes remain collective; the evidence
  does not assign every event or policy result to Jamie.
