# Artistic continuity map

This internal map preserves the Margaret Morse and Warren Sack concerns inside
the hiring argument. It asks how People and place, Interface or form, and
Learning and continuity remain connected across unlike projects.

| Project | People and place | Interface or form | Learning and continuity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open House | Collaborative living, exhibition, hospitality, and participant contribution. | A public, process-oriented art and documentation situation. | Attention to use and relationship became part of Jamie's later systems practice. |
| WOW List | Organizers, contributors, events, and city scenes produced the network being served. | Publishing, discovery, support, and geographic interfaces. | Organizer friction and public exchanges informed iterative platform work and later civic facilitation. |
| Sunday Dinner / 196 | Repeated hosting made introductions, participation, and place part of the structure. | Gathering rhythms, onboarding, documentation, and residency support. | Continuity emerged through repetition and handoff rather than a one-time event. |
| CallNYC | Resident questions and public constituent-service records shaped the problem. | Issue taxonomy, representative context, guidance, and sharing interfaces. | Public attention and visible friction produced bounded evidence while adoption remained open. |
| NYC Artist Coalition / Fair Rent NYC | Artists, organizers, cultural spaces, agencies, and Council processes met through recurring situated events. | Meetings, issue gathering, collective drafting, campaign identities, source maps, and action paths. | The structure changed through participant contribution while collective credit and institutional causality remained bounded. |
| Source-Backed Team Memory | Collaborators and their source context define what the system may know and carry. | Source maps, decision records, review loops, and reusable handoffs. | Corrections remain visible, people approve public use, and future collaborators inherit inspectable context. |

## Editorial boundary

Historical professor records inform these contemporary editorial lenses. They
are not current professor-authored endorsements. Artistic continuity should
remain visible through concrete projects, not through a detached testimonial or
an abstract claim that all projects are the same.
