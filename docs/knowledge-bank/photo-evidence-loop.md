# Photo Evidence Loop

Photographs can illustrate established claims, help an editor make a
purposeful selection, or reveal something worth researching. These are three
different operations.

## Claim to Editor Brief

1. Select approved claims and open research questions for one project,
   audience, and page purpose.
2. Translate them into observable visual needs: people doing work, physical
   systems, public programs, artifacts, places, transitions, and consequences.
3. Give the editor time and project boundaries without exposing unrelated
   private archive structure.
4. Request selection rationale, likely date and place, depicted people,
   possible project relation, rights holder, consent concerns, and sensitivity
   concerns.
5. Use cleared images only when the visual relationship to the claim is honest
   and specific.

## Editor Discovery to Research

1. A visual discovery returns as a public-safe capture or protected pointer.
2. The capture records what appears observable and what remains inference.
3. A research task seeks corroborating dates, identities, project records, and
   context.
4. Human review addresses rights, consent, dignity, safety, and collective
   credit.
5. Only corroborated observations may enter a claim. Computer vision, filename
   inference, facial recognition, or an editor's hunch is not proof by itself.

## Required Gates

- rights status;
- consent status;
- public-display status;
- identity and date confidence;
- sensitive-person and vulnerable-status review;
- relationship to a specific claim or research question;
- corroboration beyond visual inference;
- caption and credit approval.

Private photo-library paths, unapproved images, face embeddings, private people
records, and bulk metadata do not belong in this repository.
