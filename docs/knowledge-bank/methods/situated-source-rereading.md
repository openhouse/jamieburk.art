# Situated source rereading

Archival production is not complete when a source has been read once. A later
question may disclose a different pattern, contradiction, boundary, or missing
voice. The new reading should be additive and dated rather than silently
replacing the old one.

## Protocol

1. Name the present research question before reopening the source.
2. Identify the exact version, date, custody class, and redacted access state.
3. Verify that the artifact is materially reachable, not merely listed.
4. Record atomic observations with locators in the protected research layer.
5. State what the source supports and what it does not establish.
6. Compare the encounter with earlier readings and claims.
7. Preserve disagreements, corrections, and changed interpretation.
8. Record inaccessible, missing, or uncertain originals as librarian requests.
9. Keep research authorization separate from rights, consent, quotation, and
   publication approval.
10. Reread before claim promotion, consequential applications, major public
    revisions, and the review date of a foundational synthesis.

## Required encounter fields

A governed source-return record names the encounter date, reader, source
states, questions, prior readings, new observations, changed interpretations,
contradictions, affected records, limitations, librarian requests, and
publication decision.

The exact private locator, authentication material, raw excerpt, and protected
receipt stay outside public Git.

## Librarian compact

When a source cannot be found or opened, the agent asks for help rather than
inferring nonexistence. A useful request names the desired artifact, probable
collection, why it matters, what has already been checked, and the boundary on
what may be published.
