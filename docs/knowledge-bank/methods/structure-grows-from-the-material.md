# Structure Grows from the Material

Jamie's method begins by looking for the nascent structure already present in a
project: relationships, informal roles, language, recurring questions,
technical constraints, histories, care practices, and public needs. The work is
to surface that structure and give it resilient form.

In hiring language, this becomes requirements, workflows, decision records,
implementation plans, launch support, onboarding, and handoffs. In civic or
cultural work, it may also become a public meeting, a campaign identity, a
survey, a map, a shared archive, or a durable way for people to act together.

## Working sequence

1. Observe how the work already moves.
2. Name what is known, open, protected, and contradictory.
3. Translate needs into shared objects and ownership.
4. Test the structure in real use.
5. Preserve memory, correction, and handoff paths.
6. Revisit original material as the present question changes.

## Present-grounded source return

The July 19, 2026 encounter compared the writer-voice source package with the
current portfolio's operational language and evidence across multiple projects.
It confirmed that "emerging" is often more accurate and less shaming than
"ambiguous": the work is not groundless; its structure has not yet been made
usable.
