# Transition and handoff

Jamie treats transition as a normal condition of living systems, not evidence
that the people inside them have failed. Emerging work often has a structure
already present in its relationships, materials, constraints, and unfinished
questions. His craft is to surface enough of that structure that people can
orient, act, care for one another, and continue.

In professional terms, this appears as source maps, requirements, workflows,
decision records, onboarding, access handoffs, current-state packets, explicit
owners, next actions, boundaries, and durable memory. The form grows from the
material rather than being imposed as a generic administrative layer.

## Observable pattern

Across the canonical records, the pattern includes:

- portable project folders designed for continuity across devices and
  collaborators;
- working memory that distinguishes raw sources, decisions, open questions,
  owners, consent, and follow-up;
- onboarding that turns acceptance into schedule, orientation, configuration,
  and independent access;
- project transition that preserves a bounded public account without encoding
  private reasons; and
- source-backed handoff artifacts that allow a future person to see what is
  known, what remains uncertain, and what decision comes next.

## Care is an operating requirement

A durable handoff does not mean collecting everything. It means reducing the
avoidable suffering of transition while protecting privacy, rights, credit,
and room for later interpretation. Sometimes the responsible artifact is a
complete operating packet. Sometimes it is a hold, an anti-claim, a named
question, or an intentional absence.

This is why a transition should state both continuity and limit. The receiving
person needs enough structure to proceed, but not an inherited fiction that
every ambiguity has been resolved.

## Present-tense source return

- **Question brought to the source:** What distinguishes Jamie's transition
  practice from generic documentation or project administration?
- **Original material reopened:** the KC Town Hall proposal packet, the Fair
  Rent collaborative running-minutes source, the protected working-folder and
  shared-drive source records, and canonical handoff claims.
- **Scope and completeness:** a bounded cross-project comparison, not a review
  of every handoff Jamie has made or every collaborator's experience of them.
- **What changed in this reading:** the pattern is strongest when framed as
  continuity plus limit: making the next action possible while preserving
  uncertainty, privacy, and the right not to carry every record forward.
- **What did not change:** the source set does not prove that every collaborator
  adopted every system or that every transition succeeded.
- **Access boundary:** protected originals were used only through public-safe
  canonical summaries and the authorized July 19 rereading; private locators,
  circumstances, contacts, and raw text remain outside Git.
- **Next return:** seek collaborator-specific evidence about which handoff
  artifacts were actually reused, and preserve disagreement or non-use rather
  than treating adoption as automatic.

## Boundaries

- Do not turn a private transition into a public explanation Jamie has chosen
  not to give.
- Do not equate repository custody with project ownership.
- Do not infer collaborator adoption from the existence of a template.
- Do not present transition as a euphemism for an unverified outcome.
- Do not make continuity depend on publishing protected source material.
