# Collective credit and protected absence

The Wiki needs to remember both contribution and restraint.

## Collective credit

Every meaningful proposition should distinguish among:

- an individual's documented action;
- shared production by named collaborators;
- collective advocacy or cultural practice;
- institutional enactment or implementation.

One layer may inform another without becoming identical to it. A campaign can
help create conditions for a law, while the Council remains the institution
that enacted the law. A Git history can establish website implementation while
leaving policy, copy, design, and organizing credit shared or unresolved.

## Protected absence

Some sources matter precisely because the public record should not contain
them. The Wiki may preserve:

- that protected support exists;
- an opaque source identity;
- a bounded public-safe observation;
- what the source does not establish;
- the rights, consent, and publication state;
- an inquiry or human review gate.

It must not expose the locator, raw content, identities, credentials, or
unapproved media merely to make the claim look stronger.

## Decision rule

Access permits research. Custody permits preservation. Neither action grants
authorship, quotation rights, represented-person consent, collaborator
agreement, or publication approval.

The absence of private evidence from public Git is an intentional property of
the system, not a completeness failure.
