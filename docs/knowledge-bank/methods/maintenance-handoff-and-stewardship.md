# Maintenance, Handoff, and Stewardship

A usable result includes the conditions under which another person can operate,
question, repair, or replace it. Maintenance and handoff are part of delivery,
not an epilogue added after the interesting work is over.

## Working sequence

1. **Learn the inherited system.** Identify who carries knowledge, where the
   current process succeeds, which workarounds are protective, and what cannot
   change without authority or consent.
2. **Make operation visible.** Expose dependencies, ownership boundaries,
   recurring tasks, exceptions, rights, risks, and decision history.
3. **Maintain in use.** Treat corrections, questions, support, renewals, and
   changing conditions as operational evidence. Launch is not adoption.
4. **Transfer responsibility.** Name a successor, custodian, or decision body
   when evidence allows. Preserve source context, access boundaries, and open
   issues without requiring the original implementer forever.
5. **Preserve a bounded record.** State what transferred and what remains
   unknown, protected, temporary, or person-dependent.

At [Harry J. Epstein Company](../projects/harry-j-epstein.md), continuity meant
translating specialized staff and catalog knowledge into maintainable commerce
and operating forms without erasing the store's voice. In [KC Spaces
Fund](../projects/kc-spaces-fund.md), Jamie's technical support remained
distinct from organizer credit. In [KC Town Hall](../projects/kc-town-hall.md),
the public record preserves a bounded transition while omitting private family
context. In this Wiki, maintenance includes source-return dates,
contradictions, wanted records, compiler checks, human gates, and selective
projection.

Stewardship is not permanent personal ownership, invisible unpaid
availability, or permission to retain access after responsibility changes.
