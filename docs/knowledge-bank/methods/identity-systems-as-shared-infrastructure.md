# Identity Systems As Shared Infrastructure

A public identity can do operating work. A name, domain, account, visual
language, handbill, map, or recurring campaign pattern can help people recognize
a shared undertaking, find an entry point, and continue speaking through it.

The strongest evidence is not that Jamie made every artifact or authored every
message. It is that he helped establish recognizable systems other people could
use. Keep these contribution types separate:

- identity and account establishment;
- naming, visual design, and campaign architecture;
- account administration over a bounded period;
- authorship of a particular post or artifact; and
- ownership of the collective project or its outcomes.

Evidence for one does not establish the others.

## What Became Usable

- recognizable entry points across web, social, print, and in-person work;
- campaign families able to share public language without becoming one
  person's voice;
- repeatable invitations, calls to action, maps, and public-information forms;
  and
- continuity when collaborators needed to speak through an established project
  identity.

## Known / Open / Protected

**Known:** selected identity-establishment actions, durable public accounts,
named campaign relationships, and attributable uses. **Open:** contribution
splits, administrator history, recipient accounts of reuse, and maintenance
cost. **Protected:** credentials, account access, private design files,
unapproved collaborator statements, participant data, and internal strategy.

No logo, screenshot, photograph, or font becomes reusable because it is
evidence. Publication requires separate authorship, license, privacy, consent,
context, and display decisions.
