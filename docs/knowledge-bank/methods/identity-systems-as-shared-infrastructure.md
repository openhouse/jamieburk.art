# Identity systems as shared infrastructure

A name, domain, account, visual language, handbill, map, or recurring campaign
pattern can do operating work. It can help people recognize a shared
undertaking, know where to enter, and continue speaking through it. Jamie has
repeatedly worked on this layer across products, campaigns, public events, and
neighborhood projects.

## Contribution types

Keep these distinct:

- identity and account establishment;
- naming, visual design, and campaign architecture;
- account administration over a bounded period;
- authorship of a particular post or artifact; and
- ownership of a collective project or its outcomes.

Evidence for one does not automatically establish the others.

## What became usable

- recognizable entry points across web, social, print, and in-person work;
- campaign families that could share public language without becoming one
  person's voice;
- repeatable patterns for invitations, calls to action, maps, and public
  information; and
- continuity when another collaborator needed to speak or organize through an
  established project identity.

## Known, open, and protected

- **Known:** selected identity-establishment actions, durable public accounts,
  named campaign relationships, and attributable collaborator use.
- **Open:** contribution splits for many artifacts, administrator history,
  recipient accounts of reuse, and maintenance cost.
- **Protected:** credentials, private design files, unapproved collaborator
  statements, participant data, and internal strategy.

No logo, screenshot, photograph, or font becomes reusable because it is
evidence. Publication requires separate authorship, license, privacy, consent,
context, and display decisions.
