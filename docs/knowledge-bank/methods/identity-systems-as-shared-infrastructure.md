# Identity Systems as Shared Infrastructure

A public identity can do operating work. A name, domain, account, visual
language, handbill, map, campaign site, or recurring format can help people
recognize a shared undertaking, know where to enter, and continue speaking
through it.

## Contribution types

Keep these states separate:

1. identity and account establishment;
2. naming, visual design, domain, and campaign architecture;
3. administration during a bounded period;
4. authorship of a particular post, page, or artifact;
5. ownership of a collective project or its outcomes.

Evidence for one does not establish the others.

## What became usable

- recognizable entry points across web, social, print, and in-person work;
- campaign families that could share public language without becoming one
  person's voice;
- repeatable patterns for invitations, calls to action, maps, forms, and public
  information;
- continuity when another collaborator needed to organize or publish through
  an established identity.

The coalition campaign family supplies the strongest bounded example. WOW List
connected product identity with organizer communication. CallNYC translated
public data into resident-facing pathways. KC Spaces Fund demonstrates a
behind-the-scenes contribution to naming and digital coherence while public
organizer and social-publishing credit remains elsewhere.

## Boundary

Shared use does not prove Jamie authored every message, controlled an account
throughout its life, designed every artifact, or owned a collective outcome.
Visual evidence still requires separate authorship, rights, consent, context,
and display decisions.
