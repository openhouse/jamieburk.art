# Learning Through Making

Jamie's technical practice did not appear suddenly as administration. It grew
through art, media archaeology, social-software experiments, installations,
writing, and the repeated act of making an idea inhabitable enough to test.

The historical record shows a method with several forms:

- bring a media object into the present and learn from its behavior;
- model relationships in software and build an interface people can enter;
- translate an online structure into physical movement and space;
- make a participatory situation, observe what happens, and revise its rules;
- write closely enough to distinguish an arresting intuition from a supported
  argument.

The record contains both strong analytical writing and moments when embodied
projects outpaced the assigned essay form. That tension belongs in the account.
Learning through making is not an excuse to avoid writing; writing, diagrams,
source trails, and documentation are among the things Jamie makes.

This lineage supports comfort moving among physical, social, and computational
systems; recursive model-build-observe-revise practice; and documentation that
helps someone who was not present enter the work later.

It does not establish production deployment of classroom prototypes,
field-wide invention, sole authorship of collaborative installations, or
current blanket endorsement by former teachers. Grades, student identifiers,
raw evaluations, correspondence, and protected source locations remain outside
the public Wiki.
