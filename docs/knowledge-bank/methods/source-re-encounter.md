# Source Re-Encounter

Source re-encounter is the practice of periodically returning to original
material with present questions instead of relying indefinitely on an earlier
summary. The purpose is not to discard prior archival work. It is to test what
the source still supports, notice what earlier eyes missed, and preserve any
change in interpretation without rewriting history.

## Protocol

1. Resolve the original source from its opaque private locator.
2. Record the date, account or custody context, and representation inspected.
3. Prefer the native artifact when access permits; note exports, OCR, or
   screenshots as representations rather than originals.
4. Compare the source with current claims, anti-claims, and open inquiries.
5. Record what the encounter confirmed, changed, complicated, or failed to
   recover.
6. Keep source content, access coordinates, and collaborator-private metadata
   outside the public repository.
7. Treat rights, consent, attribution, and public projection as separate human
   decisions.
8. When access fails, create a specific librarian handoff rather than filling
   the gap by inference.

## First Recorded Re-Encounter

On July 19, 2026, selected original NYC Artist Coalition Google Docs were
re-opened in their native interface through Jamie's authenticated account. The
set included a public-meeting checklist, a Cabaret Law testimony guide, a
delivered DCLA letter, MARCH working notes, and 2019 Office of Nightlife
priorities.

The structured Docs connector could locate the archive but could not read these
inherited documents. The authenticated browser could. This is a methodological
finding: source accessibility depends on both account and representation, so
"located" and "read" must remain separate states.

The encounter confirmed the coalition's cross-channel meeting workflow and
participant testimony support. It also exposed a visible suggestion attributed
to Jamie in the MARCH notes, narrowing one attribution question without
settling authorship of the larger collaborative passage.

No raw document body, private coordinate, contact detail, or collaborator
revision history is published here. Full research authorization does not imply
publication permission.
