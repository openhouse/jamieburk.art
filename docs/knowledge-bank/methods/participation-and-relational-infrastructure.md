# Participation and Relational Infrastructure

Participation does not begin when a person submits a form. It begins with an
invitation that can be understood, a place or channel where someone can arrive,
conditions that let them contribute, and memory that carries what happened
into the next encounter.

Jamie has repeatedly designed this connective layer across artistic, civic,
technical, and neighborhood work. The forms change, but the recurring elements
are invitation, orientation, presence, exchange, shared action, memory, and
continuation.

## Recurring components

| Component | Working form |
| --- | --- |
| Invitation | Handbill, listing, call script, event page, direct outreach |
| Orientation | Public explanation, map, schedule, guide, issue framing |
| Situated encounter | Meal, meeting, venue, river journey, town hall, work site |
| Agency | Contribution, testimony, response, choice, hosting, shared making |
| Translation | Cultural experience into civic language; local knowledge into requirements |
| Memory | Notes, records, source maps, photographs, structured data, follow-up |
| Continuation | Next action, recurring event, handoff, campaign, maintained platform |

## Lineage

- Open House treated a gallery and home as a communally governed social form.
- Waterways projects used travel, invitation, and shared river experience as
  inquiry into social and cultural infrastructure.
- NTER CHNG joined software, public messages, installation space, and real-time
  response.
- WOW List made cultural information distributable, while Sunday Dinner made
  recurrence and hospitality a durable social practice.
- NYC Artist Coalition connected meetings in cultural spaces to letters,
  events, calls, testimony, maps, and legislation.
- KC Town Hall joined daily construction presence, neighborhood stories,
  survey infrastructure, and physical care for a long-neglected building.

## What the method produces

The result may be software, an event, a public record, a restored place, or a
handoff. The deeper product is a route by which people can become participants
rather than remain an audience or an input category.

This does not mean every invitation is inclusive, every participant has equal
power, or every event changes policy. Those are questions for evidence,
reflection, and repair, not benefits that can be assumed from the form alone.

## Present-tense source return

- **Question brought to the source:** What connects Jamie's artistic, civic,
  neighborhood, product, and event work without reducing participation to a
  slogan?
- **Original material reopened:** canonical claims for Open House, waterways,
  WOW List and Sunday Dinner, NYC Artist Coalition's participation guide and
  event population, and the KC Town Hall project overview.
- **Scope and completeness:** cross-project synthesis of documented forms and
  roles; not a participant-impact study or complete chronology of every public
  program.
- **What changed in this reading:** memory and continuation are now explicit
  parts of participation. A gathering becomes infrastructure when what people
  learn can alter the next action and when ownership can travel.
- **What did not change:** participation remains embodied, situated, and
  relational; operational artifacts support it but do not substitute for it.
- **Access boundary:** only public-safe claims and bounded summaries are used.
  Participant identities, private messages, attendance rows, survey responses,
  and unapproved media remain protected.
- **Next return:** compare selected event, survey, and onboarding records with
  collaborator and participant accounts to test where the designed route
  expanded agency and where it did not.

## Boundaries

- Do not infer impact, inclusion, attendance, or adoption from the existence of
  a participation mechanism.
- Credit hosts, co-designers, organizers, participants, and places.
- Preserve refusal, nonparticipation, and protected absence as meaningful
  states.
- Keep person-level records and unapproved media outside public Git.
