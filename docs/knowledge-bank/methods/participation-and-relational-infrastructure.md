# Participation and Relational Infrastructure

Participation does not begin when a person submits a form. It begins with an
invitation that can be understood, a place or channel where someone can arrive,
conditions that let them contribute, and memory that carries what happened
into the next encounter.

## Recurring components

| Component | Working form |
| --- | --- |
| Invitation | Handbill, listing, call script, event page, or direct outreach |
| Orientation | Public explanation, map, schedule, guide, or issue framing |
| Situated encounter | Meal, meeting, venue, river journey, town hall, or work site |
| Agency | Contribution, testimony, response, choice, hosting, or shared making |
| Translation | Cultural experience into civic language; local knowledge into requirements |
| Memory | Notes, records, source maps, photographs, structured data, or follow-up |
| Continuation | Next meeting, action pathway, handoff, maintained system, or open question |

## Public knowledge in people's own terms

Preserve self-description before translating it. Distinguish a participant's
account from Jamie's interpretation. Record authorship when attribution is
known and fair. Let public meetings, testimony, surveys, and calls change the
working model. Retain disagreement, uncertainty, and nonparticipation as
meaningful states.

People remain speakers and contributors, not an extracted audience category.
Patterns may be summarized without publishing person-level records.

## Boundary

Raw community records, participant lists, private correspondence, unapproved
quotations, sensitive testimony, and relationship graphs remain outside this
public repository. Participation is not consent to become portfolio evidence.
Recurring attendance does not by itself prove representation, adoption, or
measured impact.
