# Practices of Care and Transition

Projects change ownership, scale, technology, purpose, and participants. Jamie's
practice treats those transitions as implementation conditions rather than
evidence that the work began from failure or groundlessness.

## Care made operational

- preserve the language and knowledge already carried by the people involved;
- make emerging requirements and ownership visible without shaming uncertainty;
- keep decisions, corrections, and protected boundaries recoverable;
- prepare documentation and interfaces that another person can actually use;
- name what completed, what transferred, and what remains unresolved;
- avoid turning continuity into a claim of permanent personal control.

## Concrete anchors

[Harry J. Epstein](../projects/harry-j-epstein.md) shows technical change shaped
around a multigenerational business identity. [KC Town
Hall](../projects/kc-town-hall.md) separates construction completion, public
visioning, transition, and later municipal action. [Sunday Dinner and
196](../projects/sunday-dinner.md) preserves recurring hospitality as operating
infrastructure rather than reducing it to event production.

## Boundary

Care is not a substitute for evidence, and poetic language is not proof of
impact. Public claims still require concrete action, source position, collective
credit, and an honest limit. Private transition causes remain protected.
