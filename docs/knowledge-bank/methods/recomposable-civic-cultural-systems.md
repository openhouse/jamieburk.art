# Projects as a Recomposable Civic-Cultural System

## Central finding

Across Jamie Burkart's projects, the most reusable unit is not a whole project.
It is a bounded component: a technical module, social protocol, public artifact,
operating role, or stewardship practice with a legible purpose and a known
boundary. Projects compose by assembling those components around a situated
need. They recompose when a component is adapted for a different place, group,
medium, authority structure, or stage of work.

The record supports three different kinds of connection: direct transfer or a
shared artifact; a documented recurrence of a component without proof of
transfer; and a later structural comparison. Keeping those evidence tiers
separate lets the graph show real interoperability without inventing a single
master plan. In this model, **adaptation is not duplication**. A form travels
well only when its social obligations travel with it and its new context can
change it.

This is therefore a system of components and interfaces, but it is also a
tradition of practice. Invitation, hospitality, listening, shared
representation, circulation, collective decision, public address, resource
routing, documentation, maintenance, and handoff recur in different
combinations. Code can be forked. A social practice has to be rehosted.

## Terminology note

**KC Spaces Fund** is the canonical documented name across the reviewed source
and repository record. “KC Safer Spaces Fund” is retained here only as the
incoming search phrase that prompted this synthesis; it is not asserted as an
alias or historical project name.

## Evidence tiers

| Tier | Meaning | Graph treatment | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — documented transfer or shared artifact | A source explicitly describes inheritance, or two projects share an identifiable artifact or code history | `grew_from`, `shared_artifact_with`, or `adapted_from`, with a source | WOW List's provenance post says it grew from calendars made at Sunday Dinner; protected campaign repositories reproduce shared Git ancestry |
| B — documented component recurrence | Project-specific sources establish the same component in more than one setting, but do not establish how it traveled | `uses_method` or `has_component`, without a lineage claim | Orientation, access, follow-up, and continued availability appear in 196 Artists Residency; listening, surveys, and public-benefit documentation appear in KC Town Hall |
| C — structural comparison | A later synthesis finds a useful likeness while transmission, intent, and causality remain open | `structurally_similar_to` or an interpretive method relation | Open House, Great Accommodations, Sunday Dinner, and later civic projects all use inhabited participation frames, but resemblance alone cannot supply a historical chain |

The reading rule is strict: **structural resemblance does not establish historical transmission**.
Chronology may suggest a research question. It does not answer it.

## Component model

### 1. Invitation and entry

A handbill, personal message, public listing, event page, call script,
application route, or direct invitation makes a threshold legible. The
component includes who can enter, what they are entering, what is being asked,
and what access barriers remain.

### 2. Hosting, place, and orientation

A meal, home, gallery, residency, venue, work site, town hall, video call, map,
guide, or issue page lets someone arrive in a situation. Hosting is an
operating role with power over atmosphere, access, timing, safety, and the
decision to continue.

### 3. Listening and contribution

Conversation, a survey, an edit, a proposed event, a public question, testimony,
a name choice, or a resident report creates a way to affect the shared object.
The component is not complete merely because a form exists; people need a
credible route by which contribution can matter.

### 4. Shared representation

A calendar, list, profile, map, agenda, coalition name, letter, issue page,
proposal, or funding update carries situated knowledge beyond one encounter.
Representation makes coordination possible and also introduces editorial
power: someone defines fields, selects language, resolves conflicts, and
decides what stays absent.

### 5. Circulation and public address

Email, social publishing, embeds, campaign sites, press materials, public
meetings, calls to representatives, and testimony move a shared object toward
another participant or institution. Circulation can widen access, but a route
or response signal is not proof of reach, endorsement, attendance, conversion,
or effect.

### 6. Collective choice and institutional translation

Facilitation, collaborative editing, naming, an agenda, a requirements packet,
a proposal, public-benefit documentation, or a hearing converts plural input
into a form on which a group or institution can act. The component must name
who recommends, who decides, who implements, and who holds formal authority.

### 7. Resources and material support

Space, independent access, a donation route, a fundraising display, a grant
application, a construction sequence, printing, or service coordination turns
attention into material capacity. Resource infrastructure must keep organizer,
fiscal, grantmaking, property, institutional, and technical roles distinct.

### 8. Memory, maintenance, and handoff

Notes, source maps, structured records, Git history, correction paths,
operating sheets, meeting recurrence, renewals, documentation, and a bounded
transfer of responsibility keep a system usable after the first launch or
event. This component supplies the feedback loop through which every other
component can be questioned, repaired, replaced, or retired.

## The component interface contract

A reusable civic-cultural component should travel with more than instructions.
Its packet should state:

1. **Purpose:** the situated need it is meant to serve.
2. **Entry and exit:** how people arrive, contribute, decline, leave, or return.
3. **Inputs and outputs:** what the component receives and what usable object,
   decision, encounter, or route it produces.
4. **Decision rights:** who hosts, edits, moderates, approves, funds, maintains,
   and can stop the work.
5. **Social preconditions:** relationships, trust, accessibility, safety, time,
   language, and material support required for the form to work.
6. **Evidence tier:** whether reuse is a documented transfer, documented
   recurrence, or structural comparison.
7. **Credit and custody:** who contributed, who holds the source, and whose
   account may be missing.
8. **Rights, consent, and publication:** what may be used, retained, quoted, or
   projected, decided separately.
9. **Maintenance and correction:** who tends the component and how another
   person can challenge or revise it.
10. **Success and stop conditions:** what observable result would count, what
    remains unmeasured, and when the component should be withdrawn.

This contract is the social equivalent of an interface definition. It lets a
component change implementation while preserving the obligations that make it
legible and accountable.

## Documented artifact linkages

### Sunday Dinner calendars → WOW List

The clearest social-to-technical lineage is explicit. The [first recovered WOW
List provenance post](https://x.com/wowlist/status/433671630837919744)
credits Richard Caceres and Jamie Burkart and says the project grew from
calendars made at Sunday Dinner. A recurring hosted practice produced a need
for durable event memory and circulation; WOW List made events, lists,
profiles, follows, recommendations, and contributions operable between rooms
and dates.

This is a documented Tier A edge. It does not mean Sunday Dinner participants
automatically became WOW List users, that the projects were one organization,
or that the software reproduced the dinner's social conditions.

### WOW List / `popular.vote` → Call Script → early NYC Artist Coalition

The [continuity record](../projects/wowlist-sunday-dinner-callscript-continuity.md)
preserves a November 2016 WOW List route to `popular.vote`, Call Script's public
project connection to that route, and a January 2017 sequence of mobilization,
public-agency gathering, participatory coalition naming, collaborative letter
work, and a next meeting. The reusable component changed from cultural-event
discovery into a civic action pathway and then into collective public agency.

This is a bounded Tier A bridge, not proof that WOW List caused New York City
Artists Coalition or that a single person authored the coalition's formation.

### KC Spaces Fund and the campaign-site repository family

A read-only review of the protected repository family reproduced shared Git
ancestry across KC Spaces Fund and later campaign instances, along with a
reusable campaign theme and fundraiser-display module. The surviving commit
subjects describe configurable action buttons, forms, embeds, typography,
colors, fundraiser display, and deployment-oriented instance setup. This is
literal technical reuse: a Tier A code-and-infrastructure edge.

Its meaning is deliberately narrow. Shared code does not make campaign
organizers, positions, audiences, funds, communications, or outcomes the same.
The [summary-only source note](../sources/campaign-site-repository-family-2020-2022.md)
keeps repository paths, remotes, configuration, and protected content out of
the public Wiki.

### 196 Artists Residency, KC Town Hall, and documented recurrence

The [196 and Sunday Dinner orientation](../projects/sunday-dinner-196-orientation.md)
and protected workflow summaries document proposal review, video-call
orientation, space configuration, independent access, invitation state, and
follow-up. The [KC Town Hall orientation](../projects/kc-town-hall-orientation.md)
documents physical stabilization, site encounter, neighborhood listening,
resident-service routing, public-benefit records, municipal presentation, and
stewardship. The [KC Spaces Fund orientation](../projects/kc-spaces-fund-orientation.md)
documents collaborator-led mutual aid supported by campaign and action-route
infrastructure.

These are strong Tier B component records. The current evidence does not show
that 196 Artists Residency supplied KC Town Hall's field methods, or that KC
Town Hall supplied KC Spaces Fund's governance. Those possible transmissions
remain questions for collaborators and contemporaneous artifacts.

## How recomposition operates in community practice

### A pattern is learned through use

Repeated hosting teaches timing, welcome, orientation, care, and when a room
needs more or less structure. Editorial work teaches how a shared object can
be legible without pretending to be neutral. Campaign work teaches how a
public route meets institutions, deadlines, opposition, and uneven power.
The reusable knowledge is therefore partly explicit documentation and partly
a practiced capacity to notice and respond.

### A new context changes the component

A list in WOW List is not the same social object as a coalition name, a
neighborhood survey, or a funded-space spotlight. Each represents people and
activity, but the authority, risk, participation path, and consequence differ.
Recomposition means retaining the useful function while redesigning category,
access, accountability, and maintenance for the new setting.

### People carry traditions; artifacts carry partial memory

Collaborators, participants, hosts, organizers, builders, editors, public
servants, residents, and institutional counterparts carry practices between
projects. A calendar, form, theme, database, letter, map, meeting record, or
repository can stabilize part of that knowledge. It cannot contain the whole
relationship or grant authority to speak for everyone involved.

### Feedback turns an assembly into a system

The operating loop is:

> encounter → observe → represent → circulate → act → receive feedback → revise → steward or hand off

Sunday Dinner's recurrence, WOW List's editing and support, coalition meetings,
KC Town Hall's field encounters and administrative record, KC Spaces Fund's
campaign routes and spotlights, and Knowledge Wiki corrections all make a next
iteration possible. Without feedback and a steward, components become static
artifacts rather than a system.

### Community change is project-specific

Components can lower the cost of entry, preserve memory, make a concern
shareable, create a route to public authority, direct material support, or keep
a next meeting possible. Those are credible mechanisms. Actual community
change must still be established project by project through attributable
records and participant accounts. A deployed form, a large database, a public
response count, a funding appropriation, or a fundraising result cannot by
itself establish causal impact.

## When not to reuse

Do not reuse a component merely because it worked, looked coherent, or is
available in the archive. Stop, redesign, or leave it behind when:

- the people affected do not recognize the problem, purpose, or decision right;
- the original relationship or participant record would be exposed without
  present permission;
- a category, form, or workflow would erase language, disagreement, access
  needs, cultural difference, or nonparticipation;
- the new host lacks time, trust, safety practice, funding, or maintenance
  capacity required by the social form;
- the component transfers technical control without transferring accountable
  governance or a correction path;
- institutional, legal, fiscal, property, or public-authority roles differ in a
  way the old design conceals;
- only chronology or visual resemblance supports the proposed lineage;
- the desired outcome cannot be observed honestly, or the cost and risk exceed
  the likely public benefit; or
- the community already has a form that should be supported rather than
  replaced.

Interoperability includes the ability to refuse an integration.

## What the Knowledge Wiki adds

The Knowledge Wiki turns tacit cross-project comparison into reviewable
infrastructure. It can represent a project, method, source, observation,
claim, decision, artifact, and projection as different objects; name a relation
type; retain contradictions and protected absence; and require evals before a
new synthesis is accepted.

For component reuse, the graph should prefer precise relations:

- `grew_from` for explicit provenance;
- `shared_artifact_with` or `shared_code_with` for literal reuse;
- `adapted_method_from` when a source documents conscious adaptation;
- `uses_method` or `has_component` for project-specific recurrence; and
- `structurally_similar_to` for an interpretive comparison that remains open.

Those relations should never collapse source access, evidence maturity,
sensitivity, consent, collective credit, publication authority, portfolio
selection, adoption, or impact into one state.

## Boundaries and open questions

- The projects do not form one organization, one product suite, one linear
  career plan, or one author's property.
- **Collective credit** remains project-specific. Richard Caceres shares WOW
  List co-builder credit; NYC Artist Coalition and KC Spaces Fund are
  collective formations with wider organizers and participants; Open House,
  waterways work, Sunday Dinner, 196, and KC Town Hall likewise require
  collaborator-specific attribution.
- The evidence supports direct lineage for only some edges. Similar components
  elsewhere remain recurrence or comparison until a source documents transfer.
- A participation opportunity is not equitable participation. A shared object
  can reproduce the power of hosts, editors, administrators, technical
  maintainers, funders, property holders, and institutions.
- Repository custody does not establish authorship, rights, consent, current
  availability, adoption, or publication permission.
- Observable project results do not prove that a particular component caused
  them or that every participant experienced community benefit.
- Collaborator review could clarify how methods traveled through people, which
  components were consciously reused, what should be credited differently,
  and which proposed edges should be deleted.
