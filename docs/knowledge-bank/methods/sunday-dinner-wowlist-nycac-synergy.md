# Sunday Dinner, WOW List, and NYC Artist Coalition Synergy

## Central finding

Sunday Dinner, WOW List, and New York City Artists Coalition used differently
scaled containers for a recurring practice: invite people into a legible
situation, help them contribute, make relationships and concerns shareable,
and create a next occasion on which the collective can continue. Sunday Dinner
organized embodied encounter; WOW List organized distributed discovery and
social memory; NYC Artist Coalition organized collective public agency.

The synergy lies in a reusable pattern of hosting, orientation, list-making,
facilitation, circulation, participatory naming, collaborative editing, and
recurring convening. The governing boundary is that continuity is not inevitability.

## Terminology note

This page uses **WOW List**, the project name established across the reviewed
source set. The incoming search term **Wildlist** appears only as one automated
transcript token amid repeated spoken and written references to WOW List. It is
kept as a retrieval term for this synthesis, not asserted as a separate project
or verified historical name.

## Three containers

### Sunday Dinner: recurring encounter

Sunday Dinner created a repeated reason for people to be physically present
together. A hosted gathering can support trust, informal knowledge,
recognition, and serendipity that a directory cannot create by itself. Public
milestones and protected aggregate review support a long-running series at
300-plus scale without exposing participant rows.

Hosting also concentrates power: invitation, format, atmosphere, place, and
the decision to continue all shape participation.

### WOW List: distributed discovery and memory

WOW List translated some relationships around recurring gathering into
durable, queryable, followable objects. Events, people, places, interests, and
lists could circulate beyond one room and one evening. Facilitators, editors,
guest writers, organizers, and maintainers connected those objects back to
personal invitation and in-person activity.

The [first WOW List provenance post](https://x.com/wowlist/status/433671630837919744)
credits Richard Caceres and Jamie Burkart and says WOW List grew from calendars
made at Sunday Dinner. The software extended memory and reach, but categories,
moderation, maintenance, unequal visibility, and access remained governance
questions.

### NYC Artist Coalition: collective public agency

The public chronology around Call Script, the January 2017 DCLA gathering,
participatory group naming, collaborative letter work, and a subsequent NYC
Artist Coalition meeting shows a shift from distributing cultural activity to
coordinating public action. Participants were invited not only to find an event
but to help name a collective, revise a shared intervention, and return for a
next meeting.

That record documents collective formation. It does not establish a legal
founding vote, complete labor allocation, attendance, mandate, policy impact,
or a single author of the coalition.

## The reusable social-technical pattern

> invitation → orientation → contribution → shared representation → circulation → convening → continuation

| Function | Sunday Dinner | WOW List | NYC Artist Coalition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation | A recurring hosted gathering | Personal outreach, onboarding, guest-host request, public support | Mobilization into a shared civic-cultural problem |
| Orientation | Host, place, ritual, and encounter | Introductions, explainable recommendations, guides, tutorial | Meeting frame, public questions, naming choices, action context |
| Contribution | Presence, conversation, informal exchange | Add/edit events, make/follow lists, write an introduction | Name a group, edit/sign a letter, join meetings |
| Shared representation | A remembered recurring occasion | Events, lists, profiles, digests, shared notes | Coalition name, event record, collaborative letter, meeting agenda |
| Circulation | Invitations and participant relationships | Embeds, email, notifications, social posts | Call Script, event discussion, participant sharing, advocacy |
| Convening | Return to the room | Discovery aimed toward real-world gathering | Agency gathering followed by a coalition meeting |
| Continuation | Another dinner | Maintained lists, another digest, another guest host | Recurring coalition coordination and public-agency engagement |

The pattern joins social form to information form. A gathering without memory
can dissipate. A directory without relationship can become noise. A coalition
without repeated, legible ways to contribute can become representational in
name only.

## What changed across the transitions

- **Encounter became durable memory:** WOW List made some activities and
  relationships discoverable between gatherings.
- **Hosted recurrence became distributed maintenance:** more people could add
  and tend records, while builders and administrators retained technical and
  governance authority.
- **Discovery became public agency:** the Call Script and NYC Artist Coalition
  record organized people around a public meeting, shared naming,
  collaborative writing, and continued civic action.
- **Belonging became explicit collective identity:** a dinner can cultivate a
  “we” informally; a naming process makes representation an explicit question.
- **Cultural circulation became civic address:** event information helped
  people find activity; coalition infrastructure helped a group address a
  public agency.

The public record supports a bounded bridge from WOW List's `popular.vote`
calendar into early coalition organizing. It does not establish that WOW List
caused NYC Artist Coalition.

## Knowledge-graph value

The graph can represent the synergy without flattening the projects:

1. **Practice lineage:** hosting, invitation, orientation, facilitation,
   editorial judgment, and continuation can be linked without treating the
   projects as one organization.
2. **Evidence complementarity:** protected operating records support scale and
   method; public social and institutional sources support attributable
   chronology; collaborator accounts can refine labor allocation.
3. **Reusable design knowledge:** the pattern can inform public-interest
   products and cultural organizing if each context reopens access, consent,
   governance, and benefit questions.
4. **Maintenance visibility:** the graph connects frontstage gatherings and
   public claims to backstage editorial, technical, administrative, and care
   labor.
5. **Collective credit:** Jamie's contribution remains legible without erasing
   Richard Caceres, other collaborators, hosts, guest writers, organizers,
   participants, and the wider collective formation.

## Boundaries and open questions

- Continuity is not inevitability, sameness, institutional succession, or
  causation.
- The record does not establish that WOW List caused NYC Artist Coalition or
  that a single person founded, represented, or produced the coalition.
- Jamie Burkart and Richard Caceres are co-credited in WOW List; wider labor
  across all three projects needs additional project-specific testimony.
- Event responses, reposts, production rows, and working-sheet marks do not
  prove attendance, endorsement, mandate, retention, or policy impact.
- An opportunity to participate does not prove equitable participation.
  Hosting, administration, categories, moderation, technology, time, and
  access can distribute power unevenly.
- Visibility is not consent, and a graph relation is not authorization to
  publish people, communications, or inferred affiliations.
- Further research should seek public post-200 Sunday Dinner chronology,
  collaborator accounts, native Call Script and coalition records, and early
  meeting materials.
