# NYC Artist Coalition

NYC Artist Coalition is a collective civic and cultural practice organized
around listening to artists and small cultural spaces, meeting in the places
where culture is made, translating lived problems into public questions and
campaigns, and carrying those questions into dialogue with elected officials
and agencies.

The coalition is not reducible to a single founder, spokesperson, campaign, or
website. Its work has depended on artists, venue hosts, organizers, advocates,
officials, staff, institutional partners, and recurring participants whose
roles changed over time.

## Operating pattern

The governed record supports a recurring sequence:

1. Convene at a small cultural space.
2. Listen to artists, venue workers, and organizers describe conditions in
   their own terms.
3. Use agendas, ballots, notes, and follow-up to identify priorities.
4. Translate priorities into explainers, testimony plans, call scripts,
   websites, public forums, and meetings.
5. Maintain enough public identity and campaign memory for collaborators to
   act through the work over time.
6. Return to the field and revise the agenda.

The coalition's campaigns include [Let NYC Dance](../projects/let-nyc-dance.md),
[Office of Nightlife town halls](../projects/office-of-nightlife-town-halls.md),
[Talks Not Raids](../projects/talks-not-raids-orientation.md),
[Fair Rent NYC](../projects/fair-rent-nyc.md), and
[Save NYC Spaces](../projects/save-nyc-spaces.md).

## Jamie's bounded role

The present evidence supports Jamie as a co-founding member who contributed
facilitation, recurring meetings, campaign identity systems, websites, source
and decision memory, public information artifacts, event production, and
follow-through. Retained code and working records support technical and
operational contributions at a finer grain than they support sole authorship
of coalition strategy, policy, or public language.

One of the strongest aspects of the work is stewardship beyond the initiator.
Coalition collaborators used and developed public identities and systems over
years. That continuity is a result of collective practice, not evidence that
Jamie authored every later use.

## What the coalition accomplished

Coalition work helped make cultural-space conditions visible and actionable in
public life. Public records document campaign activity around repeal of the
Cabaret Law, creation and shaping of the Office of Nightlife, transparency and
alternatives concerning M.A.R.C.H. inspections, commercial rent stabilization,
and cultural-space stability.

Government enactments remain government actions. The available record does not
quantify any one advocate's causal share or assign the coalition exclusive
responsibility for legislation, agency creation, enforcement reform, or outcomes.

## Present work

In 2026, Jamie's documented coalition lane includes campaign memory, legal and
legislative provenance, policy-neutral public-data framing, meeting synthesis,
event and signup infrastructure, cultural-space listening, and coordination
across current and historical partner networks. Protected working records
remain outside this public repository.

The July 29 report launch adds a compact example of that pattern in current
practice. Jamie moved between draft-review quality, delivered public advocacy,
event-information handoff, and legislative-staff follow-through while the
coalition, report publishers, organizers, researchers, and public offices kept
their own authority and credit. See the
[August 12 source return](../research-runs/commercial-rent-advocacy-source-return-2026-08-12.md).

## Boundaries

- Co-founding does not mean sole founding.
- Website implementation does not prove authorship of every word or decision.
- Public-official participation does not equal endorsement.
- A passed law or changed agency practice cannot be assigned to one advocate.
- Research authorization does not clear collaborator records for publication.
- A report acknowledgment supports careful-review credit, not report authorship
  or organizational endorsement of every claim.
