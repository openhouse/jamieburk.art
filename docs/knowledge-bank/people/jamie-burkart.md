# Jamie Burkart

Jamie Burkart is a technical project manager, product-operations practitioner,
web systems builder, artist, organizer, and archival producer. Across different
domains, he tends to enter situations where important knowledge is distributed
among people, documents, places, software, and institutional procedures. His
work is to listen, map the actual conditions, make a usable structure, support
participation or implementation, and leave a future reader a durable handoff.

This page is an orientation map. It does not convert every collective project
into an individual accomplishment or every archival trace into a public claim.

The [Jamie Burkart public-record source edition](../indexes/jamie-public-record-source-edition.md)
adds a deeper route into public words, public work, and explicitly unresolved
encounter sources. Its first implementation remains a private public-safe
candidate and is not a portfolio projection.

## Current professional practice

Jamie's present application frame emphasizes:

- technical project management and implementation;
- product operations and stakeholder translation;
- documentation architecture and source-backed operating memory;
- public-facing web and open-data systems;
- quality assurance, adoption support, and handoffs;
- participatory systems that connect community knowledge with public action.

See the [role-fit and referral map](../projections/role-fit-and-referral-map.md)
for the evidence boundaries around that present-tense argument.

The [professional record and reception index](../indexes/professional-record-and-reception.md)
keeps institutional facts, documented encounters, historical evaluations, and
third-party reception discoverable without turning co-presence or old records
into current recommendations or endorsements.

The [August 2026 Commercial Rent Stabilization source return](../research-runs/commercial-rent-advocacy-source-return-2026-08-12.md)

## Working throughline

The projects differ, but a repeated cycle is visible:

1. Observe the human and material situation.
2. Identify the hidden operating structure and unresolved questions.
3. Prototype an interface, event, document, workflow, or place.
4. Let people use it and expose what the first model missed.
5. Revise, document, and transfer the work.

The [practice timeline](../timelines/practice-2004-present.md) preserves this
continuity without claiming that every period was linear or every project
successful.

## Credit boundary

Jamie's projects include individual, shared, collective, and institutional
actions. A repository commit can establish technical authorship without proving
authorship of coalition policy. Participation can establish presence without
proving leadership. Institutional outcomes belong to the institutions that
enacted them, even when advocacy helped make the issue legible.

The applicable method is
[collective credit and protected absence](../methods/collective-credit-and-protected-absence.md).
