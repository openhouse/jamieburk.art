## Scope

This pass used Jamie Burkart's authenticated access to account for the complete
accessible population returned by one declared capture and counting method. In
this context, **100% archival production** means that every accessible
descendant inside that capture received an inventory record, a broad
classification, and exactly one disposition. It does not mean that every file
was opened, that every item was created by Jamie, that shared access grants
publication rights, or that a mutable shared folder has one timeless total.

The exact private manifest, titles, paths, identifiers, and selected document
bodies are preserved outside the public repository. This record publishes only
collection-level counts, methods, bounded findings, and next gates.

## Findings promoted from close reading

- A FairRentNYC implementation checklist attributes a concrete web and data
  workstream to Jamie, spanning responsive presentation, mapped and verified
  location data, forms, sponsor sequencing, press and partner modules, social
  presentation, and campaign calls to action.
- A companion campaign-architecture note shows Jamie translating multiple
  policy asks into prioritized actions, sponsorship-progress views, and
  campaign-leadership information needs. It supports product and information-
  architecture judgment; it does not prove that every proposed feature shipped.
- Selected public-testimony drafts show Jamie translating cultural-space
  operators' experience into concrete public-data and service-design proposals.
  Drafts alone do not establish delivery in a hearing; official records remain
  the preferred basis for public testimony claims.
- A digital action guide documents a coordinated, multi-channel participation
  system for Cabaret Law advocacy. It is collective campaign evidence and does
  not establish Jamie's sole authorship of the guide or campaign.
- A shared-folder FAQ describes the collection as a collaborative, evolving
  mutual-support resource. That supports the archive's collective purpose, not
  individual ownership.

## Held findings

- One internal testimony draft describes a nightlife town hall as drawing
  several hundred people. That figure is held because a more conservative
  independently reported estimate already exists and the draft is not an
  independent attendance record.
- The archive contains extensive visual, design, form, and event material. Its
  scale is not proof of Jamie's authorship, audience reach, or public-display
  rights.
- Exact collaborator roles, authorship allocations, private strategy, contact
  records, attendance records, and working correspondence remain protected
  until item-level review establishes a safe use.

## Method and gates

1. Traverse every accessible folder and union virtualized listings until no new
   items appear.
2. Assign each item one broad type and one disposition.
3. Close-read a risk- and opportunity-prioritized document set.
4. Decompose readings into assertions, limitations, candidate claims, and
   anti-claims.
5. Promote only claims with adequate support and an explicit public-use
   boundary.
6. Keep rights, consent, collaborator credit, Jamie approval, and purpose-built
   editorial selection as separate human gates.

The census is internally complete for the population returned by this method
during this review. Its cross-capture reconciliation, interpretation, rights,
and editorial work remain intentionally open.
