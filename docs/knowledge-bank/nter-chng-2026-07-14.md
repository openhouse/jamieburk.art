# NTER CHNG Archival Production

Date: 2026-07-14; updated 2026-07-15

## Result

Three public sources and two protected project artifacts now preserve NTER CHNG
in the knowledge bank.

The public sources are:

1. the project's own archived website;
2. America: Now and Here's archived official article about the installation;
   and
3. the Nerman Museum's preserved Kansas City Star page establishing the wider
   exhibition context.

The protected artifacts are:

1. a May 2011 installation runbook for the America: Now and Here restaging;
   and
2. a working document preserving 2010 exhibit information and 2011 prompts.

The second artifact contains participant phone numbers and message text. Its
underlying Google Drive locator and raw content are excluded from the public
repository and citation registry.

Together they support a strong, bounded account:

> NTER CHNG was a collaborative interactive texting installation by Drew
> Bolton, Jamie Burkart, and Garrett Fuselier. It was presented at Kansas
> City's Arts Incubator Cocoon Gallery in January 2010 and included in the 2011
> Kansas City presentation of America: Now and Here, where visitors' text
> messages became moving projected thought clouds on a floor-to-ceiling gauze
> screen.

This report preserves the project in the bank. It does not add a new portfolio
case study or public route.

## Recovered Project Record

The [January 28, 2011 Wayback
capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20110128193350/http://nterchng.com/) of
the project site states:

- the title is **NTER CHNG**;
- it is an interactive texting installation;
- the creators are Drew Bolton, Jamie Burkart, and Garrett Fuselier;
- it was presented at Arts Incubator Cocoon Gallery at 115 West 18th Street in
  Kansas City; and
- the site linked to a full press release and a Facebook event.

The page says the installation was open until January 24, but it does not print
the year or an opening date. The page was captured on January 28, 2011. The
capture date was initially held as an unresolved chronology clue; it is not the
exhibition date.

The linked press-release PDF was not captured in the Wayback collection. The
Facebook event remains behind a login surface and did not yield recoverable
event metadata in this pass.

## Protected Working Artifacts

### Original exhibition chronology

A project-authored exhibit-information section dates the original Cocoon
Gallery presentation to January 2010 and lists January 8 as the opening date.
That corrects the earlier January 2011 hypothesis, which had conflated the
Wayback capture date with the exhibition date.

The exact closing date remains unresolved:

- the protected exhibit document lists January 29, 2010; and
- the archived project page says the installation was open until January 24.

Until a contemporaneous venue calendar, preserved press release, event listing,
or collaborator confirmation resolves the discrepancy, the bank uses
**January 2010** without selecting an exact closing date.

### Integrated installation delivery

The May 2011 installer runbook identifies its purpose as staging NTER CHNG for
America: Now and Here. It organizes an integrated implementation across:

- server-side and wall-side software concerns;
- a software refinement intended to prevent rapid incoming messages from
  back-queuing;
- hosting, display computers, projectors, networking, and wiring;
- wall framing, surface fabrication, floor anchoring, and painting;
- testing materials and projection-mounting approaches;
- gallery installation and experience fine-tuning;
- documentation planning; and
- teardown.

The other protected document describes the work as equal parts software
application and architectural installation. It frames the interaction as a
shift from private one-to-one texting toward a shared many-to-many spatial
exchange.

These records establish project scope and planning. They do not assign each
task to Drew, Jamie, or Garrett, and the runbook is not independent proof that
every planned task was completed exactly as written.

## Official Exhibition Record

The Wayback CDX index for the former official exhibition domain surfaced an
especially valuable page: [I Text, Therefore I
Am](https://web.archive.org/web/20121017090512/http://americanowandhere.org/2011/06/i-text-therefore-i-am/),
published by America: Now and Here on June 22, 2011.

The article independently names NTER CHNG and describes the interaction:

- visitors texted a prominently displayed number;
- submitted messages appeared on a projected screen;
- the screen was white gauze stretched from floor to ceiling;
- messages appeared inside overlapping thought clouds;
- the clouds moved upward, disappeared, and returned at the bottom; and
- the author observed children and adults interacting with the installation.

This is direct evidence from the exhibition's own website that NTER CHNG was
part of America: Now and Here's Kansas City presentation. The official article
does not name the installation's creators, so the knowledge bank keeps creator
credit anchored to the separate project site.

The article contains anecdotal visitor messages and personal observations.
Those details are not needed to establish the project and are not reproduced in
the knowledge bank.

## Exhibition Context

The [Nerman Museum's preserved
page](https://www.nermanmuseum.org/exhibitions/2011-05-11-kruger-barbara-america-now-here.html)
reproduces Alice Thorson's April 30, 2011 Kansas City Star reporting. It
describes America: Now and Here as:

- a multidisciplinary national project launching in Kansas City;
- a program combining visual art, theater, film, poetry, and music;
- a structure pairing national participants with Kansas City counterparts;
- an attempt to foster public dialogue through the arts; and
- a May 2011 presentation that included the Barbara Kruger truck's Nerman
  Museum stop.

This source establishes the scale and purpose of the larger program. It does
not name NTER CHNG and is therefore contextual rather than direct evidence of
the installation's inclusion.

## What Became Usable

The recovered record makes an early participatory system legible.

NTER CHNG used a familiar personal action, sending a text message, as a
low-friction contribution pathway. Individual messages immediately changed a
shared visual environment visible to everyone in the gallery. The system
connected:

- personal devices;
- a public invitation;
- text-message intake;
- real-time transformation;
- projected spatial display; and
- collective observation.

The runbook adds the implementation layer behind that public interaction:
software behavior, hosting, display hardware, network and wiring concerns,
physical fabrication, installation testing, and gallery production had to
operate as one system.

That structure belongs in Jamie's participatory-public-systems throughline. It
shows interactive media functioning as a public participation surface rather
than as a private screen or one-way presentation.

This interpretation is editorial synthesis. Neither the creators nor the
exhibition used the phrase `participatory systems`, and the archive does not
assign Jamie sole responsibility for concept, software, visual design,
fabrication, installation, production, or documentation.

## Claim Discipline

### Ready for knowledge-bank use

- NTER CHNG was an interactive texting installation.
- Drew Bolton, Jamie Burkart, and Garrett Fuselier are jointly credited as its
  creators.
- It was presented at Arts Incubator Cocoon Gallery in Kansas City.
- It was included in America: Now and Here's 2011 Kansas City presentation.
- Visitors could text messages that became moving projected thought clouds on
  a floor-to-ceiling gauze screen.
- A project-authored exhibit document dates the original Cocoon Gallery
  presentation to January 2010 and lists January 8 as the opening date.
- The May 2011 restaging required an integrated software, hardware,
  fabrication, and gallery-production workflow.
- It is a bounded early example of Jamie's collaborative participatory-systems
  practice.

### Held for more research

- the exact original closing date;
- the division of artistic, technical, production, fabrication, and
  documentation labor;
- a claim that Jamie independently programmed or designed the system;
- commission or selection terms;
- participant, attendance, or reach totals;
- critical consensus or downstream impact; and
- publication of any recovered installation photograph.

## Collective Credit

Public wording must keep all three credited creators together:

> Drew Bolton, Jamie Burkart, and Garrett Fuselier created NTER CHNG.

The archive currently supports no more specific division of responsibility.
That does not diminish Jamie's participation. It protects the strongest fact
we have: Jamie is directly named as a creator of a technically mediated,
publicly participatory installation that was later documented by a national
multidisciplinary exhibition.

## Editorial Disposition

No immediate website change is warranted. The public portfolio already has a
clear launch argument, and NTER CHNG is presently most valuable as depth for:

- interactive-media and creative-technology opportunities;
- civic-technology and public-engagement roles;
- future participatory-public-systems composition;
- collaborator research;
- photo-editor briefs; and
- a fuller account of Jamie's long technical and cultural practice.

The protected artifacts strengthen that depth without changing the editorial
decision: no immediate website copy is added in this pass.

There is no public NTER CHNG route, proofs route, knowledge-bank route, or
archive-browser route.
