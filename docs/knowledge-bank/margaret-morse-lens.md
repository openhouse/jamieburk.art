# Prof Margaret Morse Lens

## Purpose

This lens asks whether the portfolio preserves the embodied, artistic, and
relational intelligence that helps explain Jamie's present systems practice.
It is not an invitation to make every project sound like art. It protects one
threshold where artistic, civic, technical, and social work remain visibly
connected.

## Source position

Jamie Burkart and Codex derived this contemporary editorial lens from the
historical sources below. It is not a quotation, testimonial, or evaluation
written, approved, or currently endorsed by Margaret Morse.

- An unofficial protected copy of UCSC narrative evaluations contains two 2006
  evaluations by Margaret Morse. It is educational evidence, not an official
  transcript and not a testimonial for public reproduction.
- A public 2006 Good Times profile independently documents Open House, its Shop
  Shows lineage, communal responsibility, participant contribution,
  hospitality, art/life inquiry, and multi-perspective documentation.
- A user-provided screenshot preserves a 2014 public-facing LinkedIn
  recommendation. Its canonical URL was not recovered, and the screenshot is
  not cleared for republication.

Raw evaluations, correspondence, student identifiers, contact information, and
the recommendation image remain outside the repository.

## Lens

Morse's evaluations repeatedly distinguish formal written compliance from
actual accomplishment. They describe Jamie learning through artifacts,
installation, performance, media history, and close attention. The public Open
House article adds the social method: a situation becomes workable because
someone tends it, people can participate, responsibility is shared, and the
documentation lets participants perceive the system they inhabit.

The portfolio should therefore preserve:

1. **Embodied inquiry.** Ideas can be tested through situations, artifacts,
   interfaces, and performance, not only described in prose.
2. **Art/life continuity.** Work, hospitality, public life, and cultural
   practice need not become separate professional identities.
3. **Participation.** People are not merely users of a finished system; they
   can help determine what it becomes.
4. **Attention and media memory.** Documentation and older media forms reveal
   how people perceive and perform within technical environments.
5. **Irreducible value.** Atmosphere, care, agency, connection, and place are
   not expendable because they resist an efficiency metric.
6. **A public face.** The depth must become accessible without publishing
   protected records or asking a hiring reader to decode a retrospective.

## Public composition

The smallest warranted repair is a concise present-method statement on About.
Historical detail stays in the bank. Existing public case studies then carry
the proof through hospitality, participation infrastructure, public identity,
collective decision-making, and humane handoff.

## Guardrails

- Do not use instructor praise as evidence of later outcomes.
- Do not publish the unofficial evaluations or correspondence.
- Do not romanticize risk, housing precarity, or other participants' lives.
- Do not erase collaborators from Open House, Shop Shows, or later collective
  practices.
- Do not add vague creativity language merely to satisfy the eval.
