## What Was Reviewed

The v04-E fieldwork reviewed a large private photo library locally and without
external pixel upload. Approximately 9,400 freshly inspected candidates were
reduced to a 4,000-image editor field through three rounds of visual evaluation,
safety review, duplicate reduction, and project-specific retrieval.

The first evaluation round failed. Generic social context, album or project
labels, and visual resemblance were too often standing in for evidence. Later
rounds required visible process plus plausible provenance and passed only after
those rules were tightened. This matters to the knowledge bank: a photo is
corroboration when it visibly shows relevant work and has an independently
reviewable project basis. It is not proof merely because it was retrieved near a
project, person, date, or place.

No private image, face association, caption, OCR text, asset identifier, exact
location, contact sheet, or library path is included in this repository.

## Defensible Discoveries

### A material and relational operating practice

Across the archive, Jamie's work is repeatedly situated where information,
people, and physical systems meet. The visual record includes software and
projected interfaces, labeled cards arranged for review, print and design work
in progress, tools and production equipment, public meetings, hospitality
infrastructure, and building-condition documentation.

**What this supports:** The existing claim that Jamie creates operating
structure across technical, civic, cultural, and small-business settings. The
photographs add visible texture to that throughline and show that "operations"
has often meant making information and environments usable, not only managing
tasks on a screen.

**What it does not establish:** A new job title, sole authorship, causality,
metrics, or ownership of collective outcomes.

### Harry J. Epstein Company

The reviewed field contains industrial retail and production context: stocked
shelves, tools and materials, warehouse or shop interiors, machinery, a
production press in use, and product knowledge exercised in the physical store.

**What this supports:** The modernization work took place inside a real legacy
operating environment with material inventory, specialized products, and
hands-on institutional knowledge. This corroborates the need to translate
between physical operations and e-commerce.

**What it does not establish:** Jamie's authorship of every depicted process,
the revenue-growth metric, or permission to publish identifiable workers or
private operating details.

### NYC Artist Coalition and FairRentNYC

The reviewed field includes public panels, speakers at microphones, campaign
stages, visible campaign signage, and audiences gathered around cultural-space
and commercial-rent advocacy.

**What this supports:** The coalition work had a sustained public-facing layer
of convening, explanation, communication, and collective advocacy. It gives
visual corroboration to the existing claims about civic systems, campaign web
infrastructure, and coalition operations.

**What it does not establish:** That Jamie organized every event, authored every
position, speaks for every collaborator, or solely caused a policy outcome.

### CallNYC

The reviewed field surfaced presentation rooms, projected interfaces, small
working sessions, a development terminal, and information arranged on cards for
group review.

**What this supports:** A credible visual context of civic-technology
prototyping, information architecture, presentation, and collaborative review.

**What it does not establish:** That every frame depicts CallNYC, that the
prototype was an official City service, or that the photographs alone prove the
product's authorship and functionality. Public code and coverage remain the
stronger sources for those claims.

### WOWList

The reviewed field includes public text displays, organizer-facing
presentations, press ephemera, and DIY arts or event materials arranged for
distribution and discussion.

**What this supports:** The platform belonged to a lived organizer ecosystem
and involved communication, explanation, and circulation beyond software
development alone.

**What it does not establish:** User or event counts, city adoption, current
platform status, or collaborator permission. Those claims require their
existing source bases.

### 196 and Sunday Dinner

The reviewed field repeatedly shows kitchens, prepared food, bread, tables,
courtyards, gardens, and the ordinary material setting of recurring
hospitality.

**What this supports:** Hosting was operational work: preparing space, food,
invitations, welcome, continuity, and conditions for participation. The visual
record helps prevent "community" from becoming an abstraction.

**What it does not establish:** Gathering or resident counts, comprehensive
participation, consent to identify guests, or permission to make intimate
domestic life public.

### KC Town Hall

The reviewed field includes the historic brick building, roof and masonry
conditions, damaged interiors, people moving through the site, a worker on the
roof, and public-room convening context.

**What this supports:** The project combined site documentation, adaptive-reuse
planning, public-benefit communication, and attention to material conditions
over time.

**What it does not establish:** Final funding, current property status, the
identity or authority of depicted people, or sole responsibility for
redevelopment work.

### Source-backed review and material design

The field also surfaced recurring visual forms of review: documents and cards
arranged on tables, handwritten source fragments, contact sequences, printed
pieces, cutting tools, collage, casts, and handmade objects under examination.

**What this supports:** Jamie's broader practice has long included arranging
source material so it can be compared, discussed, revised, and handed off. It
also supports a material design practice behind the site's colophon.

**What it does not establish:** That older images depict the 2026 Source-Backed
Team Memory method, that a private knowledge system has been deployed for a
client, or that an aesthetically similar scene has project provenance.

## What The Photos Corrected

- Generic portraits and social gatherings are not professional proof merely
  because a known person appears in them.
- Project albums, dates, People associations, and machine labels are retrieval
  aids, not narrative authority.
- A photograph can show a public event without proving who organized it.
- A photograph can show tools, code, or documents without proving authorship.
- A photograph cannot independently support revenue, attendance, adoption,
  funding, or policy-impact metrics.
- Domestic and hospitality work can be professionally relevant while still
  requiring especially conservative consent and privacy review.
- Rare or visually quiet software work may have fewer usable photographs than
  physically visible work. Absence of a strong image is not absence of work.

## Projection Guidance

Use this note to inform selection and captions, not as a substitute for source
verification.

Before any photograph reaches the website, resolve photographer rights,
participant consent, collaborator or client approval, artwork reproduction,
caption accuracy, credit, crop, alt text, and sensitive context. Keep private
homes, minors, health or financial material, identity documents, legal
strategy, credentials, private screens, exact locations, and vulnerable
collaborator context out of public projection unless separately and explicitly
cleared.

The strongest future use is a small number of specific images that show Jamie's
work or its material setting clearly. Do not publish the editor field, a private
archive browser, or an undifferentiated gallery.
