# People, Places, And Community Testimony

This index records public participation structures without publishing a social
graph of private people. It treats places and testimony formats as historical
actors while preserving the difference among invitation, presence, speech,
response, and policy outcome.

## Places As Civic Infrastructure

Ten recovered recurring coalition meetings name ten physical cultural spaces:
Magick City, The Floasis, Muchmore's, The City Reliquary, Shoestring Press,
Chinatown Soup, Secret Project Robot, Friends and Lovers, Flowers for all
Occasions, and Ode to Babel. Other events moved through City Hall, DCLA, FDNY
certification space, Market Hotel, streets, churches, Pratt Institute, and
virtual rooms.

The sequence supports a rotating-venue practice. It does not establish an
uninterrupted monthly schedule, identical participation, or Jamie's production
role at every location.

## Ways People Could Enter The Work

- tell a short first-person story at a hearing;
- email testimony when appearing in person was not possible;
- attend a coalition meeting or town hall;
- join a Fire Guard study or certification session;
- bring a venue concern into a listening or working-group process;
- answer a neighborhood survey;
- contribute a river story or artistic work;
- call an elected representative through a prepared public route;
- return to a later meeting for report-back and revision.

These are documented participation affordances. Their existence does not prove
who used them, how many people participated, what they said, or what outcome a
specific contribution caused.

## Public People And Protected People

Public event records may name an elected official, commissioner, organizer,
speaker, or institutional representative when that role is relevant and
already public. Being named or scheduled does not prove attendance,
endorsement, agreement, or influence.

Guest lists, attendee identities, subscriber records, applicant data, donor
records, resident contact information, private testimony, comments, reactions,
and inferred relationship graphs do not belong in this public repository.
Community testimony should enter the Wiki only through consented quotation,
public testimony, responsibly summarized aggregate evidence, or another
explicitly governed route.

## Research Need

The next valuable layer is consented collaborator and participant memory about
how particular spaces changed what could be heard or decided. That work should
begin with a private interview and permissions workflow, not automatic
extraction from social-media proximity.
