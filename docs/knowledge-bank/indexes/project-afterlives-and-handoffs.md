# Project Afterlives And Handoffs

Projects do not all end in the same way. This index keeps archival survival,
continued collective activity, organizational transition, administrative
disposition, later exhibition, and editorial retirement separate.

| Project | Current supported state | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CallNYC | An archived independent prototype with surviving implementation and contemporaneous reporting; it is not an official or current Council service. | A public archive can document the project without making its old guidance current. |
| NYC Artist Coalition | Surviving public records document collective events and publishing through 2021, plus campaign and policy histories. | The Wiki does not declare the coalition ended, assign every later activity, or speak for current collaborators. |
| KC Spaces Fund | A historical 2020 collaborator-led mutual-aid campaign with a surviving public outcome and Jamie's bounded digital-infrastructure contribution. | Named organizers retain organizer credit; naming chronology and some division of labor remain open. |
| KC Town Hall | Government records establish the 2019 appropriation and a later unused-allocation action. Jamie separately recalls transitioning stewardship to a mission-aligned organization. | The recipient and date of the transition are not publicly corroborated. The municipal action and organizational transition are not the same event. Private family circumstances are intentionally outside this account. |
| NTER CHNG | Public archived records preserve joint creator credit and later inclusion in America: Now and Here. | Protected production records do not allocate every task or authorize participant-message publication. |
| Water Publics | The 2007 raft record has a documented later life in Great Accommodations, which reframed river travel as a participatory installation and public-program structure. | The connection is documented project development, not a measured account of long-term community impact. |
## Handoff Questions

For every project, ask:

- What artifact, practice, organization, or relationship continued?
- Who had authority after Jamie's participation changed?
- Is the transition publicly documented, privately remembered, or unresolved?
- Does an administrative record describe the social handoff, or only a legal or
  budget action?
- What must remain absent because naming it would expose private circumstances
  without improving the professional account?
## Editorial Rule

An unfinished record is not a failed project, and an archive is not proof of a
current service. A handoff can demonstrate stewardship, but only when the
recipient, timing, and scope are supported at the level used publicly. The Wiki
may preserve a protected or attributed transition lead without making it the
portfolio's conclusion.
