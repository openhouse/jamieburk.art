# Relational Infrastructure Atlas

This is a human-readable guide to relationships already represented in the
Knowledge Wiki. It is not a second graph, a private relationship map, or a
claim that every meaningful relationship can be reduced to an edge.

| Relation | What it can establish | What it cannot establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project uses method | A documented practice appears in a project record. | That Jamie invented it alone or applied it identically everywhere. |
| Claim uses source | A bounded source relationship has been reviewed. | That the source supports every nearby sentence or grants publication rights. |
| Event is located at place | A public record associates an occasion with a site. | Attendance, endorsement, ownership, safety, or policy effect. |
| Project supports capability | A project offers evidence for a professional reading path. | A retroactive formal title or exclusive responsibility. |
| Record projected to surface | Editorial selection connects governed knowledge to public composition. | Permission to expose protected evidence or every mature claim. |

## Four routes through the atlas

- **Occasion to civic participation:** a cultural venue, invitation,
  facilitation pattern, public testimony, and later civic route may form a
  documented sequence without proving that every invitee attended or one
  gathering caused a policy result.
- **Information to resident action:** [CallNYC](../projects/callnyc.md) connects
  an official data release, independent interpretation, issue pages, reporting,
  and bounded Council-account amplification. It does not establish Council
  adoption or constituent outcomes.
- **Fragment to governed memory:** source-backed memory connects an artifact to
  a source, bounded claim, anti-claim, inquiry, and projection decision even
  when the claim remains held.
- **Maintenance to handoff:** continued operation, repair, archival survival,
  retirement, administrative action, and social handoff are related but not
  interchangeable.

This public atlas omits contact networks, attendee identities, inferred
friendships, private messages, resident records, donor or subscriber data, and
collaborator-private revision history. The generated graph is an index, not an
argument; this page supplies a bounded interpretation for people reading it.
