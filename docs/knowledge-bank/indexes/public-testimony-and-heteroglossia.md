# Public Testimony and Heteroglossia

This index preserves public testimony as a field of distinct attributed
voices. It is meant to support research, speech preparation, historical
orientation, and later claim development without turning collective action
into a single-person story.

## Corpus

The machine-readable files in `data/public-testimony/` preserve:

- every detected speaker and transcript label;
- a reviewed issue-position disposition;
- every attributed turn, in source order, for speakers classified as
  supportive or supportive in part, including public officials as well as
  public witnesses;
- page ranges, word counts, source hashes, and attribution notes;
- census-only records for opposed, mixed, out-of-scope, procedural, and
  unresolved speakers.

As of July 28, 2026, the corpus covers nine Council transcripts and includes
complete attributed turns for 227 supportive or partly supportive speakers.
The same inclusion rule applies to public officials and members of the public:
an official title, sponsorship record, favorable vote, or presence alone is
not substituted for an attributable supportive statement.

## Commercial Rent Stabilization

The Commercial Rent Stabilization sequence includes:

1. [Int. 1796-2019](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=B2FF2D24-F642-42A1-BEBB-D8D59E079D99&ID=4230081),
   introduced on November 14, 2019;
2. the September 17, 2021 hearing, including prime sponsor Stephen Levin's
   opening statement and 38 supportive or partly supportive full-text speaker
   records;
3. [Int. 93-2022](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=0FA34310-33D2-4090-9A39-2396910C9EB5&ID=5534186),
   reintroduced on March 24, 2022, with supportive statements by Crystal
   Hudson and Carlina Rivera preserved in the stated-meeting transcript; and
4. [Resolution 496-2026](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=8F4D668A-9741-4F3F-9EC4-2F84CC8592C6&ID=8067758),
   introduced by Tiffany Cabán in support of the current state Small Business
   Rent Stabilization Act.

The 2019 stated-meeting transcript does not contain a recovered oral Int. 1796
introduction statement from Levin. The 2022 transcript does not contain a
recovered oral Int. 93 introduction statement from prime sponsor Diana Ayala.
The 2026 Council record currently supplies resolution text and an agenda, not
a stated-meeting transcript. Those absences remain explicit.

## How to Use It

These are normalized official-transcript bodies, not polished quotations or
independent fact checks. Before quoting:

1. return to the page locator in the official transcript;
2. preserve the named speaker and organizational context;
3. disclose `[inaudible]`, `[sic]`, and transcript uncertainty;
4. verify any factual assertion through an appropriate independent or primary
   source;
5. distinguish a speaker's position from coalition consensus, bill passage,
   implementation, or causation.

The value of the corpus is not unanimity. It is the recoverable public
conversation: people naming what they saw, what they needed, what they feared,
and what they believed government should do.

See the [July 28, 2026 public-testimony source
return](../research-runs/public-testimony-source-return-2026-07-28.md) for the
speaker census, inclusion method, source representations, limits, and
librarian requests.
