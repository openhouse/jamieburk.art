# Maintenance And Care

Maintenance is operational work that preserves usability, safety, continuity,
memory, or a responsible transition over time. This page makes that work
visible without treating care as an unmeasured virtue or assigning every
sustaining task to Jamie.

## Modes Of Maintenance

| Mode | Governed example | What the record supports | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Physical repair and sequencing | KC Town Hall Phase One | A project-authored packet documents a multi-trade stabilization scope; Jamie attributes daily site and trade coordination to himself. | The packet does not name the general contractor or establish formal closeout, and the work remains collective across specialists and neighborhood partners. |
| Digital continuity | KC Spaces Fund | Implementation records support Jamie maintaining the Ghost site, theme changes, fundraising widget, deployment configuration, and web-action routes. | Maintenance does not make him the public organizer, fundraiser owner, social publisher, or grant decision-maker. |
| Recurring participation | NYC Artist Coalition | Event and working-archive records support repeated meetings, testimony preparation, campaign routes, safety support, and report-back practices across changing spaces. | The collective corpus does not allocate every event, task, post, or decision to Jamie. |
| Information stewardship | Commercial-rent collaboration | Protected records support a privacy-aware working record organized around decisions, actions, questions, ownership, and consent levels. | The artifact does not establish Jamie's sole authorship of every entry or permission to publish participant information. |
| Governed memory | Source-Backed Team Memory and Knowledge Wiki | The method preserves source relationships, anti-claims, review states, and continue-revise-stop decisions so work can be revisited. | A maintained archive is not automatically complete, current, public, consented, or adopted. |
| Responsible transition | Project afterlives and handoffs | The Wiki distinguishes continued activity, archival survival, handoff, administrative action, and retirement. | A handoff is supported only at the documented level; private circumstances may remain intentionally absent. |

## Care Is Not Background

Repairing a building, maintaining a domain and deployment, preparing recurring
rooms and participation routes, carrying forward a decision record, and handing
off stewardship are different forms of care. They should not be collapsed into
one metric, but neither should they disappear behind a launch date.

Maintenance often makes another person's participation possible: a safe route
into a building, a functioning form, a readable packet, a meeting with known
next steps, a source that can be revisited, or a transition that does not depend
on one person's continued presence.

## Evidence And Credit

The evidence does not support a single continuous maintenance role across all
projects. Each example retains its own source class, role wording, time period,
collaborators, and unknowns. Repetition across the archive supports a practice
of tending systems; it does not prove sole responsibility, emotional intent, or
measured benefit in every case.

Care language must not obscure labor allocation. Architects, engineers,
tradespeople, organizers, venue teams, public servants, collaborators,
participants, and later stewards retain their work and authority.

## Editorial Use

Use this page when a hiring or project narrative overemphasizes invention and
launch. A defensible account should ask what Jamie kept usable, what required
recurring attention, what he documented for others, and how responsibility
changed over time.

This synthesis returns to governed claims and existing source reviews rather
than reopening every underlying artifact. It creates no new rights, role,
completion, or impact claim.
