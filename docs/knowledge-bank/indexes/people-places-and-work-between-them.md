# People, Places, and the Work Between Them

“Stakeholder” is too thin for many of these records. A gallery, river, store,
home, cultural venue, job site, or public chamber is not scenery around a
deliverable. Each setting changes what can be known, who can participate, and
which forms of authority become visible.

## Situated Records

- **Open House at Porter College:** Jamie initiated a frame for shared
  responsibility; participants made the inhabited project. The surviving
  article does not provide a complete roster or make every experience
  equivalent.
- **Rivers and public encounters:** [NTER CHNG](../projects/nter-chng.md) and
  waterways work treated travel and exchange as ways to encounter relations
  among cities, infrastructure, and residents. Collective labor remains
  collective.
- **The Harry J. Epstein store:** staff, customers, catalog language, paper
  records, and routines carried knowledge that digital implementation needed
  to preserve rather than replace.
- **Homes, dinners, and residency:** [Sunday Dinner and 196](../projects/sunday-dinner.md)
  used recurring hospitality as a medium. Attendance records are not a public
  relationship directory.
- **Rotating cultural spaces:** the [NYC Artist Coalition](../projects/nyc-artist-coalition-2017.md)
  organized through places whose experience informed campaigns and public
  events. The archive does not settle every venue decision or contribution.
- **KC Town Hall and its block:** physical work and neighborhood encounters
  occurred together. Listening-driven intent is supported; private family
  context and resident-level records remain outside the public Wiki.

Record who did what only at the resolution the sources support. Name public
institutional roles where appropriate, retain collective scope where work was
shared, and preserve protected absence where naming a person, relationship, or
location would exceed consent or public purpose.

This page is not a collaborator directory, private relationship map, or claim
that proximity equals consent, representation, influence, or endorsement.
