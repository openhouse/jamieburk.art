# Source-channel coverage

This is a public-safe control view. It reports source classes and maturity, not
private locators, correspondence contents, participant identities, or access
credentials. Raw evidence stays in its lawful source system or private vault.

| Source channel | Coverage date | Access state | Normalization | Identity resolution | Public-use boundary | Known gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Official public web | 2026-07-18 | available | selective captures | source-level | Public facts may be cited with date and scope | Link rot and changing pages require reverification |
| Press | 2026-07-18 | partial | article-level readings | publication and byline where known | Public metadata and bounded findings | Paywalls, removed pages, and incomplete archives |
| Public repositories and project code | 2026-07-18 | partial | repository and commit history | author identity where explicit | Public code may support implementation scope | Repository survival does not prove complete authorship or impact |
| Public data | 2026-07-18 | selective | project-specific | dataset provenance | Cite dataset limits inside product claims | Historical snapshots may be incomplete |

| Gmail | 2026-07-18 | private-vault only | partial | unresolved where needed | No raw correspondence in this repo | Coverage and consent are uneven |

## Control rules

- A located source is not automatically normalized, interpreted, publishable,
  or important.
- A source count is not a quality score.
- Private communication may guide a bounded inquiry without entering the
  public repository.
- Original-source review is required when a priority-role gap cannot be closed
  responsibly from existing governed evidence.
