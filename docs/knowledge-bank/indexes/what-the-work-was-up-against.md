# What the Work Was Up Against

Projects become legible when the situation appears before the response. This
page names recurring pressures without treating residents, artists, workers,
customers, neighborhoods, or collaborators as deficits. People and places were
holders of knowledge and agency; the problem was often that an existing system
could not receive, preserve, or act on what they knew.

| Project | Situation and pressure | Bounded response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [CallNYC](../projects/callnyc.md) | Official constituent-services data was public, but a raw release did not itself give residents an issue pathway or next step. | Jamie independently interpreted the records as resident-facing issue pages. The prototype was not an official Council product, and adoption is not established. |
| [NYC Artist Coalition](../projects/nyc-artist-coalition-2017.md) | Artists and small cultural spaces described enforcement, licensing, affordability, and representation problems that did not reliably enter institutional language. | Coalition listening, events, campaigns, testimony, and digital tools helped carry those accounts into public processes. Outcomes and credit remain campaign-specific and collective. |
| [Harry J. Epstein Company](../projects/harry-j-epstein.md) | Specialized product and service knowledge lived in people, paper, store rituals, and an inherited catalog that generic modernization could flatten. | Jamie translated parts of that knowledge into searchable commerce and operating workflows while preserving continuity. Component attribution still needs corroboration. |
| [WOWList](../projects/wowlist.md) | Cultural information was distributed across organizers, inboxes, calendars, and social ties; finding and sharing relevant events required repeated manual work. | Jamie and Richard developed a community calendar with followed interests, shared editing, and reusable distribution. The surviving evidence does not settle every feature decision or adoption claim. |
| [Sunday Dinner and 196](../projects/sunday-dinner.md) | Artists needed recurring occasions and places where relationships and work could develop outside a single transactional program. | Hosted gatherings and residency practice created situated participation. Attendance does not by itself prove representation or later impact. |
| [KC Town Hall](../projects/kc-town-hall.md) | A long-disused building required physical stabilization while its future public use remained a neighborhood question rather than a settled program. | Restoration work, site encounters, listening, and survey infrastructure were brought together. Private transition context remains protected. |
| [NTER CHNG and participatory public systems](../projects/participatory-public-systems.md) | Waterways, cities, and cultural publics were connected while ordinary systems often obscured those relations. | Collective travel, exchange, installation, gathering, and public encounters made connection experiential. Sources do not support sole authorship or every intended outcome. |

The recurring pattern is a failure of reception: an institution cannot yet hear
a community account, a dataset cannot yet answer a resident's question, a
business system cannot yet carry staff knowledge, or a handoff cannot yet
preserve why a workflow exists. Jamie's response is an interface among people,
records, places, and authority, not a claim to speak for everyone involved.

This comparison is interpretive. It does not make hardship a portfolio asset,
cast a community as broken, or imply that one person supplied all the knowledge
or authority required for change.
