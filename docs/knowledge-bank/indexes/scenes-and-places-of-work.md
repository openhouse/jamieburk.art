# Scenes and Places of Work

Place is not background, and a scene is not proof by itself. These are
source-backed retrieval seeds, not reconstructed dialogue, fictional
interiority, attendance evidence, or permission to identify everyone present.

## Basement to roof

At KC Town Hall, Jamie describes arriving with measured drawings, survey cards,
and daily coordination questions, moving among trades and dependencies across
the building. The record supports on-site coordination and a multidisciplinary
Phase One scope; it does not establish a license or personal performance of
every trade.

## Translating the tool store

At Harry J. Epstein, paper, phone, catalog, dealer, surplus, inventory, and
customer-service knowledge had to become searchable and maintainable digital
structure without losing the store's voice. The scene supports long-term
translation and stewardship, not sole responsibility for every layer or
business result.

## Rooms where culture and government met

Coalition meetings and town halls moved through small cultural spaces. The
changing venues helped cultural-space experience enter public testimony,
campaigns, agency conversations, and legislative work. Event listing does not
prove attendance, endorsement, or one person's authorship of the room.

## Kitchens, waterways, and recurring invitations

Sunday Dinner made repetition, invitation, hosting, and follow-up into a social
structure. Waterways projects treated movement and public encounter as parts of
the work. Protected attendance and participant records remain outside this
retrieval surface.

## Returning to records

In source-backed memory work, a transcript, file, or public source becomes a
bounded claim, open question, decision trail, and handoff. The professional
value is the next decision becoming more inspectable, not the archive becoming
a spectacle.

## Scene rule

Before using a scene publicly, return to its source, actor, time, credit,
privacy, image-rights, and outcome boundary. A vivid scene cannot replace
evidence.
