# Jamie Burkart testimony on Cabaret Law oversight, June 19, 2017

## Official transcript text

The text below is reflowed from committee transcript pages 200-202. Wording,
speaker label, bracketed notation, spelling, and apparent transcription errors
are preserved.

> **JAMIE BURKHART:** My name is Jamie Burkhart. I’m a member of the New York
> City Artist Coalition. I am asking New York City Council to repeal the Cabaret
> Law. The Cabaret Law was created in 1926 to halt interracial dancing in jazz
> clubs. It was used by Mayor Giuliani in the ‘90s to target and shut our gay
> bars decimating culture. This civil rights issue law was used time and time
> again. It’s still on the books and it’s prejudicial history is still felt
> today. Because of the Cabaret Law there are no legal places to dance to
> Bed-Stuy nor El Barrio for instance. There are zero cabaret licenses in
> Council Member Cumbo’s district where I live nor in Speaker Melissa
> Mark-Viverito’s. Today there are fewer than 100 active cabaret licenses in all
> five boroughs. There is nowhere legal to dance in the vast majority of New
> York’s neighborhoods. Everywhere our communities gather, at a friend’s
> performance, at a coffee shop or at a wedding in a restaurant, it’s almost
> certainly illegal to dance.
>
> My life as an advocate began with the loss of another. My good friend Nico
> Marshal (sic). He was one of 36 people killed in the Ghost Ship tragedy
> earlier this year. From the minute I heard that he was missing, I knew he was
> gone. They all were. I was filled with shock and grief. Our first response was
> safety. We facilitated fire safety walk-throughs and workshops. Our study
> groups for the Fire Department’s Fire Guard Certification exam have a 100%
> acing on pass rate. Working directly with spaces we found that though they
> were up to code, some were afraid to engage with the Fire Department because
> they knew that they didn’t have a Cabaret License and feared retribution. For
> those who claim the Cabaret Law is about safety, we know what makes community
> spaces safe, and it is not a ban on dancing. Improve the relationship with
> trust to save lives. Repeal the Cabaret Law. [bell] Give low-income
> communities spaces seats on the nightlife task force.
>
> For those who say the Cabaret Law is not being enforced, it can easily be
> used in the future by extreme conservative groups to arbitrarily shut down
> spaces. In the past few months there have been targeted outright attacks
> against community spaces in New York. They use the Internet to incite others
> to call authorities on spaces anonymously. They posted my home address on
> their website. At lest one space I know of was visited by authorities. A
> teen-age prankster in Wyoming can shut down spaces in New York with this
> outdated law. Repeal the Cabaret Law, legalize dance. Don’t Ask Don’t Tell
> isn’t good enough.

## Editorial reading notes

- The official transcript labels Jamie “Burkhart”; his name is Jamie Burkart.
- “Nico Marshal” is an apparent mistranscription of Nick Gomez-Hall. The
  correction is supported by Jamie's later testimony and public reporting.
- The 100 percent Fire Guard pass-rate statement remains attributed testimony,
  not an independently audited metric.

## Boundary

This record establishes that Jamie testified and what the official transcript
attributes to him. It does not establish his causal share in the hearing,
Office of Nightlife, Cabaret Law repeal, or any other collective or
institutional outcome.
