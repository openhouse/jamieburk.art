# CallNYC restored archived prototype, August 2026

CallNYC now opens with a clear warning: `Archived project demo (snapshot). Not
official, not current.` That boundary matters because the restored interface is
otherwise deliberately action-oriented. The home ranks historical Council
members by recorded constituent-service activity, names their leading issue
areas, and offers telephone pathways. A representative Heat/Hot Water page
repeats the pattern for one resident need: issue first, then ten historical
office pathways, service-volume context, related competencies, and a direct
call action.

This is useful evidence of Jamie's product translation. The interface did not
merely visualize a dataset; it organized public records around resident intent
and a possible next action. The sitemap contains 518 locators across housing,
transportation, sanitation, benefits, immigration, public safety, and other
issue families. That route count describes the restored static surface; it is
not the same measure as the separately documented 61 issue pages distributed
through recovered CallNYC social posts.

The restored archive also preserves risk that the portfolio must not hide. The
pages include historical officeholders, telephone numbers, and `past year`
language. The sitemap uses `http` locators and `weekly` change frequency, while
`/robots.txt` returned site HTML rather than a valid robots policy during this
review. The archive warning is present on the home and representative issue
page, but the site is not observed to be noindex.

The portfolio may link directly to the archive when it keeps `archived`,
`unofficial`, and `not current` visible. The live site does not establish
current guidance, official City status, Council adoption, usage, or resident
outcomes.
