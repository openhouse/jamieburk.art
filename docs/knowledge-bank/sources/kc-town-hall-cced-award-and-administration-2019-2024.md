# KC Town Hall CCED Award and Administration, 2019-2024

## Research Question

What is the strongest public-safe account of Jamie Burkart's role in securing
KC Town Hall's $490,539 CCED award and sustaining the project through the later
pre-disbursement process?

## Public Source Chain

1. Kansas City's June 12, 2019 CCED hearing minutes identify Jamie as KC Town
   Hall's developer/presenter for a $490,539 request.
2. The July 16, 2019 minutes record the Board's unanimous vote to recommend the
   full request.
3. Resolution 190649 records Council acceptance and authority to negotiate a
   conditional funding agreement.
4. Ordinance 190642 appropriates the exact amount.
5. A December 2021 Kansas City Star investigation leads with KC Town Hall and
   reports repeated City Hall roadblocks, an incomplete final contract, and
   newly raised insurance and tax-document requirements.
6. City-posted 2022 and 2023 pre-initial reports name Jamie and project counsel
   as developer points of contact and document progress on M/WBE and CREO
   requirements.
7. Ordinance 230316 established timelines for incentivized projects; the
   December 12, 2023 project update applied a September 26, 2024 expiration to
   KC Town Hall and still reported no funds disbursed.
8. Ordinance 240317 describes KC Town Hall as an awarded project, records its
   withdrawal, and returns the full unused appropriation to the fund.

The protected 2019 proposal independently supplies shared project context: it
identifies Jamie Burkart and Julia Fredenburg as founders and project managers
and requests $490,539 for Phase Two. Its raw contents and locator remain
private.

## Public-Safe Conclusion

Jamie secured a $490,539 public funding award for KC Town Hall by co-developing
the successful CCED proposal and serving as the City's named
developer/presenter for the exact request.

This is an action-plus-result claim. It does not assign Jamie the Board's or
Council's legal authority, claim sole authorship or sole causation, or imply
that the funding agreement was completed. Official records show no
disbursement and return of the full unused appropriation after withdrawal.

## Stewardship Conclusion

City-posted records also establish that Jamie remained a named developer point
of contact during the multi-year pre-disbursement process and that the project
made measurable progress from unresolved M/WBE requirements to established
goals and active CREO coordination.

The records do not establish who performed every task, why the project later
withdrew, or Jamie's role in the final withdrawal.

## Mission-Aligned Transition

Jamie authorizes the bounded professional statement that he transitioned the
project to a mission-aligned organization. This is a first-person account, not
independent verification of a legal transfer. Public use should not identify
the receiving organization or infer the date, legal form, scope, acceptance,
municipal approval, or relationship to the City's separate withdrawal record.
