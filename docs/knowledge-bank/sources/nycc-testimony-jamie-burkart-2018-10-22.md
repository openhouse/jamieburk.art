# Jamie Burkart commercial-rent testimony, October 22, 2018

## Official transcript text

The text below is reflowed from committee transcript pages 346-348. Wording,
speaker label, bracketed notation, spelling, and apparent transcription errors
are preserved.

> **JAMIE BURKART:** Hello. I’m Jamie Burkart. I’m a member of the New York City
> Artist Coalition, and I live in Fort Greene, Clinton Hill. It’s great to
> see-- it’s been great today to see so many Council Members supporting SBJSA
> and that we’re having this hearing. So, thank you very much. And I wanted to
> say thank you to the City’s First Director of Night Life who is here, Ariel
> Pallets [sp?]. She spent a couple of hours sitting up in the balcony. So,
> thank you to the Administration for creating that office.
>
> I’m here because New York City’s cultural spaces are small businesses, and
> cultural spaces close due to rent. My life as an advocate began because of
> the loss of another. My friend, Nick Gomez Hall [sp?], who is one of the 36
> people killed in Oakland’s tragic Ghost Ship fire. When a City’s residents
> can’t afford appropriate commercial spaces for culture, our diverse cultures
> are forced out of our neighborhoods. We’re forced out of business, or we’re
> forced underground into less safe, exploitative slumlord situations. As a
> safety advocate, affordability is a life and death issue for culture in New
> York.
>
> Cherished community spaces can’t get a fair lease here and are closing.
> Silent Barn [sic] closes here due to rent increases. Madeeba [sic] South
> African restaurant down the street from me closed due to rent increases.
> Barcepia [sic] closed when the landlord refused to come to the table for lease
> negotiations. Treasured neighborhood institutions must be able to afford to
> thrive in New York. When we lose places to come together, we lose New York.
> Culture needs the right to renew its lease in New York City. In Paris, you can
> still go to the café where Sart [sp?] and de Beauvoir wrote, but the spaces in
> the Bronx and Harlem where hip-hop was born have closed. They’re not open
> anymore. We need commercial rent protections. Save the places that make New
> York, New York with fair leases and fair rent. Pass the Small Business Job
> Survival Act, Intro number 737. Thank you.

## Editorial reading notes

- “Ariel Pallets” is an apparent mistranscription of Ariel Palitz.
- “Nick Gomez Hall” refers to Nick Gomez-Hall.
- “Madeeba,” “Barcepia,” and “Sart” appear to refer to Madiba, Bar Sepia, and
  Sartre. The official spellings remain untouched in the transcript block.
- Venue-closure causes remain attributed testimony unless a separate source
  independently supports them.

## Boundary

This record establishes Jamie's participation and transcribed argument. It does
not establish Council endorsement, enactment of the proposed act, or Jamie's
causal share in the broader commercial-rent movement.
