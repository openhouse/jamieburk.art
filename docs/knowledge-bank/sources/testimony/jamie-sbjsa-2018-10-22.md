# Jamie Burkart testimony on commercial lease protections, October 22, 2018

## Source note

The reading copy preserves the official stenographic wording with normalized
line breaks. Apparent name and place transcription errors remain visible
rather than being silently treated as fact.

## Full official reading copy

> Hello. I'm Jamie Burkart. I'm a member of the New York City Artist Coalition,
> and I live in Fort Greene, Clinton Hill. It's been great today to see so many
> Council Members supporting SBJSA and that we're having this hearing. So,
> thank you very much. And I wanted to say thank you to the City's First
> Director of Night Life who is here, Ariel Pallets. She spent a couple of hours
> sitting up in the balcony. So, thank you to the Administration for creating
> that office.
>
> I'm here because New York City's cultural spaces are small businesses, and
> cultural spaces close due to rent. My life as an advocate began because of
> the loss of another. My friend, Nick Gomez Hall, who is one of the 36 people
> killed in Oakland's tragic Ghost Ship fire. When a City's residents can't
> afford appropriate commercial spaces for culture, our diverse cultures are
> forced out of our neighborhoods. We're forced out of business, or we're
> forced underground into less safe, exploitative slumlord situations. As a
> safety advocate, affordability is a life and death issue for culture in New
> York.
>
> Cherished community spaces can't get a fair lease here and are closing.
> Silent Barn closes here due to rent increases. Madeeba South African
> restaurant down the street from me closed due to rent increases. Barcepia
> closed when the landlord refused to come to the table for lease negotiations.
> Treasured neighborhood institutions must be able to afford to thrive in New
> York. When we lose places to come together, we lose New York. Culture needs
> the right to renew its lease in New York City.
>
> In Paris, you can still go to the café where Sart and de Beauvoir wrote, but
> the spaces in the Bronx and Harlem where hip-hop was born have closed.
> They're not open anymore. We need commercial rent protections. Save the
> places that make New York, New York with fair leases and fair rent. Pass the
> Small Business Job Survival Act, Intro number 737. Thank you.

## Evidentiary boundary

The testimony is primary evidence of Jamie's public framing of commercial
affordability as cultural infrastructure and safety. It does not independently
establish the cause of every named closure or the merits of every policy claim.
