# Jamie Burkart testimony on small-business open data, March 18, 2019

## Source note

The reading copy preserves the official stenographic wording with normalized
line breaks. Apparent transcription errors remain visible.

## Full official reading copy

> Hello. I'm Jamie Burkhart. I'm a member of the New York City Artist
> Coalition. We advocate for the safety and preservation of New York City small
> diverse neighborhood cultural spaces. We strongly support the introductions
> discussed at today's hearing.
>
> Cultural spaces are small businesses. Cultural spaces close due to rent,
> harassment, and eviction. Cultural spaces strive to be in regulatory
> compliance. So I wanted to focus in and offer some specific recommendations
> to expand Intro 1466 and 1467. Both require reporting on the city's laws and
> practices related to small business, and both have the potential to fill a
> critical information gap that would help many small businesses more easily
> discover and navigate the path to legality, safety, and compliance.
>
> As a member of the Artist Coalition, I meet frequently with cultural space
> operators who strive to create and sustain small businesses with limited
> means. I meet with city agency staff and volunteer de facto community
> caseworkers who help spaces navigate licenses, permits, certificate
> inspections in pursuit of compliance. A common sentiment is the city can
> provide a clear unified way to understand the path to legality, a map of the
> process for navigating the city's many agencies and processes, and answer to
> the questions every small business must face.
>
> So we can expand 1466 and 1467 to create data sets on the city's open data
> portal and that we and city agencies can build off to create online tools for
> New York City small businesses to discover and navigate the path to legality
> and compliance. SBS has a step-by-step business wizard website that asks
> operators questions, brings forward more than 40 permits and certificates.
> Included here is a list of questions that I'd like to be rows and columns in
> a data set on the open portal that have to do with, like, how long does this
> take and what are the requirements and how much does it cost?

## Evidentiary boundary

This record supports Jamie's long-running practice of translating fragmented
requirements into navigable public systems. It does not establish that the
proposed datasets or tools were subsequently adopted.
