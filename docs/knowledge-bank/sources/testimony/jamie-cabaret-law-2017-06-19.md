# Jamie Burkart testimony on the Cabaret Law, June 19, 2017

## Source note

The official transcript identifies the speaker as “Jamie Burkhart.” The
reading copy below preserves the stenographer's words, lightly normalizes line
breaks, and retains apparent transcription errors. The transcript renders
Jamie's friend's name as “Nico Marshal”; that is not treated here as an
authoritative identification.

## Full official reading copy

> My name is Jamie Burkhart. I'm a member of the New York City Artist
> Coalition. I am asking New York City Council to repeal the Cabaret Law. The
> Cabaret Law was created in 1926 to halt interracial dancing in jazz clubs. It
> was used by Mayor Giuliani in the '90s to target and shut our gay bars,
> decimating culture. This civil rights issue law was used time and time again.
> It's still on the books and its prejudicial history is still felt today.
> Because of the Cabaret Law there are no legal places to dance in Bed-Stuy nor
> El Barrio, for instance. There are zero cabaret licenses in Council Member
> Cumbo's district where I live, nor in Speaker Melissa Mark-Viverito's. Today
> there are fewer than 100 active cabaret licenses in all five boroughs. There
> is nowhere legal to dance in the vast majority of New York's neighborhoods.
> Everywhere our communities gather, at a friend's performance, at a coffee
> shop, or at a wedding in a restaurant, it's almost certainly illegal to
> dance.
>
> My life as an advocate began with the loss of another. My good friend Nico
> Marshal. He was one of 36 people killed in the Ghost Ship tragedy earlier
> this year. From the minute I heard that he was missing, I knew he was gone.
> They all were. I was filled with shock and grief. Our first response was
> safety. We facilitated fire safety walk-throughs and workshops. Our study
> groups for the Fire Department's Fire Guard Certification exam have a 100%
> acing on pass rate. Working directly with spaces we found that though they
> were up to code, some were afraid to engage with the Fire Department because
> they knew that they didn't have a Cabaret License and feared retribution. For
> those who claim the Cabaret Law is about safety, we know what makes community
> spaces safe, and it is not a ban on dancing. Improve the relationship with
> trust to save lives. Repeal the Cabaret Law. Give low-income community spaces
> seats on the nightlife task force.
>
> For those who say the Cabaret Law is not being enforced, it can easily be
> used in the future by extreme conservative groups to arbitrarily shut down
> spaces. In the past few months there have been targeted outright attacks
> against community spaces in New York. They use the Internet to incite others
> to call authorities on spaces anonymously. They posted my home address on
> their website. At least one space I know of was visited by authorities. A
> teenage prankster in Wyoming can shut down spaces in New York with this
> outdated law. Repeal the Cabaret Law, legalize dance. Don't Ask Don't Tell
> isn't good enough.

## Evidentiary boundary

This record establishes what Jamie told the Council and how he framed the
coalition's safety and repeal work. It does not by itself verify every
historical, licensing, enforcement, or pass-rate statement within the
testimony.
