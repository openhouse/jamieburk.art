# Jamie recollection on the Layout A Council chamber photograph

## Returned source

Jamie recorded a first-person oral history on July 26, 2026 while looking at a
newly returned 1280 x 854 JPEG manifestation of the photograph. The supplied
file is bound through an opaque record retained outside public Git. Visual and
pixel comparison indicates that it is the same frame as the public derivative,
but it is differently encoded and retains a small, non-identifying EXIF
envelope. It therefore does not replace the metadata-stripped public derivative.

The transcript is a best-effort repaired working edition. It was not checked
against the audio and is not a forensic transcript or independent fact-check.

## What the image shows

The photograph shows Jamie at a New York City Council setting, wearing a blue
work jacket and carrying a clipboard and papers. A blue sheet with a striped
masthead is partly visible. These observations do not establish what the papers
contain.

## What Jamie remembers

Jamie places the image outside the hearing rooms at 250 Broadway during a 2026
NYC School of Data / Open Data Week session with the New York City Council Data
Team. He identifies the blue sheet as an NYC Artist Coalition member sign-in
form that he designed. He remembers the form offering several participation
and visibility choices, including list membership, public supporter listing,
shared-folder access, and mutual-aid participation.

He remembers carrying a packet proposing privacy-preserving geographic
aggregation of Real Property Income and Expense records as a public baseline
for commercial rent and vacancy, considered alongside vacant-storefront data.
He says he distributed and discussed the packet with members of the team.
Those actions and conversations remain first-person recollection until a
contemporary record confirms them.

Jamie also remembers that another attendee made the photograph. He offered a
partial workplace and climate-mitigation lead but expressed uncertainty. The
lead is retained only as a research direction and must not be treated as a
creator credit.

## What contemporary records corroborate

Private contemporary registration and logistics records establish the official
session title, date, venue, and organizer: **From Data to Policy: How the NYC
Council Turns City Data Into Action**, March 26, 2026, at 250 Broadway,
organized by the New York City Council Data Team within NYC Open Data Week.

A proposal document titled **Toward a Fuller Public Baseline for Commercial
Vacancy and Lease Cost in NYC** was modified the following day. That record
corroborates a closely timed proposal trail, not the exact packet version,
delivery, recipient, conversation, or outcome.

Together, the photograph, recollection, and contemporary event records support
a bounded event-level description. Photographer identity, destination-bounded
permission, the exact private archive binding, and downstream publication
approval remain open.
