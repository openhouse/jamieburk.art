>
> Thank you. Thank you, Mr. Chair, and Speaker Johnson. Sitting to the left of me, a couple

> when this bill was introduced, I guess originally, Speaker, you were, I guess, four years
> old. So,-- and we-- so, we’ve finally gotten this far, and I’m very proud of the Council
> for moving it, and thank you, Mr. Chair, for the opportunity. I’m State Senator Brad
> Hoylman. I represent the 27th district, part of the west side and east side of Manhattan. I
> hear about this problem a lot, which is why my staff and I last summer produced a report
> called Bleaker on Bleecker, which we identified as a project to look at the issue of high
> rent blight, as it’s known, as it was coined by law professor Tim Wu, in my neighborhood.
> Just to tell you what we found by virtue of going out with teams of people on the street.
> We found everything from a 6.67 vacancy rate on Second Avenue from Third to 14th Street to
> a 6.52 percent vacancy rate on Eighth Avenue from 15th to 22nd , and on Bleecker Street
> from Sixth Avenue to Eighth Avenue, an 18.44 vacancy rate, literally almost one in five
> storefronts were vacant. And this problem, as you know, cannot be viewed in a vacuum.
> Although, tell that to the operators of Desco Vacuum Sales and Services on 14th Street.
> They had been around since 1950 and lost their lease. We have to look at, of course, the
> issue of online retail and the impact of it. But, as was pointed out earlier, and they say
> you can’t manage what you don’t measure. So, we really need current data on both storefront
> vacancies and the impact of online sales on our small businesses and retailers, in
> particular. I, in this report, have proposed a number of possible solutions. First, I want
> to applaud the Council for taking steps on raising the threshold of the commercial rent
> tax. The question is, why can’t we raise it further? Why shouldn’t we eliminate it
> altogether? Secondly, in terms of the bill itself before us, one of the most important
> things it points out, as was alluded to earlier by your colleagues, Mr. Chair, is the lack
> of a level playing field between landlords and tenants when it comes to small businesses,
> and just as the Council has done in terms of residential tenants, I think commercial
> tenants do need a leg-up and do need assistance with those negotiations. The current leases
> are landlord leases. They should be designed differently to favor the tenant if anyone, not
> the landlord who generally has the most resources at his or her disposal. Let me also say
> that the State can do something, too. Currently, in-- when we talk about vacancy taxes,
> it’s legislation that I’m actually looking at with Assemblywoman Deborah Glick, the State
> is limited in its ability to impact local zoning decisions, but one way we can act is by
> utilizing our taxing authority. Landlords who leave retail storefronts vacant can’t
> deduct-- cannot deduct the lost potential rental income they would have received from their
> state income tax liability. They, like all owners of commercial real estate, are able,
> though, to receive deductions for depreciation of the property and operating expenses. To
> create a dis-incentive for leaving retail storefronts vacant, one possibility is that the
> state could explore phasing out those deductions on a sliding scale for building owners who
> leave retail spaces vacant for over a year. New York also could consider other tax
> incentives, credits or penalties to dissuade landlords from keeping a store vacant. One
> example of this type of policy is in the City of London, which provides commercial building
> owners who lose their tenants a short period of relief on their business taxes. After three
> months the tax relief expires and the owners must pay the full business rates even if the
> store is vacant. That’s meant to encourage landlords to rent out their space. So, I hope
> you take a look at the report that I created. I really commend you for moving forward with
> this legislation. We should look at other jurisdictions, of course. San Francisco, as you
