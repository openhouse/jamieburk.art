# KC Town Hall restored public site, August 2026

The restored home is a compact public record of a neighborhood-scale project,
not just a building brochure. Its six surfaced posts move between resident
listening, recurring tire pickup, COVID-19 resource distribution, park
programming, construction learning, and environmental cleanup. The About page
connects adaptive reuse to permanently affordable community space, walkable
daily necessities, and a neighborhood process, while crediting Julia
Fredenburg and Jamie Burkart together.

The [Tired of Tires page](https://kctownhall.com/tires/) is especially relevant
to resident-facing product and implementation roles. It translates an
expensive disposal problem into a legible service promise, household
eligibility, a monthly cadence, a request-or-volunteer pathway, a City recycling
handoff, and an economic-savings frame. The paired
[survey page](https://kctownhall.com/survey/) shows the site as a listening
interface as well as a publishing surface.

The public surface is historical even though it remains operational as a web
site. It still exposes dated 2022 pickup dates, a request form, contact details,
mailing-list fields, and other present-tense invitations. The sitemap exposes
three pages and six posts and uses `http` locators; the rendered pages also emit
`http` canonicals despite HTTPS delivery. Portfolio links must therefore label
this as a restored historical project record, not a currently available
resident service.

The current read supports the form, information architecture, public-service
design, and shared project credit. It does not establish that embedded forms
are monitored, contact details are current, historic programs continue, or
Jamie individually authored every page, sentence, graphic, or service element.
