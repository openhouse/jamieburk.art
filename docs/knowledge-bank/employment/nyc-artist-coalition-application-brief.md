# NYC Artist Coalition Application Brief

This brief translates approved knowledge-bank claims into application and
interview language for technical program management, product operations,
implementation, civic technology, and public-interest roles. It is a selection
surface, not a complete account of the coalition.

## Strongest Current Argument

Jamie helped turn a cultural coalition's emerging concerns into usable civic
systems. He co-founded NYC Artist Coalition, built public campaign
infrastructure, co-wrote a participation-to-action guide, organized public
learning and advocacy channels, and translated operator experience into public
records, testimony, data requirements, and accountable follow-through.

That work shows a consistent operating craft:

- listen where people experience a system's failures;
- turn situated knowledge into shared requirements and action paths;
- coordinate public events, written guidance, web surfaces, testimony, and
  government interfaces;
- preserve decisions, sources, risks, and boundaries so collaborators can
  continue the work;
- distinguish collective outcomes from Jamie's directly evidenced role.

## Application-Ready Proof

### Participation and operations

Jamie co-wrote a 2017 coalition guide documenting how recurring meetings and
issue prioritization could become shared letters, pocket-size calls to action,
meetings with officials, public events, phone calls, and hearings. Use this as
evidence of program design, facilitation infrastructure, and multi-channel
implementation inside collective work.

### Civic product thinking

In March 2019 City Council testimony, Jamie translated fragmented compliance
work into a machine-readable civic-data requirement: records for permits,
certificates, requirements, cost, timing, and agency pathways that could support
tools for small businesses navigating city government. Use this as evidence of
requirements discovery, service-journey analysis, public-data product thinking,
and communication across community and government contexts.

### Durable public infrastructure

Jamie built and supported public campaign websites and information surfaces for
NYC Artist Coalition work. Existing approved records also support direct public
testimony, source-backed policy communication, recurring event infrastructure,
and bounded contributions within the multi-group Cabaret Law repeal campaign.

## Interview Language

> In coalition work, I often met people at the point where a public system had
> become hard to navigate. My role was to listen for the structure already
> emerging in their experience, then help give it usable form: meeting formats,
> shared guidance, public events, websites, records, calls to action, testimony,
> and requirements government could receive. In one 2019 Council hearing, I
> translated cultural-space compliance problems into a machine-readable Open
> Data proposal for small-business tools. I care about the handoff as much as
> the launch: collaborators should be able to inspect the sources, understand
> the decisions, and continue the work without depending on one person's memory.

## Role Translation

For technical program management, lead with cross-functional coordination,
requirements, decision records, risks, public launch surfaces, and durable
handoffs.

For product operations and implementation, lead with feedback translation,
service journeys, multi-channel adoption support, operating documentation, and
the machine-readable compliance proposal.

For civic technology and public-interest work, lead with reciprocal translation
between cultural-space operators and formal institutions, public-data design,
transparent evidence, and collective credit.

## Anti-Claims

Do not say that Jamie alone founded or designed NYC Artist Coalition, created
the Office of Nightlife, repealed the Cabaret Law, authored Council legislation,
caused MARCH's replacement, built City datasets, or delivered City compliance
tools. Do not turn public officials' engagement with coalition work into private
dependence, endorsement, adoption, or individual causality.
