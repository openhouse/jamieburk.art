# Unfamiliar-reader portfolio validation

## Unfamiliar-reader rule

Use readers who have not participated in building the portfolio and do not
already know Jamie's project history. Record only the reader group and bounded
session findings; do not collect unnecessary identity or employment data.

## Five-minute protocol

1. Give the reader the homepage with no introduction.
2. Ask them to spend no more than five minutes finding what Jamie does, one
   convincing example, the resume, and a way to contact him.
3. Do not coach, define project names, explain the navigation, or defend the
   copy.
4. Ask the same questions in the same order after the exploration.

## Questions

- What job do you think Jamie is seeking?
- When would you bring him into a project?
- What is the strongest proof you remember?
- What did Jamie personally do in that example?
- What changed or became usable?
- What would you click or ask next?
- What made you hesitate or work too hard?
- Based only on this portfolio, would you consider an interview?

## Measures

Record yes/no results for unprompted role comprehension, strongest-proof
recall, Jamie-action recall, usable-result recall, resume discovery, contact
discovery, and next-action discovery. Record the reader's interview intent as
yes, maybe, or no, followed by one short reason.

## Interview intent

Interview intent is directional feedback, not a hiring-outcome metric. Do not
publish participant quotations or identify readers without permission.

## Stop condition

Run at least five sessions across at least two groups: public-sector hiring or
referral readers and product-operations or implementation readers. Closure
requires every session to meet role comprehension, strongest-proof recall, and
next-action discovery. Repeated failures become prioritized repair evidence.
