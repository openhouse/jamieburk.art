# KC Town Hall X full-population archival production

Reviewed: 2026-07-15

## Question

What project, operating, posted-source, engagement, and stakeholder-response
patterns survive across the full displayed `@KCTownHall` population?

## Population control

The authenticated profile displayed 183 posts. The Posts timeline yielded 170
primary records. The Replies timeline rendered 188 article cards: 183 primary
`@KCTownHall` records and five parent or conversation-context records authored
by other accounts. Removing those five contexts produced a deduplicated union
of 183 primary records, exactly matching the profile counter.

This is 100 percent of the dated profile-reported population. It is not a
platform export or a history of deleted, private, withheld, or unindexed
activity.

The 183 records span July 2, 2018, through September 24, 2022:

| Year | Records |
| --- | ---: |
| 2018 | 30 |
| 2019 | 85 |
| 2020 | 41 |
| 2021 | 17 |
| 2022 | 10 |

The population contains 142 original posts, 13 replies, and 28 reposts.

## Method

The pass:

1. exhausted authenticated Posts and Replies through repeated no-growth states;
2. deduplicated primary status IDs and reconciled them to the profile counter;
3. separated five other-account conversation contexts from project records;
4. retained public status URLs, dates, author handles, record types, timeline
   membership, posted-link metadata, classifications, and dated interaction
   aggregates;
5. close-read every record for project, neighborhood-operation, source, and
   stakeholder-response patterns;
6. inventoried all posted external links and normalized nine mission-relevant
   destinations;
7. ran bounded account, reply, domain, and project-name searches;
8. separated direct responses from one-way tags, broad-name false positives,
   and incidental conversation context.

The public fixture excludes post bodies, telephone numbers, cookies,
credentials, private account state, analytics, direct messages, and session
data.

## Operating patterns

The account repeatedly functioned as:

- a neighborhood-resource identity and project-update surface;
- a resident-survey and neighborhood-process invitation;
- free household tire-pickup intake and operating documentation;
- cleanup and community-resource distribution;
- voting, transit, housing, health, and civic-information circulation;
- a public stakeholder-dialogue and response surface.

One hundred records were classified as tire-related through close reading of
the visible post or hashtag. Twelve records were mechanically classified as
survey-linked because a preserved external destination contained `survey`.
These are record classifications, not pickup, household, resident, response,
or outcome counts.

The tire-related record spans 2019 through 2022. An archived project page
documents a monthly free residential pickup service run with Oak Park
Neighborhood Association. A June 2019 project-account update names Jamie among
participants. A November 2021 status documents household intake and operating
updates. A Bridging the Gap collaborator separately reported receiving a large
KC Town Hall tire drop-off. That last record is qualitative external
corroboration, not an independently audited count.

## Stakeholder responses

Three sitting Kansas City Council-member accounts directly responded in
mission-relevant contexts:

- Quinton Lucas quote-responded to a KC Town Hall alert about Leon's Thriftway
  and described speaking with ownership and looking for a way to help;
- Jolie Justus replied in the same context and described work with the EDC, a
  site visit, and community commitment;
- Melissa Robinson thanked KC Town Hall for work to improve community
  conditions.

The records are direct public responses, not one-way tags. They establish a
three-account floor, not a complete stakeholder census, institutional
endorsement, policy adoption, funding approval, or proof that KC Town Hall
caused later government action.

Other mission-relevant records include the Bridging the Gap tire-dropoff
corroboration, two community amplifications of an Oak Park cleanup notice, and
a KCMO 311 response in a public service thread.

## Knowledge-bank disposition

Selected for the public case study:

- Jamie's bounded first-person account-establishment role;
- the public resident-input loop;
- the recurring tire-removal operating pattern;
- external qualitative tire-dropoff corroboration;
- the three-Council-member-account response floor.

Retained in the repository but not selected for public projection:

- the population-reconciliation claim itself;
- the nine-source civic-resource curation pattern;
- the dated interaction aggregate.

The selected story remains smaller than the bank. It shows what became usable
without turning the portfolio into a public archive browser.

## Boundaries

- The account is institutional; no post is individually attributed to Jamie
  without separate evidence.
- Later programs remain under changing stewardship.
- One hundred tire-related records do not mean one hundred pickups, one hundred
  households, or a particular number of tires.
- First-party tire, savings, participation, and outcome totals remain held
  pending independent corroboration.
- Reposts and linked articles do not establish authorship or partnership.
- Direct official response is not blanket endorsement or policy causality.
- Social records do not establish funding receipt, contract execution,
  completed redevelopment, or municipal causality.
- No resident submissions, private messages, contact details, or authenticated
  session material enter the public repository.
