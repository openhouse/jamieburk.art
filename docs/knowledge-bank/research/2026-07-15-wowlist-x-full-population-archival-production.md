**Projection decision:** Preserve the complete census and findings in the

complete reconciliation of the population displayed by the profile. It is not
an account-data export and does not claim that no deleted or unavailable status
ever existed.

The strongest hiring-relevant finding is:

> The surviving account record shows a participatory product being operated in
> public: people were taught how to follow lists, use profiles, add events to
> multiple lists, join, and receive a weekly email; external users also made a
> tutorial, reported adding shows, and shared event pages.

This supports the existing product-operations framing. It does not replace the
database, code, résumé, and collaborator evidence supporting the larger user,
event, and city-ecosystem aggregates.

## Method

1. Reviewed the authenticated profile and recorded its displayed 38-post count,
   account identity, chronology, and public description.
2. Crawled the Posts timeline to exhaustion and deduplicated status URLs.
3. Crawled the Replies timeline to exhaustion and recovered the 38th object.
4. Repeated the full Posts-and-Replies crawl while capturing clean tweet text,
   posted links, timestamps, relationship type, and labeled interaction counts.
5. Reconciled all 38 statuses to the displayed population and classified each
   as a project-account post, project-account reply, or reposted status.
6. Inventoried all posted short URLs, resolving or preserving displayed targets
   and adding Wayback captures for selected dead mission-relevant sources.
7. Ran authenticated public searches for `@wowlist` and `to:wowlist`, then gave
   each recovered result a mission-relevance or exclusion disposition.
8. Derived mission and stakeholder patterns while keeping shared-account human
   authorship unassigned.

## What The Account Documents

### Origin and collaboration

The earliest project-account status says Richard and Jamie were developing a
larger project from calendars made at Sunday Dinner and resolves to the public
Sunday Dinner WOW List tag. A later [September 2016
reply](https://x.com/wowlist/status/771457416298921985) describes WOW List as a
DIY community-calendar project originating from the Sunday Dinner potluck.

These are contemporaneous project-account statements. They support the public
relationship among Jamie, Richard, Sunday Dinner, and WOW List, but do not
assign sole authorship or ownership.

### Participatory product operation

Three April 2015 replies form a compact onboarding sequence. The account tells
a participant how the home calendar follows local lists, how the profile page
collects a person's lists, and how to add an event to more than one list. Later
posts invite members to a meeting, direct people to join NYCdiy, and explain
that joining enables show entry and a weekly email.

An external participant also published [Shelby's WOWList
tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQg47LtixPI), which remains live with
the title "for my new fave events website." This is a concrete external
onboarding artifact, not an adoption total.

### External-use specimens

Three public sources supply bounded examples of external use:

- [Punks & Criminals](https://x.com/punkscriminals/status/604360847012413440)
  reported putting shows on WOW List and linked its organizer page.
- Shelby Turner published the participant-created tutorial above.
- An [external event promoter](https://x.com/jackascak/status/845116237591920640)
  used a WOW List page to circulate a Silent Barn benefit.

Together these show event entry, explanation, and public sharing. Three
specimens do not establish broad adoption, lifetime reach, attendance, or
organizer outcomes.

### Event and movement circulation

WOW List pages circulated local shows, tours, all-ages events, performance-art
festivals, community meetings, demonstrations, and memorial gatherings. The
record includes Kansas City, New York, Chicago, San Francisco, Oakland, and a
four-state tour, along with nationally framed movement calendars.

Those geographic references are examples in the social record. They must not
be substituted for the separately sourced estimate that the platform reached
roughly 35 city ecosystems.

### Documentation and cross-scene learning

The account's posted sources show an explicit interest in how decentralized
cultural work becomes learnable and durable:

- Elise Granata's archived ["HOMEWORK: In Every Town - An All-Ages Music
  Manualfesto"](https://web.archive.org/web/20150201081214/http://grasstronaut.com/2015/01/29/homework-in-every-town/)
  reviews practical knowledge about organizational structures, promotion,
  production, space, fundraising, community-building, conflict resolution, and
  documentation of defunct venues.
- Granata's archived Good Times article ["Zines
  2.0"](https://web.archive.org/web/20150907001335/http://www.gtweekly.com/index.php/santa-cruz-news/good-times-cover-stories/6548-zines-20.html)
  argues for community-authored records of underground cultural movements and
  for connecting geographically separated DIY arts spaces.
- The account announced participation in Allied Media Conference and linked
  public resources for peer learning across scenes.

These sources establish the account's intellectual and mission context. They
do not make Jamie the author of the linked texts or prove that every design
decision followed from them.

### Care and cultural-space safety

The later record shifts toward mutual aid, memorial gatherings, safer cultural
spaces, and public action after the Ghost Ship fire. It includes a fundraiser,
a Lake Merritt vigil page, Jamie's public call-your-mayor resource, [KQED's
vigil report](https://www.kqed.org/news/11207317/video-mourners-gather-at-candlelight-vigil-to-honor-victims-of-oakland-fire),
and Meow Wolf's archived [$100,000 DIY
Fund](https://web.archive.org/web/20170312084829/https://meowwolf.com/DIY/)
for infrastructure, rent, materials, equipment, and organizational support.

This pattern shows the account functioning as a care-and-resource circulation
surface. It does not establish WOW List as organizer of the linked initiatives
or reciprocal endorsement by KQED or Meow Wolf.

## Portfolio Decision

The public proof page now names the complete social census as part of its
source layer and includes a concise contribution-and-circulation artifact. It
does not publish the access-time engagement totals or claim that three external
uses prove platform-wide adoption.

The defensible public center remains:

> Jamie co-built and operated a community-calendar platform whose public record
> shows contribution, onboarding, event circulation, and community-maintenance
> workflows in use.

The stronger numeric adoption claims remain governed by their existing résumé,
database, code, and archive evidence.

## Boundaries

- Do not assign a shared-account status to Jamie without actor evidence.
- Do not treat a repost as reciprocal engagement, endorsement, or partnership.
- Do not sum source-post engagement into WOW List traction.
- Do not infer broad adoption from three external-use specimens.
- Do not publish follower identities, private account data, private messages,
  or deleted-post speculation.
- Preserve the difference between a profile-counted full population and a
  complete account export.
