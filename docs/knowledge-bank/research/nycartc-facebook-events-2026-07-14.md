# NYC Artist Coalition Facebook Event Archival Production

**Capture date:** 2026-07-14
**Authenticated revalidation:** 2026-07-14

**Surface:** [NYC Artist Coalition Facebook events](https://www.facebook.com/nycartc/events)

**Control:** 34 past events displayed on event host cards
**Accounting:** 33 recovered event records + 1 unresolved control slot = 34/34 slots accounted

## Answer First

The surviving Facebook event record makes NYC Artist Coalition's operating
practice unusually concrete. The coalition did not only publish positions. It
created repeated occasions for people to gather, compare lived experience,
learn practical safety and legal pathways, form shared priorities, and carry
those priorities into public hearings, campaigns, and conversations with city
officials.

Jamie describes creating this public event and participation layer as a
substantial part of his contribution. The event pages establish that the
collective system existed; they do not identify the individual author or
producer of every page. The knowledge bank therefore supports **helped
establish and produce**, preserves collective credit, and keeps event-level
authorship as an open research question. The wording is explicitly deferred
from the website pending audience and placement review.

## The Participation System

Twelve recovered records are recurring coalition meetings. Two were virtual;
the other ten name ten distinct physical venues:

- Magick City
- The Floasis
- Muchmore's
- The City Reliquary
- Shoestring Press
- Chinatown Soup
- Secret Project Robot
- Friends and Lovers
- Flowers for all Occasions
- Ode to Babel

That supports a **rotating-venue practice**, not an uninterrupted claim that a
meeting occurred every calendar month or that every coalition event used a new
venue.

The wider event population records a broader operating loop:

1. **Gather and listen.** General meetings invited participants to set
   priorities, propose working groups, and share what cultural spaces needed.
2. **Make support practical.** Events included Fire Guard test preparation,
   legal and architectural Q&A, community-space survey design, venue-support
   actions, and later pandemic relief coordination.
3. **Translate experience into an ask.** Meetings joined lived concerns with
   concrete campaigns around Cabaret Law repeal, CreateNYC, the Office of
   Nightlife, MARCH transparency, commercial lease protections, and Fair Rent
   NYC.
4. **Create a public path.** Event pages supplied a time, place, issue frame,
   action, and invitation to attend, testify, call, organize, or bring another
   concern into the coalition.
5. **Return and revise.** Later meetings reviewed policy developments, reported
   back, and set new priorities rather than treating a hearing or rally as the
   end of the work.

The February 2019 MARCH hearing event compressed the participation ethic into
four public words: **"Being there changes everything."** The same description
then asked participants to tell their stories. That is evidence of the event
layer's intended function; it is not evidence that Facebook responses became
testimony or that any one event caused a policy outcome.

## Stakeholder Interface

Seventeen recovered pages display a coalition-only public host byline. Sixteen
visibly name at least one cohost or associated organization alongside the
coalition. Across those records, the event surface joins cultural venues,
artist and nightlife organizations, tenant and small-business advocates,
worker and relief groups, and professional associations. A visible host byline
does not establish each organization's division of labor or formal partnership
terms.

Independent Village Voice reporting supplies a different form of evidence for
the October 2017 Market Hotel town hall: about one hundred people physically
attended, including Council members and city-agency representatives. This
supports the event layer as an institutional interface while remaining one
event-specific account, not an aggregate stakeholder-engagement measure.

## Public Engagement Signals

Thirty-two recovered event pages display a Facebook response total. The values
range from **9 to 1.7K**:

- 19 events display at least 100 responses;
- 9 display at least 400;
- 3 display at least 1K;
- 1 co-hosted resource webinar displays no response total in the recovered
  detail surface.

These figures are useful only as **event-level platform response signals**.
They cannot be summed into people reached: responders may recur across events,
and a Facebook response does not establish arrival, duration, participation,
or attendance.

The October 2017 Market Hotel town hall demonstrates the boundary. Its event
page displays 1.2K responses, while independent Village Voice reporting
described about one hundred people physically attending with Council and agency
representatives. The knowledge bank may support the reported attendance for
that one event with its citation; it must not convert Facebook responses into
turnout.

## Posted Sources And Action Paths

Expanded public descriptions contain **61 outbound-link occurrences across 38
normalized URL rows on 25 events**. The [public-safe link
ledger](../data/nycartc-facebook-event-link-ledger.json) routes every recovered
destination into one of six functions:

- campaign or organization identity;
- event registration or civic action;
- public safety or planning resource;
- published reporting;
- unresolved short-link research debt; or
- protected working-document context.

Seven rows route to published articles about Cabaret Law advocacy, M.A.R.C.H.
operations, and Commercial Rent Stabilization. The links show which sources the
event descriptions offered participants; posting a URL does not establish its
truth, Jamie's authorship, coalition endorsement of every proposition,
readership, conversion, attendance, or policy impact. Village Voice and
Gothamist reporting used for claims here received close readings. New Yorker,
Baffler, WNYC, Metro, New York Post, and Curbed destinations remain routed to
their current preservation and close-reading status elsewhere in the bank.

The public ledger withholds a working-document locator and unresolved `goo.gl`
paths. Meeting-access URLs, credentials, and raw description captures remain
outside the repository.

## Jamie's Role

The evidence supports this public wording:

> Jamie helped establish and produce the coalition's recurring participation
> system: public event pages, meetings rotated among cultural spaces, practical
> safety and advocacy sessions, and pathways from artist concerns to public
> hearings and campaign action.

Why this wording is strong and bounded:

- Jamie directly confirms that the event layer was a major contribution and
  that he brought participation lessons from WOWList into legislative advocacy.
- The complete recovered population shows the repeatable system and its public
  outputs.
- Independent reporting separately names Jamie's coalition advocacy and
  practical fire-safety work, and documents the Market Hotel town hall.
- The pages are collective event surfaces. They do not assign individual
  authorship to Jamie for every description, invitation, or facilitation choice.

Future collaborator notes can strengthen event-level production credit. They
are not required to preserve the clear contribution claim above.

## Claim Discipline

### Ready with boundaries

- 34 control slots are accounted for: 33 recovered and one unresolved.
- The recovered record spans January 2017 through January 2021.
- Twelve recurring meeting records span ten named physical venues and two
  virtual meetings.
- The event population documents a repeatable path from gathering and practical
  support to public action and institutional interface.
- Jamie helped establish and produce that participation system.

### Held

- Jamie individually authored or produced every event page.
- The coalition met every month without interruption.
- Every event took place at a different small cultural space.
- Facebook response totals equal physical attendance or unique people reached.
- The event practice alone caused legislative or agency outcomes.
- The unresolved slot was deleted or had any particular date, title, or topic.

## Public-Safety Disposition

The research pass deliberately excluded:

- guest and attendee identities;
- invite and friend context;
- comments, reactions, and participant profile links;
- private group or account-administration context;
- contact details copied from descriptions;
- online meeting links, IDs, passcodes, and dial-in information;
- private analytics and credentials.

The public census retains only public project-event metadata needed for
population accounting and professional interpretation.
