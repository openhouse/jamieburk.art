# Participation lineage archive production

Date: July 15, 2026

This pass studies three complementary records: a protected WOWList production
database snapshot, a protected Sunday Dinner operations workbook, and public
Facebook surfaces connecting Call Script, popular.vote, and NYC Artist
Coalition. Raw archives remain outside this public repository.

## Recovered evidence

### WOWList

A read-only aggregate audit of the July 22, 2017, production snapshot found:

- 1,846 user rows;
- 16,142 post/event rows;
- 23,864 list/tag rows;
- 28,837 list-follow rows;
- 20,927 star/saved-event rows;
- 2,965 going rows;
- 15,915 Google Calendar event rows; and
- 35 city/region groups with at least 50 geocoded posts/events.

The last measure is a conservative activity threshold. It does not define
official chapters. Row counts do not establish unique active people,
satisfaction, institutional adoption, or cultural impact. The snapshot is a
point-in-time record, not a complete lifetime archive.

### Sunday Dinner

The main sheet of a protected operations workbook contains 345 numbered event
columns spanning January 2012 through March 2021. Four event numbers are
duplicated, and some headers do not carry parseable dates. The safe claim is
therefore more than 300 numbered gathering records, not an exact unique-event
or attendance census.

The workbook also preserves invitation, response, participant, contact, and
operational detail. None of those rows or fields enters the repository. An
invitation or response is not physical attendance, and the workbook does not
support the separate resident-artist count.

### Call Script, popular.vote, and NYC Artist Coalition

The public Call Script page:

- describes a representative-calling purpose;
- links to popular.vote; and
- preserves routes to a March 2017 NYC Artist Coalition meeting, community
  board participation, and coalition sign-up.

The January 27, 2017, public event page for a DCLA meeting on DIY and
alternative art spaces displays Call Script and NYC Artist Coalition together
as co-hosts. It displayed 445 platform responses when reviewed. That number is
not physical attendance, unique reach, endorsement, or impact.

The currently rendered discussion surface also preserves a November 13, 2017,
NYC Artist Coalition poll asking what the coalition should do. Twelve issue
options are indicated; visible options include commercial rent stabilization,
community-led space legality, anonymous noise complaints, fire-guard testing,
and anti-displacement policy. This supports a public issue-intake pattern, not
authorship, voter identity, enacted priorities, or policy causation. Its
appearance on the current discussion surface does not make it part of the
January meeting program.

## Claim treatment

The WOWList and Sunday Dinner aggregate claims are projected with numbered
metadata-only citations. The reader can inspect what each protected source
supports and what it does not establish, but receives no archive link or
participant data.

Jamie's account of carrying participation lessons from WOWList through Call
Script and popular.vote into NYC Artist Coalition facilitation is retained as a
corroborated claim, not projected prose. Public Facebook records establish an
important project-identity intersection and participation pattern. They do not
yet independently establish Jamie's precise creation, administration,
facilitation, or founding chronology.
