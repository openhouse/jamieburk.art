# WOW List, Sunday Dinner, And Call Script Archival Production

Date: July 15, 2026

## Research Question

What can the surviving WOW List database, Sunday Dinner working workbook, Call
Script Page, and January 2017 DCLA event establish about the participation
infrastructure that preceded and entered early NYC Artist Coalition work?

## Sources And Safety

The pass used three evidence layers:

- a private WOW List PostgreSQL snapshot dated July 22, 2017;
- a protected Sunday Dinner operational workbook spanning headers from January
  2012 through March 2021; and
- the public Call Script Facebook Page plus the public January 27, 2017 DCLA
  event record already represented in the knowledge bank.

No database row, participant identity, contact field, geolocation, post body,
workbook row, private locator, or account-administration detail was committed.
The repository retains public-safe aggregates, source relationships,
limitations, and anti-claims.

## Findings

### WOW List Snapshot

The selected snapshot tables contain:

- 16,142 post records;
- 45,562 post-tag relationships;
- 23,864 tag records;
- 28,837 follow-tag records; and
- 35 source records.

Within that snapshot, the relevant tag intersections are:

- 67 post records tagged both Popular Vote and Sunday Dinner;
- 34 tagged both Popular Vote and NYC Artist Coalition;
- 33 tagged both NYC Artist Coalition and Sunday Dinner; and
- 9 tagged Popular Vote, Sunday Dinner, and NYC Artist Coalition.

The January 27, 2017 DCLA cultural-space event is one of the nine three-way
records and also carries the WOW a Day tag. Its stored project text asks people
to pack the room, support DIY spaces, seek a commissioner statement, and
discuss what the group should ask for.

These are catalog and routing relationships. They are not people, attendance,
reach, conversion, account authorship, event production, policy impact, or
proof that every tagged record originated with the tagged project.

### Sunday Dinner Workbook

The protected workbook's main sheet contains 346 numbered gathering columns.
Of those, 341 contain at least one recorded Y mark. The sheet contains 2,726 Y
marks across 411 participant rows and spans headers from January 2012 through
March 2021.

The sequence also contains duplicate labels, missing sequence numbers, and
three zero-mark columns. Workbook formulas and headings include terms such as
"yes count," "invites count," and "yesness." The aggregate therefore
strengthens the established public-safe wording "300+ gatherings" but does not
support an exact completed-event count or permit every Y mark to be called
physical attendance.

Names, contact details, notes, row histories, and the workbook remain
protected.

### Call Script To Coalition Participation

The public Call Script Page provides two direct traces:

- the Page links to `popular.vote` and describes Call Script as a way to call
  representatives; and
- a public post dated March 2, 2017 promotes the March 6 NYC Artist Coalition
  general meeting at The Floasis.

The promoted agenda says participants would break into groups for fire-guard
training, arts-space town-hall strategy, community-space survey development,
and member-led ideas. The January DCLA event separately lists Call Script and
NYC Artist Coalition among its public event relationships and asks people to
pack the room and discuss requests.

Together with the database tag graph, these records support a functional
bridge from WOW List's Popular Vote layer through Call Script into early
coalition participation. They do not independently establish who designed each
surface, wrote each post, facilitated each meeting, founded the coalition, or
made every project decision.

  mark aggregate with attendance-semantic limits.
- `CLM-CALLSCRIPT-POPULAR-VOTE-NYCARTC-BRIDGE` preserves the public/private
  bridge as a qualified, citation-ready candidate held from current site copy.

## Projection Decision

The public portfolio does not change in this pass. The new records deepen the
source palette and make a later, more precise account possible. Any projection
of the Call Script bridge should cite the public Page and event source, name
the work as collective, and keep Jamie's exact share attributed until
collaborators or stronger person-level records corroborate it.
