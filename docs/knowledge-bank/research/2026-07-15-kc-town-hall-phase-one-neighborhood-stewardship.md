# KC Town Hall Phase One and Neighborhood Stewardship: July 15, 2026

This public-safe research note preserves a substantial body of Jamie's East
Kansas City implementation work without promoting first-person memories beyond
their current support. It separates four connected but distinct claim areas:

1. KC Town Hall Phase One construction leadership;
2. the KC Town Hall neighborhood survey system;
3. TiredOfTires recurring field operations; and
4. Cleveland Avenue and cross-neighborhood communications support.

The attached March 2019 project packet is a contemporaneous, project-authored
source. Jamie's July 2026 account is direct first-person evidence. Neither is
treated as independent third-party corroboration of Jamie's individual role.

## What the Phase One packet establishes

The packet reports that KC Town Hall's 2018 work included replacing the roof,
repairing major structural masonry, removing several tons of debris, and
opening long-shuttered egress. It describes the Phase One cold-shell scope as
roof structure and membrane, structural masonry, related properties, and floor
framing. Its budget identifies roof deck repair, insulation and TPO membrane,
masonry brickwork, floor framing, and a water connection among the work.

The packet also reports hiring more than 15 minority-owned contractors in 2018,
with neighborhood proximity and masonry-skills development among the project's
stated priorities. Because the packet dates to March 2019, its 2019 completion
language is treated as prospective rather than as independent proof of the
year-end endpoint.

## Jamie's construction account

Jamie reports serving as general contractor for Phase One, arriving first each
day with measured drawings, coordinating work from basement through roof deck,
and aligning architect, engineer, masonry, roofing, carpentry, welding, and
plumbing teams. He specifically recalls sequencing the TPO membrane with the
restored parapet and historic ceramic caps.

This account is preserved as a research-stage claim with moderate confidence.

## Participatory survey system

The packet reproduces a compact survey card asking neighbors what they wanted
at 36th and Indiana. It names Oak Park Neighborhood Association and New Horizon
Missionary Baptist Church as partners and says the results directly shaped the
proposal. Supporting material describes an active listening process intended
to respond to the community and support neighborhood-owned businesses and
local employment.

Jamie reports designing the handbill and the backing data-collection system.
That authorship remains research-stage pending original files or collaborator
confirmation. Raw responses, contact information, and any unsupported claim of
statistical representativeness remain protected.

## TiredOfTires

Public KC Town Hall posts establish a recurring free first-Saturday tire-pickup
program in a bounded East Kansas City service area. A project-maintained ledger
contains 26 dated columns from May 2019 through September 2022 and records 25
months with a positive count. Jamie reports designing and coordinating the
program with Oak Park Neighborhood Association and the City, performing the
pickup and recycling-center delivery, distributing neighborhood information,
and later extending service to Indian Mound with collaborators.

The ledger sums to 1,970 tires and models $44,890 in avoided disposal cost, but
neither figure is ready for public use. A captured May 2022 post reports 139
tires while the ledger and later social copy record 146. The research task
therefore requires month-level reconciliation, City or disposal records, and a
documented avoided-cost method. The savings figure cannot be described as cash
paid to residents or a City-certified benefit.

## Cleveland Avenue and neighborhood communications

Jamie credits Pastor Lee with the originating insight behind Cleveland Avenue
Unify to Beautify: the avenue could make several historically underserved East
Kansas City neighborhoods legible as a connected place and corridor. Jamie
reports co-founding the program through the Historic East Neighborhoods
Coalition and contributing its identity, logo, photography, social media,
listening-session maps, resident reporting tools, and public-meeting materials.

Jamie also describes a recurring pro bono design, print, and distribution
practice across KC Town Hall, Oak Park, HENC, New Horizon, Chestnut Avenue, and
related neighborhood service activity. These are strong professional leads,
not yet public claims. The next pass should recover representative dated
artifacts, collaborator accounts, public event records, and official capital
improvement records before attributing co-founder status or municipal impact.
