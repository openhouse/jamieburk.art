## Purpose

This pass returns to the three iCloud Teams collections after additional files
materialized. It looks for evidence that changes the professional record rather
than repeating prior synthesis. The main questions were:

1. What made NYC Artist Coalition useful to DCLA and City Council?
2. Which current Commercial Rent Stabilization artifacts show Jamie's work as
   an operating and implementation practice rather than only advocacy?
3. Which older creative-technology traces can be preserved without joining
   similar names or incomplete program records too aggressively?
4. Does the job-hunt dossier introduce independent evidence, or is it a routing
   layer that must continue to point outward to primary sources?

## Access And Method

The authenticated in-app iCloud Drive browser backend was unavailable during
this run. All three required local iCloud collections were sufficiently
materialized for bounded close reading, so the review proceeded against those
protected local copies. A file that did not open or materialize was not treated
as absent.

The pass:

1. read the anchor overview for each required collection;
2. routed high-value statements back to underlying project pages, government
   records, program records, or authored work products;
3. separated collective process, institutional recognition, Jamie's individual
   role, policy outcome, and causal interpretation;
4. retained protected sources through opaque locator IDs rather than filesystem
   paths; and
5. reconciled every new proposition against existing intake, governed proofs,
   and publication boundaries.

Raw correspondence, transcripts, contacts, private strategy, participant data,
and unapproved media remain outside the repository.

## New Source Chain: Listening To Institutional Record

The Jamie Projects History collection preserves a useful process chain that is
stronger than any one event page:

| Stage | Source | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listen | NYC Artist Coalition DCLA meeting record, February 3, 2017 | DCLA asked participants to contribute final recommendations; the page records attendee-generated issues and a follow-up meeting | Jamie's individual role, an independently verified attendance count, or City adoption |
| Prioritize | NYC Artist Coalition recommendation vote, February 7, 2017 | The coalition published ranked member-vote results for recommendations to the Commissioner and CreateNYC | Voter denominator, voting method, individual authorship, or policy causality |
| Synthesize | NYC Artist Coalition CreateNYC recommendation page | The coalition published a structured recommendation set organized around criminalization, access to support, and affordability | Jamie's authorship or the City's adoption of every proposal |
| Enter the record | Official CreateNYC Appendix Section 5 | DCLA preserved the coalition letter to Commissioner Tom Finkelpearl and March 30 town-hall notes in the cultural-plan appendix | Jamie's role or adoption of every recommendation |
| Institutional recognition | Final CreateNYC plan, pages 145-146 | DCLA says its January meeting spurred the coalition's establishment and credits the coalition with organizing, recommendations, and thoughtful dialogue about artist-led spaces | Individual formation credit or sole causality for later law, office, budget, or implementation |

This chain supports a bounded collective proposition: NYC Artist Coalition
translated dispersed experience from informal artist-led spaces into a
prioritized, reviewable public record that entered the City's cultural-planning
process.

It also explains the institutional value without claiming that an official
personally "needed Jamie." CreateNYC says broad artist participation helped the
City understand artists' complex and varied needs. The coalition supplied a
way for informal spaces, which were not reliably represented by conventional
institutional channels, to become legible without flattening their concerns
into one issue. Its public workflow connected lived experience, collective
prioritization, recommendations, agency listening, and cross-agency strategy.

For DCLA, this offered field knowledge and an organized counterpart. For City
Council, it offered constituent experience translated into policy questions,
public asks, and an accountable record. For Council Member Rafael Espinal, the
coalition's public process was relevant to the work of bridging independent
venues, residents, and City government. These are evidence-based functional
interpretations, not person-specific causality findings.

The official plan also places the artist-space issue inside a wider strategy:
safe and open environments for DIY, artist-run, and alternative spaces, plus a
Night Life Ambassador working across DCLA, Consumer Affairs, MOME, City
Council, DOB, NYPD, and FDNY. The coalition's exact causal contribution to that
strategy remains unassigned.

## New Source: Legislative Provenance As Requirements Traceability

The CRS collection contains Jamie's May 17, 2026 **Commercial Rent
Stabilization: Legislative Provenance Redline, 2019-2025**. The protected work
product begins with NYC Council Intro 93 and uses tracked source layers to make
visible:

- Fair Rent NYC's 2022 recommendations;
- language traceable to the Small Business Survival Act lineage;
- Albany revisions leading to Senate Bill S8319; and
- the relationship between inherited text and the current state proposal.

The document says reviewer names identify source layers rather than individual
drafting authorship. It labels itself unofficial, for discussion only, and not
legal advice.

The strongest portable claim is therefore about analytical infrastructure:
Jamie made policy-version lineage inspectable for collaborators. The artifact
shows requirements traceability across legislative versions, not legal
authority, sponsor adoption, enactment, or policy causality. Evidence of who
used the redline and what decision it changed remains a targeted research gap.

## New Research Lead: BAPLab 2006

Two institutional program records independently list Jamie Burkart in the July
2006 BAPLab New Media Program at 3rd Ward:

- Wave Farm's event record; and
- Bushwick Art Project's archived program.

That program credit is directly supportable and adds an early dated point to
Jamie's creative-technology chronology.

A separate article about BAPLab describes an analog video installation titled
**Time Is Long**, in which visitors encounter delayed images of themselves as
recorded tape travels through the gallery. The article prints the artist's name
as `Jamie Burkhart`. The knowledge bank preserves this as a research lead and
does not silently join it to Jamie Burkart's program credit. An unambiguous
artist statement, program caption, image caption, course record, or collaborator
account is still needed.

The archived Bushwick Art Project wrapper also displays another artist's detail
below the full program roster. Only Jamie's roster entry is used; the unrelated
detail is excluded.
