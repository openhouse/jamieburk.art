# UCSC Evaluations - Margaret Morse and Warren Sack

## Why these sources matter

The portfolio's hiring argument emphasizes Jamie's ability to turn complexity
into usable operating structure. These evaluations show an older and equally
important root: Jamie investigated how people inhabit media, spaces, social
relations, and systems. This helps explain why his later workflows are concerned
with participation, memory, access, atmosphere, and collective agency rather
than administration alone.

## Margaret Morse record

Morse's protected 2006 evaluations describe Jamie presenting early video
equipment and feedback phenomena, working as an artist and media archaeologist,
and demonstrating theoretical understanding through embodied and performative
responses. One evaluation says Time is Long was realized in a New York gallery.
The exact venue, date, configuration, and credits remain open.

A held 2014 recommendation independently preserves Morse's teacher and adviser
relationship to Jamie and her strong appraisal of an ambitious installation.
The screenshot is not cleared for publication.

The public Good Times account strengthens the larger practice claim. It records
Open House as a ten-day communal gallery experiment shaped through hospitality,
shared responsibility, participation, continual reinvention, and distributed
documentation. It also records Jamie interpreting the project through social
software and relational representation.

## Warren Sack record

Sack's protected evaluations describe a program using online dictionary
sources, source-backed writing on social networks across physical and online
space, and a Flickr prototype that recursively related group membership,
structural equivalence, image analysis, and an interactive Max/MSP Jitter
interface.

The final course project was collective. Jamie and classmates designed two
architectures for turning Flickr browsing into a physical walk through projected
images and instrumented doorways, documented through models, films, sketches,
diagrams, prose, and an HTML mock-up.

## Interpretive synthesis

The Morse lens asks whether the portfolio can recognize embodied thought,
attention, media history, atmosphere, hospitality, art-life continuity, and the
value of experiment beyond organizational utility.

The Sack lens asks whether the portfolio can recognize recursive social
analysis, software as a medium for understanding relationships, movement among
research, code, interface, and physical environment, and collective authorship
of social-information systems.

Together they require one public threshold where Jamie's artistic, civic,
technical, and social practices remain connected.
