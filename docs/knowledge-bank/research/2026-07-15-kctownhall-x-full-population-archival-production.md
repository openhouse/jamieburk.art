## Method

1. Reviewed the authenticated profile and recorded its displayed 183-post
   count, account identity, chronology, and public description.
2. Crawled the Posts timeline to exhaustion, repeating the terminal no-growth
   pass and deduplicating status URLs.
3. Crawled the Replies timeline to exhaustion and separated primary account
   records from parent and conversation-context cards authored by other
   accounts.
4. Reconciled the union of Posts and Replies to the profile counter.
5. Classified every primary object as project post, project reply, or reposted
   external status.
6. Inventoried every posted short URL and preserved displayed destinations.
7. Closely reviewed the corpus for recurring public functions, including tire
   removal and resident survey pathways.
8. Ran bounded authenticated searches for direct replies, quote-responses,
   domain references, and project-name references, excluding unrelated broad
   name matches.
9. Verified three Council-account response records in the authenticated live
   interface and checked contemporaneous or official service-date evidence.
10. Derived aggregate patterns while keeping shared-account human authorship
    unassigned.

This is a full reconciliation of the objects displayed by the profile. It is
not a platform account-data export and does not establish that no deleted,
restricted, deindexed, or otherwise unavailable post ever existed.

## What The Account Documents

### Public identity and invitation

The [pinned launch post](https://x.com/KCTownHall/status/1013893135695601665)
invited people to participate in building a permanent neighborhood resource
and cultural center on Indiana Avenue. The account continued that public-facing
identity through project updates, neighborhood information, survey invitations,
and calls to participate.

This supports an intentional public project identity and participation system.
It does not prove completion of the building project, current property status,
funding receipt, or Jamie's sole authorship of the public language.

### Resident input and participation pathways

Twelve records linked directly to a KC Town Hall survey pathway. Across the
corpus, the account also circulated neighborhood-process invitations, public
meetings, voting information, transit planning, and other civic resources.

The recurring pattern is operational: the account did not only state a vision;
it repeatedly gave people a next action. The 12 survey-linked records are a
mechanical URL classification, not a count of survey responses, unique
participants, or completed engagements.

### Tire-removal operating surface

One hundred of the 183 records are tire-related under a transparent
case-insensitive classification that includes visible text and hashtags such
as `#TiredOfTires`. They span free pickup intake, scheduling, service updates,
community cleanup, collaborator coordination, and later program continuity.

This concentration shows that the public identity became an operating surface
for recurring neighborhood service. It does not independently validate each
quantity, environmental impact estimate, pickup completion, or savings figure
reported by the account.

An external [Bridging the Gap collaborator
post](https://x.com/trutheresme/status/1148277187583389703) reported receiving a
large KC Town Hall tire drop-off. That is useful corroboration of a concrete
program relationship, not a complete program audit.

## Relationship To The Funding Record

The social census and the municipal funding record answer different questions.
The account shows a public identity and neighborhood operating surface. The
official legislative record shows that the CCED Sales Tax Board recommended
$490,539, the Council accepted that recommendation and appropriated the amount
in 2019, and a 2024 ordinance later recorded that KC Town Hall withdrew and the
unused funds were reappropriated.

Neither record establishes that KC Town Hall received or spent the allocation,
that the building redevelopment was completed, or that Jamie individually
caused the Council action. The social continuity record also must not be used
to infer the legal mechanics of Jamie's stated stewardship transition.

## Portfolio Decision

The existing KC Town Hall proof can now name two distinct, source-backed forms
of accomplishment:

1. Jamie co-led redevelopment planning and public-benefit documentation that
   advanced through CCED recommendation to Council acceptance and appropriation
   of $490,539, while the later unused-funds outcome remains attached.
2. The shared project account became a durable public operating surface for
   resident input, recurring neighborhood service, civic-resource circulation,
   and direct stakeholder dialogue.

The portfolio should not claim that Jamie authored every post, operated every
later program, received or spent the public allocation, completed the adaptive
reuse, or personally produced each stakeholder response.

## Boundaries

- Do not assign a shared-account status to Jamie without actor evidence.
- Do not attribute post-transition program outcomes to Jamie.
- Do not treat a repost, mention, like, or reply as formal endorsement or
  partnership.
- Do not convert access-time interaction labels into reach, adoption, or impact.
- Do not treat first-party quantities in posts as independently audited.
- Do not collapse recommendation, appropriation, receipt, expenditure, and
  completion into one funding claim.
- Do not connect the 2024 Council withdrawal record to Jamie's family crisis or
  stewardship-transition account.
- Do not publish follower identities, private account data, private messages,
  post bodies, phone numbers, or deleted-post speculation.
- Preserve the difference between a profile-counted full population and a
  complete platform export.
