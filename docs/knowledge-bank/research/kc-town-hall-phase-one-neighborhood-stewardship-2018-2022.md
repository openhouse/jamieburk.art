## Phase One

The project-authored packet defines Phase One as a cold-shell restoration with
a total recorded value of $189,629. The line-item scope includes:

- roof-deck repair, insulation, and an energy-efficient TPO membrane;
- structural brick masonry;
- ceiling and floor framing;
- water-service connection;
- construction staging and storage;
- debris and tree removal;
- basement egress;
- material transportation;
- property acquisition;
- site safety and security;
- particulate and moisture controls; and
- tools, materials, and supporting costs.

The packet reports that the roof was replaced and major structural masonry was
repaired in 2018. Its narrative says Phase One was 66 percent complete and
scheduled to finish in 2019; its following budget page is labeled "Phase One:
Cold Shell - Completed 2019." Jamie remembers Phase One reaching completion in
2019. This chronology remains `use-with-care` until an independent closeout,
permit, inspection, final-invoice set, or collaborator account is linked.

Jamie states that he served as general contractor for Phase One and coordinated
historic masonry, roofing, carpentry, welding, engineering, architecture,
plumbing, measurements, safety, and daily sequencing from basement through roof
deck. The packet corroborates the specialized scope, not the general-contractor
title. The claim therefore remains attributed, internal-only, and pending
contracts, permits, correspondence, or collaborator confirmation.

General-contractor coordination must not become a claim that Jamie personally
performed licensed design or every trade. Credit remains with Julia Fredenburg,
the trade teams, architects, engineers, neighbors, workers, and other
collaborators.

## TiredOfTires

A public Cleveland Ave KC post describes a monthly free residential
tire-pickup service offered by KC Town Hall and the Oak Park Neighborhood
Association for historic east Kansas City. The post routes residents to request
pickup or volunteer. The reconciled KC Town Hall X corpus contains 107
TiredOfTires-tagged records, supporting a recurring public-service and operating
communications pattern.

A private project calculator contains 26 monthly columns from May 2019 through
September 2022. Its total-count row records 1,970 tires across 25 nonzero logged
months. This is a project operating record, not an independent audit, city
disposal ledger, unique-household count, or verified impact measure.

Jamie states that he designed the program, coordinated with Kansas City on
behalf of the Oak Park Neighborhood Association, produced outreach materials,
conducted monthly pickups, delivered tires to the city recycling center, and
maintained the spreadsheet. Public traces and the calculator corroborate the
program, not the actor responsible for each task. This role remains attributed
and internal-only pending collaborator, city, receipt, route, or association
records.

Jamie also remembers the program later expanding to Indian Mound. No dated
public trace or collaborator confirmation has yet been linked, so that expansion
remains an open inquiry.

## Cleveland Avenue

HENC's strategic plan documents a coalition of neighborhood associations and
community stakeholders working to improve and empower Kansas City's east-side
neighborhoods. It does not describe Cleveland Ave Unify to Beautify or Jamie's
role.

Jamie states that he was a co-founding member of Cleveland Ave Unify to
Beautify and supported the initiative as a pro bono design and print studio,
producing identity work, photography, social media, corridor maps, listening
session materials, and handbill packets. Jamie credits Pastor Lee with the core
idea that Cleveland Avenue connected multiple neighborhood-association areas
and could help residents and city leaders understand the corridor as a shared
place.

The role remains internal-only pending recovery of source files, public posts,
meeting materials, HENC records, and collaborator confirmation. No claim should
say Jamie originated Pastor Lee's concept, solely founded the initiative, spoke
for participating neighborhoods, or caused a discretionary funding or capital
decision.
