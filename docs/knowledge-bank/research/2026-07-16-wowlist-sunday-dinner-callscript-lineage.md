# WOW List, Sunday Dinner, and Call Script archival production

Date: 2026-07-16

## Purpose

This pass tested three related but non-interchangeable evidence questions:

1. Can the historical WOW List scale claim be reproduced from a protected
   production database snapshot?
2. What can a protected Sunday Dinner attendance-signals workbook establish
   about recurring participation infrastructure?
3. What public records connect Call Script's `popular.vote` surface to early
   NYC Artist Coalition organizing?

The pass produced source, claim, evidence, intake, and inquiry records. It did
not publish either protected archive or select a new website claim.

## WOW List database

A July 22, 2017, PostgreSQL production snapshot was parsed without importing
row-level data into the repository. The review reproduced:

- 1,846 user rows;
- 16,142 post and event rows;
- 23,864 tag and list rows;
- 45,562 post-to-tag links;
- 28,837 list follows;
- 20,927 stars;
- 2,965 going signals;
- 92,114 activity rows;
- 25,938 geolocation rows; and
- 35 city-region pairs with at least 50 geocoded posts.

The final threshold reproduces the portfolio's "roughly 35 city ecosystems"
language. It does not turn those places into official chapters. Database rows
also do not equal active people, verified attendance, adoption, reach, or
impact. The public citation remains a redacted aggregate summary; the snapshot
is non-rendered protected support.

## Sunday Dinner workbook

The supplied workbook's `main` sheet contains 349 event columns spanning
January 22, 2012, through March 7, 2021. A conservative positional analysis
counted only canonical `Y` and `N` values and found:

- 344 event columns with at least one affirmative mark;
- 2,767 affirmative marks;
- 409 named rows with at least one affirmative mark; and
- a median of seven affirmative marks among event columns with any affirmative
  mark.

These are operational signals, not an audited attendance census. A row may
represent more than one person; names were not independently deduplicated;
blanks and noncanonical annotations were excluded; and duplicate, missing, and
unnumbered event headers remain data-quality limits. Names, contact fields,
annotations, and row-level histories remain outside the repository.

The source materially strengthens the existing 300-plus-gathering record. It
does not establish the separate 20-plus resident-artist aggregate.

## Call Script and coalition formation

The authenticated public review recovered this sequence:

| Date | Public record | What it supports | Where it stops |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2016 | [popular.vote archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20161211233030/http://popular.vote/) | WOW List civic event-sharing surface | No participation total or complete team ownership |

| 2017-01-27 | [DCA event and discussion](https://www.facebook.com/events/388137698233507/?active_tab=discussion) | Public DIY-space gathering; current display includes Call Script and NYC Artist Coalition among the hosts | Current host display may reflect later edits; response labels are not attendance |
| 2017-01-28 | Same discussion | Call Script called for a united voice and a group email | Account authorship is not individual authorship; attributed turnout language is not independently verified |
| 2017-01-31 | Same discussion | NYC Artist Coalition invited participants to choose a date and later set the agenda | Poll responses are not attendance, reach, or mandate |
| 2017-02-06 | [First recovered general meeting](https://www.facebook.com/events/406505576359490/) | The description invited people to create a coalition, direct its work, build mutual support, and develop recommendations together | No complete founding group, division of labor, or individual authorship |

Together, these records support a held historical synthesis: public project
records document a transition from Call Script's `popular.vote`-linked civic
coordination practice into early participatory NYC Artist Coalition organizing.
The synthesis is not a sole-causality or sole-founder claim.

## Jamie's role

Existing public reporting identifies Jamie as a founding member of NYC Artist
Coalition. The Facebook records reviewed here document the collective
formation sequence and project-account practice, but they do not identify who
authored every post, designed every interaction, or facilitated every meeting.

Jamie remembers connecting WOW List practice, Call Script, and coalition
formation. That first-person account remains a research lead rather than
independent corroboration. A more specific public role claim requires a dated
role-bearing artifact, safe page-administration evidence, or collaborator
confirmation. Collective credit remains required in every case.
