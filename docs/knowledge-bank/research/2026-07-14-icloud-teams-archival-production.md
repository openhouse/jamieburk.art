**Projection decision:** Knowledge-bank intake only. No website change.

## Purpose

This review develops public-safe professional knowledge from working archives
without copying their private contents into the public repository. It treats
folder indexes and synthesis documents as navigation aids, closely reads a
small set of high-value sources, separates plans from completed work, and
records unavailable cloud placeholders as unavailable rather than absent.

The result is twelve source records and three intake threads:

1. interactive media, sound, and public-memory practice;
2. Commercial Rent Stabilization operating and public-data infrastructure; and
3. AI evals completion and bounded source-backed sprint design.

Every new intake remains `no-public-projection`.

## Method

1. Read the anchor overview for each required collection.
2. Route from overview claims to the underlying public page or protected work
   product.
3. Confirm that a readable file body, not merely a filename, supports each
   proposition.
4. Separate project existence, Jamie's role, collaborator credit, output,
   outcome, chronology, and current status.
5. Register protected sources by stable locator ID only. Do not record local
   filesystem paths.
6. Exclude raw correspondence, transcripts, contact details, participant
   records, internal strategy, private coursework, and pricing.
7. Treat source conflicts and cloud placeholders as research constraints.

## Developed Knowledge

### Interactive media and public memory

The public record supports three portable actor-action-output claims:

- Jamie co-designed NTER CHNG with Drew Bolton and Garrett Fuselier; Mary
  Nichols helped engineer and construct the wall.
- Jamie built a Max/MSP hackathon program that segmented audio and reordered
  clips by pitch.
- Jamie and Michael Rees created Claudette's Theatre On Wheels as an
  app-accessed interactive public-memory work.

These projects preserve a concrete historical line from participant messaging,
through computational sound, to interactive archival presentation. The Matmos
tour-video source remains below claim threshold because it prints `Burkhardt`,
not `Burkart`.

### CRS operating and data infrastructure

The protected sources support artifact-specific claims about Jamie's work:

- a ninety-day operating plan built around six shared public goods;
- a running-minutes and action-tracking system with consent-aware story
  handling;
- a policy memo asking for open aggregate commercial rent and vacancy
  indicators; and
- a two-page pilot specification with indicator, coverage and suppression, and
  methods tables.

The sources show authored plans, specifications, and working infrastructure.
They do not establish completion of every proposed deliverable, adoption by all
collaborators, agency adoption, or policy causality. They also do not
independently verify the existing 30+ page aggregate, which retains a separate
inventory trigger.

### Evals and source-backed sprint design

The certificate directly supports course completion. The preparation packet
supports a strong product-operations claim about scoped service design: one
approved source surface, explicit exclusions, a reviewable artifact, human
correction, and a continue, revise, or stop decision.

The packet does not establish a client engagement or deployment. The
cross-archive map remains context only, preventing circular proof in which an
AI-generated synthesis becomes evidence for its own summaries.

## Public-Safety Exclusions

The ingestion does not reproduce:

- filesystem paths;
- personal phone numbers or email addresses;
- private correspondence or raw transcripts;
- coalition contact lists, private stories, targets, or strategy;
- private course, cohort, or submission material;
- private collaborator or company context;
- pricing or negotiation details; or
- unapproved images, audio, or video.

The public repository contains only source metadata, bounded summaries,
propositions, correction triggers, and research questions.
