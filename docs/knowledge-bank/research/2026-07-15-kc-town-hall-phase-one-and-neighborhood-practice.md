# KC Town Hall Phase One and Neighborhood Practice

Date: 2026-07-15  
Status: integrated into the knowledge bank; held from public-site projection.

## Purpose

This pass records a layer of KC Town Hall work that the municipal funding
sequence does not show: multidisciplinary construction coordination,
site-based listening, resident-input infrastructure, recurring neighborhood
service, and pro bono civic communications.

The record keeps four evidence classes distinct:

1. a protected 2019 CCED application package;
2. Jamie's July 2026 participant-memory account;
3. an archived Tired of Tires program page jointly authored as Julia and
   Jamie;
4. an authenticated 2019 KC Town Hall public post naming Jamie among program
   participants.

The source PDF is not committed or linked from the repository. Its appendices
contain personal financial, banking, signature, and contact information.

## Phase One

Jamie reports serving as general contractor and daily field coordinator for
Phase One of the KC Town Hall restoration, completed in 2019. His account
describes coordinating dependencies across historic masonry, roofing,
carpentry and framing, welding, engineering, architecture, and plumbing. One
specific sequence joined the TPO roof membrane to restored parapet work so the
membrane could land beneath the historic ceramic caps.

The 2019 application independently corroborates important surrounding facts:

- Jamie Burkart and Julia Fredenburg are named as founders and project
  managers;
- a multidisciplinary team is named across historic masonry, architecture,
  roofing, concrete, electrical work, and legal support;
- Phase One is defined as a cold-shell scope covering roof, masonry, floor
  framing, water service, staging, debris removal, access, transport,
  acquisition, safety, tools, and soft costs;
- the package describes substantial 2018 progress and uses a 2019 completion
  label;
- the package displays $189,629 as the Phase One total.

The package does not independently assign Jamie the general-contractor title,
verify every duty, or function as a permit history, completion certificate, or
reconciled audit. Its visible budget arithmetic does not fully reconcile, so
the $189,629 figure is retained only as the package's displayed project total.
Phase One cold-shell completion is not full adaptive-reuse completion.

## Participatory Infrastructure

Jamie reports designing a four-by-six-inch neighborhood survey handbill and a
backing data-collection system. The application reproduces the survey card,
names Oak Park Neighborhood Association and New Horizon Missionary Baptist
Church as partners, and states that the ongoing survey directly shaped the
proposal.

Jamie also describes daily presence on the construction site as a listening
practice: neighborhood histories, appreciation, and ideas about the building's
future accumulated alongside field coordination. A third-party support letter
in the application corroborates that Jamie and Julia were engaged in a
listening-driven process centered on surrounding-neighborhood assets and
needs. It does not independently verify each exchange or assign the practice
to Jamie alone.

Respondent identities, contact details, raw responses, and sensitive stories
remain protected. The survey is not described as statistically representative,
and the record does not claim that every request was implemented.

## TiredOfTires

Jamie reports designing and coordinating TiredOfTires with the City on behalf
of Oak Park Neighborhood Association and conducting recurring pickup,
recycling-handoff, tracking, and cross-promotion work.

The archived program page documents a monthly free residential pickup service
run by KC Town Hall with Oak Park. It includes resident-request and volunteer
pathways and is jointly authored as Julia and Jamie. A June 2019 public project
update separately names Jamie's account among participants after a pickup and
routes residents to the next collection.

These public records corroborate the operating system and Jamie's direct 2019
participation. They do not independently establish his full design and city-
coordination role, audited tire or savings totals, every later service date, or
the remembered expansion into Indian Mound.
