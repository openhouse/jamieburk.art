# Google Drive Shared Drives Archival Production

**Review date:** 2026-07-14  
**Collection:** Jamie Burkart's Google Drive Shared Drives  
**Projection decision:** Knowledge-bank intake only. No website change.

## Purpose

This review tests whether Jamie's Shared Drives contain public-safe evidence of
professional accomplishments and handoff practice that is not already captured
in the knowledge bank. The Drive account is a collaboration and working-memory
surface, not a publication surface. Shared access therefore does not establish
authorship, adoption, endorsement, acceptance, or consent to publish.

The result is four protected source records and three intake threads:

1. 196 Artists Residency selection, onboarding, and access operations;
2. longitudinal vacancy-source stewardship and mixed-format archive automation;
3. Shared Drive workspace and handoff practice as a research map.

Every new intake remains `no-public-projection`.

## Method

1. Inventory all 110 Shared Drives visible to the authenticated account.
2. Exclude personal, relationship, legal, financial, participant-sensitive,
   wedding, account-repair, and synchronization-remediation collections.
3. Map the top-level structure of 24 professional project roots across civic,
   cultural, technical, community, education, data, and job-hunt work.
4. Route from folder topology to readable file bodies, not filenames alone.
5. Closely read the high-value file bodies and inspect revision attribution
   where authorship materially affects a proposition.
6. Compare recovered artifacts with existing knowledge-bank sources to avoid
   counting duplicate cloud copies as independent evidence.
7. Register protected sources with opaque locator IDs only. Do not record Drive
   IDs, links, local paths, correspondence, participant logistics, or private
   media.
8. Separate actor, action, artifact, outcome, adoption, chronology, current
   status, collaborator credit, and publication rights.

## Developed Knowledge

### Residency operations

The recovered acceptance document is more operationally specific than a generic
claim that Jamie hosts artists. It shows a sequence:

1. review a proposal and portfolio;
2. select and welcome the resident;
3. schedule a pre-arrival conversation;
4. configure the physical space around the proposed work; and
5. arrange independent access.

The surrounding archive includes reusable acceptance messaging and
resident-specific workspaces. Together these support one bounded workflow
claim. They do not establish that every resident followed the same process, the
outcome of the selected residency, the program's complete operating model, or
publication permission for the resident's identity and logistics.

### Longitudinal vacancy-source stewardship

The working archive preserves 84 source packages representing 80 distinct
quarterly HUD-USPS vacancy snapshots from December 2005 through December 2025.
It also preserves 2010 and 2020 Census geography materials. The inventory makes
two quality conditions visible:

- March 2019 is absent from the recovered quarterly sequence;
- four 2012 quarters appear in both older and newer source-format groupings.

Representative revision records from the beginning, middle, and end of the
sequence identify Jamie as the last modifying user. This supports a bounded
claim that Jamie assembled and maintained the working collection. It does not
validate every file, establish a complete series, produce an analysis result,
or turn the archive into a published dataset.

### Mixed-format archive automation

Revision metadata identifies Jamie as the author of a reusable Bash script that
generates project overviews from mixed working folders. The script's documented
behavior includes:

- directory inventory;
- PDF text extraction with OCR fallback;
- DOCX conversion with tracked changes and comments when available;
- bounded treatment of large CSV and JSON files;
- exclusions for dependencies and generated files;
- output-line controls; and
- an explicit warning that the generated overview may expose sensitive data.

The last point matters. The tool does not confuse making an archive legible
with making it public. It builds a review artifact and names the need for human
care before handoff or publication.

### Shared-workspace topology

Across the professional roots, the archive repeatedly uses project-scoped
drives, collaborator-specific workspaces, dated delivery folders, and reusable
templates. This is meaningful context for a handoff practice, but it remains
below claim threshold without source-associated evidence of who created a
workspace, how it was used, what changed for a collaborator, and whether its
contents may be described publicly.

## Duplicate And Below-Threshold Findings

- A public-data handout in a Shared Drive is substantively the same artifact
  already registered from the iCloud CRS archive. It is not counted as a new
  independent source. Its Shared Drive placement does not establish official
  event acceptance or agency adoption.
- A cleaned course-research CSV demonstrates structured comparison work but is
  retained outside the bank because the present evidence does not establish a
  professional project, recipient, or consequential use.
- Dated photographs, animations, and videos appear in several cultural-project
  drives. File presence does not establish Jamie's role, delivery acceptance,
  public credit, or publication rights, so these remain a research queue.
- Sunday Dinner and residency photographs remain outside the repo pending
  subject, photographer, rights, and editorial review.
- Raw recordings, private minutes, access information, legal and financial
  records, and collaborator-only materials were not close-read for public claim
  development where their likely sensitivity outweighed their evidentiary need.

## Public-Safety Exclusions

The ingestion does not reproduce:

- Drive names that expose personal or sensitive context;
- Drive or file IDs, links, permissions, or account metadata;
- resident or participant names, dates, contact requests, addresses, keys, or
  access instructions;
- private correspondence, minutes, transcripts, or recordings;
- donor, subscriber, applicant, guest, stakeholder, or collaborator lists;
- legal, financial, insurance, property, credential, or account records;
- source datasets, generated private overviews, or local filesystem paths; or
- unapproved photographs, animations, audio, or video.

The public repository contains only public-safe metadata, bounded summaries,
propositions, non-claims, and research questions.
