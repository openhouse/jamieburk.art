# WOW List Facebook Posts Archival Production

**Run date:** July 14, 2026  
**Public Page:** [facebook.com/wowlist](https://www.facebook.com/wowlist/)  
**Private artifact ID:** `wowlist-facebook-public-post-census-2026-07-14`

## Result

This pass close-read 100% of the unique posts currently recoverable from the
WOW List public Facebook Page timeline in Jamie's authenticated Page-management
view.

- Two terminal-scroll traversals at different step sizes produced an exact
  post-level match.
- Facebook rendered 56 post-like variants.
- Two variants were platform artifacts: one duplicate autoplay rendering and
  one Featured composite wrapper.
- The retained current public-timeline population is **54 unique posts**.
- Both Featured records occur within those 54 records.
- Meta's Lifetime Content Library displayed **five** rows. All five occur in the
  timeline population, but the library omits older Featured material and is not
  a historical population control.
- Every retained post exposed Facebook's admin-only publisher field, and all
  **54 of 54 named Jamie Burkart**.

Here, “100%” means 100% of the currently recoverable public Page timeline. It
does not mean every post ever created. Deleted, unpublished, migration-omitted,
or otherwise unavailable records cannot be counted from these surfaces.

The public-safe aggregate control is
[`wowlist-facebook-post-controls.json`](../data/wowlist-facebook-post-controls.json).
The record-level text, links, and platform metadata remain outside the public
repository.

## Method

1. Switched the authenticated Facebook session into the WOW List Page identity.
2. Inspected the public Page, Featured area, Posts timeline, and Professional
   Dashboard Content Library.
3. Set the Content Library date filter to Lifetime and recorded all five rows.
4. Traversed the virtualized public timeline to terminal scroll twice, using
   different scroll increments.
5. Isolated individual `Published by` records from Facebook's changing
   virtualized containers.
6. Removed only two documented render duplicates and compared exact retained
   fingerprints between both passes.
7. Close-read all 54 retained posts for project identity, local-organizer use,
   public-source routing, cultural-space support, civic action, stakeholders,
   and current engagement displays.
8. Decoded currently exposed Facebook redirect destinations and removed
   tracking parameters.
9. Preserved the full record-level census privately and promoted only
   public-safe aggregates, selected public posts, source leads, claims,
   anti-claims, and inquiries into the repository.

## Role Finding

Jamie's recollection is that WOW List was his and Richard Album's project and
that Jamie believes he managed all of its social presence. The recovered
Facebook evidence materially strengthens that recollection: every one of the 54
currently recoverable Page posts identifies Jamie in Facebook's Page-manager
publisher field.

The strongest current formulation is:

> Jamie published the complete currently recoverable WOW List Facebook Page
> corpus. Jamie also recalls managing the project's social presence; that
> broader management claim remains attributed pending an account-administration
> record or collaborator confirmation.

This does **not** establish that Jamie was the sole Page administrator, that he
alone set every social strategy, or that he authored material quoted or shared
from other people and organizations. WOW List remains jointly credited to Jamie
Burkart and Richard Album.

## What The Corpus Shows

### A public identity system for local organizers

The Page did more than announce product releases. It repeatedly routed people
into joinable calendars and highlighted organizers maintaining local cultural
records. Recovered examples include Phoenix, Los Angeles, Santa Barbara,
Seattle, Chicago, Iowa City, and the Sylvan Annex community, alongside artist
and tour calendars.

Several posts preserve attributed organizer language describing WOW List as an
alternative to Facebook for finding underground events or as a tool for helping
people spend more time together in the physical world. Those statements support
an adoption and design-intent record; they are not independent product-analytics
metrics.

### Community-first design as a stated value

A May 23, 2016, Page post shared a member's reflection after an online meeting.
The reflection described WOW List as putting community first and making it easy
for people to gather. A separate October 5, 2015, video post said members in nine
cities had introduced community calendars and linked a join route.

The member quotation is evidence of that member's experience and the Page's
editorial choice to amplify it. The nine-city formulation remains an attributed
WOW List statement until independent city-by-city records are associated with
it.

### Cultural infrastructure, safety, and mutual aid

The recovered Page repeatedly routed attention and resources toward artist-run
and all-ages spaces. Posts surfaced or amplified campaigns involving Meow Wolf's
DIY Fund, a Denver DIY spaces fund, Shea Stadium, pehrspace, Silent Barn, Trunk
Space, Orange County DIY, The Know, Ghost Ship victim support, and safer-spaces
organizing.

Two destination articles were recovered and close-read in this pass:

- Patricia Calhoun's [Westword report on a Denver Arts & Venues and Meow Wolf
  DIY-space fund](https://www.westword.com/arts-culture/city-partners-with-meow-wolf-on-20-000-denver-diy-spaces-fund-8782025/).
- Matthew Singer's [Willamette Week report on The Know's closure and intended
  relocation](https://www.wweek.com/bars/2016/07/01/the-know-is-closing/).

Other current cards preserve title-level routes to KQED, East Bay Express,
This Used to Be Us, Meow Wolf, Patreon, GoFundMe, YouCaring, and other campaign
or publication surfaces. Those remain research leads where the current card no
longer exposes a stable destination URL or the destination body has not yet been
close-read.

### Civic and movement routing

The Page connected event discovery to broader public action. Recovered posts
routed readers to Women's March events, Standing Rock and Water Is Life events,
a post-election resistance calendar, artist-to-artist support for underground
scenes, and a July 2017 Cabaret Law repeal action shared from NYC Artist
Coalition.

This demonstrates a public-routing pattern and a visible lineage between WOW
List and Jamie's later cultural-policy work. It does not establish that WOW List
created every campaign, that linked organizations endorsed WOW List, or that a
Facebook post caused attendance or policy change.

## Engagement Snapshot

At capture, the 54 retained posts displayed **88 visible reactions across 39
posts**. The October 2015 nine-city video displayed **13 likes, 3 comments, and
53 views**. The current Page displayed **185 followers**.

These values are preserved as dated platform observations only. They are
mutable, incomplete as historical analytics, and not measures of attendance,
conversion, community size, or impact. A shared agency-event card's “303
interested” display is likewise not a WOW List attendance or audience count.

## Stakeholder Pattern

The corpus shows public interaction with several kinds of stakeholders:

- local calendar maintainers and DIY-scene organizers;
- artists, performers, touring projects, and festival organizers;
- artist-run venues and cultural-space support campaigns;
- intermediary organizations including Allied Media Projects and Meow Wolf;
- movement and civic-action campaigns; and
- NYC Artist Coalition and a New York City Council action surface.

For this pass, “stakeholder pattern” means recurring public routing,
attribution, and shared-source context. A tag, share, event-interest display, or
linked Page is not silently upgraded into endorsement, partnership, attendance,
or causal impact.
