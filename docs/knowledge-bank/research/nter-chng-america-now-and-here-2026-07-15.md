# NTER CHNG and America: Now and Here

Date: 2026-07-15
Status: Public-safe source accession and claim review

## Finding

America: Now and Here included NTER CHNG in its 2011 Kansas City launch. The
program's own archived Kansas City site has a dedicated visual-artist page
titled "Drew Bolton, Jamie Burkart, and Garrett Fuselier." It identifies the
three collaborators as visual artists and publishes their NTER CHNG artist
statement.

This is direct program evidence. It moves the exhibition-inclusion fact beyond
inference from later institutional context.

## Recovered sources

### NTER CHNG project site

[A January 28, 2011 Wayback capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20110128193350/http://nterchng.com/)
identifies NTER CHNG as an interactive texting installation by Drew Bolton,
Jamie Burkart, and Garrett Fuselier. It lists Arts Incubator Cocoon Gallery in
Kansas City as the venue. The page does not print a year for its exhibition
notice, so the capture date must not be treated as the presentation date.

### America: Now and Here program context

[The program's archived Kansas City about page](https://web.archive.org/web/20110503025503/http://kansascity.americanowandhere.org/about-anh-kc/)
describes Kansas City as the first America: Now and Here installation. It says
the program used visual art, poetry, music, theater, and film to invite public
dialogue and participation.

### Official visual-artist record

[The program's archived artist page](https://web.archive.org/web/20110518071626/http://kansascity.americanowandhere.org/the-visual-artists/drew-bolton-jamie-burkart-and-garrett-fuselier/)
provides the strongest evidence. The official page:

- lists Drew Bolton, Jamie Burkart, and Garrett Fuselier as visual artists;
- publishes the NTER CHNG artist statement;
- describes the work as an interactive text-messaging experience that made a
  private one-to-one exchange public and many-to-many;
- describes the collaborators as combining scenic design, computer
  programming, motion graphics, and experiential production.

The capture also contains historical phone numbers and visitor-response
excerpts. Those details are not reproduced in the knowledge bank.

### Nerman Museum context

[The Nerman Museum record](https://www.nermanmuseum.org/exhibitions/2011-05-11-kruger-barbara-america-now-here.html)
reproduces Alice Thorson's April 30, 2011 Kansas City Star account of the
program's Kansas City launch. It records a Barbara Kruger truck at the Nerman
Museum on May 11 and 12, 2011.

The Nerman source does **not** name NTER CHNG. It establishes institutional and
program context, not NTER CHNG's presentation at the museum.

## Public-ready claim

> America: Now and Here included NTER CHNG in its 2011 Kansas City launch; the
> program's own archived site listed Drew Bolton, Jamie Burkart, and Garrett
> Fuselier as visual artists and published their artist statement for the
> participatory texting installation.

The claim is preserved as public-ready and deferred from website projection.
It expands the compositional palette without automatically expanding the live
portfolio.

## Chad's lens

The useful professional story is not only that the work appeared in an
exhibition. Jamie helped create a usable participatory system: software, a
constructed interface, and visitors' own phones became a social information
space in which strangers could contribute to a shared dialogue. The
America: Now and Here inclusion supplies meaningful independent context for
that collaborative result.

Collective credit remains essential. Drew Bolton, Jamie Burkart, and Garrett
Fuselier were the three makers. The linked project claim also preserves Mary
Nichols's wall-engineering and construction support and the performer credits
for Megan Mantia and Elisha Stetson.

## Boundaries and anti-claims

- Do not describe Jamie as the sole creator of NTER CHNG.
- Do not assign the software, wall engineering, motion graphics, or other
  implementation work to one collaborator without a source that does so.
- Do not describe Jamie as an America: Now and Here organizer or curator.
- Do not say NTER CHNG was presented at the Nerman Museum.
- Do not use the Nerman page as direct evidence for NTER CHNG.
- Do not infer audience size, critical reception, adoption, or civic impact
  from exhibition inclusion.
- Do not republish historical phone numbers or visitor-response excerpts from
  the archived artist page.

## Open research

A formal checklist, catalog, program schedule, installation view, or
venue-specific record could establish NTER CHNG's exact location and dates
within the Kansas City launch. Until that is recovered, the safe language is
"included in the 2011 Kansas City launch" or "listed in its official
visual-artist roster."
