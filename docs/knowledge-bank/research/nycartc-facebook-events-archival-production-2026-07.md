# NYC Artist Coalition Facebook Event Archival Production

**Capture and revalidation date:** 2026-07-14
**Surface:** [NYC Artist Coalition Facebook events](https://www.facebook.com/nycartc/events)
**Control:** 34 past events displayed on event host cards
**Accounting:** 33 recovered event records + 1 unresolved control slot

## Answer First

The surviving Facebook event record makes NYC Artist Coalition's operating
practice concrete. The coalition did not only publish positions. It created
repeated occasions for people to gather, compare lived experience, learn
practical safety and legal pathways, set priorities, and carry those priorities
into campaigns, public hearings, and conversations with city officials.

Jamie describes creating this public event and participation layer as a
substantial contribution, joining lessons from WOW List with legislative
advocacy. The event pages prove that the collective system existed and was
used. They do not identify the individual author or producer of every page.
The portfolio therefore uses **helped establish and produce**, preserves
collective credit, and keeps individual event-page authorship outside the
claim.

## Participation System

Twelve recovered records are recurring coalition meetings. Two were virtual;
the other ten name ten different physical venues:

- Magick City
- The Floasis
- Muchmore's
- The City Reliquary
- Shoestring Press
- Chinatown Soup
- Secret Project Robot
- Friends and Lovers
- Flowers for all Occasions
- Ode to Babel

That supports a rotating-venue practice, not a claim that the coalition met in
every calendar month or that every coalition event used a new venue.

Across the wider event population, a repeatable operating loop appears:

1. **Gather and listen.** Meetings invited participants to set priorities,
   propose working groups, and share what cultural spaces needed.
2. **Make support practical.** Events included Fire Guard test preparation,
   legal and architectural Q&A, community-space survey design, venue-support
   actions, and later pandemic relief coordination.
3. **Translate experience into an ask.** Meetings joined lived concerns with
   Cabaret Law repeal, CreateNYC, Office of Nightlife accountability, M.A.R.C.H.
   transparency, commercial lease protections, and Fair Rent NYC.
4. **Create a public path.** Event descriptions supplied a time, place, issue
   frame, action, and invitation to attend, testify, call, organize, or bring
   another concern into the coalition.
5. **Return and revise.** Later meetings reviewed developments, reported back,
   and set new priorities rather than treating a hearing or rally as the end.

The February 2019 M.A.R.C.H. hearing event put the participation ethic in four
words: **"Being there changes everything."** It then asked participants to tell
their stories. That documents intended function; it does not prove that a
Facebook response became testimony or that any one event caused a policy
outcome.

## Public Institutions

The October 2017 Market Hotel description named Council Members Rafael Espinal,
Antonio Reynoso, and Stephen Levin, Department of Cultural Affairs Commissioner
Tom Finkelpearl, and Mayor's Office of Media and Entertainment Commissioner
Julie Menin in its public program. The March 2018 event named the first nightlife
director, Ariel Palitz, with Council Members Espinal and Reynoso. Later meeting
descriptions also named Council members as scheduled participants.

Names on an event page are not proof of attendance. Independent Village Voice
reporting separately described Council and city-agency representatives at the
October town hall.

## Response And Attendance

A prior authenticated accounting retained event-level Facebook response
displays for 32 records, from 9 to 1.7K. This fresh pass re-observed 25; seven
detail surfaces had become unavailable or limited. Those values are platform
response signals, not people reached or attendance.

The October 2017 Market Hotel event demonstrates the boundary. Facebook
displayed 1.2K responses. Independent Village Voice reporting described a crowd
of about one hundred people. The portfolio uses the journalistic description
for bounded physical scale and never converts response signals into turnout.

The wording is strong and bounded because Jamie directly confirms the

## Public Safety

The pass excluded guest and attendee identities, invite and friend context,
comments, reactions, participant profile links, private administration, contact
details copied from descriptions, meeting links and credentials, and raw
captures. The public bank retains only institutional event metadata and
professional interpretation.

## Eval Contract

The lifecycle eval fails if an event disappears from the 33-record set, the
unresolved slot is invented, Page association becomes coalition hosting,
Facebook responses become attendance, scheduled officials become confirmed
attendees, private research artifacts become public, Jamie becomes sole author,
or the recurring pattern becomes an uninterrupted monthly claim.
