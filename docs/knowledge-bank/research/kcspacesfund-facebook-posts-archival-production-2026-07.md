# KC Spaces Fund Facebook Posts Archival Production

**Run date:** July 14, 2026

**Public Page:** [facebook.com/KCSpacesFund](https://www.facebook.com/KCSpacesFund/)

**Private artifact ID:** `kcspacesfund-facebook-public-post-census-2026-07-14`

## Result

This pass close-read 100% of the Page-level posts currently recoverable from
the KC Spaces Fund public Facebook timeline in Jamie's authenticated view.

- Two traversals with different scroll cadences reconciled the same **37**
  Page-level records.
- The traversals reached **22** and **20** terminal no-addition controls.
- The currently visible chronology runs from April 7 through July 9, 2020.
- **33** records carry outer Page text, **four** are no-message attachment
  shells, and **20** carry a photo attachment.
- Every retained record received a date, message-or-attachment, mission,
  grantee, route, and current-reaction disposition.

Here, “100%” means complete disposition of the Page-level population currently
recoverable on July 14, 2026. It does not mean every post ever created. Deleted,
unpublished, migration-omitted, and otherwise unavailable records cannot be
counted from the observed surface.

The public-safe aggregate control is
[`kcspacesfund-facebook-post-controls.json`](../data/kcspacesfund-facebook-post-controls.json).
The record-level corpus, authentication state, and relational details remain
outside the public repository.

## Method

1. Used Jamie's authenticated Facebook session and opened the KC Spaces Fund
   public Page.
2. Applied Facebook's 2020 post filter.
3. Ran two timeline traversals with different scroll increments and terminal
   controls.
4. Selected outer records whose Page identity began with `KC Spaces Fund`,
   including location-qualified Page posts.
5. Excluded nested shared-story cards from the Page-post denominator.
6. Reconciled date plus photo ID where exposed; otherwise used date plus the
   expanded outer Page message or attachment class.
7. Close-read all 37 records for mission, action, grantee, regional, print,
   source, and current visible-response patterns.
8. Collapsed duplicate anchor-plus-inline URL renderings and normalized current
   route strings.
9. Preserved the full record-level census privately and promoted public-safe
   aggregates, selected public sources, claims, anti-claims, and inquiries.

## Campaign Practice

The Page documents more than a fundraiser announcement. Its recurring pattern
connected:

- donation and application routes;
- emergency grants up to $500;
- explicit priority for BIPOC, LGBTQIA+, Disabled, and Immigrant communities;
- a rolling April-through-June process;
- expansion from the Kansas City area to Lawrence, Kansas;
- public grantee and disbursement announcements; and
- a wider mutual-aid print network.

The public [application page](https://kcspacesfund.com/apply/) corroborates the
eligibility, priority, timing, geography, fiscal-sponsor, and named-organizer
context. One close-read [Oddities Prints artifact](https://www.odditiesprints.com/odd-shop/frank-norton-kaiju)
documents a Mutual Aid Print Exchange item whose proceeds partially benefited
KC Spaces Fund and KC Tenants.

The current Facebook corpus includes **12 named grantee or disbursement
announcements**: Latino Foundation for the Arts, UN/TUCK, Battery Tour / AY
Young, Get Woke, Trans Women of Color Collective, Blackbox on Troost, One Mic
Stand, Farewell Transmission, Parker 2, Kansas City Textile Arts Center, SWAN,
and Vulpes Bastille.

That is an announcement chronology, not a complete grant ledger. It does not
identify Jamie as an organizer, grant decision-maker, fundraiser owner, or
fiscal sponsor.

## Jamie's Role

Jamie's separately accessioned first-person statement says that he was not the
stakeholder or owner posting through the account. The Page corpus is therefore
treated as the campaign's institutional public voice, not as Jamie's authored
social record; the statement does not identify every human publisher or provide
a native publisher ledger.

Separate archival evidence strongly supports a behind-the-scenes digital role:

- the surviving campaign-site repository contains 73 commits authored by
  Jamie;
- the fundraising-widget repository contains 10 commits authored by Jamie;
- the campaign-theme history contains 34 Jamie-authored commits; and
- a protected launch checklist assigns website-template changes to Jamie.

The strongest current formulation remains:

> Jamie supported KC Spaces Fund as behind-the-scenes digital infrastructure,
> implementing and maintaining the Ghost campaign site, campaign-theme changes,
> fundraising-display widget, deployment configuration, and web action
> affordances.

The public campaign named Caitlin Horsmon, Jordan Carr, Kendell Harbin, and
Megan Pobywajlo as organizers. That credit remains intact.

Jamie also recalls supporting selection of a project name available uniformly
across social platforms and domain names. The surviving
[campaign site](https://kcspacesfund.com/) corroborates the uniform public
identity outcome through its domain and linked social accounts. It does not
identify who proposed, selected, approved, registered, or administered the
name. The naming contribution therefore remains attributed and open to
collaborator or contemporaneous-record corroboration.
