## What the source supports

### Publication and project context

- The article appeared on the November 15, 2007, front page and continued on
  page A4.
- It identifies Libby Hendon, James Burkart, and Laura Mattingly as the crew.
- It describes travel from Kansas City toward Louisiana and places the crew
  south of Baton Rouge at the time of publication.

### Jamie's initiating role

The article reports that the project began with a dream, vision, or idea by
Jamie. A separate 2007 Pitch article corroborates his initiating role. This is
strong evidence for originating the project concept, but it is not sole credit
for construction, navigation, facilitation, or the expedition as a whole.

### Collective apparatus

The article describes a roughly 12-by-13-foot raft built in three weeks from
discarded building materials, civic refuse, and beverage-industry drums. Two
bicycles were linked to a paddlewheel. The article does not allocate the design,
fabrication, or maintenance work person by person.

### Participatory method

The article describes the work as a living experience on the rivers. It reports
that the crew stopped for supplies and interaction, invited people aboard, and
welcomed friends for stretches of the journey. This supports a participatory
form, not a complete participant census or a measured social outcome.

### Civic interpretation

The article attributes to Jamie an interpretation of the river as an invisible
space, dividing line, historic novelty, or industrial pipeline in Kansas City,
along with the hope that cultural connection between the West Bottoms and Delta
communities could be reawakened through the river. This is a contemporaneously
reported viewpoint and aspiration, not independent proof that all residents
shared the interpretation or that the expedition produced that outcome.
