# Jamie Burkart Personal Facebook Post Archival Production

Date: 2026-07-14

## Result

An authenticated review accounted for **100 percent of the surviving records
returned by Facebook's `Manage Posts > Posted by: You` population control** in
this session.

The terminal cursor run returned:

- 621 cursor pages;
- 3,728 returned nodes;
- 1,243 unique stable story records after deduplication;
- 998 records with readable message text; and
- 245 records whose text was unavailable or primarily media-led.

Facebook returned its terminal pagination flag. The result is complete for that
surviving filtered surface in the July 2026 authenticated session. It is not a
claim that the interface contains every post Jamie ever created.

See the
[1,243-row aggregate-only census](../data/jamie-facebook-post-census-2026-07-14.csv).

## Population Control

Jamie's ordinary profile timeline is not a reliable population control. It
mixes authored records with other social surfaces, renders a moving window, and
does not expose a simple total.

The authenticated Manage Posts interface supplied `Anyone`, `You`, and
`Others` authorship controls. This pass selected `You`, confirmed that the
corresponding owner filter was active, and followed Facebook's server cursor to
its terminal flag.

The 1,243 records are therefore records Facebook placed in Jamie's
owner-authored surface. They are not posts by other people, a list of everything
in which Jamie was tagged, a native Meta export, or a reconstruction of his
full social graph.

## Replay And Deduplication

The cursor did not return a simple one-pass sequence. Facebook replayed nearly
the entire unique population before terminating:

- 1,242 unique records appeared three times; and
- one unique record appeared twice.

The archive preserves the distinction between 3,728 returned nodes and 1,243
unique records. Every public census row represents one stable unique record
using a sequential aggregate-only identifier.

## Posted Destinations And Source Discovery

Every record was checked for posted external destinations. Across the full
population:

- 430 records carried at least one external URL;
- 718 URL occurrences resolved to 564 unique URLs across 195 domains;
- 139 professionally relevant records carried 242 URL occurrences; and
- those occurrences resolved to 176 unique URLs across 74 domains.

The complete URL inventory remains protected because a destination can expose
the subject, audience, or relationship context of a personal post. Aggregate
patterns show repeated routing to project infrastructure, including WOW List,
Sunday Dinner, NYC Artist Coalition, Let NYC Dance, Talks Not Raids, and Save
NYC Spaces surfaces.

The inventory also recovered routes to public sources already represented in
the bank, including the Good Times Open House profile, Charlotte Street
Foundation's Great Accommodations record, NPR and WNYC Cabaret Law coverage,
and public government and cultural-planning records. These are source-discovery
associations. Posting a link does not establish that its author, institution,
or any named stakeholder engaged with Jamie's post or endorsed his
interpretation.

One route materially strengthened the source bank: Blair Schulman's 2009
[ArtTattler review of Great Accommodations](https://web.archive.org/web/20141018214944/http://arttattler.com/commentaryjamieburkart.html).
The review independently documents the participatory exhibition, the river as
connective social infrastructure, and the installation's trust and mutual-help
premise. It repeats Jamie's account of traveling down the Missouri and
Mississippi until the water tasted salty; it does not independently establish
an exact Gulf endpoint. The originating Facebook post remains protected
provenance.

## Close-Reading Findings

### Recurring implementation practice

Across the candidate set, Jamie's record repeatedly moves from an emerging
purpose to a usable public structure. The recurring actions include:

- translating a goal into a clear invitation or call to action;
- creating routes through which people can join, contribute, learn, respond,
  or attend;
- maintaining recognizable project identities across websites, social
  accounts, events, and documentation;
- turning recurring activity into formats people can understand and repeat;
- sharing instructions, scripts, training, and process updates;
- crediting collaborators and community contributors; and
- preserving enough documentation for work to continue and be understood.

This is first-person, contemporaneous role evidence. Independent sources remain
necessary for external outcomes, policy causality, attendance, audience, and
contested credit.

### Project clusters

The close reading selected 47 WOW List-related records. They show product and
community operations such as recruiting calendar stewards, supporting local
calendars, inviting contributions, recognizing members' work, and joining
community values to product behavior. They do not establish sole administration
or adoption.

Forty-three Sunday Dinner-related records preserve a repeatable participation
structure: recurring invitations, changing themes, shared contribution through
food and presence, documentation, continuity across locations, and a documented
100th-gathering milestone. Private addresses, phone numbers, relationships, and
media context remain outside the bank.

Thirty-three NYC Artist Coalition or campaign-related records make Jamie's
implementation contribution legible through meetings, hearings, call scripts,
action routes, fire-safety learning, public-input workflows, milestone
communication, and collective credit. Campaign outcomes remain grounded in
independent press and government sources.

Smaller clusters preserve continuity across waterways, place-based public
programs, technical support, web practice, and cultural documentation. They
remain research leads pending project-specific corroboration and visual review.

## Traction And Stakeholder Boundary

The Manage Posts population query did not recover complete interaction values.
No reaction, comment, or share total is asserted. Missing values are unknown,
not zero.

Record-level actors, tags, organizations, and destinations are outgoing
references. They are not evidence that those people or institutions viewed,
responded to, endorsed, partnered in, or acted on a post. The current pass
therefore does not claim a population-level stakeholder engagement census.

Post counts do not measure labor, reach, attendance, endorsement, adoption,
policy influence, cultural impact, or Jamie's share of collective credit. The
bank retains an open research task for a privacy-safe native-export or
individually verified public-interaction method.

## Public-Safety Boundary

Privacy labels were recovered for only a minority of records. The raw corpus is
protected as a whole rather than presumed public.

The repository census includes only a sequential ledger ID, year, primary form,
broad theme, professional-relevance disposition, accounting status, and
public-detail status. It excludes raw responses, request tokens, stable story
IDs, Facebook URLs, exact dates, post text, names, locations, contact details,
privacy labels, relationships, comments, interactions, and media.

The protected corpus is a research source, not a new dossier. Ordinary life,
relationships, care, grief, health, home, and unreviewed visual context remain
outside the public repository even when they helped Jamie understand his own
history.

## Editorial Disposition

This pass makes no immediate website change. The ArtTattler source strengthens
the reserve Great Accommodations claim. The operating-practice and NYC Artist
Coalition implementation claims are public-ready reserve depth, but the current
portfolio already makes those arguments through stronger public sources and
clearer case studies.

There is no public Facebook archive route, personal timeline, proofs route, or
knowledge-bank route. Future composition should use this depth only when it
materially improves clarity for a defined audience.
