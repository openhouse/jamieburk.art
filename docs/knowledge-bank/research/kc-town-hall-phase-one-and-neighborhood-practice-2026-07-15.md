# KC Town Hall Phase One And Neighborhood Practice

Date reviewed: 2026-07-15  
Status: public-safe research memo; underlying private records remain outside the repository

## Why this accession matters

Earlier KC Town Hall records in the knowledge bank emphasized the 2019 public
funding process and the later return of an unused allocation. That chronology is
important, but it did not describe the substantial building stabilization,
field coordination, neighborhood listening, service operations, and civic
communications work already performed before that allocation.

This accession separates five related practices:

1. Phase One restoration implementation;
2. a neighborhood survey and contact workflow embedded in the site process;
3. recurring Tired of Tires service operations;
4. Cleveland Ave KC: Unify to Beautify; and
5. pro bono neighborhood design, print, and distribution support.

The records preserve Jamie's agency while keeping collective labor, first-party
memory, contemporaneous documentation, public corroboration, and unresolved
research distinct.

## Source position

### Protected contemporaneous proposal

A March 2019 KC Town Hall proposal packet directly documents:

- roof replacement, major structural masonry repair, debris removal, and a
  reopened egress reported complete by 2018;
- a Phase One cold-shell scope spanning roof deck repair, insulation and TPO
  membrane, masonry, framing, water, storage, access, safety, air quality, and
  soft costs;
- a 2019 plan to complete Phase One;
- a neighborhood survey conducted with Oak Park Neighborhood Association and
  New Horizon Missionary Baptist Church whose results directly shaped the
  proposal; and
- a project-reported 2018 local-industry practice involving more than fifteen
  minority-owned contractors and masonry skill development.

The source was created in March 2019. It is strong evidence of work already
complete, the remaining scope, and the participatory process. It is not an
after-the-fact completion report and does not assign Jamie every role. Banking,
contact, address, and other sensitive contents are not stored here.

### Jamie's first-person accounts

Jamie reports that he:

- served as general contractor and daily owner-side field coordinator for Phase
  One;
- coordinated masonry, roofing, carpentry, welding, engineering, architecture,
  and plumbing work from basement conditions through roof and parapet
  interfaces;
- completed Phase One in 2019;
- designed a four-by-six-inch neighborhood survey card and supporting contact
  and data workflow;
- designed and coordinated the Tired of Tires workflow and performed recurring
  pickup, transport, unloading, information-distribution, and logging work;
- later extended that service to Indian Mound;
- helped Pastor Lee establish Cleveland Ave KC: Unify to Beautify through
  identity, photography, social media, corridor maps, listening-session
  materials, and print production; and
- functioned as a pro bono design studio and print shop for neighborhood groups.

These are authorized first-person records. They are evidence of Jamie's account,
not independent verification of every role, date, quantity, or outcome. In
particular, `general contractor` currently describes a reported project
function; it does not establish a professional license.

## Bounded findings

### Phase One implementation

The evidence supports a bounded internal claim that Jamie performed the
reported general-contractor and daily field-coordination function while a
multi-party team stabilized the building. The contemporaneous packet proves
substantial 2018 work and defines the remaining Phase One scope. Jamie supplies
the 2019 completion account. Until dated completion records or collaborators
confirm that last step, public wording should preserve the attribution.

This work is separate from the later $490,539 City Council appropriation. The
official record establishes that the allocation remained unused. It did not pay
for Phase One.

### Participatory site practice

The survey itself and its proposal-shaping purpose are contemporaneously
documented. Jamie's authorship of the card, supporting data workflow, and daily
field use are first-party. Raw responses and contact data remain protected.

The strongest future composition is not that construction happened near a
community-engagement exercise. It is that the construction site became an
ongoing listening surface, allowing building decisions and neighborhood
knowledge to encounter one another over time.

### Tired of Tires

The recurring partner workflow and Jamie's hands-on pickup participation are
publicly corroborated. Jamie's workflow-design and coordination role is
first-party, and the Indian Mound expansion remains a research lead. Social
workflow records, public acknowledgments, and project-reported figures must not
be converted into audited pickup, tire, savings, health, environmental, or
neighborhood-impact totals.

### Cleveland Avenue

The public event proves the initiative, its corridor purpose, resident priority
prompts, and collective hosts. Jamie's implementation role remains first-party.
Pastor Lee retains originating credit for the corridor idea. The evidence
supports public-priority collection and civic routing; it does not yet support
the claim that this work caused a particular discretionary or capital
allocation.

### Neighborhood design and print practice

Jamie's account preserves an important operating pattern: using design, print,
bundling, and field distribution to connect multiple neighborhood workflows
rather than treating communications as a separate layer. Artifact and
collaborator recovery remain open before this becomes a public accomplishment
claim.

## Anti-claims

Do not say:

- Jamie held a general-contractor license;
- Jamie alone owned, built, restored, or completed KC Town Hall;
- the March 2019 proposal independently proves completion later in 2019;
- the unused $490,539 appropriation funded Phase One;
- one hundred social workflow records equal one hundred completed pickups;
- project-reported tire or savings totals are audited;
- the Health Forward grant funded Tired of Tires;
- Indian Mound expansion is independently verified;
- Jamie alone created or led Cleveland Ave KC;
- the Cleveland Avenue initiative caused a particular capital allocation; or
- handbill distribution proves readership, participation, or impact.
