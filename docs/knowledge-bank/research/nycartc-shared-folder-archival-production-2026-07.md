# NYC Artist Coalition Shared-Folder Archival Production

**Snapshot:** July 19, 2026 UTC

**Method:** Authenticated, read-only, recursive Google Drive traversal with
stable private identifiers, scroll-aware item capture, population
reconciliation, one primary disposition per descendant, and selected protected
close reading.

**Public-safety posture:** The exact Drive identifiers, resource key, folder
lineage, filenames, owners, raw exports, comments, and source bodies remain in
an ignored protected manifest outside git. Folder access is not publication
permission.

Completeness here means complete accounting and governance of the accessible
population. It does not mean that every file body was read, that every artifact
is evidence, or that any private item is cleared for publication.

## Priority Close Reading

The first content pass reviewed 18 lower-risk, high-value working documents
across public-meeting production, Cabaret Law testimony, agency
recommendations, Office of Nightlife priorities, and Talks Not Raids campaign
operations.

### Public Meetings As An Operating System

A surviving three-week playbook connects public meetings to:

- participant, partner, and elected-official outreach;
- testimony preparation and printed copies for press and officials;
- one-pagers, letters, websites, handbills, and signs;
- press notification and interviews;
- social and email sequencing;
- live-video coordination; and
- day-before and day-of reminders and follow-through.

This makes the collective event practice legible as implementation work. It
does not identify the original author or assign every task to Jamie. See the
[public-meeting operating-system claim](../claims/nycartc-public-meeting-operating-system.md).

### Participation Design For Testimony

A Cabaret Law testimony guide asks participants to tell a true first-person
story within the Council's short speaking window. It offers prompts about
discrimination, dancing, cultural-space loss, trust, safety, and zoning, and it
offers an email route for people unable to testify in person.

The guide shows that coalition mobilization included support for people to
enter a formal government process. It does not establish who authored the
guide, how many people used it, or what happened to every submission.

### Jamie's Testimony And Safety Argument

Two named testimony drafts deepen the existing public record of Jamie's 2017
Cabaret Law advocacy. They connect repeal to practical fire-safety walkthroughs,
workshops, study groups, and the need for trust between cultural spaces and city
agencies.

Public reporting remains the citation basis for the website. The protected
drafts add interview and application depth while preserving these limits:

- a draft is not proof of verbatim delivery;
- draft metrics require independent verification;
- safety work and repeal were collective; and
- advocacy does not establish sole legislative causality.

### Agency-Facing Recommendation Continuity

The reviewed sequence begins with early 2017 recommendations to DCLA, continues
through an Office of Nightlife letter and Council testimony, and reaches a 2019
priority document explicitly organized around what the Office could implement.
Across the sequence, the coalition repeatedly proposed concrete mechanisms:

- confidential cultural liaisons or caseworkers;
- funding and professional support for safety improvements;
- clearer regulatory navigation;
- M.A.R.C.H. transparency;
- access to underused public space;
- lease and landlord mechanisms;
- zoning and dance-law reform; and
- affordability and anti-displacement measures.

This supports continuity and requirements translation. It does not establish
that Jamie authored every proposal, that agencies adopted every proposal, or
that the coalition alone created an office or caused an outcome. See the
[recommendation-continuity claim](../claims/nycartc-nightlife-recommendation-continuity.md).

### Cross-Channel Campaign Implementation

Talks Not Raids guides connect partner organizations, Council hearings,
testimony, campaign websites, calls to action, text and social messages, live
coverage, photography requirements, and post-event source links. They show a
collective public identity being used as operational infrastructure across
people, channels, and time.

Task initials and Drive metadata are not enough to assign all of that work to
Jamie. They are leads for later collaborator and revision-history review.

### M.A.R.C.H. Data-Design Lead

A collaborative working document contains a technically significant design
lead: require machine-readable M.A.R.C.H. reporting fields that map to
frequently updated public datasets, use the reported data to explore preventive
alerts, and explicitly address the danger that selective alerts could reproduce
cultural bias.

The document includes a Jamie-initialed edit invitation, but the first pass
cannot allocate the complete design passage to him. No shipped or validated
predictive system was recovered. The item remains an internal inquiry, not a
portfolio claim. See the [held data-design lead](../claims/nycartc-march-data-design-lead.md).

## Application Use

The archive strengthens four professional readings without changing Jamie's
formal titles:

1. **Technical and product operations:** complex work decomposed into timed,
   cross-channel workflows with dependencies, owners, artifacts, and day-of
   delivery.
2. **Public-interest implementation:** lived experience translated into
   testimony, requirements, agency proposals, public information, and
   accountability mechanisms.
3. **Participation systems:** multiple routes for people to enter, understand,
   tell their stories, and act in formal civic processes.
4. **Knowledge and handoff:** a shared identity and production system that
   collaborators could use across campaigns while individual authorship
   remained distributed.

The [Technical Operations capability path](../capabilities/technical-operations.md)
now points to this governed project record. The public portfolio remains a
smaller projection; no protected-only claim is promoted to the site in this
cycle.

## Snapshot Reconciliation

The accepted proposal recorded an earlier visible root count of 61 items. This
fresh authenticated snapshot exposed 50 root items before recursive traversal.
The cause may be folder movement, sharing or interface changes, or an earlier
counting error; it was not resolved in this pass. The new named snapshot governs
the reported 2,192-descendant population, and the discrepancy remains part of
the research record rather than being silently reconciled.

## Excluded Scope

This pass does not claim coverage of:

- deleted or trashed items;
- revision histories or resolved comments;
- items no longer shared with Jamie;
- access logs;
- collaborator materials outside the folder;
- the contents of external webpages or databases referenced by files; or
- rights, consent, and credit for private media.

Those are separate research and authority scopes.

## Public-Safety Controls

- No raw source body entered git.
- No exact Drive identifier, resource key, private path, or account coordinate entered git.
- No completed form, contact list, participant data, meeting note, private correspondence, working comment, or unapproved quotation entered git.
- No proprietary font or private media entered git.
- Drive owner and modifier labels were never treated as authorship.
- Access was never treated as consent to publish.
- Evidence value and publication rights remained separate.
