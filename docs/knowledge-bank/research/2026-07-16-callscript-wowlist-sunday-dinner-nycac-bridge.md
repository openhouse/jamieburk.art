# Call Script, WOW List, Sunday Dinner, and NYC Artist Coalition bridge

Date: 2026-07-16

## Purpose

Test and document Jamie's account that the civic adaptation of WOW List, the
Call Script project, Sunday Dinner, and the emergence of NYC Artist Coalition
formed a connected operating sequence. This note retains only public-safe
findings. It does not reproduce participant records, private contact data,
ordinary Facebook users' identities, or raw database rows.

## Sources and method

1. Close-read the authenticated public Call Script Facebook page and the full
   surviving discussion for the January 27, 2017 Department of Cultural
   Affairs meeting.
2. Compared the public records with the December 2016 Wayback capture of
   `popular.vote`, which identifies the surface as a WOW List adaptation for
   marches, meetings, and in-person connection.
3. Queried a read-only July 22, 2017 WOW List production-database snapshot for
   relevant public event titles, dates, and the Facebook event identifier.
4. Profiled the protected Sunday Dinner working workbook without copying names,
   contact information, row-level response markers, or formulas into the repo.
5. Separated public records, protected project corroboration, Jamie's
   first-hand role account, platform counters, and causal inference.

## Public sequence recovered

The surviving Call Script page links to `popular.vote`. Because the page is a
current snapshot, it establishes public continuity between the projects but
does not establish when the link was added.

The January 27, 2017 event record identifies Call Script, NYC Artist Coalition,
and two other displayed cohosts for a meeting with the New York City Department
of Cultural Affairs about DIY and alternative cultural spaces after the Ghost
Ship fire. Its surviving discussion preserves a practical participation
sequence:

- gathering issues and requests before the meeting;
- circulating public safety and peer-organizing resources;
- inviting collaborative review and sign-on for a letter to the commissioner;
- asking participants to choose a group name;
- routing participants into a February 6 follow-up meeting and open agenda
  setting.

NYC Artist Coalition is the leading displayed naming-poll option at 57%. The
page does not display the denominator, so the percentage cannot be converted
into a vote total, representative mandate, or complete founding roster.

The protected WOW List snapshot independently preserves the adjacent public
event sequence: January Sunday Dinner civic gatherings, the January 27 DCLA
meeting, a February 6 NYC Artist Coalition general meeting, a later February
steering-group potluck, shared-letter work, and subsequent coalition meetings.
This establishes continuity of event infrastructure and chronology. Sequence
alone does not prove causality or individual authorship.

## Bounded role claim

Jamie identifies Call Script as his project and describes using the event and
discussion as part of his facilitation of the coalition's emergence. Public and
protected records corroborate the operating sequence while leaving post-level
authorship and the complete founding history open.

The defensible professional claim is:

> Jamie used Call Script and WOW List's civic event-sharing adaptation to help
> emerging coalition work acquire usable structure through public invitation,
> issue gathering, collaborative drafting, collective naming, and follow-up
> convening.

Use `helped establish and produce`. Preserve collective credit for cohosts,
artists, organizers, venues, agency participants, and coalition collaborators.
Do not say one tool created the coalition, Jamie authored every post, or the
sequence alone caused a policy outcome.

## Sunday Dinner ledger finding

The protected workbook is a working operating instrument rather than a clean
attendance audit. Its main sheet preserves numbered gathering columns from 001
through 345, including a contemporaneous 300th record, across event labels from
2012 through 2021. The numbering contains gaps and duplicates, and the marker
vocabulary mixes response channels and statuses.

This strengthens the existing 300-plus Sunday Dinner history as protected
project-record support. It does not establish verified attendance, unique
participant totals, a clean census, or publication permission for participant
data. Public wording remains attributed to Jamie's approved resume.
