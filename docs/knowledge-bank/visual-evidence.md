# Visual Archive Findings

This note records the public-safe professional findings from a July 2026 local
curation of Jamie's Apple Photos library. It does not publish photographs,
filenames, private album labels, People metadata, locations, or private-life
context.

The curation produced a 4,000-image editor field after 11,283 candidates were
freshly inspected. A separate 960-image safety hold remained outside the field.
Project albums are **Editor Hypothesis** views, not factual labels or
publication permission.

## What The Photographs Add

### Work becomes visible through material forms

Across projects, the recurring evidence is not a single title or office. It is
the movement from an emerging need toward something people can use: documents,
signs, interfaces, cards, maps, workshop materials, tools, maintained rooms,
public-facing spaces, meals, and repeatable routines.

This corroborates a careful cross-project throughline: Jamie gives emerging
work usable form. It does not prove that Jamie authored every visible artifact
or directed every visible activity.

### The strongest professional story is systems in use

The archive is strongest when people, artifacts, and environments appear
together: a laptop in a workshop, notes being arranged on a table, a public
sign at a storefront, tools in a working space, a meal being prepared, or a
building under repair. These scenes show implementation as social and situated,
not as the work of a lone operator.

When the site eventually uses photographs, it should favor this relationship
between people and apparatus over generic portraits or isolated atmosphere.

### Hosting is operating infrastructure

The 196 Artists Residency / Sunday Dinner field repeatedly shows tables, food,
tableware, art, preparation, and people in shared rooms. The visual pattern
corroborates the existing claim that continuity came through practical hosting,
onboarding, facilitation, documentation, and follow-through.

The photographs do not establish gathering counts, attendance, consent, or
ownership of participants' work. Those claims continue to depend on project
records and review.

### Civic work is spatial as well as textual

The FairRentNYC / NYC Artist Coalition field repeatedly returns to storefronts,
doors, windows, signs, public gatherings, and campaign artifacts. The KC Town
Hall field returns to building fabric, repair, construction traces, and the
physical conditions of adaptive reuse.

Together these views clarify that Jamie's civic work connects documentation and
digital systems to places people are trying to keep usable. They do not prove
policy authorship, campaign leadership, property ownership, funding receipt, or
the meaning of any single scene.

### Technical work is also facilitation work

The CallNYC, WOWList, Technical Operations, and Source-Backed Team Memory views
show laptops, presentations, workshop rooms, handwritten notes, printed
documents, tables, and collaborative review. This supports an editorial account
of technical delivery as translation, coordination, and shared sense-making.

The Source-Backed Team Memory view contains only four strong candidates. That is
an important limit: use photographs to illustrate the method's working context,
not to imply a mature deployed product or a larger body of completed client
work.

### Material practice is not decorative

The Harry J. Epstein, Technical Operations, and Colophon views include tools,
parts, shop and workspace conditions, paper systems, cards, swatches, and
handmade artifacts. These images support the proposition that structure grows
from the material at hand: technical, organizational, spatial, and physical.

They do not establish business impact, job responsibility, or product
authorship without the corresponding project record.

## Methodological Finding

Pixels alone were a poor project classifier. The first broad thematic sample
had 15 percent observed fit because civic, social, and cultural scenes collapsed
into one another. After bounded prior project provenance was used only as a
retrieval cue, every candidate was freshly inspected, and stricter visible-fit
gates were applied, the third-round sample reached 80.95 percent observed fit.
A targeted FairRent review then reached 10 of 10 fit decisions.

This is a workflow result, not a claim that every album assignment is correct.
The durable lesson is:

1. Use records to retrieve candidate context.
2. Inspect the actual image again.
3. Keep uncertain scenes unclassified.
4. Reduce a project field rather than force a quota.
5. Preserve every project assignment as an editorial hypothesis until a human
   with context confirms it.

## Publication Rules

- Album membership is not publication permission.
- Prefer artifacts, environments, and systems in use before identifiable
  portraits when either can tell the story.
- Confirm project context and collaborator credit before writing a caption.
- Review rights, consent, location sensitivity, minors, private-life context,
  and possible reputational harm for every image.
- Do not infer authorship, leadership, impact, or endorsement from proximity.
- Keep raw images, previews, filenames, People associations, private album
  labels, and precise private locations outside this repository.
- Preserve collective work as collective work.

## Website Implication

No website claim should become stronger because a photograph exists. The photo
field should instead help editors choose images that make already-defensible
claims concrete, relational, and materially legible.

There is no `/proofs`, `/knowledge-bank`, or photo-archive route. This remains a
repo-level evidence and editorial-governance layer.
