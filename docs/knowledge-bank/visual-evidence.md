# Visual Evidence

This register records public-safe findings from a versioned, local photo-field
review conducted for the portfolio. It does not publish photographs, identities,
private file locations, raw OCR, guest records, or private correspondence.

The review produced an editor field rather than a publication edit. Three
recursive evaluation rounds tested whether selected images visibly fit their
assigned views. Safety-sensitive material was separated, and uncertain context
remained labeled for review.

## What the Photo Field Can Support

The photo field can corroborate visible environments, material practices,
relationships, public settings, and recurring forms of work. It can help an
editor locate a scene that makes an already supported claim legible.

It cannot independently establish project attribution, dates, aggregate counts,
causal impact, role ownership, the identity of an unnamed person, or consent to
publish an image.

## Public-Safe Findings

### Hosting is operational work

The Sunday Dinner material repeatedly shows room preparation, kitchen and meal
work, tables, provisions, guests, and the physical rhythms of welcome. Combined
with Jamie's direct confirmation, this supports the plain statement: Jamie
hosts Sunday Dinner.

This evidence strengthens the qualitative account of hosting as participation
infrastructure. It does not independently prove the 300+ gathering or 20+
resident-artist counts, and it does not authorize publication of guest images.

### Jamie's systems practice is material

Across the field, operating structure appears through tools, packing and
storage systems, product inventory, laptops and projected interfaces, printed
data, meeting tables, labels, source containers, and working rooms. The visual
throughline is not abstraction for its own sake. Jamie's practice gives
emerging work a usable material form.

Use this pattern to support the general Technical Operations story. Do not use
an unattributed object or room as proof of a specific project.

### Collective work should remain collective

Meeting rooms, roundtables, public gatherings, demonstrations, campaign signs,
and shared work surfaces show that much of the civic and cultural work happened
among people. The images support language about coordination, facilitation,
public participation, and coalition context. They argue against solo-causality
framing.

### Sunday Dinner and 196 include care infrastructure

The visual record makes visible work that professional summaries often erase:
preparing food and rooms, maintaining provisions, welcoming people, supporting
gatherings, and making recurrence possible. This is operations, not decorative
community context.

### FairRentNYC and NYC Artist Coalition have public-facing context

Public demonstrations, campaign materials, coalition gatherings, storefronts,
and streetscapes provide visual context for cultural-space and commercial-rent
advocacy. They can support collective public-facing framing after image-level
approval. They do not prove authorship of policy, legal authority, or causality
for campaign outcomes.

### CallNYC joins information with public participation

Public presentations, printed data, reference materials, cards, maps, and
shared work tables reinforce the project's information-translation and civic
sensemaking context. They do not make the archived prototype an official or
current City service.

### Harry J. Epstein work lives among products and workflows

Equipment, tools, product displays, packing and storage systems, and digital
interfaces provide plausible visual context for product operations and legacy
e-commerce stewardship. Only images with confirmed provenance should be used
as Harry J. Epstein artifacts. The photo field does not support the 2x growth
metric or causal claims about revenue.

### KC Town Hall is grounded in physical conditions

Building exteriors, neighborhood streets, work sites, and interior repair
conditions reinforce the built-environment and implementation character of the
project. They do not prove the public-funding recommendation, current property
status, or any private financial detail.

### Source-backed memory images are often metaphors

Archive rooms, labeled containers, accumulated papers, and storage systems can
help explain bounded memory, source organization, and retrieval. They are
editorial metaphors unless direct project provenance is established. They must
not be presented as screenshots or proof of a deployed AI product.

### Material design has a recognizable vocabulary

Thresholds, temporary signs, wire structures, architecture, storage, and
assembled public environments recur across the field. These images may inform
the portfolio's colophon and visual edit, but they are design context rather
than accomplishment claims.

## Meaningful Gap

No dedicated WOWList photo view cleared both the direct visual-fit and project-
provenance threshold in this review. Do not fill that gap with generic
nightlife, performance, or gathering images. A future WOWList image should come
from stronger project-specific provenance or collaborator confirmation.

## Safety and Publication Rules

- Treat all photo-field albums as editorial retrieval surfaces, not public
  galleries.
- Keep guest/share lists, identity documents, child/private-context images, and
  intimate domestic records out of public projection.
- Treat Photos person associations as retrieval clues, never consent.
- Require image-level review for consent, credit, context, redaction, and
  safety before publication.
- Preserve collective credit and do not infer ownership from who held the
  camera or appears in the frame.
- Keep private source images and local inspection artifacts outside this repo.

## Projection Decision

This review directly strengthens one public statement now: Jamie hosts Sunday
Dinner. The remaining findings should guide image selection, source notes, and
anti-claims. They should not create new metric-bearing or causality claims.
