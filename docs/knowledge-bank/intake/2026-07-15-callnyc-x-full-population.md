# CallNYC X full-population archival production

- **Research date:** July 15, 2026
- **Access posture:** authenticated X session belonging to Jamie Burkart
- **Corpus:** [callnyc-x-public-corpus.json](../corpora/callnyc-x-public-corpus.json)
- **Publication posture:** public posts, URLs, dated counters, and explicit
  non-claims only; no credentials, cookies, private messages, settings, private
  audience data, or authenticated-user interaction state were retained.

## Population accounting

X displayed **110 posts** on the live `@CallNYCapp` profile.

The pass recovered:

| Surface | Unique status records |
| --- | ---: |
| Profile Posts / Replies union | 106 |
| Date-partitioned Latest search additions | 1 |
| **Recovered status records** | **107** |
| Explicitly unavailable residual | **3** |
| **Displayed population accounted for** | **110 / 110** |

This is **100% population accounting** and **97.3% status-level recovery**. It
is not a complete 110-status export. The residual three could reflect deleted,
unavailable, search-omitted, or otherwise unexposed posts; the reviewed surfaces
do not establish which.

The corpus stores one row per recovered status ID. It excludes X analytics links
and the signed-in user's `Liked` or `Reposted` state.

## Corpus composition

The 107 recovered records contain:

- **92 account-authored posts**, March 5 through October 4, 2016;
- **15 third-party posts reposted by CallNYC**;
- a complete recoverable date range through November 14, 2016.

The distinction matters. Counters on a repost belong to the original
third-party post, not to CallNYC's act of reposting it.

## Public documentation system

Seventy account-authored posts use a repeated
`provides/gives the most ... help` construction. The posts translate
constituent-service records into issue-specific public recognition and point
back to resident-facing CallNYC paths.

The recovered pattern addresses:

- **24 distinct Council-member accounts**;
- two city-agency accounts, `@NYCHA` and `@NYCHousing`;
- **63 distinct CallNYC destinations** across 16 service categories.

Among 83 CallNYC link occurrences in account-authored posts, housing and
buildings led with 23, transportation with 14, immigration with six, and
finance and general welfare with four each.

This supports a stronger systems reading: the social account was not only a
launch or promotion channel. It repeatedly carried the product's
data-to-guidance model into public, issue-specific interactions.

It does not establish endorsement, adoption, commissioning, or official
Council status.

## Traction and stakeholder patterns

Eighty-two of 92 account-authored posts mention `@NYCCouncil`. The 70 repeated
recognition posts show sustained outbound engagement with Council offices and
two housing agencies.

Incoming engagement remains a separate evidence class. Complementary timeline
and direct-mention research documents at least six then-Council-member accounts
replying to, amplifying, or promoting CallNYC between April 2016 and July 2017.

As of July 15, 2026:

- 59 of 92 account-authored posts displayed at least one public interaction;
- visible counters summed to 8 replies, 74 reposts, and 111 likes.

These are mutable counter events, not unique people. They do not identify the
accounts behind the actions, stakeholder categories, sentiment, or endorsement.

## Lifecycle decisions

**Projected to the case study**

> CallNYC's public account extended that translation layer into 70 recovered
> issue-recognition posts, addressing 24 Council-member accounts and two
> city-agency accounts across 63 distinct CallNYC destinations.

**Knowledge-bank only**

- full counter totals and top-post rankings;
- the 110 / 107 / 3 recovery accounting;
- the complete post-level JSON corpus;
- full URL and topic distributions;
- adjacent source articles not directly about CallNYC.

**Open**

- status-level recovery of the three residual posts;
- a first-party account export;
- actor-level identification behind aggregate counters;
- historical follower and counter trajectories.

## Governing boundary

Use **full-population-accounted**, not **complete account export**.

Use **70 recovered issue-recognition posts**, not **all CallNYC posts**.

Use **24 Council-member accounts addressed**, not **24 endorsements**.

Keep CallNYC visibly archived, independent, unofficial, and unsuitable for
current constituent-service guidance.
