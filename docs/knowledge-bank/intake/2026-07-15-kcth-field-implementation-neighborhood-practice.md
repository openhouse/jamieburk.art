## Proposal Close Reading

### Role and Team

PDF page 2, Project Narrative page 1, names Julia Fredenburg and Jamie Burkart
as founders and project managers. It also presents specialists for historic
masonry restoration, architecture and consulting, roofing, concrete,
electrical work, and real-estate law.

This supports a multi-disciplinary project-management context. It does not use
the title `general contractor` for Jamie or establish the contract, duration,
or completed contribution of every listed team member.

### Neighborhood Process

PDF page 4, Project Narrative page 3, reproduces a compact survey headed
"What would YOU like to have at 36th and Indiana? Pick Three." The proposal
says the project worked with Oak Park Neighborhood Association and New Horizon
Missionary Baptist Church and that responses directly shaped the proposal.

PDF pages 18-19 contain a January 2019 Council support letter saying the survey
directly influenced the choice of proposed retail uses. The letter also notes
the project's commitment to local and minority-owned construction
participation.

These sources establish the project-level survey process. They do not assign
the handbill's design, form ownership, data system, or analysis to Jamie.

### Phase One Scope and Time Boundary

PDF pages 11-12, Finance pages 10-11, define Phase One as a cold shell and list
roof, structural masonry, floor framing, water, storage, debris and tree
removal, basement access, vehicles, safety and security, air quality, and soft
costs. The proposal reports that major roof and masonry work, debris removal,
and egress work had occurred by 2018.

The same material describes Phase One as fully complete in 2019. The file was
created in March 2019, and nearby prose says completion was still planned for
2019. It is therefore evidence of the project's contemporaneous plan and
representation, not later independent proof of completion.
