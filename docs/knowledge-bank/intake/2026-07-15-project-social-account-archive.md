**Research date:** July 15, 2026  
**Access posture:** authenticated X session belonging to Jamie Burkart  
**Publication posture:** public-safe metadata, public posts, bounded counts, and

## Why this record exists

Project social accounts are both engagement surfaces and public documentation
systems. They can preserve how an identity was carried across years, who used
it in public, which institutions responded, which articles circulated, and how
campaign work connected across projects.

Jamie reports that he established these accounts and their public-facing
identity systems. Multiple teammates posted and communicated over time. The
bank therefore keeps five things separate:

1. account establishment and identity design;
2. account administration or credential custody;
3. authorship of an individual post;
4. collective campaign leadership and outcomes;
5. public response to the identity.

The first is currently a bounded first-person Jamie claim. The fifth is
measurable from public sources. The middle three require post-level or
administrative evidence and must not be inferred.

## Method

The authenticated research pass:

- opened each profile directly and recorded visible public metadata;
- inspected selected mission-relevant posts on the smaller project timelines;
- ran X Latest search for `@CallNYCapp` and `@NYCArtC`;
- scrolled each result set to stable exhaustion;
- deduplicated results by public status URL;
- separated coalition-authored and externally authored posts;
- joined visible author handles to dated official Council handle rosters;
- retained Council posts only when the officeholder was serving on the Council;
- applied overlapping, case-insensitive keyword patterns to normalized visible
  text for mission discovery;
- directly inspected high-value Council and city-agency posts.

The result is a bounded public corpus, not an X account export.

## Collaborator use and continuity

Olympia Kazi authored **89** recovered posts mentioning `@NYCArtC` between
November 2019 and June 2022, the largest author count in the corpus. Twenty-eight
matched the FairRentNYC or commercial-rent pattern.

This supports an important systems claim: the coalition identity was usable by
an admired collaborator across years and campaigns. It does not establish that
Olympia authored those posts from the `@NYCArtC` account or had account
credentials.

The wider corpus also shows multi-organizational circulation. The most frequent
authors after Olympia included League of Independent Theater New York, United
for Small Business NYC, NYC Cultural Affairs, Music Workers Alliance, Dance/NYC,
and several organizers, elected officials, and community organizations.

Jamie's complete individual role in every event.

## What this pass does not claim

- The authenticated search is not a complete account export.
- Current profile totals are not historical maxima or stable KPIs.
- A mention is not automatically support or endorsement.
- An office account does not identify the staff member who typed a post.
- Public interaction does not prove commissioning, adoption, or official
  project status.
- Jamie did not necessarily author every post on an account he established.
- External posts mentioning a project account do not imply credential access.
- Current absence of a handle does not prove it never existed.
- No Facebook or Instagram inventory was completed in this pass.
