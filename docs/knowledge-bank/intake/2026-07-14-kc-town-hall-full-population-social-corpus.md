# KC Town Hall Full-Population Social Corpus

Date: 2026-07-14

## Purpose

Treat the complete surviving `@KCTownHall` public account as a source-discovery,
engagement, and project-identity record. The goal is not to put every post on
the portfolio. It is to give every observed profile slot a durable disposition,
associate mission-relevant sources and claims, and preserve the boundaries
needed for future composition.

## Population Control

The authenticated profile displayed **183 posts**. Two traversals of the
Replies surface, including a second pass in 420-pixel increments, recovered
**181 primary timeline items**:

- **155 account-authored statuses**;
- **26 reposts from 16 public accounts**;
- **2 profile-count slots not recovered and retained as unresolved count debt**.

The Posts tab yielded 121 primary items. The Replies surface yielded all 181
item-level records and also rendered seven third-party conversation-context
statuses. Those seven context records were used to understand thread
relationships but were not counted as KC Town Hall timeline items.

This is a **100 percent disposition of the 183-slot live-profile control**, not
100 percent item-level recovery: 181 records plus two explicit unresolved slots
equals 183. It is not a platform export, historical deletion log, or proof that
no older status disappeared before July 2026.

The public ledger is
`docs/knowledge-bank/data/kc-town-hall-public-post-ledger.json`. It stores
metadata and summaries, not full post text, authentication material, private
messages, or account analytics.

## Tired Of Tires

The strongest recurring operational pattern is Tired of Tires. The 99 surviving
account-authored records form a substantial recurring-program trace. The first
recovered launch record, from May 3, 2019, announced free residential pickup
with the Oak Park neighborhood. Ninety-nine surviving account-authored records
route pickups or report program activity through September 2022.

Individual posts report concrete monthly results. An October 3, 2020 record
states that the program had saved East Kansas City residents **$20,023 in tire-
disposal fees**. This remains a project-reported figure in a shared social
account, not an independently audited metric. Ninety-nine is a count of social
records, not pickups, households, participants, tires, or unique program events.
The corpus does not assign later program operations to Jamie.
