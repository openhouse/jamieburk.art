## Direct finding

Tired of Tires is strong evidence of resident-facing service design and
operations. The recovered system connected a simple public promise, recurring
4x6 handbills, household request and volunteer routes, field pickup, a City
recycling handoff, project-maintained measurement, and neighborhood feedback.

The record now supports a careful statement that Jamie **helped design and
operate** the service. A contemporaneous coordination record identifies James
Burkart as the driver for the May 2019 launch pickup. The archived page names
KC Town Hall and Oak Park Neighborhood Association as the service partners and
displays the shared author label `Julia and Jamie`. Jamie supplies a first-party
professional account of his service, web, handbill, route, pickup, and tracking
work. These strands support a bounded individual-role claim while retaining
collective credit.

## Aggregate result and method boundary

The protected calculator's aggregate cells record:

- **1,970 tires** across recorded monthly entries from May 2019 through
  September 2022; and
- **$44,890 in estimated avoided disposal fees**, calculated under the
  workbook's own stated per-tire assumptions.

These are project-maintained operating figures. They are not audited City data,
an independent economic-impact study, unique residents or households, completed
requests, proof of every pickup, or proof of complete ten-minute-walk coverage.
The monthly columns include gaps. The aggregate is therefore presented as the
tracker's bounded recorded result, never as the service's all-time total.

Resident names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, request notes, and
row-level records remain protected.

## Resident-service blueprint

1. **Promise:** a dated 4x6 handbill and web page explain free curbside pickup,
   household eligibility, and how to request service or volunteer.
2. **Intake:** residents identify a pickup need through public request routes;
   private resident details remain outside the portfolio.
3. **Field work:** tires move from curbs and neighborhood locations into the
   project vehicle and trailer. The launch record identifies Jamie as driver.
4. **Institutional handoff:** City coordination makes a no-fee first-Saturday
   recycling handoff available for the launch service.
5. **Measurement:** a project tracker records monthly counts and a formula-based
   avoided-fee estimate.
6. **Feedback:** the service contact also carried a short neighborhood survey,
   giving residents a physical way to influence proposed uses near home.

This is a service blueprint reconstructed from bounded evidence. It does not
assign every step to Jamie or claim that the same people performed every step
in every month.

The derivatives preserve the graphic composition while excluding obsolete

## From service contact to facility requirements

The project-level record establishes three points: KC Town Hall used a compact
neighborhood survey; the proposal says survey results shaped what followed; and
the proposal included a restaurant and kitchen-incubator concept. Jamie reports
that tire-route conversations also surfaced home-catering ambitions and the
need for a licensed shared kitchen, health-department compliance, and fire
suppression.

That more specific chain is an important research lead, not yet an active public
claim. A future pass should seek de-identified survey aggregates, program and
architectural iterations, meeting notes, health-department guidance, and
architect or collaborator proof notes. Resident and home-business identities
remain protected.
