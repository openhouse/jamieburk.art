# Intake - UCSC Morse and Sack Narrative Evaluations

## Public-safe receipt

This intake develops two critical lenses from protected, unofficial copies of
UCSC narrative evaluations written by Margaret Morse and Warren Sack for Jamie
Burkart's 2004 and 2006 coursework. It also reviews a held screenshot of a 2014
Margaret Morse recommendation and the already-governed 2006 Good Times article
about Open House.

## What entered the public repository

- source metadata and bounded summaries;
- atomic observations about embodied theory, media archaeology, recursive
  social-network analysis, software and image-analysis prototyping, interface
  design, and collective installation design;
- two claims with explicit academic, collective-credit, deployment, and
  historical-priority boundaries;
- one inquiry into the exact public record for Time is Long;
- two evaluation protocols derived from the instructors' documented concerns.

## What did not enter

- student identifiers or private filesystem locations;
- raw narrative-evaluation text;
- private email, phone, address, or relationship correspondence;
- the recommendation image or unapproved reproduction of its full text;
- any claim that Jamie historically invented structural equivalence;
- any conflation of Time is Long with BAPLab without a connecting source.

## Source posture

The narrative evaluations are protected institutional records recovered as an
unofficial copy. The recommendation screenshot is held pending rights and
context review. Laura Mattingly's public Good Times article provides independent
contemporaneous context for Open House, shared governance, distributed
documentation, and Jamie's social-software interpretation.
