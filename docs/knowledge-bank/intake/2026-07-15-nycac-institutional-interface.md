# Intake Receipt: NYC Artist Coalition as Institutional Interface

Date: 2026-07-15

## Prompt

Locate any NYC Council hearing transcript in which DCLA Commissioner Tom
Finkelpearl referred to NYC Artist Coalition, then ask why the coalition's work
was useful to DCLA, the Council, and Rafael Espinal.

## Direct Finding

At the May 19, 2017, joint Executive Budget hearing, Finkelpearl explicitly
named NYC Artist Coalition in his prepared testimony. The reference appears in
a passage about CreateNYC, DCLA's desire for more direct public feedback, the
power of convening people around a common cause, and closer reciprocal
relationships with the public.

This is direct institutional proof that Finkelpearl found the coalition useful
as an example when explaining DCLA's public-engagement work to the Council. He
did not name Jamie individually, endorse every coalition recommendation, or
claim that the coalition caused a policy outcome.

## Institutional Reading

### Why DCLA benefited

Finkelpearl's testimony identifies a structural gap. DCLA already worked with
roughly 1,000 funded nonprofit cultural organizations, but he said the agency
wanted more direct public feedback. NYC Artist Coalition helped make
experience from informal and community-driven cultural spaces available in
forms the agency could use: convenings, recommendations, public exchanges, and
continuing relationships.

The evidence supports saying that DCLA benefited from a credible civic
counterpart beyond its ordinary grant relationships. It does not support
saying DCLA created, controlled, or spoke for the coalition.

### Why the Council benefited

Council hearings require more than a policy abstraction. Coalition
participants supplied affected-community testimony, safety and implementation
detail, organized turnout, and public accountability. The coalition made
informal cultural spaces legible as a constituency while preserving those
participants as speakers in their own right.

### Why Espinal benefited

Espinal brought legislative authority, a committee forum, and policy
leadership. Coalition participants brought issue discovery, witnesses,
community legitimacy, communications and event infrastructure, and continued
feedback from people experiencing the rules and enforcement practices under
discussion. The record supports interpreting these roles as complementary.

It does not establish that Espinal could not act without the coalition, that
Jamie was individually indispensable, or that coalition advocacy alone caused
Cabaret Law repeal or creation of the Office of Nightlife.

## Public Projection

The website receives one concise, directly sourced fact: Finkelpearl cited the
coalition's formation in May 2017 Council testimony while explaining DCLA's
effort to build more reciprocal public relationships.

The fuller institutional analysis remains in the knowledge bank as an
explicitly labeled inference. This keeps the site clear while preserving the
depth needed for future briefs, applications, and project histories.
