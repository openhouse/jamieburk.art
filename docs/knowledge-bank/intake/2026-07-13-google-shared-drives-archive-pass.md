# Google Drive Shared Drives archival-production pass

Date: 2026-07-13
Reviewer: Codex archival review with Jamie Burkart
Disposition: integrated with selected, reserve, protected, photo, and research lanes

## Scope and method

The connected Google Drive account exposed 110 Shared Drives at the time of
review. The complete inventory was inspected privately and reduced to a
public-safe collection map. The repository retains the count, method, selected
project categories, and resulting dispositions; it does not publish the full
drive-name inventory, file IDs, access links, membership, or private filenames.

This was a collection-scale accession pass plus focused close reading, not a
claim that every file in every drive was reviewed. The high-value cohort was
selected against current portfolio claims and open proof debt. It included:

- NYC Artist Coalition and Fair Rent NYC campaign archives;
- Commercial Vacancy and Vacancy Data collections;
- 196 Artists Residency and Sunday Dinner collections;
- KC Town Hall public-process and media collections;
- WOW List project and member-meeting materials;
- ACT@MIT, NYC Open Data Week, and selected art/documentation collections; and
- a collaborator collection containing the bounded Source-Backed Team Memory
  Sprint proposal.

For each candidate record, the pass tested four questions separately:

1. What does the artifact establish about the project?
2. What does it establish about Jamie's specific role?
3. What remains collective, unattributed, proposed, or unverified?
4. What can be described safely without publishing the underlying record?

Shared Drive presence was never treated as proof of Jamie's authorship,
distribution, institutional adoption, public permission, or project outcome.
The connector reported access visibility as not verified for sampled files, so
underlying Drive URLs remain protected even when a public-safe claim is usable.

## Selected public claims

### Commercial vacancy public-data brief

The Commercial Vacancy Shared Drive contains a March 27, 2026 brief authored by
Jamie, plus three candidate council-district map images. The brief proposes a
privacy-preserving, geography-aggregated open-data pilot for commercial vacancy,
occupancy, and lease-cost indicators derived from RPIE filings alongside New
York City's existing storefront reporting.

The artifact is unusually strong because it specifies a smallest useful pilot:
an indicator table, coverage and suppression table, and methods note. It also
names explicit exclusions including raw filings, tenant identities,
parcel-level rent rows, and lease documents.

- Source: `SRC-GDRIVE-COMMERCIAL-VACANCY-BASELINE-BRIEF-2026`
- Claim: `CLM-COMMERCIAL-VACANCY-BASELINE-BRIEF-2026`
- Decision: selected for Fair Rent NYC and Technical Operations
- Boundary: the artifact establishes Jamie's proposal, not City endorsement,
  adoption, implementation, publication, or access to confidential filings.

### 196 Artists Residency onboarding

A dated acceptance record signed by Jamie documents one concrete residency
workflow: proposal review, acceptance, pre-arrival coordination, configuring the
space around the artist's needs, and arrival/access handoff.

- Source: `SRC-GDRIVE-196-RESIDENCY-ACCEPTANCE-2023`
- Claim: `CLM-196-RESIDENCY-ONBOARDING-2023`
- Decision: selected for the 196 / Sunday Dinner case study
- Boundary: the resident's identity, proposal, dates, contact information, and
  access details remain protected. One record does not independently verify the
  larger residency aggregate or establish an identical process for every
  residency.

`SRC-GDRIVE-SUNDAY-DINNER-PHOTO-SET-2025`. No image, filename, face, participant,
or home detail was published. The project photo brief now records candidates as

## Private material excluded from ingestion

The public repository does not contain:

- the complete Shared Drive inventory or private collection names;
- Google Drive or Google Docs URLs, file IDs, parent IDs, or access metadata;
- participant, resident, guest, applicant, subscriber, donor, or contact rows;
- access instructions, credentials, administrative links, meeting links, or
  private correspondence;
- family, wedding, divorce, personal, health, legal, banking, tax, property, or
  financial records;
- raw coalition notes, unapproved quotations, legal-review material, strategy,
  membership data, or stakeholder lists;
- raw ZIP exports, private media, or image contents; or
- pricing, collaborator-company context, or correspondence from the private
  Source-Backed Team Memory proposal.
