# WOW List Full-Population Social Corpus

Run date: 2026-07-14

## Answer First

The live [@wowlist profile](https://x.com/wowlist) displayed **38 posts**. An
authenticated read-only pass through the Posts and Replies surfaces recovered
**all 38 unique items**:

- 16 account posts;
- 6 account replies; and
- 16 reposts from 13 other public accounts.

The [item-level ledger](../data/wowlist-public-post-ledger.json) closes exactly
against that current profile control with no unresolved slots. This is 100
percent recovery of the surviving profile population observed on July 14,
2026. It is not a platform export, a deletion history, or proof that no older
item was deleted before capture.

## Method

1. Used the profile's displayed 38-post count as the population control.
2. Harvested Posts and Replies separately to reduce omissions from X's
   virtualized timeline.
3. Deduplicated every rendered item by canonical status ID.
4. Reconciled 37 Posts-tab items with one additional account reply found on the
   Replies tab, then directly reconfirmed that status.
5. Classified all 38 records by account relationship, primary theme, mentions,
   hashtags, public outbound destinations, and measurement boundary.
6. Resolved all 35 posted `t.co` URLs to 34 unique destinations.
7. Closely read mission-relevant destinations while distinguishing source
   context from coverage of WOW List.

Authentication was used only to read public material. No credential, cookie,
session, private-message, account-recovery, or private-analytics material was
captured or committed. The ledger stores concise public-safe summaries and
content digests rather than reproducing full third-party repost text.

## Findings

### Product support

All six account replies function as support, onboarding, or local-calendar
identity guidance. Together they explain:

- how followed calendars populated a home feed;
- where a person could find their WOW Lists on a profile;
- how to submit an event to multiple lists;
- what NYCDIY was;
- how to join NYCDIY, add shows, and receive a weekly email; and
- how NYCDIY, WOW List, and Sunday Dinner related.

This is concrete product-operations evidence. It is attributed to the shared
account because the public record does not identify the teammate who authored
each reply.

### Stakeholder touchpoints

The 22 account-authored records mention 14 external handles. Direct support
exchanges include people and project accounts such as `@juliafredenburg`,
`@all_ages`, `@285KENT`, `@lil_seltz`, `@selfdfens`, and `@showpaper`.
Reposts draw from 13 accounts spanning DIY arts, cultural spaces, public
projects, and collaborators, including The Silent Barn, Meow Wolf, and + POOL.

These are public touchpoints, not a complete stakeholder graph. A mention,
reply, or repost does not by itself establish partnership, endorsement,
adoption, or organizational affiliation.

### Distribution and community use

Five account posts distribute events, conferences, festivals, tours, or public
demonstrations. Five reposts amplify organizers using event or calendar links.
A 2015 post also thanks a community member for creating a product tutorial.

These are public traces of distribution and contribution. They do not measure
total event volume, user population, geographic adoption, support workload,
satisfaction, attendance, or impact.

### Scene knowledge

Three account posts route knowledge about DIY cultural infrastructure and
connections among scenes. Two destinations were recovered through the Wayback
Machine:

- [Grasstronaut's all-ages music manualfesto](https://web.archive.org/web/20150406041311/http://grasstronaut.com/2015/01/29/homework-in-every-town/)
  discusses organizational structures, space, fundraising, community building,
  conflict resolution, and legal and social conditions around all-ages venues.
- Elise Granata's [Good Times article, “Zines 2.0”](https://web.archive.org/web/20150907001335/http://www.gtweekly.com/index.php/santa-cruz-news/good-times-cover-stories/6548-zines-20.html)
  describes efforts to document and connect geographically separated
  grassroots arts spaces.

These sources clarify the mission context of links WOW List shared. They are
not press coverage, reviews, or endorsements of WOW List.

### Civic mobilization and care

Five account posts and five reposts center civic mobilization or care. The
recovered routes include a Black Lives Matter demonstration, nationwide
post-election gatherings, popular-vote organizing, Standing Rock support,
Ghost Ship mutual aid and memorials, a call to mayors about safe conditions for
DIY spaces, and a DIY-space fund.

[KQED's memorial-vigil report](https://www.kqed.org/news/11207317/video-mourners-gather-at-candlelight-vigil-to-honor-victims-of-oakland-fire)
and [Meow Wolf's DIY Fund page](https://meowwolf.com/blob/meow-wolfs-diy-fund)
confirm the context of two shared resources. Sharing them documents public
routing and care; it does not make WOW List or Jamie their organizer, author,
participant, beneficiary, or cause.

### Visible reactions

At the July 2026 public snapshot, 12 of 22 account-authored statuses displayed
at least one reaction. Their visible totals were two replies, 20 reposts, and
21 likes. Metrics on the 16 reposted source statuses are excluded because those
reactions belong to the source posts, not to WOW List's repost action.

This snapshot is retained as a research record, not promoted as an impact
claim. The counts are mutable, non-contemporaneous interface observations and
do not establish impressions, unique people, clickthrough, conversion,
adoption, satisfaction, or resulting action.

### Retained in reserve

- The account routed practical and historical knowledge among DIY scenes.
- The account record extends from event distribution into gathering, mutual
  aid, mourning, and cultural-space support.
- The July 2026 visible-reaction snapshot is available for later research but
  is not an approved reach or impact metric.

Reserve status preserves future compositional range. It does not make every
mature fact necessary for the current hiring-focused page.

## Boundaries

- Jamie has stated that he established the account and co-built the product;
  the public social record does not expose creator or administrator history.
- The shared account does not identify which teammate composed each post.
- A complete current profile population is not a complete platform export or
  proof against prior deletion.
- Reposts and links do not establish authorship, organizing credit,
  participation, endorsement, causality, or impact.
- Visible reply, repost, and like totals are mutable July 2026 snapshots, not
  historical analytics or project-owned performance measures.
- The social corpus does not validate larger user, event, or geographic totals
  held elsewhere in the knowledge bank.
