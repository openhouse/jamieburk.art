### 27:03

Good morning, New York City.

First of all, it's truly an honor to be here, and I want to say thank you to the Black business owners across Brooklyn who trusted me to be a stand for them in this time, because they are right now fighting hard to keep their businesses open.

Let me give you a few: [unclear business name], Kombi Lounge, Savannah Spice, Cold Peppers, Sugarcane BK—these are just some of the Black businesses that have been closed, not because of economic development within the community, but because the prices are so outrageously high they had no other choice but to either relocate or close their business permanently, losing their mark in the neighborhood for decades. Is that fair? And that's right.

You have businesses right now like Nicholas BK in Bed-Stuy, [Lacou/Lacoux] Cafe in Crown Heights, that are fighting hard to hold on because the landlords are charging them outrageous prices, and it is not fair to them, nor to the people that are supporting them.

And I'm here to say, on behalf of the Black businesses across Brooklyn and the state of New York, we're tired of it, and we are so proud to stand with my friends, authentic leaders who are taking this challenge to be so important. Because let's be real: who's trying to pay $15 for a Heineken, right? Who's trying to pay $25 for a Hennessy and ginger ale when you have local neighborhood businesses like Jimmy's Corner, where I've told plenty of stories from when I was a grad student at Baruch College? Because when I was working on my capstone, I needed a drink, and I went to Jimmy's Corner—part of the many stories where I'm a four-time CUNY grad today.

But the point is that I'm here to stand with our Assembly members, because we're going to bring this across the finish line. Because guess what? There are groups like Kwanzaa Crawl. We're getting ready to have a spring edition of Kwanzaa Crawl this June 6. Groups like this are making sure that they drive economic development to businesses that are struggling, but they shouldn't have to do that when we have the power to change government right now.

So I'm asking all of you to stand with me. Members of the press, connect with Black businesses that are waiting to hear from you, that are waiting to tell their story, so we can bring this home across the finish line and keep progressives in New York State.

Thank you so much.
