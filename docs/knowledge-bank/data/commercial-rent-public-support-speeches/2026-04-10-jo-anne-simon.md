### 23:39

Thank you, Emily. And thank you, Senator Salazar as well, and the other speakers.

You know, Emily mentioned something that has been happening in my district for a long time, and that is when somebody comes in and they decide to capitalize on the work that community has done—setting down those roots, keeping those thriving businesses going. And you know, as I have said at plenty of opportunities, you are here because we were there at the time, right?

And so a couple of things that are not just about small business, but about the history of New York: our small businesses were really critical pieces of the history of New York. One of the dive bars in my community that opened in 1904 and closed in 2018 was a dive bar that was the place where the steel workers, who were Mohawks from upstate, came, and they built the Empire State Building, right? They were the high-steel workers, and this was their place, and that is a critical, important historical fact in our city's history.

And also Montero's, which is now only 90 years old, just got bought over by somebody who promises to keep it what it was. It was the longshoremen's bar and still looks like it was the longshoremen's bar. They haven't done anything to it, and it's great, and everybody loves it and still populates it.

But it's also those delis. The delis that used to be the safe haven, the refuge—if you were being followed by somebody, you could go into that deli. And those were delis that often fed some of the homeless people in our communities as well, on a regular basis, and they were very integral parts.

And so one down the corner from me is now a very fancy, shishi place. They got more than twice the rent. And nobody goes into that place. Lovely windows, very nicely laid out, but nobody goes into that place, and it's soulless, and it's a vacant, dead corner of the block that used to be very alive.

So it's about our communities. It's about our small businesses. It's about the role they play in our community, the business life of our city, and the history of our city, that we need to protect these businesses and keep them from being displaced.

So thank you very much for your support, and thank you to Emily.
