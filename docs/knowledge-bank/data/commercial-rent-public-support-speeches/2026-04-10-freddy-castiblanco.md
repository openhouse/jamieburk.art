### 17:22

Yes, good morning, distinguished members of the New York State Legislature. My name is Freddy Castiblanco. I'm a physician and Colombian immigrant. I came to this country 26 years ago with the firm conviction that honest work and commitment to community could build something lasting for everyone.

Twenty-five years ago, I founded Terraza 7 in Queens. My business, my dream, has developed into what is today an artistic and cultural landmark of New York City. In this space, we have presented more than 7,000 concerts that tell the story of the Americas through music and show how migration, and in particular the African diaspora, have built the musical soul of an entire continent—from Morocco and Andalucía, through the Caribbean and the sounds of Veracruz, all the way to jazz, cumbia, samba, candombe, and tango.

But Terraza 7 has not only been a stage for that musical phenomenon. It also has been a platform for justice. We have fought for workers' rights, for [unclear] leave, for thousands of New Yorkers who need it, and testified before Congress in support of the Affordable Care Act with a public option.

We have always believed that community business has an obligation to fight for the dignity of everyone involved in it and around it.

But despite all that work, that commitment, and love for this city, I have come close to losing Terraza three times—not for lack of effort, not for lack of community support, but because landlords who witnessed our success decided that our success was their opportunity, that what we built over years through sweat, sacrifice, and community trust could be turned into profit for them.

Overnight, my rent went up from $7,500 to $12,500 a month, nearly double the industry standard, even in the middle of the pandemic when our doors were shut and our community was suffering. I had no way to pay such an exorbitant amount, so I was taken to court and faced $150,000 in back charges. I have spent years and thousands of dollars raised from this very community simply to keep the doors of Terraza open.

That is not a market failure. That is an injustice. And I am not the only one with such a story. All across New York City, the independent businesses that give our neighborhoods their soul are disappearing, replaced by chains that can afford the high rent but contribute nothing to the distinctive culture and human fabric of the community that we are losing forever.

When a bodega closes its doors, when a family restaurant shuts down, when a music venue goes dark, it's not just a business that disappears. A piece of neighborhood identity dies with it, and that will never come back.

This is why I stand with Small Business United today and urge you to pass the Small Business Rent Stabilization Act, which will establish a commercial rent regulation board to set fair limits on annual rent increases and will guarantee the right to lease renewal so that small businesses can plan, invest, and grow without fear of being displaced overnight by the unilateral decision of a single powerful landlord.

Nearly 90% of new jobs annually in this city are created by small businesses, and New York's neighborhoods are defined by their people and by their local businesses. Without commercial rent protection, we will lose both.

I'm asking you to pass this law not only for Terraza 7, but for every business that is experiencing today what I have gone through, and for those that do not yet know what awaits them if we don't act now.

Please pass the Small Business Rent Stabilization Act. The future of spaces like ours, and of the communities that sustain them—and that they, in turn, sustain—depends on what we and you decide today. Thank you.
