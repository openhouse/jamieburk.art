### 34:58

Everybody, you know why we're all here on this sunny day: because we're fed up. That's right. This is not fair.

We sit on the backs of giants. This business was started by Adam's dad 54 years ago. Guess what else was happening 54 years ago? A young person named Ruth Messinger—some of you may have heard of her—was working on a commercial rent stabilization bill, and it hasn't happened. And why hasn't it happened? Because our leaders haven't had the pressure that you are hearing today.

Okay, our voices need to be heard. The fact that we don't have a microphone is not a problem, as you can see. The only problem that we have is people of privilege who don't want to be taxed around this time at the budget, and they don't want the tilted scales to be fixed.

We are not asking anything radical. We're not asking anything radical. What we are asking is a basic protection. What this bill does is a basic protection.

We build the success. We build the community. We make the culture that generates millions for the Real Estate Board, and we are happy for them to take their millions, but we ask for our fair share, right? We said it right: our success is not yours to go all the way to the bank laughing on our account because we're disempowered.

We have leadership at the state. And I want to talk about what is happening now. The real culprit here is Governor Hochul. It's going to be Mayor Mamdani. It's going to be Speaker Menin. Speaker Menin was the chair of the Small Business Committee when she was a council member, and this bill went in front of her. Did we even get a hearing from her? We didn't. And now she can allow it to be introduced at the city.

But guess what? We're going to pass it in the state. We're going to pass it in the city. Because what is happening right now is very, very sad. Actually, it's worse than what Emily said. Emily said, "Oh, they're waiting for a corporate tenant that is going to give you more money." Here is the sad truth: real estate has been financialized to that level that they don't even want a tenant. They just say, "This lease is $50,000 now. I'm never going to find any idiot to give me $50,000, but what I can do is state that in my bank and get a loan of the equivalent of whatever number makes sense on the paper."

But it doesn't make sense for us. It doesn't make sense for the families. I'm also a mother of two kids, and we need to change this. It cannot be another generation that suffers through that, right?

So it doesn't make sense for the people that, as Julia said, employ more than 50% of our workforce. It doesn't make sense for the people that give us affordable services. When we talk about affordability, it is not just a slogan for people to win elections. Affordability is about displacement, and not just housing displacement. There is secondary displacement, because if your house is protected, but you can't afford to do anything in your neighborhood anymore—if you don't feel welcome going into businesses like in the stories that you heard—how is this not an issue of affordability and displacement?

And then, last but not least, all my life I work for culture. I feel culture is what unites us. Community comes through culture, and our small businesses are the best ambassadors of our culture, and it's high time that we're going to save them.

And I want to thank also Small Business United that is taking the mantle and organizing people, because with Action Lab support and with its amazing leaders at this stage, we are going to pass commercial rent stabilization.
