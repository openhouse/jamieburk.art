## Source Layers

### Jamie's public review confirmation

Jamie confirms that, as an Oak Park Neighborhood Association member, he:

- designed and coordinated the initial monthly free Tired of Tires operation
  with city services;
- routed requests, collected tires, delivered each haul to the city recycling
  center, and recorded monthly counts;
- distributed KC Town Hall survey cards and public information for Oak Park,
  HENC, Cleveland Ave Unify to Beautify, and Chestnut neighborhood services
  while conducting pickup routes;
- served related neighborhood groups as a pro bono identity, photography,
  mapping, social, design, and print resource;
- was a co-founding contributor to Cleveland Ave Unify to Beautify; and
- credits Pastor Lee with the originating insight that Cleveland Avenue could
  help connected neighborhoods become legible as a shared corridor.

This is firsthand role evidence. It is not independent proof of every task,
date, count, program handoff, Indian Mound expansion, or funding effect.

### Public Tired of Tires record

The archived Tired of Tires page independently identifies Julia and Jamie as
authors of the public workflow and credits KC Town Hall with Oak Park
Neighborhood Association for the monthly service. It records request and
volunteer routes, home-only eligibility, recurring dates, and contact and
follow-through pathways.

The complete KC Town Hall account census separately accounts for all 183
profile-counted records. One hundred concern tire pickup or program
follow-through, and 98 explicitly carry the Tired of Tires hashtag. That record
establishes recurring public service and continuity, not the person responsible
for every pickup or post.

A [Cleveland Ave KC public post](https://www.facebook.com/ClevelandAveKC/posts/1633775000115273)
also describes the monthly service, credits KC Town Hall and Oak Park, and
routes people to request pickup or volunteer.

### HENC context

The plan supplies organizational context. It does not describe Cleveland Ave

## What Became Usable

### Initial resident-service operations

The role is no longer flattened into "community engagement." The operating loop
is visible:

1. define eligibility and intake;
2. receive and route resident requests;
3. coordinate with neighborhood and city interfaces;
4. perform collection and recycling delivery;
5. distribute relevant neighborhood information;
6. record monthly activity; and
7. publish visible follow-through.

Jamie's initial field role is directly confirmed. Julia and Jamie retain public
workflow authorship credit; KC Town Hall, Oak Park, Chestnut, city staff,
residents, volunteers, other partners, and later operators retain collective
program credit.

### Field communications practice

Jamie's account shows a recurring pattern in which direct service, print
production, participation, and documentation were not separate workstreams.
Pickup routes also carried survey cards, cleanup information, listening-session
materials, and other neighborhood pathways. This remains reserve depth because
the individual role is currently supported primarily by Jamie's firsthand
account.

### Cleveland Avenue civic design

The bounded reserve claim credits Jamie with helping give Pastor Lee's corridor
idea public and participatory form through identity, photography, social,
mapping, listening-session, resident-reporting, and print systems.

It does not say Jamie originated the concept, solely founded the program,
represented every resident, or caused a funding outcome.

## Public-Safety Boundaries

Do not publish:

- resident addresses, phone numbers, contact forms, or route details;
- raw survey responses or contact records;
- private city, neighborhood, or partner correspondence;
- raw operational spreadsheets or receipt sets;
- unapproved photographs or identifiable participants; or
- private project transition or handoff details.

Do not say:

- Jamie alone created or operated Tired of Tires through 2022;
- every KC Town Hall post was written by Jamie;
- Tired of Tires was owned, funded, or endorsed by the City;
- the Indian Mound expansion is independently confirmed;
- Jamie originated Pastor Lee's Cleveland Avenue concept;
- Jamie alone founded or operated Unify to Beautify;
- the Cleveland Avenue process caused a specific capital allocation; or
- every resident priority was representative or implemented.

## Editorial Disposition

The Tired of Tires operating role strengthens the current KC Town Hall case
study because it makes Jamie's implementation practice concrete from intake
through public follow-through.

The field-communications and Cleveland Avenue claims remain in the bank as
reserve depth. They can support future civic-operations, service-design,
participatory-design, neighborhood-development, and field-implementation
applications after audience-specific composition.

No public proofs, knowledge-bank, archive, resident-record, or analytics route
is added.
