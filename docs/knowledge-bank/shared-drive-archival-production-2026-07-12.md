# Shared Drive Archival Production - 2026-07-12

## Purpose

This pass treats Jamie's Shared Google Drive practice as an institutional archive:
a place where projects preserve working structure, handoffs, media, decisions,
and relationships across time. The goal was to strengthen the knowledge bank,
not to publish the drives or reproduce private records.

The review used the authenticated Google Drive connection directly. Online
Drive was treated as the current source of truth; no assumption was made that a
local sync mirror was complete.

## Collection Map

The authenticated account could access 110 Shared Drives during the review
window. The collection includes civic coalition work, public-data development,
civic events, artist residency and hospitality, community platforms, creative
technology, institutional collaboration, media documentation, personal
collaboration, and dated sync snapshots.

Access does not establish that Jamie created every drive, authored every file,
or has permission to publish every record.

A representative work-relevant cohort was mapped across:

- civic coalition and Commercial Rent Stabilization work;
- public-data implementation;
- civic event and municipal-project history;
- artist residency and recurring hospitality;
- community-platform history;
- creative-technology and institutional collaboration;
- photo, video, and other media-heavy documentation.

Personal, legal, financial, media-only, sync-recovery, and non-probative roots
were not opened merely because they were accessible.

## Close Readings

Six claim-bearing artifacts and one collection-level research record were
developed into protected source records:

1. A shared Commercial Rent Stabilization running-memory system.
2. A consent-aware outreach and action tracker.
3. Cross-organization alignment minutes.
4. A staff-oriented commercial vacancy public-data implementation brief.
5. A Sunday Dinner recurring-event operations tracker.
6. A 196 Artists Residency onboarding letter.
7. The bounded Shared Drive inventory and selection run itself.

For the selected Google-native artifacts, revision history was checked alongside
content. This was important because a shared file's location does not establish
authorship. Revision history corroborated Jamie's creation and sustained
maintenance of the selected trackers and documents while preserving the fact
that coalition memory was collaborative.

## Findings

### Shared operating memory

The strongest material shows Jamie building more than a notes archive. The
Commercial Rent Stabilization system connects current language, decisions,
action owners, open questions, public-data work, consent levels, and follow-up.
It also states what belongs elsewhere: sensitive stories, private contacts,
legal-review context, and detailed source material.

This matured into the canonical claim
`CLM-CRS-SHARED-MEMORY-OPERATIONS`. The case study now projects that claim from
the registry while its private evidence remains non-renderable.

### Consent-aware outreach operations

The outreach schema separates consent to contact from permission to quote. It
also distinguishes relationship ownership, outreach ownership, next action,
status, source, and data-quality flags. This is strong future-use evidence for
product operations, stakeholder operations, data stewardship, and public-safe
implementation work.

The candidate remains held from the current site. Its value can be expressed
without directing public attention toward a private stakeholder system, and a
future public diagram should be designed from the method rather than copied
from the tracker.

### Public-data implementation

The staff-oriented data brief corroborates Jamie's public Commercial Rent
Stabilization pilot: a bounded ask, minimum indicator set, privacy and
suppression rules, methods documentation, and a realistic staff follow-up path.
It was added as protected support for the existing public-data claim. It does
not establish adoption or implementation.

### Hospitality and residency operations

The Sunday Dinner tracker shows repeatable invitation, response, attendance,
and follow-through structure. The residency letter shows a concrete onboarding
sequence from proposal review through scheduling, space preparation, check-in,
and access handoff.

Both deepen the knowledge bank. Neither artifact independently establishes the
approved aggregate totals already used on the site, and neither private record
should be reproduced publicly.

## Public-Safety Boundary

The repository does not contain:

- Drive IDs or private Drive URLs;
- raw drive or folder listings;
- participant, guest, resident, stakeholder, or attendee names;
- phone numbers, email addresses, quotations, or relationship notes;
- invitation, response, attendance, consent, or outreach rows;
- private strategy, legal-review, correspondence, access, or meeting details;
- private photos, video, transcripts, or working links.

Protected locator tokens preserve the existence of source relationships without
publishing source coordinates.
