# Chad Lens

This lens is the editorial standard for making Jamie's record legible without
flattening it or inflating it.

## Reader Burden

The reader should not have to infer Jamie's role from a pile of artifacts. Every
page should answer:

- What was unclear?
- What did Jamie make usable?
- Who needed to use it?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- What stays protected?

## Claim Shape

Use this sentence pattern when strengthening copy:

> Jamie did [operating work] so that [people/team/public] could [usable outcome].

Good examples:

- Built campaign-memory infrastructure so a coalition could track bill language,
  public testimony, research artifacts, and next steps without exposing private
  strategy.
- Translated paper-and-phone business operations into e-commerce workflows,
  catalog structure, onboarding materials, and customer-support patterns.
- Hosted and stewarded recurring community infrastructure so artists and
  neighbors could keep showing up across years, not just one event.

## Language Rules

- Use action verbs: mapped, translated, built, coordinated, documented,
  facilitated, reviewed, launched, maintained, handed off.
- Say "toward what end" before listing tools.
- Preserve collective credit. Say "co-founded," "co-built," "worked with," and
  "helped steward" where appropriate.
- Do not hide the actual professional value behind poetic language.
- Do not let AI language imply replacement of human judgment.
- Do not treat private archival access as proof by itself.

## Preferred Lead

Use this first:

> I turn under-structured work into usable systems.

Use this as role-fit proof language:

> I create operating structure for complex public-facing teams.

## Boundaries

The site should feel generous, but it should not become a public archive browser.
It should show enough to earn trust, then leave sensitive evidence protected.
