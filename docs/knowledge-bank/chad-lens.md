# Chad Lens: Professional Legibility

This is the standing editorial standard for making Jamie legible to hiring,
public-sector, civic-tech, implementation, and product-operations readers.

## Rules

1. Make Jamie visible as the actor. Avoid tool lists without verbs.
2. Name the purpose. Each project should answer: toward what end?
3. Show what became usable. Hiring readers need outputs and outcomes.
4. Prefer reader-legible language. Translate or define specialized terms.
5. Avoid underselling verbs. Replace weak verbs such as "tracking" when the work
   was stewardship, synthesis, facilitation, coordination, translation, or
   analysis.
6. Respect collective work. Use `co-built`, `supported`, `helped`,
   `contributed to`, or `stewarded` when accurate.
7. Tailor facts to the receiving room. A true fact can still be distracting if
   it weakens perceived focus.
8. Do not publish unapproved contact info, metrics, screenshots, collaborators,
   quotes, or private material.

## Good Patterns

- "Built and stewarded shared campaign-memory infrastructure."
- "Translated ambiguous stakeholder context into decision records and handoffs."
- "Contributed to 2x revenue growth" rather than "caused 2x revenue growth."
- "Co-built" where the work was collaborative.
- "Public-safe summary" when the evidence exists but the raw material should
  not be shown.

## Bad Patterns

- Tool-only summaries that make Jamie disappear.
- Claims that imply sole ownership of coalition, community, or team outcomes.
- Metrics without boundaries.
- Public pages that require insider context to understand why the work mattered.
