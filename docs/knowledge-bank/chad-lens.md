# Chad Lens

The site should make Jamie legible as a Technical Project Manager - Product
Operations & Implementation lead who creates operating structure for complex
public-facing teams.

## Editorial Standard

Every public page should answer four questions quickly:

1. What did Jamie do?
2. Toward what end?
3. For whom or with whom?
4. What became usable?

If a sentence does not help answer those questions, shorten it, move it to the
knowledge bank, or remove it.

## What To Strengthen

- Make Jamie visible as an actor without erasing collaborators.
- Name the operating artifacts: requirements, workflows, source maps, running
  minutes, action trackers, public guidance, decision records, QA/UAT support,
  adoption materials, launch support, and durable handoffs.
- Translate specialized civic or community language into concrete delivery
  behavior.
- Use strong but accurate verbs: built, led, co-built, stewarded, translated,
  structured, supported, contributed to.
- Keep purpose close to artifact: not just "documentation," but documentation
  that let people coordinate, decide, remember, launch, or maintain work.

## What To Avoid

- Do not flatten Jamie into a generic web designer, writer, community organizer,
  or communications person.
- Do not inflate Jamie into a sole movement leader, legal authority, public
  agency representative, or owner of collective outcomes.
- Do not ask the reader to decode private context or specialized shorthand.
- Do not use private records as spectacle to prove credibility.
- Do not publish an exact metric on the homepage proof strip when a softer claim
  serves the same reader need.

## Public Voice

Precise, warm, modular, ethically grounded. Documentation is care. The voice can
be confident without becoming grandiose: "I helped make this usable" is often
stronger than "I transformed everything."
