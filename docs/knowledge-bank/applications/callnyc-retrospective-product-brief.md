# CallNYC retrospective product brief

**Document status:** Written in 2026 as a retrospective account of a 2016
independent public prototype. It was not a contemporaneous PRD and should not be
represented as one.

## Product context

CouncilStat constituent-services data contained civic signal, but the dataset
was not itself a resident pathway. People still needed an understandable way
to move from a concern to district context, possible next steps, and the limits
of what the data could establish.

## Discovery frame

The operative question was not "How do we display every record?" It was:

> How might a resident enter through a recognizable issue, understand the
> relevant civic context, and reach a bounded next step without first learning
> the structure of the entire dataset?

The work treated administrative categories, public records, interface
structure, and disclaimers as one product problem. Source limitations stayed
inside the experience rather than being relegated to a detached legal note.

## Options and decision

- **Publish the dataset more directly:** rejected as insufficiently usable for
  a resident who did not already understand its categories.
- **Build a comprehensive constituent-service platform:** rejected as too broad
  for the evidence, time, and authority available.
- **Build a bounded issue-oriented prototype:** selected to test whether a
  person could move from a familiar concern to district context and possible
  next steps.
- **Do not present the work as official:** retained as a product boundary.
  CallNYC was independent, not commissioned by the Council and not an official
  City service.

## What Jamie made

Jamie independently built the prototype, organized records into issue-oriented
pathways, and translated administrative categories into public-facing guidance
patterns. The resulting interface combined source data, information
architecture, district context, possible next steps, scope limits, and public
documentation.

## Launch and evidence

The prototype reached public use and received Politico New York coverage in
March 2016. The surviving implementation is preserved in the public repository.
Those facts support a launched historical product example; they do not prove
official adoption, Council ownership, comprehensive resident outcomes, or a
causal role in the later data release.

## Retrospective learning

The strongest transferable lesson is that discovery can reduce harm and waste
before a team commits to scale. A narrow interface, explicit authority
boundary, and source limitations made the prototype more honest and usable
than a larger solution claim would have been.

## Application use

This brief may support an application that asks for a product brief from a
launched product if the retrospective status remains prominent. Before any
submission, Jamie should review the exact employer prompt, confirm that a
retrospective brief is acceptable, and tailor the format without strengthening
the underlying claims.
