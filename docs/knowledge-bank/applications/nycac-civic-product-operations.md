# NYC Artist Coalition application brief

## Core story

Jamie helped turn listening into coordinated public action. Recurring meetings
and priority ballots became testimony plans, run-of-show documents, call
scripts, public forums, websites, and sustained follow-through across small
cultural spaces and City Hall.

## Role-fit translation

**Technical operations:** Jamie created durable structure across concurrent
campaigns, public events, coalition decisions, source material, and follow-up.
The evidence shows requirements becoming artifacts, assigned work, public
interfaces, and reusable operating knowledge.

**Product operations:** Jamie helped create feedback loops from lived experience
to prioritized needs, from prioritized needs to public actions, and from public
actions to reviewable next steps. The system used meetings, ballots, calls,
public-data framing, and campaign surfaces as connected parts.

**Implementation:** Retained Git history and a protected task checklist document
Jamie's direct work on Fair Rent NYC, Talks Not Raids, Let NYC Dance, and the
coalition web stack. Use implementation and maintenance language, not sole
authorship of campaign policy or public outcomes.

**Stakeholder translation:** Jamie's testimony and town-hall work connected
cultural-space operators, advocates, public officials, and agencies while
making trust, safety, affordability, and regulatory navigation legible across
different institutional languages.

## Strong interview examples

- Co-authoring a repeatable listening-to-action guide with Julia Fredenburg.
- Helping structure a public Office of Nightlife town hall in which cultural
  organizations and officials could hear one another in the same room.
- Translating campaign requirements into responsive public web components,
  data-backed presentation, calls to action, and source routes.
- Using agendas, priority ballots, and action assignments so a coalition could
  move from broad concern to focused work without erasing disagreement or
  collective credit.
- Framing small-business regulatory requirements as machine-readable public
  infrastructure that could support clearer resident-facing tools.

## Safe wording

> Helped build and steward NYC Artist Coalition's civic operating system,
> connecting recurring cultural-space meetings and priority-setting with public
> forums, testimony, campaign websites, calls to action, and sustained
> government-facing follow-through.

## Boundaries

- Do not say Jamie solely founded or led NYC Artist Coalition.
- Do not say Jamie produced every event, authored every artifact, or controlled
  collaborator decisions.
- Do not assign the Cabaret Law repeal, Office of Nightlife, M.A.R.C.H. reforms,
  or Commercial Rent Stabilization work to one person.
- Keep participant records, raw notes, private correspondence, strategy, legal
  review, contact data, and unapproved quotations out of applications.
- Application use still requires Jamie's judgment about audience, emphasis,
  length, and the job's actual requirements.
