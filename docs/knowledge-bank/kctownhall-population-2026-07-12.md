# KC Town Hall Full-Population Archival Production

Run date: 2026-07-12

## Answer First

The live [@KCTownHall profile](https://x.com/KCTownHall) displays **183
posts**. A fine-grained authenticated pass through the Posts and Replies
timelines recovered **all 183 unique profile records**:

- 142 account-authored standalone posts;
- 13 account-authored replies;
- 28 reposts from 16 other public accounts.

The [record-level census](kctownhall-post-census-2026-07-12.csv) closes exactly
against the July 2026 live-profile control. Complete means all 183 records
represented by that control were recovered. It does not prove that no older
record was deleted before capture.

## Population And Method

The population is the 183-post count displayed beside the account name. A
record is one unique primary status ID published or reposted on the profile.

1. Read the live profile control and March 2018 join date.
2. Harvested Posts and Replies separately in 650-pixel increments so X's
   virtualized timeline could not skip large regions.
3. Recovered 170 records from Posts.
4. Recovered 13 additional KC Town Hall-authored replies from Replies.
5. Excluded five parent posts rendered only as conversation context.
6. Deduplicated the union by primary status ID, producing exactly 183 records.
7. Classified every record by authorship type, primary theme, mentions, and
   hashtags.
8. Omitted full post text, phone numbers, resident-submitted locations, and
   private contact information from the public census.
9. Recovered and closely read the archived Tired of Tires workflow page and
   searched for independent corroboration of its project-reported totals.

## What The Complete Population Shows

### A public neighborhood process

The [first recovered post](https://x.com/KCTownHall/status/1013893135695601665)
invites people to follow and participate in creating a permanent neighborhood
resource and cultural center. Another [launch-day
post](https://x.com/KCTownHall/status/1013902884285681665) asks what neighbors
would want in the site and describes the project as committed to a neighborhood
process.

The early account connects building history, adaptive reuse, walkable local
businesses, community space, a public survey, cleanup, and municipal context.
It functioned as a project interface rather than only a promotional feed.

### A recurring resident-service workflow

One hundred records concern tire pickup or program follow-through. Ninety-eight
explicitly carry `#TiredOfTires`. The account repeatedly made the operating loop
visible:

1. invite residents to submit locations;
2. define home-only eligibility and neighborhood scope;
3. publish recurring pickup dates;
4. invite volunteers;
5. document pickup and before-and-after updates;
6. report results;
7. announce the next cycle.

The [launch post](https://x.com/KCTownHall/status/1124416898064580608) opens the
resident intake pathway. A [first-month
update](https://x.com/KCTownHall/status/1124810411302359040) reports a completed
cycle, asks for more locations, and credits Oak Park Neighborhood and named
collaborators. A later [reply](https://x.com/KCTownHall/status/1302003767831912448)
documents an after-update and invites additional reports.

### Direct evidence of Jamie's contribution and collective credit

The archived [Tired of Tires? Free Tire
Pickup](https://web.archive.org/web/20210806195823id_/https://kctownhall.com/tires/)
page identifies **Julia and Jamie** as its authors. It credits **KC Town Hall and
the Oak Park Neighborhood Association** with the monthly service and provides a
request form, volunteer pathway, home-only eligibility, and recurring pickup
dates.

This supports a strong but bounded contribution claim: Jamie and Julia
published the resident-facing operating workflow; the program itself was
collective. The evidence does not assign every account post, pickup, or later
program action to Jamie.

### Civic information, public documentation, and care

Beyond the service program, the population includes:

- candidate and mayoral forums, voting information, transit, and affordable
  housing context;
- community-asset advocacy around Leon's Thriftway and New Horizon Church;
- neighborhood cleanup and municipal-service follow-through;
- COVID-19 resource translation;
- 12 posts documenting a June 2020 Black Lives Matter demonstration;
- amplification of local schools, neighborhood organizations, community
  leaders, public officials, and resident resources.

The account record preserves public dialogue and community context. A mention
or repost is not represented as municipal adoption, causal impact, or Jamie's
individual authorship.

## Claim Boundaries

- Complete means all 183 records represented by the current profile control
  were recovered; it does not establish that no older record was deleted.
- The account does not identify the individual teammate who authored each
  record.
- Julia and Jamie are credited as authors of the archived service page. KC Town
  Hall and Oak Park Neighborhood Association are credited with the program.
- The account continued through 2022. The census does not assign Jamie
  responsibility after his documented transition.
- Tire and avoided-fee totals are project-reported until independently
  corroborated.
- Resident-submitted locations and contact information are intentionally absent
  from the public census.
- Reposts, mentions, and dialogue do not by themselves establish official
  adoption or causal impact.
