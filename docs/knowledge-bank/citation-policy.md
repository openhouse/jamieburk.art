# Citational Care Policy

Last reviewed: 2026-07-11

Citational care connects consequential public claims to bounded evidence while
keeping the portfolio readable. A citation is not a decorative seal of
authority. It should help a reader understand what is claimed, what supports
it, what the source cannot establish, and what remains protected.

## Placement

Place a citation immediately after the precise proposition it supports. Cite
consequential facts, dates, metrics, press coverage, official actions, and
reconstructed historical claims. Do not collect unrelated sources at the end of
a long paragraph, and do not cite ordinary positioning language merely to make
it look scholarly.

## Source precision

Every source record states what it establishes and does not establish. An
official source can be incomplete. An archive capture may preserve embedded
evidence without being the original event page. A timestamp is not a schedule.
A promotional graphic is not automatically a formal title.

Evidence records describe the relationship between a source and a claim. A
reader-facing citation note may join closely related claims and sources, but its
summary must preserve the important limitations.

## Negative research

Use "not recovered in this search." Do not convert that into "did not exist."
Store the research question, method, search scope, recovered material,
not-recovered material, finding, and limitations.

## Private evidence

Private evidence may support a carefully bounded record. Public citation does
not imply public access to the underlying source. Never expose private paths,
raw filenames, signed URLs, unapproved files, face tags, GPS data, or private
correspondence. A label-only or summary-only source renders only its approved
public-safe representation. A not-public source cannot enter a public note.

## Photographs

Distinguish evidentiary photographs from representative photographs. Record
rights, consent, public-use status, evidentiary scope, and credit separately.
Factual relevance does not grant permission to publish. A photograph supports
only what is visibly or technically established; its timestamp is not an event
schedule, and a placard does not necessarily name the full event.

## Corrections

Preserve correction history rather than silently replacing old language. Store
the prior value, corrected value, reason, supporting evidence, status, and
resolution date. Superseded claims remain available for audit but do not render
publicly. Binary artifacts are changed only through an approved source and
generation workflow.

## Numbering

Stable semantic IDs live in the Knowledge Bank. Numerical markers are generated
per page from a citation note's first appearance. Repeated notes reuse their
number and receive a distinct backlink for every occurrence. Never hardcode
visible numbers into prose.

## Public repository rule

Everything committed here is public. Knowledge Bank records and documentation
must themselves be safe to quote in a newspaper. Private research and source
artifacts remain outside the repository.
