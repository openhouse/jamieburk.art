# Source Policy

The knowledge bank may mention source classes. It must not contain protected
source contents.

Never commit private source files to the repo for the purpose of proving a
claim.

## Source Classes

### public-source

Public web pages, public records, public campaign pages, public press, public
GitHub repositories, and other materials already intended for public access.

Safe behavior: link when appropriate, but do not overstate what the public
source proves.

### public-project-artifact

Public project pages, public campaign materials, public repositories, public
documents, or approved public screenshots created as part of the work.

Safe behavior: use the artifact to show the existence and shape of the work, not
private intent, authorship beyond what is shown, or collaborator approval beyond
the artifact itself.

### public-record

Government records, public meeting materials, public funding records, public
legislation, agency pages, or other formal records.

Safe behavior: keep the verb precise. Public records may prove that a process,
recommendation, authorization path, or document exists; they do not automatically
prove causation, completion, or Jamie's sole role.

### public-coverage

Press, public interviews, articles, public profiles, or public event coverage.

Safe behavior: cite exactly and avoid turning coverage into endorsement unless
the source explicitly says so.

### approved-resume

Claims present in the current approved resume PDF.

Safe behavior: use only the exact approved resume or narrower public wording.
The resume may include contact information that should not automatically appear
in HTML.

### approved-public-page

Current public pages or artifacts that Jamie has approved as source context.

Safe behavior: cite or summarize the public page. Do not infer private facts
from it.

### public-safe-summary

A summary of protected or mixed source material that has been rewritten to avoid
private details, names, records, paths, screenshots, or quotes.

Safe behavior: use the summary as confidence and wording support. Do not commit
the protected source behind it.

### private-archive-summary

Aggregate or summary information drawn from private archives, such as rounded
counts, project chronology, or source-class descriptions.

Safe behavior: use aggregate claims only. Keep user records, contact records,
private media, and raw exports outside the repo.

### private-source-outside-repo

Protected source material that may inform confidence but must not be committed:
private emails, unedited meeting records, private coalition notes, legal-review
materials, stakeholder lists, dashboards, client materials, private screenshots,
private fonts, and credentials.

Safe behavior: reference only the source class and public-safe summary.

### conversation-summary

A public-safe synthesis of a private or semi-private conversation.

Safe behavior: do not quote private calls, attribute private comments, or name
participants unless explicitly approved.

### firsthand-context

Jamie's firsthand recollection, professional history, or project knowledge.

Safe behavior: use for internal confidence and public-safe summaries. Do not use
firsthand context alone to publish sensitive details, private names, exact
metrics, or collaborator claims that require approval.

### collaborator-context

Context supplied by collaborators, advisors, reviewers, or project partners.

Safe behavior: summarize only with consent and avoid naming the person or
organization unless publication is approved.

### approval-pending

A claim or artifact that may be true and useful but still needs Jamie,
collaborator, source, or publication approval before production projection.

Safe behavior: keep in the knowledge bank with the appropriate status; do not
ship to public pages.

### staging-only

Material that may be reviewed on staging or in internal docs but should not ship
to production indexing.

Safe behavior: keep off production-facing pages until approved.

### do-not-publish

Material or claim types that must not appear on the public site.

Safe behavior: keep out of public app content and public metadata.

## Source Rules

- Public sources may be linked if appropriate.
- Approved resume claims may be used only if the exact resume PDF is approved.
- Public-safe summaries may be used without exposing protected evidence.
- Private archive summaries may inform confidence but must not reveal source
  contents.
- Conversation summaries must not quote private calls unless explicitly
  approved.
- Do not paste private local file paths into source notes.
- Do not publish private email, raw transcripts, stakeholder lists, legal-review
  materials, internal dashboards, source bundles, or private correspondence.
- Do not use source material to create new public obligations for collaborators
  who have not approved the publication.
