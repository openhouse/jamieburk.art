# Office of Nightlife town hall at Market Hotel

The October 11, 2017 event titled "Tell NYC's Night Mayor: Save NYC Spaces"
placed the newly established Office of Nightlife in public dialogue with
cultural-space communities at Market Hotel. The surviving event record names
House of YES, Dance Liberation Network, and cohosts and retains a rounded
historical response count of 1.2K.

## Jamie's role

Protected production records identify Jamie and Olympia Kazi as organizers
opening the program. The working plan coordinates public officials, cultural
speakers, questions, projection, sound, seating, volunteers, documentation,
and follow-up. This supports substantial event-systems and production work by
Jamie while preserving Olympia's distinct credit and the labor of cohosts,
speakers, volunteers, venue workers, officials, and participants.

The record is a working production plan rather than a complete labor ledger.
It cannot assign every task, confirm every planned element, or transfer the
event's collective authorship to two named organizers.

## Why the event mattered

The town hall made governance encounterable. Instead of asking small cultural
spaces to translate themselves only through formal testimony or private
meetings, it brought public authority into a cultural venue where operators,
artists, and community members could frame the questions from lived practice.

This is the coalition's intermediary function at event scale: convene in the
affected place, produce a shared civic setting, document priorities, and build
routes for follow-up. A Facebook response is not verified attendance, and an
event is not proof that every recommendation was adopted.

## What the evidence establishes

- A dated public event at Market Hotel connected to Office of Nightlife
  dialogue and small-cultural-space priorities.
- A broad, multi-part production system recorded in protected working
  material.
- Jamie and Olympia Kazi named as organizers opening the program.
- A public historical response signal that must remain labeled as rounded
  Facebook actions rather than people or attendance.
