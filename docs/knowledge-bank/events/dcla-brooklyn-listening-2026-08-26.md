# Brooklyn artist listening session at 651 ARTS

## Why this matters to the portfolio

Jamie participates as an organizer who connects people's lived circumstances,
shared needs, policy, and usable information. His formal contribution makes a
common condition visible: artists, studios, theaters, and other cultural spaces
depend on places they rent. He then asks how that condition can inform action
inside government. Subsequent conversations carry the question into cultural
data, records, and implementation.

This is a contemporary example of situated discovery and practical follow-through.
It adds depth to the coalition case study without requiring another homepage
claim or a longer résumé. The photograph can eventually bring this work into view;
it should accompany an account of Jamie's activity, not substitute for one.

## Jamie's agency, plainly stated

Jamie **participated, asked, translated, connected, and followed up**. DCLA and
the host institutions convened and framed the event. Other artists supplied
their own needs, analyses, and proposals. Jamie's contribution belongs within
that collective setting.

The account does not add a new claim that Jamie hosted this listening session,
authored the legislation, secured the commissioner's support, or caused an
enactment. His references to earlier coalition achievements remain references;
their underlying evidence stays with the existing historical records.
