# Source Classes

Source classes describe the kind of support behind a claim. They do not authorize publication of the underlying evidence.

## Classes

### public-url

A public web page that can be linked from the site or repo.

### public-document

A public PDF, report, filing, presentation, or other document that can be cited or linked without exposing private context.

### approved-resume

The current resume Jamie has approved for public distribution. The resume may contain details that are not repeated on website pages.

### approved-screenshot

A screenshot Jamie has approved for public use. Approval should include crop, context, caption, and whether collaborator or client review is required.

### approved-artifact

A work artifact Jamie has approved for public use, such as a redacted diagram, public handout, public map, or public-safe representative example.

### public-record

A government, legislative, court, procurement, archive, or institutional record that is already public.

### public-safe-summary

A summary that is accurate and defensible but intentionally avoids private records, raw data, names, screenshots, or sensitive detail.

### private-source-not-committed

A private source can inform internal confidence, but it is not committed to the repo and should not be quoted or linked.

### protected-do-not-publish

Material that should not be published or paraphrased because privacy, consent, client trust, law, civic sensitivity, community safety, or collaborator approval requires omission.

### candidate-needs-approval

The claim may be true or useful, but needs Jamie approval, collaborator approval, source confirmation, screenshot approval, citation confirmation, or metric review before production indexing.

## Rule

Private sources may justify Jamie's internal confidence, but they should not be quoted or committed unless explicitly approved. When a private source supports a claim, publish the safest summary and keep the protected evidence out of the repo.
