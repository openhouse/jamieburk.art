# Public testimony source return, July 28, 2026

## Question

What complete public record of Jamie's New York City Council testimony can be
recovered from official transcripts, and what does it reveal about the
continuity of his practice?

## Declared source population

The pass searched Council transcript records under the known variants “Jamie
Burkart,” “Jamie Burkhart,” and “Jaime Burkhart,” then read the surrounding
official pages. It recovered five personal appearances:

1. June 19, 2017: Cabaret Law and Office of Nightlife hearing.
2. September 14, 2017: Cabaret Law repeal hearing.
3. October 22, 2018: Small Business Jobs Survival Act hearing.
4. February 11, 2019: M.A.R.C.H. transparency hearing.
5. March 18, 2019: small-business legislative package and open data.

“Five” is complete against this declared source-and-name search, not an
all-time guarantee. Written submissions, testimony indexed under another
speaker, agency hearings, rallies, community-board appearances, and other
public forums remain outside this bounded population.

## Findings

The sequence makes a coherent progression visible:

- make social dancing legal and reject discriminatory regulatory inheritance;
- build trust-based safety capacity with cultural-space operators;
- establish an Office of Nightlife able to listen and intervene;
- connect commercial affordability to cultural survival and safety;
- replace opaque, disproportionate multi-agency raids with transparent
  problem-solving; and
- publish requirements as data that people and agencies can use to navigate
  legality, safety, and compliance.

These are records of advocacy and proposed implementation. They should not be
converted into sole-causation claims about legislative or administrative
outcomes.

## Transcription and credit

Official transcripts repeatedly misspell Jamie's surname and render several
proper names incorrectly. Reading copies preserve those errors in substance
and label them as stenographic limitations. Collective NYC Artist Coalition
statements are not recast as Jamie's personal testimony; collaborators who
read or authored them retain collective credit.
