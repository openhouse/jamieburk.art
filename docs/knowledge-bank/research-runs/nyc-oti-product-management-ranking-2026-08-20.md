# NYC OTI product-management search ranking, August 20, 2026

## Decision

The official search returned 11 roles. `ceil(11 × 0.38)` admits five roles;
four would represent only 36.4% and under-shoot the requested share. The five
admitted records are the PIT Crew Senior Product Manager, SPEED Operations
Manager, Product Manager, SPEED Senior Product Manager, and PIT Crew Senior
Product Designer.

## Rubric

Two 0-100 judgments are kept separate:

- **Fit**: overlap between the actual work and Jamie's maintained evidence.
- **Secure**: relative likelihood of surviving minimum, preferred-skill,
  conventional-title, and portfolio screens. It is not a predicted probability.

The composite is `55% fit + 45% secure`. Application momentum affects the
secure score only modestly. A deterministic hard screen overrides any high
semantic score.

## Close reading

The first four roles belong to one coherent lane: Jamie turns ambiguous,
multi-party public work into product direction, implementation, operating
structure, and durable memory. The SPEED Operations Manager is especially
important because it names the founder-level work Jamie has struggled to make
legible: budgets, procurement, vendors, onboarding, dependencies, requests,
metrics, and team conditions.

The fifth role is a deliberate edge of the set. Jamie has designed and shipped
public websites, visual systems, information architectures, handbills, service
interfaces, and participatory research processes. That makes Senior Product
Designer truthful to consider. It does not establish every preferred signal of
a conventional senior product designer.

The remaining roles are retained in this research run, not promoted to full
opportunity records. This avoids confusing thematic affinity with a good use of
application time. The Senior DevOps role fails before any model call because
the posting is restricted to eligible City employees.

## Research boundary

The review used public official postings, official SPEED program sources, and
the maintained public-safe career record. It did not use employer-private
information or infer how any actual reader will decide. Scores support queueing
and evidence research; they do not establish an interview, an offer, civil-
service eligibility, a particular salary, or one-year success.
