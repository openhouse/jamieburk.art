# The archive answers back

## A proposal for a first photographic archive residency at 196

I propose to give my photographic archive a residency in the place where I
live. For up to two weeks, my home apartment in Brooklyn, near Fort Greene Park,
will become a studio for a first encounter with a lifetime-scale field of
images.

The starting gesture is to assemble a rough field of approximately 1,000
photographs. That number is a way to make a large archive touchable. It is not a
quota, a representative sample, a ranking, or a promise that the field will be
finished. The work may become smaller, larger, slower, stranger, or something
other than a photo selection.

## Why write a proposal

I often tell artists that a 196 residency begins when they write the proposal.
The proposal is not a contract. No resident is judged by whether they do what
they promised. Writing establishes intent: some part of the artist has said it
needs time and space, and another person has received that voice and answered
that it matters.

This proposal makes that promise inward. I will treat the desire to return to
the photographs as real. I will nourish it with time, attention, room, and
encouragement before asking it to become useful.

## The invitation

Come into the archive without already knowing what it is for.

Look for images that explain themselves and images that refuse. Keep the
technically imperfect frame when it carries force. Keep sequences, repetitions,
bursts, mistakes, intervals, and ordinary time long enough to learn whether
their relationship matters. Let one photograph enter several constellations or
none. Let an unnamed detail become the center of the day.

The archive is allowed to contradict memory, the portfolio, and this proposal.

## The place

The residency takes place at 196 as a home practice, not a public venue. The
apartment offers a table, walls, floors, light, rest, food, conversation, and a
daily relationship to the neighborhood and Fort Greene Park. The private street
address, building access, archive storage, and presence of other people remain
protected.

The apartment is not neutral. It is the place where Sunday Dinner and the 196
Artists Residency have practiced invitation, hospitality, onboarding, and
continuity. For this pass, those capacities turn inward: can I host my own
attention with the generosity I try to offer another artist?

## Proposed practice

- Establish a stable, private identity for the rough field and a route back to
  each exact source photograph.
- Look with and without project labels. Preserve sequence and burst context.
- Make temporary constellations on a screen, table, wall, or floor without
  treating any arrangement as final.
- Move between looking, walking, resting, cooking, writing, and returning.
- Keep dated public-safe notebook entries about questions, changed readings,
  contradictions, absences, and librarian requests.
- Hold exact images, private metadata, contact sheets, safety notes, and rights
  or consent work in the protected local layer.
- Stop when attention has become dull, coercive, extractive, or unsafe.

These are possible practices, not required deliverables.

## Questions I want to live beside

- How can an image operationalize love and inclusion in the diegetic worlds of
  these projects?
- Where is active accommodation visible: the tending of a vessel, room, table,
  meeting, tool, invitation, or shared field of agency?
- What does it look like when a person knows there is room for them in a story?
- Where do thresholds appear: the moment of arriving, joining, staying,
  refusing, changing course, or carrying the work onward?
- Which photographs show care for the container without making me the hero of
  what other people made?
- What ordinary gestures reveal that everyone was already working on the same
  project?
- What has not been photographed, should not be shown, or needs another person
  to help me understand it?

## What might emerge

The first pass might produce a provisional sequence, a set of visual motifs, a
photo-editor brief, a group of project questions, a new research lead, a
slideshow, an installation thought, a book shape, a room arrangement, a walk,
or no coherent artifact yet.

Photography might remain the medium. It might become writing, conversation,
mapping, performance, sound, hospitality, or a different way of caring for the
archive. A useful residency may end with fewer certainties and better attention.

## The residency lesson

Jamie remembers Teju Cole's account of arriving at a Zurich writing residency
with a manuscript about Lagos and finding himself drawn into the Swiss
landscape and photography. The identified essay is *Far Away From Here*, later
collected in *Known and Strange Things*. In a published interview, Cole
describes writing some of the intended project while most of his residency time
moved toward traveling, looking, and making photographs.

The lesson used here is Jamie's: a proposal can establish serious intent
without foreclosing discovery. This proposal does not claim that Cole stated
the 196 residency philosophy, modeled this program, or endorsed this project.

## Care and public boundaries

Access is not permission. Selection is not consent. Beauty is not safety.
Custody is not authorship. A photograph does not disclose a represented
person's inner life, role, relationship, or agreement to become evidence.

No image enters the public portfolio through this proposal. Any later use must
return as an exact asset with its own authorship, rights, consent, safety,
caption, crop, context, collective-credit, and surface-specific approval.

Hospitality is not an outcome claim. This proposal does not claim that every
196 resident had the same experience, that a stay changed a life, or that
hosting caused another person's later work or relationships.

## What counts as success

Success is not fidelity to this proposal, a completed 1,000-image edit, a daily
output, a public exhibition, or a portfolio update.

Success is that the voice asking for this time was heard; that the conditions
for sustained attention were made real; that the archive was approached with
curiosity and care; and that the work was allowed to go where it needed to go.

## Acceptance record

On July 22, 2026, after reviewing this proposal, Jamie Burkart, acting as both
the artist and the host of 196, replied: "Your proposal is accepted. Welcome."

This is a documented human decision, not an inference from the proposal's
wording or an acceptance issued by an AI system. It gives the protected
first-pass residency permission to begin. It does not certify that the
residency has occurred, set a compulsory start or end date, promise an outcome,
or approve an image, interpretation, claim, or public surface.

The 196 practice receives this proposal as accepted. The time is legitimate.
The questions deserve nourishment. Deviation, incompletion, rest, refusal, and
surprise remain available throughout.

Acceptance authorizes the experiment, not its interpretation or publication.
The residency begins with this proposal. Its form remains open.
