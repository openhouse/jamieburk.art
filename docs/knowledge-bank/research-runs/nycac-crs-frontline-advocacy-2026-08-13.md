# NYCAC and Fair Rent NYC 30-day frontline advocacy source return

## Scope

This run close-read the period from July 14 through August 13, 2026. It used
bounded, read-only searches of authenticated mail and documents; the public
report and event listing; an authenticated view of the final campaign social
post; a governed internal event record; and the locally available transcript
derivatives. Raw messages, private document bodies, recordings, contact
details, authenticated URLs, and private locators were not copied into the
repository.

The July 29 cloud source directory did not materialize during the run. Existing
governed event records and transcript derivatives were sufficient to establish
formal participation, but not to authorize verbatim speech. That gap remains
explicit.

## Source classes and disposition

| Source class | What it establishes | Public disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Final public report | report identity, public acknowledgment, findings, methods, limitations | cite directly |
| Public event listing | date, report-release/rally purpose, campaign context | cite directly |
| Authenticated public social post | final collaborator list at the review cutoff | cite only for bounded distribution state |
| Protected partner correspondence | invited role, edit integration, acknowledgment approval, media request | summary only; no raw text or locator |
| Protected event and transcript family | formal participation, affiliation, speaking lane, prepared/delivered separation | summary only; quotation held |
| Protected elected-office correspondence | completed city routing and scheduled state-staff alignment | summary only; no contacts or messages |

Access is not consent. Evidence is not publication permission. An attributed
statement is not automatically an institutional decision, and an event
appearance is not automatically a media placement.

## Close-reading findings

### Strongest public proof

The report's acknowledgment is the cleanest new public credential. It names
Jamie, names his coalition and campaign relationship, and characterizes his
review. Because the same report identifies its lead author, publishers, and
research-design advisers, the credit can be made legible without erasing
collective production.

### Strongest protected proof

Partner correspondence says many of Jamie's detailed edits were incorporated.
His review focused on factual accuracy, methods clarity, current legislative
language, and whether claims could travel beyond what the data establishes.
The final report's careful distinction between registered and market rent and
its explicit causal limits make that contribution especially relevant, while
the report alone cannot attribute any particular sentence to Jamie.

### Strongest public-action proof

Jamie moved from reviewer to formal speaker at the July 29 press conference.
The event record places him in a shared program with small-business advocates
and state elected officials and identifies the cultural-space and public-data
lane he carried. No reviewed source establishes a resulting article, interview,
broadcast, or verbatim quotation.

### Strongest operating proof

The government-relations record shows two different maturity states: a City
Council office handoff completed, while a recurring state-staff alignment
channel was proposed and its first future meeting scheduled. Treating the
verbs as data prevents outreach from becoming endorsement or scheduling from
becoming a completed meeting.
