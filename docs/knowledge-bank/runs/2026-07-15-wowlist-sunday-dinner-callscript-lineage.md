# WOW List, Sunday Dinner, and Call Script Lineage

**Reviewed:** July 15, 2026

**Disposition:** Sources and atomic assertions integrated; no new public claim or website projection selected

## Purpose

This pass tests a possible professional lineage: Sunday Dinner as a recurring
practice of relational operations; WOW List as community-created calendar
infrastructure; `popular.vote` as a civic deployment of that infrastructure;
Call Script as an agenda-gathering surface; and NYC Artist Coalition as a
collective participation system that emerged across these connected practices.

The goal is not to force a single origin story. It is to identify what the
surviving records establish, what they only suggest, and what must remain
protected.

## Methods

1. Streamed aggregate table counts from three historic WOW List production
   database snapshots without retaining raw rows or person identifiers.
2. Inspected and attributed the Git change that added `popular.vote` routing to
   the WOW List codebase.
3. Analyzed dates, gathering labels, count cells, and formulas in a protected
   Sunday Dinner operating workbook without ingesting participant records.
4. Traversed the public Call Script Facebook Page and fully materialized the
   January 27, 2017, Department of Cultural Affairs event discussion in an
   authenticated browser session until repeated scrolling produced no new
   content.
5. Compared the recovered chronology with the existing NYC Artist Coalition
   Facebook event census and knowledge-bank claims.

The aggregate outputs are preserved in the
[public-safe corpus](../corpora/wowlist-sunday-dinner-callscript-lineage-2026-07-15.json).

## Findings

### WOW List operated at meaningful production scale

The three snapshots show growth from 995 users and 10,136 posts/events in June
2016 to 1,846 users and 16,142 posts/events in July 2017. The July 2017 snapshot
also contains 23,864 tags/lists, 28,837 list follows, 20,927 stars, and 2,965
going records.

These are database table totals at dated snapshots. They are not unique reach,
attendance, endorsement, conversion, or present-day activity.

### Popular Vote was a substantial civic-calendar deployment

The Popular Vote list was created November 12, 2016. By July 22, 2017, it
contained 933 linked post/event records, 196 follows, and contributions from 10
anonymous contributor IDs. A Git commit authored by Jamie on the list's creation
date added `popular.vote` and `www.popular.vote` routing to WOW List.

The commit establishes Jamie's direct implementation work. It does not establish
sole conception, ownership, editorial authorship, or later operation of Popular
Vote.

### Sunday Dinner preserved an instrumented hosting practice

The protected workbook contains 345 numbered gathering columns spanning January
22, 2012, through March 7, 2021. Its participant-total formula displays 2,783
meals served, while the gathering-sequence columns sum to 2,769. Five gathering
numbers appear on duplicate columns, and four additional sequence columns are
unnumbered.

The workbook supports a decade-long, instrumented invitation, response, and
attendance practice. The 14-instance difference means 2,783 remains protected
working evidence rather than an audited public attendance or unique-person
total.

### Call Script made the civic-calendar connection visible

The public [Call Script Page](https://www.facebook.com/callscript) links directly
to `popular.vote`. The public
[January 27 event discussion](https://www.facebook.com/events/388137698233507/?active_tab=discussion)
shows NYC Artist Coalition and Call Script among the hosts for a Department of
Cultural Affairs meeting on DIY and alternative art spaces after the Ghost Ship
fire. The event displays 445 responses, not verified attendance.

Before the meeting, Call Script asked participants what the group should request
and circulated an example of peer-city warehouse organizing in Oakland. Later,
an NYC Artist Coalition poll on the same discussion surface invited people to
add and assess priorities spanning compliance support, displacement, safety,
mutual aid, professional services, and advocacy.

The production database dates the NYC Artist Coalition list to February 7,
2017, and records 82 linked post/event records by July 22. This establishes a
formation-era sequence and connected participation surfaces. It does not prove a
single linear cause or sole founder.
