## Answer First

One explicit instance was recovered in which Commissioner Tom Finkelpearl
referred to NYC Artist Coalition in New York City Council testimony. At the
[May 19, 2017 Fiscal 2018 Executive Budget hearing](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/View.ashx?GUID=68A2E207-53EA-43CA-A03E-F879A113464E&ID=5271559&M=F),
he named the coalition while explaining DCLA's interest in reciprocal public
relationships, expanded direct feedback, and the power of bringing people
together around a common cause. He said the coalition formed after DCLA's
January meeting with the DIY arts community.

This is **one explicit recovered instance**, not a claim that no other mention
exists. Public search and OCR can miss records, and the Legistar API requires a
token, so a complete denominator of Finkelpearl's Council appearances remains
an open research task.

## Why The Work Mattered To DCLA

This section is institutional analysis grounded in the records above, not a
claim that Finkelpearl privately stated these exact reasons.

DCLA already had formal relationships with funded cultural organizations. NYC
Artist Coalition made another field more reachable: informal, DIY, and
community-driven spaces whose experience did not always arrive through the
usual institutional channels. The coalition converted one listening event into
continuing civic capacity: collective recommendations, recurring convening,
public testimony, issue framing, and an entity with which government could keep
talking.

Finkelpearl's placement of the coalition in his testimony matters. He was
making an argument that CreateNYC public engagement could be consequential
rather than ceremonial. NYC Artist Coalition was useful evidence for that
argument because participants had continued to organize and produce work after
the meeting.

## Why The Work Mattered To Council

Council needed more than broad support or opposition. It needed people able to
explain how rules behaved in lived conditions, how cultural operators navigated
agencies, where trust and compliance broke down, and what public tools might
make implementation more usable.

The recovered hearings show Jamie doing exactly that. In 2017 he joined personal
experience to field safety and compliance practice. In 2019 he moved from
recurring operator and agency experience to specific recommendations for public
datasets and online tools. The coalition made an informal constituency more
legible and reachable while preserving its connection to the places affected.

## Why The Work Mattered To Espinal

The direct record supports **benefit and sustained collaboration**, not literal
dependency. Espinal thanked Jamie for his personal story and advocacy in 2017.
In 2019 he publicly acknowledged that NYC Artist Coalition had worked closely
over multiple years on improving nightlife.

The source-backed interpretation is that Espinal benefited from a prepared,
participatory constituency: people who could connect cultural freedom, safety,
regulation, affordability, and implementation; bring that knowledge into
hearings; and help public issues become public participation. That strengthened
the information and constituency available to legislative work. It did not
transfer Council's authority or establish that Espinal could not have acted
without the coalition.

## Defensible Synthesis

> NYC Artist Coalition made an informal cultural constituency more legible and
> reachable to government. It converted lived conditions into recommendations,
> testimony, civic tools, and recurring public forums. DCLA cited the
> coalition's formation as evidence that public listening could create durable
> civic capacity; Council and Espinal gained an informed constituency and
> implementation feedback around nightlife, cultural-space safety, regulation,
> and affordability.

## Boundaries

- Finkelpearl's chronology does not establish that DCLA solely created, owned,
  directed, or spoke for NYC Artist Coalition.
- The records do not establish that Jamie alone founded or led the coalition or
  represented every participant.
- Testimony and recommendations do not establish legislative authorship,
  adoption of every recommendation, or sole causation of a policy outcome.
- “Needed” is best understood as institutional usefulness: recognition,
  exchange, field knowledge, participation, and implementation feedback. It is
  not evidence of indispensability.
- The transcript search is partially recovered until a complete Finkelpearl
  appearance manifest can be searched.
