# Outcome evidence model

Use these fields independently for every major project.

## Output

What was built, delivered, convened, documented, repaired, published, or handed
off? An output establishes work product, not use or impact.

## Observed use

What did another person or institution demonstrably do with the output? Use
direct records where possible. Publication, display, responses, and account
counters are observations, not automatic adoption.

## Outcome

What changed for a user, team, organization, public process, business, place,
or policy? State the evidence class and preserve negative or incomplete
endpoints.

## Causal contribution

What supports attributing a bounded part of the outcome to Jamie's work? Name
collaborators, alternative causes, time sequence, and uncertainty. Prefer
contribution language unless evidence supports a stronger causal claim.

## Non-equivalence rules

- A published page is not proof of readership.
- Readership is not proof of comprehension or action.
- Social interaction is not endorsement, adoption, or impact.
- Attendance is not agreement or downstream change.
- A recommendation or appropriation is not receipt, expenditure, or completion.
- Training completion is not delivered implementation.
- A proposal is not a completed engagement.

Do not promote use into outcome or outcome into individual causality. Unknown
is an acceptable status and a research instruction.

## Closure target

At least four major case studies should eventually carry a corroborated bounded
outcome while retaining all four fields. Until then, the inventory should make
the missing layer visible rather than substituting output volume.
