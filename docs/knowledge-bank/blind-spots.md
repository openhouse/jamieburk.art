# Portfolio blind-spot ledger

Date: 2026-07-16

## Purpose

This ledger converts seven known weaknesses into governed research and
application-readiness work. It does not treat an eval, protocol, source count,
or empty intake form as evidence that a weakness has been resolved.

The baseline before this pass contained 284 source records, of which 72 were
explicitly close-read, 42 were metadata-reviewed, and 170 carried no structured
review status. Of 62 research tasks, 34 were open or in progress. Twenty-one of
27 research inquiries were partial, inconclusive, or not recovered. Eleven of
16 public proof records were careful rather than ready.

These counts describe the shape of the system. They are not impact metrics.

## Closure and governance

Each blind spot has two states:

- **Governance readiness:** a criterion, task, evidence contract, protocol, and
  honest current disposition exist.
- **Evidence closure:** the required corroboration, human observation, rights
  decision, source review, outcome evidence, or audience composition actually
  exists.

The repository check requires governance readiness and rejects false closure.
It reports evidence closure separately.

## 1. Collaborator corroboration

The current bank contains strong public records and protected first-party
artifacts, but several job-critical role and outcome claims still need bounded
confirmation from direct collaborators. Use the collaborator-proof intake
protocol. An AI archival review, a request email, or Jamie's memory is not a
collaborator confirmation.

## 2. Contemporary implementation proof

The current-role proof inventory separates ongoing operations, delivered
handoffs, professional development, proposals, and undelivered methods. A
current-role proof does not become stronger through repetition across the
homepage, resume, and case studies. Closure requires three corroborated
2022-2026 implementation outcomes approved for application use.

## 3. Unfamiliar-reader validation

Deterministic and LLM evaluation cannot establish how an unfamiliar hiring
reader understands the portfolio. Use the five-minute protocol and preserve
aggregate findings without identifying participants.

## 4. Visual proof and rights

The media lifecycle is rich but unresolved. Visual evidence must pass separate
tests for factual relevance, authorship, rights, contextual consent, caption
accuracy, and display destination. Possession and public availability do not
equal permission.

## 5. Claim-bearing source maturation

The first inventory is scoped by front-door consequence: homepage, HTML
resume, Technical Operations, and downloadable-resume claims. Every priority
source receives a disposition and next action. The closure target is 80 percent
explicit close-read coverage or replacement by stronger evidence, with the
remainder bounded.

## 6. Outcome evidence

Every major project should distinguish output, observed use, outcome, and
causal contribution. Publication, counters, attendance signals, appropriations,
and testimony are not interchangeable with adoption or impact.

## 7. Audience-specific compositions

Four internal application briefs now select from the same canonical facts for
OTI and public-sector delivery, private-sector implementation, source-backed
team memory, and civic-cultural infrastructure. These briefs do not widen
public approval or create new claims.

## Operating rule

Research should now improve the decision value of the bank before increasing
its volume. Prefer evidence that strengthens current technical delivery,
independent corroboration, observed use, outcomes, and visual inspectability.
Keep unresolved work explicit.
