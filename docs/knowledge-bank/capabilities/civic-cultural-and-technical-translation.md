# Civic, cultural, and technical translation

This capability joins listening, public language, data interpretation,
information architecture, interface design, facilitation, and institutional
follow-through. Translation does not mean making unlike worlds identical. It
means building a form through which each can become legible enough to act while
its authority and differences remain visible.

## What it looks like

- turning constituent-services records into resident-facing issue pathways in
  [CallNYC](../projects/callnyc.md);
- carrying artists' and venue operators' accounts into campaigns, town halls,
  testimony, and agency-facing questions through the [NYC Artist
  Coalition](../organizations/nyc-artist-coalition.md);
- connecting neighborhood encounters, survey infrastructure, building work,
  and public-benefit documentation in [KC Town
  Hall](../projects/kc-town-hall-orientation.md);
- translating project fragments, institutional records, and protected archives
  into source-bounded knowledge records through [Source-Backed Team
  Memory](../methods/source-backed-team-memory.md).

## Professional value

In implementation and product-operations work, this appears as requirements
discovery, cross-functional translation, data interpretation, workflow design,
facilitation, public communication, risk clarification, and the creation of
artifacts different actors can use without pretending they hold the same role.

## Boundary

Translation is not representation without consent, advocacy without collective
credit, or institutional authority Jamie did not hold. A usable interface or
well-attended event does not prove that a public body adopted the input or that
all participants agreed.
