# Civic, Cultural, and Technical Translation

This capability joins listening, public language, data interpretation,
information architecture, interface design, facilitation, and institutional
follow-through. Translation does not mean making unlike worlds identical. It
means building a form through which each can become legible enough to act while
its authority and differences remain visible.

It appears in:

- resident-facing issue pathways built from constituent-services records in
  [CallNYC](../projects/callnyc.md);
- campaigns, town halls, testimony, and agency-facing questions that carried
  artists' and venue operators' accounts through the [NYC Artist
  Coalition](../projects/nyc-artist-coalition-2017.md);
- neighborhood encounters, survey infrastructure, building work, and public
  documentation connected in [KC Town Hall](../projects/kc-town-hall.md); and
- project fragments, institutional records, and protected archives translated
  into bounded, inspectable records through [Source-Backed Team
  Memory](../methods/source-backed-team-memory.md).

In implementation and product-operations work, this becomes requirements
discovery, cross-functional translation, workflow design, facilitation, public
communication, risk clarification, and artifacts different actors can use
without pretending they hold the same role.

Translation is not representation without consent, advocacy without collective
credit, or institutional authority Jamie did not hold. A usable interface or
well-attended event does not prove that a public body adopted the input or that
all participants agreed.
