# Implementation, Adoption, and Handoff

Jamie works beyond artifact production: he helps emerging structures enter
use, become maintainable, and move into other people's hands. The record is
strongest when each stage is named separately.

## Evidence states

| State | Minimum supported meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Designed | Requirements, workflows, interfaces, plans, or operating materials exist. |
| Launched | The structure reached a real public or team use context. |
| Used | People demonstrably acted through it. |
| Adopted | A team incorporated it into recurring practice. |
| Handed off | Ownership, documentation, or operating custody transferred. |
| Sustained | The structure remained useful after Jamie's direct work changed or concluded. |

One state does not prove the next. Artifact existence is not adoption, launch
is not durable transfer, and later continuity is not proof that Jamie authored
every subsequent decision.

## Cross-project reading

- Harry J. Epstein connects inherited operating knowledge with a maintained
  commerce and content system over a long stewardship period.
- NYC Artist Coalition shows public identity and campaign infrastructure used
  by collaborators without assigning every later action to Jamie.
- KC Town Hall separates Phase One delivery, later stewardship transition, and
  subsequent municipal actions.
- Sunday Dinner preserves evidence of recurring participation while keeping
  person-level attendance and transfer claims bounded.
- Fair Rent NYC preserves authored plans and implementation work without
  turning a proposal into wholesale adoption.

## Boundary

This capability is a cross-project synthesis, not a universal project history.
Every use must retain the project-specific source, credit allocation, time
period, and transition boundary.
