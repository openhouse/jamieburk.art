# Implementation, Adoption, and Handoff

Jamie works beyond artifact production: he helps emerging structures enter use,
become maintainable, and move into other people's hands. The evidence is
strongest when each stage is named separately.

## Evidence states

- **Designed:** requirements, workflows, interfaces, plans, or operating
  materials exist.
- **Launched:** the system reached a real public or team use context.
- **Used:** people demonstrably acted through it.
- **Adopted:** a team incorporated it into recurring practice.
- **Handed off:** ownership, documentation, or operating custody transferred.
- **Sustained:** the structure remained useful after Jamie's direct work
  changed or concluded.

One state does not prove the next.

## Cross-project evidence

- [Harry J. Epstein](../projects/harry-j-epstein.md) connects legacy operating
  knowledge to a usable e-commerce and content system over a long stewardship
  period.
- [NYC Artist Coalition](../organizations/nyc-artist-coalition.md) shows a
  public identity and campaign infrastructure that collaborators continued to
  use, without assigning every later action to Jamie.
- [KC Town Hall](../projects/kc-town-hall.md) distinguishes completed Phase One
  delivery, later project transition, and subsequent municipal actions.
- [Sunday Dinner and 196](../projects/sunday-dinner.md) preserves recurring
  participation infrastructure while keeping attendance and transfer claims
  bounded.
- [FairRentNYC](../projects/fair-rent-nyc.md) separates an authored operating
  plan and implementation work from unproven completion or wholesale adoption.

## What this page does not claim

Artifact existence is not adoption. A public launch is not durable transfer.
Later continuity is not proof that Jamie authored every subsequent decision.
Each public use should retain the project-specific evidence, collective-credit,
and transition boundary.
