# Participatory public systems

Participatory public systems are the practical bridge among listening,
facilitation, information design, software, events, and public institutions.
They help people recognize a shared condition, enter a process, contribute
knowledge, understand the next step, and see whether follow-through occurred.

## Typical artifacts

- agendas, ballots, surveys, and listening-session maps;
- handbills, call scripts, QR pathways, and public explainers;
- websites, forms, directories, calendars, and open-data interfaces;
- run-of-show documents, testimony plans, action trackers, and decision logs;
- onboarding, consent, follow-up, and handoff systems.

## What the capability makes usable

- community knowledge that institutions can hear without flattening it;
- public information that residents can act on;
- recurring events that accumulate memory rather than resetting each time;
- implementation work that remains legible after the original organizer leaves;
- disagreement and uncertainty that can be revisited rather than hidden.

## Evidence map

The capability is visible across WOWList, Sunday Dinner, CallNYC, NYC Artist
Coalition, KC Town Hall, neighborhood service, and Source-Backed Team Memory.
Each project retains its own credit and evidence boundaries.

## Boundary

Participation is not measured by attendance alone. A form or event does not
prove representation, consent, influence, adoption, or impact. The system must
record who was invited, what could be contributed, how decisions were made,
what remained unresolved, and who held authority to act.
