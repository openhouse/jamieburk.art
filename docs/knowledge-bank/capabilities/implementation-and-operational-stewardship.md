# Implementation and operational stewardship

Implementation is the work of making a chosen form survive contact with
reality. Operational stewardship extends that work through correction,
maintenance, documentation, access boundaries, and transfer.

## Operating pattern

1. Establish the real users, operators, authority, constraints, and inherited
   system before fixing the solution.
2. Make the smallest complete working path, including failure states and the
   human work around it.
3. Observe use through support, data, field conditions, and collaborator
   feedback; distinguish launch from adoption and activity from impact.
4. Keep ownership, renewals, credentials, source provenance, decisions, and
   unresolved risks legible.
5. Prepare another person to continue, revise, or retire the system.

## Evidence map

- [Harry J. Epstein Company](../projects/harry-j-epstein.md): bounded evidence
  of e-commerce and operating-system implementation shaped by inherited store
  knowledge, with component corroboration still open.
- [KC Spaces Fund](../projects/kc-spaces-fund-orientation.md): behind-the-scenes
  site, theme, widget, and platform continuity supporting a campaign led by
  publicly named organizers.
- [CallNYC](../projects/callnyc.md): independent implementation of a public-data
  interpretation layer, with official affiliation explicitly disallowed.
- [Knowledge Wiki](../indexes/living-archive.md): schemas, graph compilation,
  source encounters, evals, projection controls, and human review gates.

## Boundary

Implementation does not imply exclusive authorship, product ownership,
organizational authority, adoption, or outcome. Stewardship does not require
Jamie to remain the permanent operator, and a maintained artifact is not proof
that every surrounding workflow was documented or transferred.
