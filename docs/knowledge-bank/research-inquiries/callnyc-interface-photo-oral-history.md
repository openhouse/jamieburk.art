# Recover the context of a CallNYC interface-in-use photograph

## What can be seen

A held photograph in the private editor field appears to show a hand holding a
phone while a CallNYC issue-route interface is visible on the screen.

## What the image does not establish

The frame does not establish the photographer; the represented person's
identity; the exact date, place, or event; who else was present; who was being
shown the interface; whether the moment represents a demonstration, test, or
ordinary use; stakeholder adoption or endorsement; a project outcome; consent;
or publication permission.

## Oral-history prompts

- Who made the photograph, and whose hand is shown?
- Where and when did this happen?
- What was happening immediately before and after this moment?
- Who was the interface being shown to, if anyone, and what question were you
  trying to answer?
- What did this moment teach you about CallNYC, civic interfaces, or how people
  encountered the project?
- May this exact photograph be used on the portfolio, on which route, with what
  crop, caption, and credit?

## Publication rule

The photograph remains in the private editor field. An oral history may create
new research leads, but it cannot by itself clear rights, represented-person
consent, public safety, exact credit, crop, caption, editorial use, production,
deployment, or indexing.
