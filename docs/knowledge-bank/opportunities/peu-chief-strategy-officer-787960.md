# NYC PEU - Chief Strategy Officer 787960

## Answer first

Yes: this role is also on the ordinary NYC Jobs site. The official listing is
[Chief Strategy Officer, Job ID 787960](https://cityjobs.nyc.gov/job/chief-strategy-officer-in-manhattan-jid-45904).
Its Apply control opens the City's SmartRecruiters workflow. That is the
canonical formal application route.

Civic Match adds a second, agency-specific route:
[PEU Chief Strategy Officer Interest Form](https://forms.gle/r3gkBwEUQKD5LsnA6).
The form appears to be City-created and asks candidates to show interest, but
it does not say whether it replaces or supplements the City Jobs application.
The safe preparation plan is to preserve both routes, ask that question if
needed, and never record either as submitted without a confirmation.

## Why this is unusually relevant

The role is not simply senior strategy. It asks someone to build the learning
and operating conditions around person-to-person public outreach: help field
teams set goals, make result capture useful, synthesize what staff know with
what the data shows, and improve the route from contact to City service.

Jamie's strongest evidence constellation is:

- Tired of Tires for a recurring resident-facing service joining outreach,
  intake, field delivery, City recycling, reporting, and neighborhood
  listening;
- NYC Artist Coalition and Let NYC Dance for coalition facilitation,
  public-information systems, government interfaces, and collective action;
- CallNYC for translating constituent-services data into resident-facing next
  steps; and
- Technical Operations for turning ambiguous cross-functional work into
  decisions, documentation, implementation, and durable handoffs.

Those parallels support a serious application. They do not answer the
supervision requirement.

## PEU interest-form preparation

The inspected form asks for contact information, a resume or online-resume
URL, and three required answers of no more than 200 words:

1. Why are you a good fit for this role?
2. Briefly describe a project in which you used data to shape a program's
   goals or strategy.
3. Give the facts and figures of your management experience: direct and
   indirect reports, whether you managed managers, years of management
   experience, and other management statistics.

The third question is a hard evidence gate. A polished answer cannot substitute
for a fact-checked direct-supervision chronology. Collaborator coordination,
vendor management, coalition facilitation, and community stewardship must not
be relabeled as employee supervision.
