## Answer first

The surviving WOW List Facebook Page contains 53 distinct post records dated
from April 2015 through March 2018. Two earlier terminal traversals each
rendered 54 records because one May 2016 community-philosophy post appeared in
both Featured and chronology. A fresh July 15 traversal recovered 53 unique
message records from top to bottom and 53 from bottom to top, with exact
message-level agreement across all 53.

Every retained post has a public-safe disposition in the ledger. The complete
record is not published as raw prose. Instead, it is represented through a
stable ID, timeline slot, primary theme, and concise route label.

This is a census of the surviving Page surface, not a native Meta export. It
cannot account for deleted, hidden, or otherwise unavailable historical posts.

## Jamie's role

Facebook's authenticated Page-management interface displayed `Published by
Jamie Burkart` on all 54 rendered records in each of the two complete earlier
traversals. After resolving the Featured/chronology duplicate, that evidence
supports a bounded conclusion:

> Jamie operated WOW List's Facebook publishing surface across the complete
> surviving 2015-2018 Page record.

That conclusion is narrower than saying Jamie personally authored every post
or was the sole lifetime manager of every project social account. Shared
sources, quotations, events, campaigns, images, and participant work retain
their original authorship.

WOW List was Jamie Burkart and Richard Album's project. Its public life also
depended on members, organizers, artists, venues, and local scene maintainers.

Jamie remembers managing the project's social presence. That broader memory is
preserved as an attributed research lead. It remains held from public use until
a native Meta role/export record or collaborator corroboration establishes the
administrator chronology more fully.

## Participation and stakeholder patterns

The record contains public participation by local-scene maintainers and members
who promoted WOW List, updated local calendars, added event records, made a
tutorial, and articulated community-first project values.

One member statement describes WOW List as a community-first alternative to
ownership-heavy platforms and recalls a member hangout in which Jamie discussed
the design. The statement supports participant experience and project values.
It is not a representative user survey or proof of platform-wide adoption.

The Page also addressed or routed through several stakeholder classes:

- local organizers and calendar maintainers;
- artists, performers, and touring projects;
- DIY and all-ages venues facing closure, rent pressure, fire recovery, or code
  enforcement;
- cultural organizations, funders, and public agencies;
- civic campaigns and distributed public gatherings;
- publishers and documentarians covering cultural-space conditions.

Addressing, tagging, linking, or amplifying a stakeholder does not establish
that the person or organization saw, endorsed, or acted on the post. The pass
did not recover a defensible count of stakeholder-group reactions.

## Selected source readings

### Community-first design

The [May 23, 2016 community-philosophy
post](https://www.facebook.com/wowlist/posts/515811585292320) republishes a
member account of a WOW List online hangout. It describes infrastructure meant
to reduce ownership friction and make it easier for people to gather. The
member retains authorship of that account.

### Civic calendar reuse

The [January 13, 2017 Women's March calendar
post](https://www.facebook.com/wowlist/posts/616983925175085) routes readers to
a distributed WOW List calendar for marches. It demonstrates reuse of the
calendar form for civic gathering. It does not establish that WOW List
organized the marches or generated attendance.

### Cross-project civic bridge

The [July 3, 2017 Let NYC Dance
post](https://www.facebook.com/wowlist/posts/702379893302154) frames Cabaret Law
repeal as support for community spaces and routes readers into NYC Artist
Coalition action and reporting. Facebook's manager view attributes publication
of the WOW List post to Jamie. The shared campaign material remains collective
NYC Artist Coalition work, and the post does not establish causality for the
law's repeal.

### Cultural-space policy and funding

Patricia Calhoun's [Denver Westword
report](https://www.westword.com/arts-culture/city-partners-with-meow-wolf-on-20-000-denver-diy-spaces-fund-8782025/)
documents a Denver Arts & Venues and Meow Wolf funding arrangement for local
DIY spaces following closures and heightened safety scrutiny. WOW List routed
the article as cultural-space support context. It is not press coverage of WOW
List and does not establish that Jamie or WOW List organized the fund.

### Venue affordability and continuity

Matthew Singer's [Willamette Week
report](https://www.wweek.com/bars/2016/07/01/the-know-is-closing/) documents a
Portland venue's planned closure following a substantial rent increase and its
intention to relocate. WOW List routed the article as cultural-space
sustainability context. It does not establish WOW List involvement in the
venue or impact from sharing the article.
