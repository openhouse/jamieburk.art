# Participation Infrastructure Lineage, 2012-2026

This is a lineage of recurring methods and documented project relationships,
not a claim that one project single-handedly caused the next. It makes visible
how gathering, routing, public identity, source association, and handoff recur
across different projects and collaborators.

## Documented Sequence

| Period | Surface | What the record supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2012-2021 | Sunday Dinner | A protected workbook supports the bounded public phrase "300+ gatherings" and a long-running participation practice. Its marks are not automatically physical attendance. |
| 2017 snapshot | WOW List and Popular Vote | Aggregate database relationships show that Popular Vote, Sunday Dinner, NYC Artist Coalition, and WOW a Day could coexist as routing tags. Tag overlap is not authorship, attendance, conversion, or causality. |
| January-March 2017 | Call Script | The public Call Script Page linked to popular.vote and routed readers to an early NYC Artist Coalition meeting whose agenda joined safety, town-hall strategy, survey design, and member-led work. |
| 2017-2021 | NYC Artist Coalition | The recovered event sequence documents a recurring collective participation system across cultural spaces, practical support, public hearings, campaigns, and relief work. |
| 2026 | Source-backed team memory and Knowledge Wiki | The current method turns fragments into governed sources, claims, boundaries, inquiries, and selective public projections. It is a later operating practice, not evidence that the earlier projects anticipated this exact architecture. |

The source-centered [WOW List, Sunday Dinner, and Call Script pass](../research/wowlist-sunday-dinner-callscript-archival-production-2012-2017.md)
provides the aggregate and public-source basis for the middle of this sequence.
The [NYC Artist Coalition event chronology](nycartc-events-and-venues-2017-2021.md)
provides the collective event record.

## Recurring Operations

Across the sequence, the evidence supports recurring operational concerns:

1. create a recognizable public identity;
2. make occasions and opportunities findable;
3. give people a route into a gathering or action;
4. connect a concern to practical context and next steps;
5. preserve enough structured memory to return, revise, and hand work onward.

These are cross-project resonances. The Wiki does not infer that the same
people, governance, technology, or decision rights carried across every
project.

## Attribution Boundary

Jamie describes bringing lessons from WOW List and event organizing into the
birth and development of NYC Artist Coalition. Current records support a
functional bridge among the project surfaces. They do not yet allocate the
complete design, publishing, facilitation, or founding work among Jamie,
Richard, early coalition collaborators, or other participants. A future
person-level account requires collaborator corroboration and artifact-level
provenance.
