# Jamie Burkart practice timeline, 2004-present

This chronology traces recurring questions and methods. It is not a claim of
continuous employment, linear progress, sole authorship, or equal documentation
across every period.

## 2004-2006: Social information spaces and embodied media theory

Recovered UCSC evaluations describe Jamie building a program that used online
dictionary sources, analyzing social networks across physical and online
space, prototyping Flickr-based structural-similarity and image-retrieval
ideas, and co-designing an interactive installation for navigating a projected
image space by walking through doorways.

The same record describes media-archaeological presentations and an installation
realized in New York as a performative response to theories of time and art as
attention. The exact installation title remains an
[open inquiry](../research-inquiries/ucsc-installation-title.md).

## 2003-2009: Open House, Shop Shows, waterways, and public encounter

Public reporting and later institutional records describe experiments in
communal living, open participation, shared documentation, and waterways as
social infrastructure. A 2007 newspaper report documents Jamie, Libby Hendon,
and Laura Mattingly more than 1,000 miles into a reclaimed-material raft
journey from Kansas City into Louisiana. The report does not establish the
final endpoint.

See [Waterways and participatory art](../projects/waterways-and-participatory-art.md).

## 2009-2011: Legacy commerce and networked installation

At Harry J. Epstein Company, Jamie's documented portfolio practice centered on
translating a specialized paper-and-phone business into e-commerce, analytics,
publishing, and maintainable web operations while preserving the store's
distinctive voice. Component-level and outcome claims remain separately
bounded.

NTER CHNG, co-created with Drew Bolton and Garrett Fuselier, treated text
messaging, software, and gallery architecture as a live public conversation.
Public records place the work in Kansas City in 2010 and in America: Now and
Here's Kansas City presentation in 2011.

## 2012-2016: Hosting systems, community software, and civic data

Sunday Dinner developed as a recurring participation and hospitality practice.
WOWList translated organizer behavior into followable keyword communities,
event-distribution workflows, and city-specific ecosystems. CallNYC translated
CouncilStat constituent-services records into resident-facing issue pathways.

## 2017-2020: Coalition infrastructure and neighborhood implementation

NYC Artist Coalition linked recurring cultural-space meetings to campaign
websites, public events, testimony, call scripts, and institutional dialogue.
KC Town Hall linked adaptive-reuse planning, construction coordination,
resident surveys, neighborhood service, and public-benefit documentation.
KC Spaces Fund used a coherent cross-channel identity and web infrastructure
to support a collaborator-led mutual-aid campaign.

## 2020-present: Continuity, source-backed memory, and current role fit

The 196 Artists Residency extended recurring hosting into a longer support and
onboarding structure. Commercial Rent Stabilization work in 2026 combines
campaign history, source maps, legal and legislative provenance, public-data
framing, running minutes, and stakeholder follow-through. Source-Backed Team
Memory and the Knowledge Wiki make that operating-memory method explicit and
testable.

## Throughline

Across the chronology, the recurrent movement is from observation to model,
prototype, social use, learning, revision, and handoff. The artistic, civic,
technical, and operational practices are related, but no single later role
retroactively determines what every earlier project meant.
