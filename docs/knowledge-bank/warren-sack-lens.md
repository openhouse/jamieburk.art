# Prof Warren Sack Lens

## Purpose

This lens asks whether the portfolio makes Jamie's recursive systems
intelligence visible: finding structure in social and technical material,
testing an interpretation through a prototype, exposing it through an
interface, and building forms collaborators can inspect and extend.

## Source position

Jamie Burkart and Codex derived this contemporary editorial lens from the
historical sources below. It is not a quotation, testimonial, or evaluation
written, approved, or currently endorsed by Warren Sack.

An unofficial protected UCSC narrative-evaluation copy contains Warren Sack's
2004 and 2006 evaluations. It supports bounded educational claims about
source-backed analysis, recursive social inference, interactive prototyping,
classroom demonstration, and collective installation design. It is not an
official transcript, and its full text remains protected.

## Lens

The evaluations preserve a repeated chain:

1. **Observe material closely.** Jamie examined words, group memberships,
   images, physical social networks, and online social networks.
2. **Infer latent structure recursively.** A group's form could be explored
   through other groups its participants also inhabited.
3. **Build a prototype.** The inference became a similar-image search
   experiment combining social and image analysis.
4. **Create an interface and demonstrate it.** The prototype became an
   interactive Max/MSP Jitter interface shown in class.
5. **Design collective architecture.** A group project translated photo
   browsing into a physical installation and documented multiple designs
   across models, films, sketches, diagrams, prose, and HTML.
6. **Learn from situated use.** A prototype, interface, process, or social
   situation remains open to what people do with it; observed use can change
   the next version of the structure.
7. **Support interpretation with sources.** Earlier work joined an original
   physical/digital social-network argument to primary and secondary evidence.

This does not establish deployment, adoption, or professional impact. A
demonstration is not adoption; a prototype is not a production service; a group
design is not individual authorship.

## Public composition

The About page should name the present method, not the protected classroom
history: structure grows from material and relationships; Jamie looks for
latent patterns, makes them inspectable through prototypes and shared
artifacts, and builds interfaces people can use to understand and change a
system together. It should also close the loop: Jamie learns from what happens
in use and revises without erasing the relationships that produced the work.

CallNYC, WOW List, Source-Backed Team Memory, and the portfolio's knowledge
lifecycle can then demonstrate the continuity without turning the About page
into a project inventory.

The Source-Backed Team Memory lab now supplies one bounded correction episode.
An early portfolio pass treated a participant photograph's approximate 2:10
p.m. timestamp as an event time. Review recovered a direct Civic Hall
announcement stating 1-3 p.m.; the public claim changed, the weaker inference
remained in the correction trail, and the photograph stayed held pending rights
and consent review. This is evidence of the method operating inside this
portfolio, not external client delivery or impact.

The NYC Artist Coalition sequence supplies the complementary situated-use
episode. A public event surface gathered issues before a DCLA meeting; the
surviving discussion then supported a shared letter draft and group-naming
poll; the record routed people into a February follow-up meeting and later
recurring coalition work. Jamie helped shape those next steps around what
participants contributed. This is evidence of an evolving participation
system, not sole authorship, attendance, a complete founding history, or proof
that one event caused the coalition or any policy outcome.
