# WOWList Full-Population Archival Production

Run date: 2026-07-12

## Answer First

The live [@wowlist profile](https://x.com/wowlist) displays **38 posts**. A
fine-grained authenticated pass through the Posts and Replies timelines
recovered **all 38 unique records**:

- 16 account-authored standalone posts;
- 6 account-authored replies;
- 16 reposts from 13 other public accounts.

The [record-level census](wowlist-post-census-2026-07-12.csv) closes exactly
against the current profile control with no unresolved slots. This is 100%
recovery of the population displayed in July 2026. It does not prove that no
older post was deleted before capture.

## Population Definition And Method

The population is the 38-post count displayed by X on July 12, 2026. A record
is one unique primary status ID rendered in the account's Posts or Replies
timeline.

1. Read the profile control total and February 2014 join date.
2. Harvested Posts and Replies separately in 650-pixel increments so X's
   virtualized interface could not skip large regions.
3. Deduplicated every rendered item by primary status ID.
4. Reconciled the Posts population of 37 with one additional authored reply
   recovered from Replies, producing a union of 38.
5. Classified every record by account, publication type, primary theme,
   mentions, and hashtags without reproducing full post text.
6. Compared the census with earlier oEmbed recovery and local WOWList archive
   context.

## What The Complete Population Shows

### Product lineage

The [first recovered WOWList-authored post](https://x.com/wowlist/status/433671630837919744)
names Richard and Jamie and says the project was based on the calendars they
made at Sunday Dinner. A later [account reply](https://x.com/wowlist/status/771457416298921985)
explains that NYCDIY ran on WOWList and describes WOWList as a DIY
community-calendar project from the Sunday Dinner potluck.

This is unusually direct public evidence for the conceptual lineage: recurring
community calendar-making became a reusable technical platform, then supported
distinct local calendar identities.

### Product support and onboarding

All six account-authored replies function as product support, onboarding, or
calendar-identity guidance. The account explained:

- how followed local calendars populated a home feed and a planned
  local/everywhere mode;
- where a person could find their WOW Lists on their profile;
- how to submit an event and add it to multiple lists;
- how to join NYCDIY, add shows, and receive a weekly email;
- how NYCDIY, WOWList, and Sunday Dinner related to one another.

Representative records include the [feed-scope reply](https://x.com/wowlist/status/591664757473673216),
[profile-navigation reply](https://x.com/wowlist/status/591666366215811073),
[multi-list submission reply](https://x.com/wowlist/status/591668857670148096),
and [NYCDIY joining reply](https://x.com/wowlist/status/771455571501416448).

This makes the operational work concrete: the public identity was not only a
broadcast channel. It helped people understand and use the product.

### Event distribution and scene connection

Five authored records directly distribute events, conferences, festivals, or
tours. Others connect scene knowledge across Kansas City, Detroit, Chicago,
New York, multiple western states, and nationwide organizing routes. The
account also credited people adding events, circulated a community-made product
tutorial, and linked writing about documenting and connecting DIY scenes.

The social record is a small public sample, not the basis for WOWList's larger
user, event, or city-scale claims. Those remain grounded in the platform archive.

### Civic mobilization and care

Five authored records and five reposts carry civic-mobilization or care themes.
The recovered record includes a Black Lives Matter demonstration, nationwide
anti-Trump marches, popular-vote organizing, Standing Rock support, Ghost Ship
mutual aid and vigils, a call to mayors to help DIY spaces thrive safely, and a
Meow Wolf DIY-space fund.

This supports a bounded continuity claim: the account moved from event
distribution into public gathering, mutual aid, mourning, and support for the
conditions that allow cultural spaces to survive. Amplification does not make
WOWList or Jamie the organizer of every linked effort.

## Claim Boundaries

- Complete means all 38 records represented by the current profile control were
  recovered. It does not establish that no older post was deleted.
- The shared account does not identify which teammate authored each post.
- Jamie is credited with establishing the account and co-building the product,
  not with personally writing every record.
- The social census does not measure all WOWList users, events, cities, support
  interactions, impressions, or outcomes.
- Reposting a resource does not establish authorship, leadership, attendance,
  or causality for the underlying effort.
