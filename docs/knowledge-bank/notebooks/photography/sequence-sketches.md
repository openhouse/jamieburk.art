# Photography sequence sketches

No image has been assigned to these sketches. They are questions for the field,
not narratives the photographs must illustrate. Preserve versions that fail or
contradict one another; the comparison may be more useful than a premature winner.

## Sketch A: Release, passage, salt

1. **Release yourself:** thresholds, preparation, invitation, uncertainty, and choice.
2. **Onto the water:** shared labor, apparatus, duration, weather, repair, and companionship.
3. **Until it tastes of salt:** confluence, transformation, departure, handoff, and afterlife.

This structure originates in the waterways work but may illuminate other
projects. It should not flatten every project into the river metaphor.

## Sketch B: Threshold, container, agency, afterlife

Begin with what permits entry. Reveal what sustains the situation. Let
participants alter the frame. End where the work continues beyond Jamie's
direct role.

Test whether the sequence makes the stranger feel invited without suggesting
that invitation erases risk, conflict, refusal, or unequal access.

## Sketch C: Care in the apparatus

Move between public moments and the operational work around them: setup and
gathering, repair and use, form and response, listening and decision, cleanup
and continuation.

Test whether this arrangement makes operational production visible without
reducing love, friendship, or collective agency to organizational utility.

## Revision questions

- Which lives and voices are centered, and which disappear between frames?
- Does Jamie appear as a participant and steward without becoming the hero of
  every collective scene?
- What must be understood through caption, source, or protected context rather
  than inferred from an image?
- Where does the sequence need an interruption, silence, or withheld image?
- Does the ending close the project too neatly, or leave its afterlife open?
