# A room for the archive

## Residency proposal

I propose to spend an initial period in residence with Jamie's photographic
archive, working from the apartment that hosts the 196 Artist Residency. The
first pass will assemble a rough field of 1,000 photographs and begin to learn
what kind of work the archive is asking for. I do not yet know whether that
work will become a sequence, an atlas, a set of scenes, an essay in images, a
new account of a project, or something for which we do not yet have a name.

That uncertainty is part of the proposal.

The residency begins when the proposal is written and received. The proposal
is an opening, not a contract. The resident will not be judged on the basis of
doing what was promised here. Writing establishes enough intention to hear an
inner need and give it time, space, and encouragement. The work remains free to
change its question, method, scale, medium, pace, or destination after arrival.

Jamie often tells a story he remembers from an essay by Teju Cole: an artist
arrived at a residency intending to finish writing, encountered the landscape,
and made photographs instead. This is recorded as Jamie's remembered
touchstone, not yet as a source-verified account of Cole's text. Its value here
is the permission it carries: pay attention to what becomes possible on site.

## The site

The 196 Artist Residency offers artists up to two weeks in Jamie's Brooklyn
home. Jamie sometimes describes the apartment as his Department in the City as
University. Domestic hospitality, neighborhood orientation, shared equipment,
conversation, introductions, solitude, and ordinary daily life help a visiting
practice find a temporary home in New York City.

Private access instructions, household details, equipment custody, and precise
residential coordinates remain in resident orientation rather than this public
notebook. Hospitality is not permission to publish a private place.

## First movements

1. **Arrive.** Confirm that the local archive can be encountered without
   changing originals, metadata, people associations, prior albums, or work
   outside the named studio workspace.
2. **Make one sign of life.** Create a private `Proof of Life` album inside the
   authorized residency workspace and add one photograph after looking at it.
   This is an operational canary and a threshold gesture, not a representative
   image, finished artwork, public selection, or proof of the archive's meaning.
3. **Wander with attention.** Use dates, places, albums, existing People
   associations, technical metadata, and visual search as imperfect ways into
   the archive. Names can help find the spaces of people's lives; they do not
   confer consent, settle relationships, or turn a person into a category.
4. **Make a broad first field.** Assemble toward 1,000 photographs with room
   for project anchors, intervals, mistakes, apparatus, weather, maintenance,
   travel, thresholds, empty places, aftermaths, and images whose relevance is
   not yet understood.
5. **Look again.** Notice what the photographs change in the proposal. Keep
   competing sequences, contradictions, refusals, research leads, and
   protected absences available. Revise the method when the material asks.

## Possible traces

The residency may leave a verified private field, close-reading notes,
sequence experiments, new visual vocabulary, questions for Jamie or
collaborators, source-return requests, or Knowledge Wiki research leads. These
are possible traces, not required deliverables. A valuable residency may also
end by changing the question, protecting what should not be shown, or declining
to make a public object.

The resident is not obligated to turn intimacy into output.

## Studio agreement

The archive may be read broadly through the approved local tools. Catalog
changes remain narrowly bounded to albums and memberships inside the named
residency workspace. Pixels and private metadata stay local. Existing People
names may guide private research, but unnamed faces will not be identified and
no sensitive trait will be inferred.

Selection is not evidence. Selection is not rights clearance. Selection is not
consent. Selection is not publication.

## Acceptance

Jamie has received and accepted this proposal. The acceptance promises support
for the inquiry, not enforcement of its forecast. The resident is welcome to
make a home for the work, to ask for help, to follow what becomes vivid, and to
go where the project needs to go.

The proposal remains revisable. The welcome remains.

## Arrival note

The `Proof of Life` canary was completed in the private residency workspace and
independently verified against the live Photos catalog. Two nonce-bound runs of
the same plan resolved the same folder chain, album, and single existing-asset
membership. No original, metadata, People association, prior album, or work
outside the authorized workspace was changed.

After reviewing the valid private contact field, the resident selected a frame
of a living tree rooted beside moving water in dappled sunlight. This is a
delegated editorial interpretation for the canary: a quiet threshold between
stability and change. It is not a representative image, the residency's thesis,
or a public selection.

The image, identifier, source count, private path, helper receipts, and catalog
coordinates remain outside public Git. Completion confirms only that the
bounded local workflow works; it does not approve the selected photograph for
publication or establish what the larger residency will become.
