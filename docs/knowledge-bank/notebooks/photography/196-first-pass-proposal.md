# 196 Artists Residency proposal: The First Pass

- **Artist:** Jamie Burkart
- **Place:** At home in Brooklyn, near Fort Greene Park
- **Proposed duration:** Up to two weeks
- **State:** Accepted by Jamie Burkart on 2026-07-22 as an opening intention,
  not a promised output

## Proposal

I propose to make a first sustained encounter with a rough field of
approximately 1,000 photographs from my lifetime archive.

I am not proposing to summarize the archive. I am asking for time and space to
be with it: to notice repetitions, interruptions, people, thresholds, forms of
labor, accidental compositions, changes of light, and pictures that resist the
account I currently give of my life and work.

I want to find out what becomes possible when I stop asking each photograph to
prove something and begin allowing the photographs to alter the questions.

## The invitation I am accepting

At 196, I often say that the artist residency starts when the proposal is
written. The proposal is not a contract. The artist will not be judged by
whether they do what they promised.

Writing establishes an intention: some part of the artist has said that it
needs time, space, and nourishment. Sharing that intention lets another person
receive it and say that the voice matters. The promise is to make room for the
need, not to predict its eventual form.

For this self-directed residency, I am both artist and host. I want to keep
those roles distinct enough that the host does not become an internal manager
demanding proof of productivity from the artist. As host, I receive and accept
this proposal. As artist, I have permission to go where the work needs to go.
Changing course is not a failure of the residency. It may be the clearest sign
that I have paid attention.

## A remembered orientation

I often introduce the residency with a story I remember from one of Teju
Cole's books. In my memory, Cole arrived at an overseas residency intending to
finish a piece of writing, encountered the landscape, and found that his work
there became photography.

The exact essay, book, and page have not been recovered in this source pass.
This memory is recorded as Jamie's residency orientation, not presented as a
verified quotation or exact paraphrase from Cole. Identifying and rereading the
source remains an open librarian inquiry.

What I carry from the story is permission: arrival is information. A proposal
can bring an artist to the threshold, but attention must be free to discover
what happens after the door opens.

## Place and duration

For up to two weeks, the apartment can become a studio, reading room, editing
table, listening chamber, and place to return after walking through Fort Greene
and the city. The archive is already here. The residency makes a temporary
clearing inside ordinary life where looking can take priority.

Up to two weeks is a container, not a deadline. The pass may pause, shorten,
continue in another form, or redirect itself when the material asks for a
different rhythm. Duration does not determine success.

## What I think I need

- Permission to look before explaining.
- Enough images in the room for unexpected relations to become visible.
- Time to live with sequences rather than selecting only immediate favorites.
- Walks, sleep, meals, conversation, and silence as parts of looking.
- A record of changed questions, not only chosen photographs.
- Protection from the demand to make every encounter professionally useful.
- A supportive witness who can receive uncertainty without rushing it toward
  a conclusion.

## An opening practice

I may:

- assemble the rough field without treating it as complete or representative;
- move images into provisional constellations and take them apart again;
- follow a single gesture, color, room, river, face, threshold, or form of
  labor across years;
- make alternate sequences that disagree with one another;
- write beside the images without requiring the writing to explain them;
- notice what the camera repeatedly misses or pushes toward the edge; and
- bring public-safe changes in understanding back to the field notebook.

These are affordances, not requirements. The project may become a photographic
edit, an essay, a book dummy, an installation sketch, a conversation, a map, a
new question, or work in another medium. It may yield no public object during
the residency. Photography is the point of entry, not a rule governing the
exit.

## Questions I am bringing

- What has my camera attended to before I had language for its importance?
- Where does hospitality appear as space, gesture, material preparation, or
  shared time?
- What do the photographs know about transition that my project histories do
  not yet say?
- Which images make a life feel continuous, and which insist on rupture?
- Where is collective agency visible without turning another person into a
  supporting character in my story?
- Which beautiful images should remain private?
- What new city, practice, or relationship opens when I follow the language of
  one photograph closely?

## Freedom and care

Artistic freedom does not erase responsibility. The residency does not clear
rights, consent, credit, privacy, safety, caption, context, or publication
review. It does not require another person's image or story to become public.
The source archive remains unchanged, and the working field remains private.

The public-safe notebook may hold questions, aggregate observations, sequence
ideas, and changes in interpretation. Exact image records and protected
context remain outside public Git. Acceptance of this proposal is not
publication clearance for any photograph or account encountered through it.

## The promise

I am not promising a deliverable. I am promising to protect the conditions for
attention: to return, to notice, to let the material answer back, to care for
the people and histories inside the frame, and to record honestly when the
project changes.

## Acceptance

**Jamie Burkart, host:** I receive and accept this proposal. The voice asking
for this time matters. It may grow, wander, rest, contradict its opening
language, or discover another project. For up to two weeks, it has a home here.

### Host response, 2026-07-22

> Your proposal is accepted. Welcome.

This acceptance opens the residency. It does not turn the proposal into a
contract, determine its medium or outcome, or close any publication gate.

The residency has begun.
