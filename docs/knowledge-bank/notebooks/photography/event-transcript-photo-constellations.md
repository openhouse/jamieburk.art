# Event transcript and photograph constellations

## The archival clue

Jamie reports that when he made an audio recording of a public event, he was
often making stop-motion documentary photographs at the same time. The
transcript and the photographs may therefore be parallel records made from the
same situated act of listening and looking.

His prior workflow adds another research surface. After photographs entered
Apple Photos and an audio recording was transcribed, Jamie often exported an
event field through his `photo-filter` tool and refined it with his
`photo-select` tool. Nested `_keep` directories preserve a tree of prior
selection decisions: greater depth may indicate a more refined historical
edit.

This is a methodological lead supplied by Jamie, not a completeness claim.
Some events may have no surviving recording, photographs, export, select tree,
or reliable date alignment.

## Tool lineage

Jamie's public tool branches provide recoverable context for the workflow:

- [`photo-filter` / `feature/big-album`](https://github.com/openhouse/photo-filter/tree/feature/big-album)
  describes the export surface used to create bounded working fields from
  Apple Photos.
- [`photo-select` / `feature/repair-large-context`](https://github.com/openhouse/photo-select/tree/feature/repair-large-context)
  describes the filesystem-based selection practice whose nested `_keep`
  directories can preserve successive edits.

These repositories explain a method; they do not enumerate every historical
run. A future private archival pass should inventory candidate select trees
across Jamie's MacBook and connected storage, bind each recovered tree to an
exact path checksum and event hypothesis, and report the searched volumes and
date of access. Public Git should receive only redacted aggregate findings.

## Private-first reconciliation

For one governed event at a time:

1. Freeze the event anchor: source transcript, event date and time range,
   venue when public-safe, named speakers, source checksum, and attribution
   confidence.
2. Query the private Apple Photos inventory using the event window, existing
   album context, camera and import relationships, broad place context, and
   existing People associations. Use only names Jamie already assigned; do not
   identify unnamed faces or infer sensitive traits.
3. Search private working storage for related `photo-filter` exports and
   `photo-select` trees. Record the tree shape and `_keep` depth in the private
   research ledger without exposing filenames, paths, faces, or source
   identifiers in public Git.
4. Treat `_keep` depth as a prior-curatorial signal, never as proof of image
   quality, identity, factual meaning, consent, rights, or present suitability.
5. Inspect the photographs themselves. Reconcile visible evidence, event
   timing, existing People associations, transcript speaker order, and prior
   selects. A label or timestamp may retrieve a candidate; it cannot settle the
   interpretation.
6. Build a private event constellation containing the complete candidate
   family, near-duplicates and sequences, prior selects, counterexamples,
   uncertain matches, and explicit holds. Preserve unclassified photographs.
7. Add only membership pointers inside the authorized Apple Photos workspace.
   Do not alter originals, metadata, People associations, favorites,
   pre-existing albums, or prior select trees.
8. Record a public-safe encounter note describing aggregate discoveries,
   limitations, and new research questions. Keep the image-level join private.

## Speaker-to-image annotation

A transcript turn may help locate frames made while a person was speaking.
That temporal association can support questions such as:

- Which frame best preserves the speaker's agency and the room's response?
- What labor, listening, or disagreement remains outside the podium view?
- Does a sequence show how the room changed before, during, and after the
  statement?
- Which named participants are absent from the surviving edit?
- Does the photograph complicate the transcript-derived account?

The private annotation should distinguish:

- existing Apple Photos People association;
- transcript speaker attribution;
- visible but unnamed person;
- uncertain temporal match;
- photographer and camera, when supported;
- prior select depth; and
- rights, consent, dignity, credit, context, and publication state.

No automated match becomes an identity or narrative claim without human
review. No person is represented as endorsing Jamie or the portfolio merely
because they spoke at, attended, or were photographed at an event.

## Two useful directions

The transcript can lead back to a photograph, and the photograph can lead back
to the transcript. A strong frame may invite close reading of the speaker's
full statement, surrounding voices, event chronology, or institutional
context. A transcript may disclose the importance of a frame that an
image-only edit overlooked.

This is a living research loop, not an automatic publishing pipeline.

## Publication boundary

Prior public appearance, a deep `_keep` placement, a known People association,
or a strong relation to testimony does not grant publication permission.
Every proposed use still requires review of the exact source and version,
creator and credit, depicted-person dignity and consent, rights, safety,
supported caption, crop, alt text, destination, duration, and Jamie's final
approval.

The public portfolio remains selective. The private constellation may be large
because its job is to preserve possibility, contradiction, and future
research.
