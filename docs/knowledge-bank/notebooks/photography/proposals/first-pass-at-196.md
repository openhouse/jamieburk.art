# First Pass: A Photo Residency at 196

## Proposal

I propose to become a resident of my own archive, here at 196.

For up to two weeks, I will give sustained time and space to a first working
field of roughly 1,000 photographs drawn from a lifetime archive Jamie
describes as containing more than 600,000 images. I am not proposing to find
the best thousand photographs, finish a portfolio, prove an existing account
of my work, or process the archive at scale. I am proposing to encounter a
field large enough that the photographs can begin speaking back.

The residency begins when the proposal is written. Writing it is a promise to
myself: the voice asking for this time matters. It deserves time and space to
be nourished, to grow, and to surprise me.

## The need

The archive has accompanied many lives and projects, but it has rarely been
given a room of its own. I need time to look without immediately asking an
image to illustrate a claim, win an argument, document an accomplishment, or
earn a place on the public site.

I want to listen for the unique language of the photographs: what they know
about presence, hospitality, ordinary labor, friendship, infrastructure,
thresholds, water, waiting, maintenance, aftermath, and the ways people make a
place together. I also want to notice where my present vocabulary fails them.

## Working question

> What becomes possible when this image is present?

The question is intentionally larger than selection. It leaves room for an
image to orient, interrupt, complicate, withhold, invite, grieve, delight, or
open a path the proposal cannot yet name.

## A provisional way in

I may:

- assemble the private working field and look across it before sorting down;
- make temporary constellations, contact sheets, sequences, and wall-sized
  arrangements outside public Git;
- speak, write, walk, rest, return, and change the scale of attention;
- notice recurring gestures, places, people, materials, weather, and forms of
  care;
- ask what each frame withholds and what another participant might see;
- bring discoveries back to project records, source research, photo-editor
  briefs, or the structure of the Knowledge Wiki;
- follow another medium, method, question, scale, pace, or direction if the
  work asks for it.

These are invitations, not required deliverables. The structure grows from the
material; it is not imposed in advance.

## Permission to depart

This proposal is not a contract. I will not be judged against the work
forecast here. The project may change medium, method, question, scale, pace, or
direction. Departure is evidence of attention, not failure.

The residency may end with a sequence, a set of questions, a changed Wiki, a
new research lead, a room rearranged by looking, a changed question, needed
rest, or no public artifact at all. I retain the right to stop. Success is not
counted in images processed, categories completed, pages published, or claims
confirmed.

## The place

196 Artists Residency is Jamie's home-based hosting practice in Brooklyn, near
Fort Greene Park. Its exact address remains private. The invitation is for up
to two weeks: enough time to arrive, be changed by the place, and begin making
a practice at home in New York City.

For this first pass, Jamie is both resident artist and host. The apartment
offers the same essential gesture Jamie describes offering visiting artists:
someone receives the need expressed in the proposal and says yes, there is
room for this here.

## A remembered permission from Teju Cole

Jamie remembers an essay from one of Teju Cole's books in which Cole arrived
at an overseas residency intending to finish writing, became immersed in the
landscape, and made photography. The available public record confirms that
Cole was a writer in residence in Zurich from June through November 2014 and
that he traveled and photographed Switzerland during that period while also
working on a writing project related to Lagos. Those photographs became part
of the multi-year body of work *Fernweh*.

The exact essay, book, and wording Jamie remembers have not yet been recovered.
This proposal therefore uses the memory as an artistic permission, not as a
verified quotation or a claim that Cole abandoned writing entirely. The open
[citation inquiry](../../../research-inquiries/teju-cole-residency-photography-reference.md)
preserves the distinction.

## Care and public boundaries

No photograph is selected, cleared, captioned, or published by this proposal.
The working cohort, contact sheets, filenames, identifiers, locations, and
image-level notes remain private. Any later public use requires separate
review of authorship, rights, consent, represented-person agency, collective
credit, sensitivity, crop, caption, alt text, context, and named surface.

The archive contains other people's lives. Looking is not ownership, and
discovery is not permission. A photograph may matter deeply and remain
unpublished.

## Acceptance

196 Artists Residency receives and accepts this proposal.

Acceptance means the need named here deserves protected time, attention,
encouragement, and room. It does not bind the work to its forecast. Jamie may
go where the work needs to go.
