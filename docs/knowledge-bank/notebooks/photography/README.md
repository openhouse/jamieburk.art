# Photography working notebook

This is the Knowledge Wiki's public-safe sketchpad for developing a photo layer.
It is a place to notice, arrange, question, remember, revise, and discover. It
does not require a photograph to know in advance what it means.

The first working field aims to bring together 1,000 photographs from Jamie's
larger archive. The field is a rough editorial draft, not a ranked shortlist,
representative sample, complete archive census, or declaration of the best
images. Its purpose is to give close reading enough breadth for unexpected
patterns, scenes, absences, and relationships to emerge.

## What belongs here

- public-safe field charters and version notes;
- provisional visual observations and competing interpretations;
- sequence sketches and editorial questions;
- motifs that become visible across projects, places, and years;
- research leads created by looking closely;
- links to separately governed sources, assets, claims, and project records;
- decisions to keep, defer, protect, revisit, or decline an editorial direction.

## What remains outside public Git

- original or preview image files unless separately approved for this use;
- local library paths, Apple Photos identifiers, and private source coordinates;
- people-tag exports, face embeddings, and unreviewed identifying metadata;
- private rights, consent, relationship, or contact records;
- interpretations that would expose another person's protected circumstances.

Opaque photo IDs may enter a public-safe note only after review. The private
field manifest remains the authority for reconnecting a note to an original.

## Working principles

1. **The field is not the verdict.** Keep outliers and difficult images when
   they may change the reading later.
2. **Observation is not interpretation.** Record what is visibly present apart
   from metadata, memory, inference, and source-backed claims.
3. **Selection is not publication.** Editorial interest, evidence value,
   rights, consent, safety, and public-surface approval are separate decisions.
4. **People remain agents.** Do not use faces as shorthand for community,
   infer identity or interior states from appearance, or make Jamie the default
   center of collective scenes.
5. **The apparatus belongs in the story.** Tools, repair, food, forms, maps,
   weather, travel, setup, cleanup, and maintenance can show how participation
   became possible.
6. **A photograph can open research.** It can create an intake or inquiry, but
   it cannot automatically mature a memory or interpretation into a claim.
7. **Sequences are revisable.** Keep competing arrangements long enough to
   learn what each reveals and excludes.
8. **Protected absence is meaningful.** An image may matter to understanding
   the work and still remain unshown.
