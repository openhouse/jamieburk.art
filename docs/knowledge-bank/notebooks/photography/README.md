# Photography working notebook

This is a public-safe studio surface for learning what photography can make
possible in the Knowledge Wiki and portfolio. It can hold provisional themes,
questions, visual grammars, sequence sketches, counter-readings, and leads for
future research. It is allowed to be playful, contradictory, incomplete, and
revised.

It is not the private photo catalog. Do not place photographs, contact sheets,
private asset identifiers, filenames, source paths, coordinates, protected
identities, or private image-level notes here. The initial 1,000-photo working
cohort remains outside public Git.

## What this notebook is for

- Encounter images before requiring them to illustrate an existing argument.
- Preserve mystery, affection, friction, technical imperfection, and surprise.
- Notice visual patterns across projects without turning proximity into
  causality.
- Separate what is visible from what a viewer feels or infers.
- Let photographs suggest new people, places, events, artifacts, sources, and
  research questions.
- Develop photo-editor briefs and possible sequences without clearing an image
  for publication.
- Record counter-readings, absences, uncertainty, and what the frame withholds.
- Revise the Wiki's structure when the photographic material warrants it.

## Initial field

Jamie plans to assemble roughly 1,000 photographs as a first working field.
The cohort is a purposive rough selection. It is not an audited sample. It is
not a ranking of the best photographs, not a complete account of the archive,
and not a public media set. Inclusion does not establish authorship, identity,
chronology, consent, rights, evidence, factual support, or editorial selection.

No fixed taxonomy governs the first reading. Categories may emerge, overlap,
split, disappear, or contradict one another. A photograph may remain simply
`mystery` or `not understood yet`.

## Working modes

### Field reading

Look across the cohort without sorting it down. Notice repetitions, ruptures,
ordinary labor, thresholds, objects, gestures, weather, waiting, maintenance,
and aftermaths.

### Constellation

Place images from different dates or projects near one another to test a
resonance. A constellation is a question, not proof of relationship or cause.

### Sequence

Explore how one image changes another through adjacency, rhythm, duration, and
return. A sequence may be narrative, spatial, emotional, procedural, or
deliberately unresolved.

### Counter-reading

Ask what another participant, represented person, collaborator, stranger, or
future reader might see differently. People are agents, not scenery around
Jamie's work.

### Stranger test

Ask whether the image offers a stranger orientation, dignity, and room to
enter without overdetermining what they must feel.

## First question

> What becomes possible when this image is present?

This question does not ask whether the photograph is attractive or useful. It
asks what new relation, feeling, question, contradiction, scale, or form of
agency the image introduces.

## Notebook lifecycle

1. **Encounter:** Record a public-safe visible observation or open question.
2. **Resonate:** Note an interpretation, association, or possible relation as
   provisional.
3. **Counter-read:** Preserve another plausible reading and what is unknown.
4. **Research:** Return project, date, identity, place, authorship, and context
   questions to governed source research.
5. **Brief:** If useful, develop a bounded photo-editor brief for a named
   editorial need.
6. **Review:** Handle rights, consent, credit, sensitivity, caption, crop, alt
   text, and exact surface as separate human decisions.
7. **Project or hold:** Jamie may approve a specific derivative for a specific
   surface, keep it in research, defer it, or withdraw it.

Notebook language cannot automatically become a claim, source, asset record,
caption, portfolio selection, or publication decision. A compelling reading
may remain unused. Jamie retains editorial approval, and rights holders,
represented people, collaborators, or other reviewers may hold additional
authority for a particular image or use.
