# Visual proof protocol

## Purpose

Visuals should let a reader inspect the actual product, artifact, place,
workflow, or working situation. Decoration does not satisfy this criterion.

## Rights are not evidence

An image can be evidentially useful without being publishable. Authorship,
ownership, license, and display permission require their own records.

## Consent is contextual

Public appearance in an old event photograph does not automatically authorize
reuse in a present-day employment portfolio. Resolve depicted identities,
reasonable expectations, vulnerability, and project context. Prefer redacted
artifacts or interface captures when participant consent is unresolved.

## Caption contract

Every approved visual needs:

- project, date or bounded period, and location when known;
- what is visibly established;
- Jamie's role, if independently supported;
- collaborator and institutional credit;
- what the image does not establish;
- source lineage and image-level authorship;
- rights and consent disposition.

Do not infer outcome, attendance, endorsement, identity, authorship, or causal
credit from appearance alone.

## Display decision

Evidence review and display approval are separate decisions. A visual proof
closes this gap only when rights are cleared, consent is cleared or not
applicable, the caption is approved, and an exact public destination is named.

## Initial target

Clear three high-value visuals across three distinct domains: private-sector
implementation, civic technology or government-facing delivery, and community
or coalition operations. One strong annotated artifact can be more useful than
a large gallery.
