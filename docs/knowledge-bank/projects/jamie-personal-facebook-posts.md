# Jamie personal Facebook posts: archival production

Date: July 15, 2026

Status: public-safe research dossier. The raw record-level corpus remains
outside the public repository.

## Research question

What can the complete population returned by Jamie Burkart's authenticated
personal Facebook **Manage Posts > Posted by: You** surface add to the
knowledge bank without turning private life, mutable social counters, or
platform associations into public professional claims?

## Population and denominator

The July 13 cursor traversal reached `has_next_page: false` after 621 pages. It
returned 3,728 nodes that deduplicated to 1,243 owner-filtered records, with
zero missing dates and zero records lacking Jamie in the returned owner data.
The returned chronology runs from December 19, 2006 through June 12, 2022.

On July 15, an authenticated browser review switched from a project Page to
Jamie's personal profile, opened **Manage posts**, applied the **Posted by:
You** filter, and confirmed that the filtered surface began in June 2022. The
six selected project posts had also been reopened individually and displayed
the public audience state.

Here, **100 percent** means every unique record returned by the July 13
owner-filtered cursor connection received a disposition. It does not mean a
native Meta export, deletion history, or proof that no hidden, removed,
migrated, or otherwise unavailable post ever existed.

## Public-safety treatment

Audience labels were not exposed for 973 of the 1,243 crawl records. The full
corpus therefore remains protected. Raw text, exact dates, record identifiers,
addresses, phone numbers, ordinary-life material, comments, responder rosters,
media, authentication context, and record-level counters are not committed.

Only posts individually reopened in the authenticated interface and rechecked
as **Shared with Public** were promoted as public sources. The repository stores
aggregate controls, source relationships, claims, anti-claims, and one opaque
private-artifact ID rather than the record-level census.

## Full-population findings

A deterministic second pass routed 181 unique records into overlapping
mission-related categories. The most frequent routes were WOW List (48),
Sunday Dinner (44), NYC Artist Coalition (34), Let NYC Dance (33),
creative-technical practice (19), civic-technical practice (13), water publics
(10), and Save NYC Spaces (10). Categories overlap and are research aids, not
effort measures, exclusive semantic judgments, public metrics, or impact
scores.

The corpus contains 430 records with external URLs and 549 unique normalized
external destinations. Posted destinations include project sites, official
processes, action routes, independent reporting, and cultural media. A posted
URL is a source lead, not provenance or endorsement by itself.

Deterministic mention and link rules found recurring civic routes: New York
City Council appeared in 20 records, Rafael Espinal in 18, Market Hotel in nine,
the Office of Nightlife in six, Antonio Reynoso in five, Quinton Lucas in one,
and Helen Rosenthal in one. These are Jamie's mentions, tags, quotations, and
routes. They are not actions by those stakeholders and do not establish
engagement, attendance, endorsement, partnership, response, or impact.

## Posted articles and source discovery

The corpus preserved routes to sources already governed elsewhere in the bank,
including NPR's September 2017 Cabaret Law reporting and The Pitch's Great
Accommodations reporting. The latter independently documents the collective
raft expedition and Jamie's originating idea; later Pitch reporting documents
the collective Gulf completion.

This pass adds Jake Offenhartz's March 31, 2017 Gothamist article, *Movement For
Repealing NYC's Archaic 'No Dancing' Law Gains Momentum*. The article reports
that hundreds gathered at Market Hotel for the repeal effort. It supplies
independent campaign context, not Jamie's individual role or a precise
attendance count.

## What the corpus changes

The selected public record supports a bounded practice-level finding: Jamie
repeatedly connected project explanation to a usable participation route. The
examples move from a WOW List join path, to a Cabaret Law press-and-call script,
to a neighborhood-process invitation, to a City Hall hearing and bill-action
route. This is implementation and public communication work, while the archive
does not establish whether people clicked, attended, called, converted, or
caused an outcome.

The post record also strengthens existing claims without replacing their
primary evidence:

- NTER CHNG gains a dated public opening trace with collaborator credit.
- WOW List gains a personal-account counterpart to its nine-city Page record.
- KC Town Hall gains a contemporaneous first-person co-initiation trace.
- The Cabaret Law record gains independently published campaign context.
- The waterways record retains independent reporting on the raft project.

No public website copy changes in this pass. The material is stronger as
governed bank depth for future audience-specific composition, source research,
and photo-editor briefs.
