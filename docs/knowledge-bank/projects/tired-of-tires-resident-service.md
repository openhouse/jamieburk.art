# Tired of Tires resident service

## Why this matters

Tired of Tires is one of the clearest examples in Jamie's record of a service
that met residents where they were. It combined a simple public promise with a
repeatable operating system: household eligibility, recurring scheduling,
multiple intake channels, volunteer participation, field collection, a
municipal recycling handoff, and public result reporting.

That combination is directly relevant to resident-facing product and service
roles. The work was not only a website, flyer, meeting, or cleanup. It linked
communication, intake, fulfillment, partnership, field contact, and learning.

## Publicly supported service model

The [archived service page](../sources/kc-town-hall-tired-of-tires-wayback-2020.md)
establishes that KC Town Hall and Oak Park Neighborhood Association jointly
presented a recurring free household tire-pickup service for historic East
Kansas City neighborhoods. The page documents:

- residential rather than business eligibility;
- a recurring Friday-before-first-Saturday schedule;
- form and phone intake;
- a pathway to volunteer;
- neighborhood collection followed by City recycling;
- a shared “Julia and Jamie” page byline; and
- a project-reported $17,768 in avoided disposal costs.

The savings figure is first-party reporting, not an independently audited
outcome.

## Jamie's supported role

Two separate public records narrow the individual-role question. A June 2019
KC Town Hall update names Jamie among participants in the recurring pickup and
free-disposal workflow. Jimmy Fitzner later gave a first-hand public account of
riding with Jamie in a dump truck to pick up tires around Northeast Kansas
City.

Together, those records support this bounded professional statement:

> Jamie helped deliver the recurring resident service and directly
> participated in pickup operations.

They do not establish that Jamie alone designed or operated the program, ran
every shift, authored every component, or controlled the City's recycling
decision.

## Research and requirements loop

Jamie remembers pairing the tire-schedule handbill with KC Town Hall's compact
neighborhood survey during pickup rounds. He describes the field service as a
reliable point of contact: residents could receive an immediate service, share
what they wanted within walking distance, and help translate lived needs into
future program requirements.

The 2019 CCED proposal independently shows the survey, says responses shaped
the proposal, presents a restaurant and kitchen-incubator model, and specifies
a commercial-kitchen hood and fire-suppression system. That supports a
project-level listening-to-requirements pattern. It does not independently
prove Jamie's authorship of the handbills or the exact causal chain from one
resident conversation to one architectural requirement.

## Visual bridge: promise to delivery

The authenticated project archive contains rendered outreach designs and a
curated matched field-photograph pair showing one collection site before and
after tire removal. Jamie authorized three exact public derivatives on August
14, 2026: a cropped flyer that excludes obsolete contact details and dates, and
the matched before-and-after photographs.

The case study presents these as a sequence: the resident-facing promise, the
field condition, and the same frame after collection. This is evidence of the
project-level service interface and one completed collection. It is not evidence
of a resident identity or address, a project-wide total, Jamie's individual
authorship of every visual component, or the identity of the photographer.

## Held details and next research

Jamie also reports designing the service and its graphics, driving a complete
block-by-block route in KC Town Hall's ten-minute walkshed, helping residents
move tires, keeping the month-by-month log, and pairing two 4-by-6-inch
handbills during the rounds. Those details remain valuable research leads.

Authenticated review recovered the protected operating-workbook structure,
rendered design files, and a curated field-photo pair. The next evidence pass
should seek file-creator and editable-history evidence, form ownership metadata,
City recycling records, route notes, neighborhood-association records,
photographer attribution, and collaborator proof notes. Any workbook-derived
aggregate requires a separate accuracy and publication decision. No resident
addresses, contact details, raw reports, private aggregates, or private survey
responses should enter the public repository.
