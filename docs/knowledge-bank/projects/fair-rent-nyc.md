# Fair Rent NYC

Fair Rent NYC is a collective campaign for commercial rent stabilization and
storefront stability. It connects small-business and cultural-space experience
with public education, coalition relationships, legislative language, legal
questions, public-data framing, and city and state strategy.

Jamie's bounded role includes campaign web infrastructure, source and decision
memory, running minutes, legislative provenance, public-data materials,
movement mapping, action tools, and stakeholder follow-up. The work is shared
with organizers, small-business owners, cultural-space participants, lawyers,
policy partners, elected officials, and staff.

## Present focus

The 2026 record documents work to preserve earlier Fair Rent NYC campaign
history while coordinating with current small-business and policy partners. It
keeps city and state lanes distinct, identifies legal and policy questions,
and treats data, contacts, stories, and publication as governed resources.

The [August 12 source return](../research-runs/commercial-rent-advocacy-source-return-2026-08-12.md)
adds a consequential recent operating sequence: Jamie performed a careful
prepublication review that the final report acknowledges, delivered public
advocacy remarks at the report launch, coordinated event information with City
Council staff, and developed a recurring work channel with state legislative
staff. These are strong evidence of research-quality review, public narrative,
stakeholder translation, and campaign operations. Their portfolio and resume
projections remain held pending Jamie approval and the documented source and
rights gates.

## Boundary

Do not describe Jamie as sole campaign leader, bill author, legal authority, or
cause of legislative action. Running minutes document coordination and assigned
work; they do not prove unanimous agreement or final institutional decisions.
A planning run of show does not prove attendance, staff coordination does not
prove an elected official's endorsement, a scheduled meeting does not prove it
occurred, and a footage request does not prove press coverage.
