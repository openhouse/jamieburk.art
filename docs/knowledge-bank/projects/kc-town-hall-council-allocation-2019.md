# KC Town Hall Council allocation record

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-13

**Status:** Public-safe Knowledge Bank research record

## Best-supported chronology

- On June 12, 2019, Jamie Burkart presented KC Town Hall's proposal to renovate
  an existing building at 36th Street and Indiana Avenue for four retail spaces
  and three apartments. The requested CCED amount was $490,539.
- On July 16, all four members of the Central City Economic Development Sales
  Tax Board voted to recommend the project and amount to City Council.
- On September 26, the City Council adopted substituted Resolution 190649,
  accepting the Board recommendation and authorizing the City Manager to
  negotiate a funding agreement.
- At the same meeting, the Council passed substituted Ordinance 190642. Section
  2 appropriated $490,539 for KC Town Hall as part of the Round Two CCED project
  appropriations.
- Independent reporting on December 27, 2021 documented that KC Town Hall still
  lacked a final City contract more than two years after the Board
  recommendation.
- Jamie's first-person account is that he later transitioned stewardship of KC
  Town Hall to a mission-aligned organization. No public source recovered in
  this pass independently identifies the successor or establishes the timing
  and terms of that transition.
- In 2024, Ordinance 240317 recorded that KC Town Hall had withdrawn and would
  not proceed, then reappropriated the unused $490,539.

## Claim decision

The direct public claim may say that the CCED Board unanimously recommended
$490,539 and that the City Council subsequently accepted the recommendation and
appropriated $490,539. The Board and Council actions must not be collapsed into
one vote: the available Council action records say "Adopted as Substituted" and
"Passed as Substituted" but expose no member-by-member tally.

The full lifecycle belongs in consequential portfolio copy. The favorable 2019
actions do not become proof of disbursement or completion, and the 2024 record
must remain visible wherever the funding outcome is material.

Jamie's correction adds a second evidentiary layer: he transitioned stewardship
to a mission-aligned organization. The portfolio may state that as his bounded
first-person account. It must not collapse that handoff into the City's later
withdrawal record, imply that the City transferred the award, or name the
successor before a public-safe source and Jamie's approval are in place.

offline. The reason for transition is not stored or projected. The public
records reviewed here do not independently establish the successor organization
