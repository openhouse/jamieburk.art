# Kansas City Neighborhood Operations

## Tired of Tires

A protected archive of KC Town Hall's published site documents a monthly free
tire-pickup service co-presented with Oak Park Neighborhood Association for
homes in Kansas City's historic east neighborhoods. Its 2020 update reported
$17,768 in avoided tire-disposal fees. An official current City page
corroborates the municipal operating mechanism: approved neighborhood groups
may deliver residents' tires without charge to the Environmental Campus.

Jamie reports designing and coordinating the program, conducting the monthly
pickup and City drop-off workflow, tracking each haul, producing and
distributing neighborhood handbills, and later extending service to Indian
Mound. Those individual duties and the Indian Mound expansion remain
firsthand research leads. The $17,768 figure is a project-published estimate,
not an independently audited City measure, and it must not be converted into an
unsupported tire count.

## Cleveland Avenue Unify to Beautify

Jamie reports co-founding Cleveland Avenue Unify to Beautify within the
Historic East Neighborhoods Coalition, building from Pastor Lee's idea that
Cleveland Avenue could help the City's east-side neighborhoods appear in
public planning as a connected place and corridor. Jamie describes contributing
identity, logo, photography, social media, listening-session maps, public
meeting materials, resident-reporting affordances, and pro bono print
production.

HENC's 2024 strategic plan corroborates the broader context: a coalition of
east-side neighborhoods including Oak Park, with stated goals around cleanup,
beautification, resident surveys, PIAC engagement, and capital funding. It does
not establish the specific Cleveland Avenue program, Jamie's role, Pastor
Lee's authorship, elected-official participation, or a funding outcome. No
accomplishment claim or website projection has been created yet.
