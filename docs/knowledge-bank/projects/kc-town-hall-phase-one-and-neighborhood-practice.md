## Decision

The current evidence supports three bounded professional claims:

1. Jamie served as KC Town Hall's Phase One general contractor and daily field
   coordinator. A contemporaneous project package independently corroborates
   his founder/project-manager role, a named multidisciplinary team, the
   2018-2019 cold-shell scope, and a displayed $189,629 Phase One total. The
   exact general-contractor title and complete duty list remain first-person
   evidence.
2. Jamie designed a compact neighborhood survey handbill and backing data
   workflow. The proposal reproduces the card, names two neighborhood partners,
   and says the ongoing survey directly shaped the application. The individual
   design and system attribution remains first-person evidence.
3. Jamie reports designing and city-coordinating the Oak Park TiredOfTires
   service and conducting recurring field operations. A shared project archive
   documents the monthly resident-intake and volunteer system; two public
   records independently corroborate his direct participation.

Cleveland Avenue Unify to Beautify and the broader pro bono neighborhood
design-and-print practice remain preserved as first-person research leads. They
are not discarded, but they are not yet promoted to governed public claims.

## Why This Matters

This record makes a substantial part of Jamie's technical project-management
practice legible:

- multidisciplinary contractor and consultant coordination;
- dependency-sensitive field sequencing;
- daily site operations across multiple elevations and work zones;
- resident research translated into a repeatable intake system;
- public communication and relationship infrastructure;
- recurring service design spanning intake, routing, field execution,
  tracking, and city handoff; and
- collective-credit and privacy discipline around neighborhood work.

The professional accomplishment is not merely that a building received repairs.
Jamie describes holding construction dependencies, people, field information,
and neighborhood imagination together long enough for a neglected place to
receive concrete care and for residents to have a structured way to shape what
could follow.

## Phase One Scope

- roof deck repair, insulation, and TPO membrane;
- brick masonry repair;
- structural floor and ceiling framing;
- water-service connection;
- construction staging and secure storage;
- debris and tree removal;
- basement access and egress;
- materials transport;
- property acquisition;
- site safety and security;
- moisture and air-quality control;
- tools and materials; and
- legal, financing, and tax costs.

The narrative says 66 percent of Phase One was complete after 2018 and planned
full completion in 2019. The budget page is labeled "Phase One: Cold Shell -
Completed 2019." Jamie independently states that Phase One was completed in
2019. Until a later invoice ledger, permit history, inspection record, or dated
completion artifact is associated, public wording should keep the proposal's
contemporaneous framing and Jamie's completion account visible as separate
evidence layers.

## Budget Data Quality

The proposal displays $189,629 as the total Phase One value, but the table does
not fully reconcile:

- the visible 2018 and 2019 column totals add to $191,895, not $189,629; and
- the legal-fee row's visible year values add to $7,003, not its displayed
  $6,530 row total.

The displayed category totals do add to $189,629. This is a medium-severity
citation risk rather than a reason to discard the source. The safe formulation
is "the proposal displays $189,629 as the Phase One total," not "Phase One cost
exactly $189,629" or "Jamie managed a verified $189,629 expenditure."
