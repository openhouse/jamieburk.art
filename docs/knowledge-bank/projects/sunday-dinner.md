# Sunday Dinner

## Current defensible record

Public Facebook event titles currently preserve two milestones in the recurring
Sunday Dinner practice:

- *SUNDAY DINNER Turns 100!* on March 9, 2014;
- *200th Sunday Dinner* on June 26, 2016.

The 2016 event identifies Julia Fredenburg and Jamie Burkart together and links
to a corresponding WOW List event page. Other recovered events preserve the
practice moving through a park, an independent cultural space, private homes,
and a rotating eight-week neighborhood-hosting pattern.

These records support Sunday Dinner as collective, participant-made cultural
infrastructure and document part of its relationship to WOW List. They do not
establish attendance, unique participants, every gathering, or sole authorship.

## Protected Operating Workbook

A protected working spreadsheet adds operational depth without entering any
participant record into the repository. It contains 345 numbered gathering
columns spanning January 22, 2012, through March 7, 2021. This supports a
long-running, instrumented practice of invitation, response, attendance, and
follow-through.

The workbook's participant-total formula displays 2,783 meals served, while the
gathering-sequence columns sum to 2,769. The 14-instance difference and duplicate
gathering labels remain unresolved. Neither number is approved as audited
attendance or unique people, and neither is projected to the website.

## Unresolved chronology

A separate archived public index contains March 2016 posts previously read as
“Sunday Dinner 100.” That label conflicts with the 2014 hundredth-event and 2016
two-hundredth-event titles. The knowledge bank retains both sources and holds
all milestone-date projections while the archive context is re-examined.
The records must be reconciled before any exact milestone chronology is used on
the public site.

Possible explanations, such as a repost, post title, series label, archive-order
artifact, or differing count convention, remain hypotheses rather than facts.

## Public-safe boundary

Several Sunday Dinner events took place in homes. The knowledge bank records the
residential mode of convening but does not publish addresses, phone numbers,
guest identities, comments, or private-event data.

Facebook response labels are historical platform observations, not attendance.
