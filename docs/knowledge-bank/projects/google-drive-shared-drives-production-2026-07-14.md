# Google Drive Shared Drives archival production

**Review date:** 2026-07-14
**Reviewer:** Codex archival production with Jamie Burkart
**Status:** Bounded, non-exhaustive, public-safe pass
**Public projection:** One strengthened existing Sunday Dinner projection; five mature claims held

## Purpose

Jamie's Shared Drives are collaborative working and handoff infrastructure. They
carry project files between devices, preserve operating context, and create
spaces where collaborators can enter, contribute, review, and continue work.

This pass asked what professional evidence could be matured from that practice
without turning private access into public permission.

## Scope

The authenticated account exposed 110 Shared Drives on the review date. The
review:

- mapped the full drive inventory at aggregate level;
- inspected 26 portfolio-relevant roots across civic, cultural, technical,
  residency, media, public-data, and collaborator contexts;
- inspected selected second-level folders;
- close-read seven unique high-signal documents or workbooks;
- used revision metadata where it materially clarified authorship;
- compared candidates against the canonical bank before creating new source
  identities;
- retained no drive names, IDs, URLs, permission lists, private file listings,
  participant rows, or credentials.

The sample was purposefully selected for portfolio relevance. It is not an
exhaustive review of all 110 drives.

## What matured

### Sunday Dinner

A protected operating ledger contains a numbered event sequence through Sunday
Dinner 345, dated March 7, 2021, and an aggregate labeled 2,783 meals served.
This directly strengthens the existing public-safe `300+ gatherings` wording.

The ledger also contains participant-level contact, invitation, attendance, and
working data. None of that material is reproduced in the repository.

### 196 Artists Residency

A Jamie-authored 2023 acceptance workflow documents a concrete operating loop:
acceptance, a pre-arrival conversation, space configuration, arrival logistics,
and independent access planning for a resident and collaborator.

The resident, correspondence, contact request, access method, and private
instructions remain protected. One workflow does not prove the `20+ resident
artists` aggregate.

### Commercial vacancy and lease-cost data

Jamie's March 2026 brief turns a public-data gap into a smallest serious pilot:

1. an annual geography-aggregated lease and vacancy indicators table;
2. a coverage and suppression table;
3. a plain-language methods note.

The proposal explicitly excludes raw confidential filings, tenant identities,
tax identifiers, parcel-level rent rows, unit-level lease records, and uploaded
leases. It is strong product-design evidence. It is not evidence of agency
adoption, a released dataset, or policy impact.

### Small Business United

A Jamie-authored starter credits a collaborator named Gabriel with the identity
system and proposes documenting it as a style guide that teammates could refine
and apply cohesively. The claim retains this division of credit. Completion and
team adoption were not recovered in the inspected folder.

### Collective communication infrastructure

Two December 2020 onboarding guides document role-based project and coalition
email, correction paths, and personal-account privacy considerations. Revision
metadata attributes the surviving documents to Julia Fredenburg, not Jamie.

The records are useful as collective operating-system context and as a prompt
for further component-role research. They are not converted into a Jamie
authorship claim.

### Shared Drives as a handoff practice

Across the aggregate map and selected close reads, the drives function as
project-scoped and collaborator-scoped working rooms for media, documentation,
onboarding, public-data products, cultural programs, and handoffs. This is a
bounded archive-practice claim, not a claim that Jamie owns or authored every
drive or artifact.

## Deduplication

The sample contained copies of sources already normalized from iCloud and local
project archives. Matching carriers were not counted as new evidence identities.
The bank records the cross-carrier finding in the research audit while keeping
the existing canonical source record.

Matching title and content does not prove that every carrier is the same
revision. Revision-level comparison remains available if a future claim depends
on the distinction.

## Projection decision

The public Sunday Dinner case study now states:

> A protected operating ledger records 345 numbered gatherings and 2,783 meals
> served through March 2021.

The claim is presented without a public citation because its source is private.
The raw ledger and all participant-level evidence remain outside the repository.

The five other mature claims remain held. They improve the bank's depth for
future applications, role-specific portfolios, collaborator proof requests, and
photo-editor briefs without burdening the current site.

## Known / Open / Protected

### Known

- 110 Shared Drives were accessible on the review date.
- 26 portfolio-relevant roots were inspected at top level.
- Seven unique high-signal artifacts received close reading.
- The Sunday Dinner ledger supports a 345-event sequence and a 2,783-meal
  aggregate.
- Jamie authored the inspected 196 acceptance workflow, commercial-vacancy
  pilot brief, and Small Business United documentation starter.
- Julia Fredenburg authored the surviving project and coalition email guides.

### Open

- Independent support for the `20+ resident artists` aggregate.
- The complete division of Sunday Dinner and 196 production labor.
- Public presentation, submission, adoption, or implementation of the vacancy
  pilot.
- Completion and adoption of the Small Business United style guide.
- Jamie's component role in coalition account provisioning, server
  administration, and maintenance.
- Unreviewed drives and deeper folders that may contain future source leads.

### Protected

- drive names, IDs, URLs, permissions, and member lists;
- private file names and folder listings;
- participant names, contact details, attendance, invitations, and notes;
- access methods, account instructions, server settings, and credentials;
- raw coalition and collaborator records;
- private correspondence and unapproved media;
- local or cloud-storage locators.

## Research limits

- The 26-root sample was selected for relevance and is not exhaustive.
- Empty or inaccessible folders do not prove that evidence never existed.
- Authenticated access does not prove ownership, authorship, consent, or current
  project status.
- A working document establishes what was designed or recorded, not necessarily
  what was adopted, completed, or caused.
- The Sunday Dinner aggregate was read from the workbook's own summary; it was
  not independently reconstructed from participant-level cells.

## Eval contract

The Shared Drives production gate fails if:

- expected intake, source, observation, claim, or inquiry records disappear;
- a private source gains a Drive URL or enters the public registry;
- a held claim receives a public surface;
- the Julia Fredenburg guide credit is reassigned to Jamie;
- the 345-event sequence or 2,783-meal aggregate loses its scope boundary;
- the `20+ resident artists` aggregate is represented as established by these
  sources;
- the non-exhaustive sampling and access limitations disappear;
- duplicate carriers inflate the source count;
- the public Sunday Dinner projection omits the protected-data boundary.
