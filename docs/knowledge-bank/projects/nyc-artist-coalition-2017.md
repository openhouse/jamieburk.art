# NYC Artist Coalition: 2017 Civic Advocacy

## Promoted Public Claims

Contemporaneous reporting directly documents Jamie advocating through NYC
Artist Coalition for full repeal of the Cabaret Law, organizing fire-code study
groups, and participating as a coalition speaker at a public Office of Nightlife
town hall.

Independent reporting described NYC Artist Coalition as instrumental in
advocacy around the Office of Nightlife and Cabaret Law repeal. Official records
establish the creation of the Office and the Council's repeal vote. The public
case study therefore uses `helped`, `advocated`, and `participated`, preserving
the distinction between coalition advocacy, legislative sponsorship, and
official enactment.

Talks Not Raids is separately supported as a collective public-action surface
around Introduction 1156. The campaign site documents explanatory sources,
calls to Council, sponsor tracking, and coalition participation. The Council
record establishes that the bill became Local Law 220 of 2019 with reporting
and advance-notice requirements.

## Held Claims

- Jamie's exact individual share of NYC Artist Coalition's instrumental policy
  influence needs more collaborator and production evidence.
- The record establishes a sequence from Talks Not Raids to enacted
  transparency requirements and later MARCH disbanding, but not a single causal
  chain.

These claims remain in the bank with explicit research questions. They are not
weakened into disappearance or strengthened beyond the evidence.
