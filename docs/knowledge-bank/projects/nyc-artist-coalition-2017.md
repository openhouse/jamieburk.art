## Campaign press indexes

The campaign sites also preserve a larger source-discovery layer: **45 press
appearances representing 44 distinct articles** across Let NYC Dance, Talks Not
Raids, Save NYC Spaces, and Fair Rent NYC. One NPR article appears in two
campaign indexes and retains both memberships while resolving to one canonical

These entries are normalized as bounded source leads rather than treated as

## Defensible claims

- Gothamist documented Jamie organizing fire-code study groups for DIY venues,
  rallying at City Hall for full Cabaret Law repeal, and articulating how
  licensing fear could deter spaces from seeking fire-safety guidance.
- NPR described Jamie as a founding member of the coalition. Because the
  article prints the name as "NYC Arts Coalition," public use attributes the
  description to NPR and does not convert it into a sole-founder claim.
- Bedford + Bowery identified Jamie as an NYC Artist Coalition organizer and
  reported more than 100 publication-time signups to a mutual-aid network
  offering skills and resources.
- Public coalition pages show an operating system that combined issue
  explanation, public data, Council contact pathways, campaign action, member
  intake, and mutual-aid intake. These pages do not by themselves establish
  Jamie's authorship of each component.
- Bedford + Bowery reported that NYC Artist Coalition spearheaded a public
  town hall about the new Office of Nightlife and named Jamie among coalition
  speakers. The Greene Hill profile corroborates the community invitation.
- Official Council records establish that the Office of Nightlife and
  Nightlife Advisory Board were enacted in September 2017 and that the
  Cabaret Law licensing requirement was repealed in November 2017.
- The coalition's own repeal account credits a broad field of advocates and
  public leaders, preserving collective credit alongside the official legal
  record.
- An official 2018 Council transcript documents Jamie testifying as an NYC
  Artist Coalition member in support of commercial-rent protections and
  connecting affordability with cultural-space safety and continuity.

## Boundaries

The sources support a clear record of founding attribution, organizing,
mutual-aid infrastructure, public communication, testimony, town-hall work,
and policy-facing participation. They do not establish that Jamie alone
founded or led the coalition, caused either law, wrote the legislation,
produced every event, authored every campaign component, or spoke for every
collaborator.

The 100-plus figure is a signup count reported in February 2017. It is not a
current membership count, an active-participation count, or a measure of people
served.
