# WOW List Facebook post archival production

Date: 2026-07-15

## Scope and population

This pass accounts for every distinct post exposed by the authenticated WOW
List Facebook page feed on the capture date. Repeated lazy scrolling from the
top reached the same 40,095-pixel document height and terminal post pair across
seven consecutive checks. The deduplicated population is 57 dated posts from
April 25, 2015 through March 22, 2018:

| Year | Posts |
| --- | ---: |
| 2015 | 22 |
| 2016 | 27 |
| 2017 | 7 |
| 2018 | 1 |

Every exposed post has a public-safe ledger row. The fixture retains dates,
cleaned posted URLs, displayed shared-source labels, bounded mission and
stakeholder tags, content hashes, and displayed interaction counts. It omits
raw post bodies, comment text, reaction identities, follower identities,
contact details, tracking parameters, authenticated URLs, and session data.

This is 100 percent of the capture-date authenticated live-feed population. It
is not a native Meta export or proof of complete lifetime history. Deleted,
hidden, private, unpublished, or no-longer-retained posts can remain outside
the recovered population.

## Interface reconciliation

Meta Business Suite's Content view labeled a March 31, 2019 through July 14,
2026 window as `Lifetime` and returned no activity. That window begins after
the recovered feed's final post. The Business Suite result therefore cannot
account for the 2015-2018 population and cannot support a claim that no
historical posts existed.

The live page, rather than the bounded Business Suite table, is the population
source for this pass. Facebook did not display a native total-post count, so
the completion control is the stable terminal crawl plus the reconciled
57-row ledger.

## Project identity and practice

The page describes WOW List as an event-sharing and community-building project
and preserves the motto `Being there changes everything.` The post population
shows what that identity did in practice.

- Seventeen posts document product onboarding or use: join routes, local
  calendars, event loading, tutorials, and invitations to use WOW List instead
  of Facebook for event discovery.
- Sixteen posts document cross-city organizer infrastructure, including
  Phoenix, Santa Barbara, Los Angeles, Seattle, Iowa City, Chicago, Kansas City,
  and touring projects.
- Three posts preserve participatory product governance: a public site-design
  video chat and a member-hangout reflection on community values, shared
  ownership, and distributed contribution.
- Thirty-eight posts distribute cultural events, tours, festivals,
  performances, screenings, or talks.

Several of the strongest product-use records are displayed as other
organizers' or artists' source cards. They describe WOW List as an alternative
discovery layer, direct people into local join routes, publish local calendars,
or demonstrate the product through a public tutorial. That is stronger than
self-description, but it remains a bounded set of examples rather than a
platform-wide adoption census.

## Source and URL inventory

The 57 posts contain 55 distinct cleaned URL routes: 30 to WOW List and 25 to
external sources. Each inventory row separately records mission context,
evidentiary role, access disposition, preservation disposition, and a governed
source ID where one exists. The evidentiary-role population is:

| Evidentiary role | Routes |
| --- | ---: |
| WOW List project route | 30 |
| Fundraising | 9 |
| Issue context | 5 |
| Organizer resource | 5 |
| Event information | 3 |
| Independent product use | 1 |
| Civic-mobilization resource | 1 |
| Cultural-network resource | 1 |

Seven routes resolve to governed source records. The other 48 are explicitly
`not-rechecked-in-this-pass` and `route-inventory-only`. This is an honest
research queue: not rechecked is distinct from dead, live, or historically
nonexistent.

The external inventory includes:

- organizer infrastructure such as Phoenix DIY, Santa Barbara DIY, Seattle
  DIY, DoDIY, and Grasstronaut;
- venue-safety, survival, and fundraising routes concerning Meow Wolf's DIY
  Fund, Ghost Ship, Pehrspace, Silent Barn, Trunk Space, Shea Stadium, the Know,
  and an Orange County all-ages space;
- mutual-aid and civic routes concerning Standing Rock, post-election
  organizing, the Women's March, fire relief, and vigils; and
- cultural-network resources from Allied Media Projects, artists, touring
  projects, and supporter platforms.

The source role is essential. Shelby Turner's tutorial is classified as
independent product-use evidence and resolves to its governed source record.
Westword's Denver DIY Fund reporting, East Bay
Express's Ghost Ship reporting, Willamette Week's account of the Know's closure,
and Meow Wolf's fund announcement establish the issue context WOW List
distributed. They are not coverage of WOW List and do not establish that WOW
List caused funding, venue recovery, safety improvements, or policy change.

## Care and civic routing

The account was not only a promotional surface. Twelve rows route venue-safety
or survival information, and six route mutual aid or civic mobilization. The
2016 sequence around Ghost Ship moves among first-person community accounts,
relief and vigil routes, safer-space resources, funding, and reporting on why
punitive crackdowns do not resolve the need for safe, accessible cultural
space. Other posts connect the event-discovery infrastructure to Standing Rock,
post-election organizing, and the Women's March.

This supports a bounded claim about information practice: WOW List used event
and social distribution for care, mutual aid, venue survival, and civic
mobilization alongside cultural events. It does not establish outcome,
attendance, endorsement, policy impact, or causation.

## Stakeholders and displayed interactions

Twenty-four posts display a shared-source card. Overlapping public ledger tags
identify 12 artist or community sources, eight local-organizer or
resource-network sources, five arts-and-civic advocacy sources, five cultural
spaces, and four published-media sources. The tags describe displayed source
relationships, not unique people, formal partnerships, or reaction identities.

At capture time, Facebook displayed:

| Interaction | Rows with a count | Displayed total | Maximum on one row |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Likes | 41 | 87 | 13 |
| Comments | 12 | 16 | 3 |
| Shares | 10 | 49 | 29 |

The October 5, 2015 post introducing WOW List community calendars in nine
cities had all three population maxima: 13 displayed likes, three comments,
and 29 shares. This is the clearest within-account traction signal in the
capture. It is still a volatile interface count, not reach, attendance,
conversion, endorsement, unique people, mandate, or impact.

The pass does not publish liker, commenter, sharer, or follower identities.
Accordingly, it does not claim a complete stakeholder-engagement census from
social-graph identities. The more defensible stakeholder evidence is the
distribution of public source cards, organizer routes, and linked resources.

## Credit and authorship

Jamie remembers managing WOW List's social presence, and his authenticated
account currently has page-management access. The project itself was Jamie and
Richard Caceres's shared work, with many organizers, artists, and contributors
participating in the surrounding network.

Neither current access, the WOW List page identity, nor a displayed source card
identifies the human author of every historical page post. The knowledge bank
therefore preserves Jamie's social-stewardship memory as a research lead while
leaving post-level authorship unassigned pending collaborator confirmation or
native account audit data.

## Knowledge-bank and website decision

The pass adds one intake, ten observations, eleven sources, three claims, three
research inquiries, a 57-row public-safe census, and a 55-route URL inventory
with explicit evidentiary, access, and preservation dispositions.
All three new claim projections remain held in the archive layer.

The public case study already has stronger, more legible proof of shared
product creation, substantial platform data, multi-city use, and a separate
complete X-account analysis. The Facebook archive adds depth, process, care,
and source discovery without yet supplying better individual-role attribution.
Making no website copy changes in this pass prevents a rich archival layer from
overloading the public argument or converting project-account evidence into
personal authorship.
