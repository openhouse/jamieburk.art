# WOW List Facebook posts

Reviewed: 2026-07-15

## Public-safe conclusion

Facebook's surviving management record supports a strong, bounded role claim:
Jamie stewarded WOW List's Facebook publishing from 2015 through 2018. All 50
post details that still render display the admin-only attribution **Published by
Jamie Burkart**. Four additional posts remain visible only in Facebook's
Lifetime management table, producing a 54-record complete-as-materialized
inventory.

WOW List was co-built by Jamie Burkart and Richard Album. A Page publisher
byline does not make Jamie the sole author of quoted or shared material, the
sole owner of the project, or the exclusive manager of every WOW List account
or platform. Those distinctions remain attached wherever the role claim is
projected.

The authenticated WOW List Page was reviewed through **Professional Dashboard
> Content Library > Published > Lifetime**. The virtualized table was scrolled
until it reached a terminal no-growth state. Every materialized post ID was
then opened directly.

- 54 unique records materialized.
- The chronology runs from April 25, 2015, through March 22, 2018.
- 50 detail pages rendered.
- Four records remained table-only.
- All 50 rendered details displayed Jamie's Page-publisher byline.
- Every row has a stable post ID, recovery state, thematic disposition, public
  link inventory, and bounded metric snapshot in the fixture.

This is 100% of the surviving records exposed by the authenticated Lifetime
library on July 15, 2026. It is not a Meta owner export and cannot establish
that deleted, removed, or otherwise unexposed posts never existed.

The immutable public fixture is
`apps/www/src/data/knowledge-bank/fixtures/wowlist-facebook-posts-full-population.json`.
It excludes full post text, commenter identities, private profile links, and
authenticated-session state. The protected raw capture remains outside the
public repository.

## Mission patterns

Record-level close reading shows that the Page was more than an announcement
feed. It connected product adoption and community action across overlapping
patterns:

- 18 records support community-calendar onboarding.
- 17 distribute events and artist work.
- 19 support cultural spaces or mutual-aid activity.
- 12 support civic mobilization.
- 13 concern community governance or participatory product feedback.
- Nine concern community care and remembrance.
- Nine concern cultural-space funding.

These are archival classifications, not Facebook categories. They overlap and
must not be summed into a unique-post denominator.

Representative plot points include city-scene onboarding in Phoenix, Santa
Barbara, Seattle, Los Angeles, Chicago, and other local networks; invitations
to kitchen-to-kitchen product-design conversations; Silent Barn's post-fire
recovery; all-ages and sober-space fundraising; DIY-space safety and funding;
the Women's March and Standing Rock calendars; and Cabaret Law repeal advocacy.

The Page also carried other people's language and projects. That is evidence of
editorial and distribution stewardship, not a reason to absorb collaborators'
authorship into Jamie's record.

## Source inventory

The 54 records preserve 42 normalized public destinations after Facebook and
campaign tracking parameters are removed. They include WOW List city and event
surfaces, local DIY calendars, artist tours, cultural-space fundraising and
recovery pages, civic-action calendars, and published reporting.

One source was close-read in this pass: Patricia Calhoun's March 9, 2017,
*Denver Westword* article, "City Partners With Meow Wolf on $20,000 Denver DIY
Spaces Fund." The article documents a Denver Arts & Venues contribution to a
Meow Wolf-administered fund for DIY and alternative spaces. WOW List circulated
the article on its publication date. That establishes dated source curation; it
does not imply that WOW List or Jamie created, administered, or caused the
fund.

The other destinations remain in the research lifecycle. A posted URL proves
association and circulation, not authorship, endorsement, current availability,
audience use, or the truth of the destination.

## Engagement boundary

On July 15, 2026, Facebook's legacy table displayed 108 interactions, 512
impressions, and 11 comments in aggregate across the 54 rows. The current Page
displayed 185 followers.

These are migration-sensitive current displays, not historical lifetime reach,
unique people, attendance, conversion, endorsement, or impact. The interface
did not expose a complete population of reactor and commenter identities, so
this pass does not report engagement by artists, venues, elected officials,
agencies, or other stakeholder groups. That question remains open pending an
identity-complete, owner-authorized source.

## Platform migration

displayed no activity, while the legacy Professional Dashboard exposed 54
earlier records. The modern empty table is therefore treated as a product-
migration boundary, not evidence of historical inactivity.
