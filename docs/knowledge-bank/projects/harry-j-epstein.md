# Harry J. Epstein Company

Jamie helped a multigenerational Kansas City tool and industrial-supply business
adapt to e-commerce while preserving its distinctive public voice, customer
relationships, and long-lived inventory knowledge.

## Jamie's role

Jamie translated legacy operating knowledge into catalog architecture, search,
checkout, dealer-aware account logic, content systems, analytics, marketing,
performance work, and repeatable operational workflows. Public reporting
independently documents the 2009 online-store transition, including search,
country-of-origin information, checkout, and the distinctive "flying tools"
interaction. The approved proof language says Jamie contributed to a period of
2x revenue growth; it does not assign all growth to him.

## What became usable

- a searchable and purchasable online catalog;
- digital forms for dealer relationships and store-specific customer service;
- analytics and content infrastructure for iterative improvement;
- a public web experience that remained recognizably Epstein's.
