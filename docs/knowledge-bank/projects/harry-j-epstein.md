# Harry J. Epstein Company digital operations

## Situation

Harry J. Epstein Company had deep catalog, surplus-buying, product, customer,
dealer, and family knowledge before e-commerce. Much of that operating
intelligence lived in paper, phone calls, store practices, photographs, coded
pricing, and staff memory.

## Jamie's documented portfolio role

Jamie dates the Harry J. Epstein Company client relationship 2009-2015. He
identifies it as an engagement that began before he formalized his practice and
then became Thick Arts LLC's first client relationship after the LLC was
formed. New York Department of State data independently records Thick Arts LLC,
DOS ID 4267566, as a domestic LLC filed July 6, 2012 in Kings County. The state
record does not identify the LLC's members or clients; first-client status and
the relationship dates remain Jamie's authorized first-party professional
account.

Jamie worked on e-commerce, navigation, search, interface behavior, checkout,
dealer access, analytics, publishing, database performance, marketing, and
operational workflows. The work aimed to make the business discoverable and
shoppable online without replacing its distinctive voice or relationship-based
practice with a generic storefront.

Archive.org observes the commerce host in January 2010, preserves a functioning
storefront through July 3, 2015, and records a redirect to the successor website
by August 1, 2015. These captures support storefront observation bounds and the
2015 transition; they do not independently establish client or contract dates,
Jamie's component-level responsibility, or present maintenance of the successor
website. Finer-grained implementation claims draw from Jamie's account and
protected project records and remain subject to corroboration.

## What became usable

- a searchable and navigable product catalog;
- online ordering and account workflows;
- digital publishing and store voice;
- analytics and conversion visibility;
- operational support that could continue after Jamie moved away.

## Public-safe workflow specimen

The portfolio projects a derived maintenance-to-handoff sequence: listen for a
recurring customer or team question; frame a clear requirement; implement an
incremental change; verify the public path and available signals with
stakeholders; and preserve reusable patterns, ownership, and next actions for
staff and later maintainers.

This is an editorial reconstruction from the governed claim set, not an
original company document. It publishes no customer records, private analytics,
revenue detail, vendor terms, credentials, or sensitive operating practices.

## Boundary

Do not attribute company outcomes solely to Jamie. The system depended on the
Sackin family's ownership and product knowledge, extensive photography and
content labor, staff operations, customers, vendors, and later maintainers.
Public company reporting does not independently prove every component Jamie
recalls building. The live successor website may be linked as present-day
business context, but it is not evidence of Jamie's current stewardship.
