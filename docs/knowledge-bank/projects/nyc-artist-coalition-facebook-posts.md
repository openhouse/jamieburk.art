# NYC Artist Coalition Facebook post population

Reviewed: July 15, 2026

## Answer first

An authenticated, terminal-scroll pass recovered every distinct post Facebook
exposed through the NYC Artist Coalition Page feed on the capture date: **445
dated posts from January 29, 2017 through September 15, 2021**. The record
documents a sustained coalition communications surface connecting cultural-space
survival, Cabaret Law repeal, M.A.R.C.H. accountability, nightlife governance,
commercial-rent advocacy, relief work, artists, agencies, elected officials, and
published media.

This finding is intentionally narrower than a lifetime archive. A native Meta
owner export was not available, so deleted, hidden, private, unpublished, or
no-longer-retained posts may be absent. The shared Page identity also does not
identify the historical human author of each post.

## Population and method

Repeated authenticated lazy scrolling reached the bottom of the live Page feed.
Seven consecutive terminal checks produced no new records or document-height
growth. Private platform-generated post identities reconciled 153 duplicate
render variants among 598 encountered rows, producing 445 distinct posts. Those
private identities, authenticated URLs, and raw bodies are excluded from the
public repository. Each public row instead carries a unique one-way
reconciliation hash derived from its private platform identity. All 445 hashes
are unique. They make row distinction auditable without disclosing the
underlying identity; content hashes may repeat when captured bodies or source
cards repeat and are not used as the row identity.

For this report, **100%** means 100% of the distinct posts exposed by the
authenticated capture-date feed. It does **not** mean a complete lifetime
history or a native owner export.

## Civic interfaces

Overlapping stakeholder classifications connect 256 rows to artists, cultural
spaces, or organizers; 66 to NYC Council or elected-official subjects; 66 to
enforcement or regulatory agencies; 35 to published media; 33 to the Office of
Nightlife or nightlife-governance subjects; and 15 to Cultural Affairs or other
city cultural-policy interfaces.

Within those rows, public account references appeared for Rafael Espinal in 23,
NYC Council in 25, Stephen Levin in eight, Antonio Reynoso in one, Cultural
Affairs in four, the Office of Nightlife in four, and the mayor in three.
These are references, tags, routes, or issue interfaces. They do **not**
establish that the named accounts engaged with, endorsed, or formally partnered
with NYC Artist Coalition. Reaction and comment identities were not captured,
so incoming engagement by stakeholder group remains unmeasured.

## Displayed interaction signals

At capture time Facebook displayed reactions on 371 posts and comments on 128,
for 2,291 reactions and 212 comments in total. The largest displayed reaction
count was 95 on an October 30, 2017 Cabaret Law repeal post; the largest comment
count was seven. Share counts were not displayed on this surface.

These are volatile interface labels. They are not reach, attendance, conversion,
endorsement, unique people, mandate, or policy impact. Zero displayed shares
does not mean no sharing occurred.

A posted source documents distribution through the Page. It is not automatically

historically nonexistent.

## Authorship and stewardship

Jamie remembers that he predominantly used the Page while believing other
collaborators also used it. That participant memory is preserved as a research
lead, not promoted as settled fact. Current authenticated management access
establishes current custody only. Collaborator testimony or native publishing
records are needed to distinguish account creation, identity design, publishing,
moderation, campaign work, and period-specific stewardship.

The defensible public interpretation remains collective: the corpus documents a
durable NYC Artist Coalition communications and civic-relay system. It does not
support saying Jamie authored every post.
