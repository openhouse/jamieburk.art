# Talks Not Raids

Talks Not Raids addressed New York City's Multi-Agency Response to Community
Hotspots enforcement through a public campaign for transparency, notice, and a
cooperative path to compliance. Its website connected issue explanation,
source material, a Council call script, sponsor tracking, coalition support,
testimony, and reporting.

## Jamie's role

Jamie built the campaign website and represented NYC Artist Coalition in 2019
City Council testimony. He called for transparent, cooperative alternatives to
surprise raids and described coalition fire-safety walk-throughs, workshops,
and study groups. Official hearing records also show Council Chair Rory Lancman
using the coalition's FOIL-derived analysis to question NYPD and proposing a
follow-up meeting with the coalition.

## What became usable

- a public explanation of an otherwise difficult-to-see enforcement system;
- a constituent action path tied to Introduction 1156 and its sponsors;
- source-backed material for Council oversight and public testimony;
- a communication-first alternative grounded in support and correction.

Introduction 1156 became Local Law 220 of 2019, requiring reporting and advance
notice around MARCH operations. In 2023 the City phased out MARCH and announced
CURE, a communication-first compliance model, while acknowledging NYC Artist
Coalition among advocates. These are institutional and collective outcomes, not
proof that one campaign or person caused them alone.

## Present-grounded source return

The July 19, 2026 encounter reread the live campaign site, the official Council
hearing and legislative records, and the City's 2023 CURE announcement. The
sources strengthen the implementation and government-interface account while
preserving a clear anti-causality boundary.
