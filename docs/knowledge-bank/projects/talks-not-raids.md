# Talks Not Raids and M.A.R.C.H.: bounded public record

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-13

**Public projection:** One Jamie-centered sequence selected for
`/work/fair-rent-nyc`; government-only outcome claims remain held.

## What the sources establish

1. A City-preserved March 2017 NYC Artist Coalition submission asked for
   transparency about M.A.R.C.H. operations and target selection and for
   alternatives to raids when addressing noncompliance.
2. Retained Git history documents Jamie's repository-level implementation and
   maintenance of the Talks Not Raids campaign site.
3. The NYC Government Publications Portal identifies M.A.R.C.H. as an NYPD-led
   multi-agency inspection program and preserves the first biannual report
   published under Local Law 220.
4. The Council enacted Int 1156-A as Local Law 220 on December 15, 2019, with
   19 sponsors. The law required semiannual machine-readable reporting and at
   least 30 days' notice before an operation, with investigation and serious
   health-or-safety exceptions.
5. The Office of Nightlife's 2022 report documents centralized reporting and
   notice implementation and says cooperative notice helped reduce operations.
   That effect is an institutional self-assessment, not an independent causal
   study.
6. The Office's 2023-2024 report states that the City replaced M.A.R.C.H. with
   CURE in December 2023. It describes direct, in-person communication and
   opportunities to address conditions before enforcement.

## Agency boundary

- Jamie implemented and maintained campaign web infrastructure.
- NYC Artist Coalition and campaign partners conducted collective advocacy.
- The New York City Council enacted Local Law 220.
- The Office of Nightlife and City agencies implemented reporting, notice, and
  the later CURE process.

The chronology is defensible. The available public record does not quantify
the causal contribution of Jamie, NYC Artist Coalition, Talks Not Raids
partners, any Council sponsor, or any other advocate to the later government
decisions.

## Public-safe wording

Jamie implemented and maintained Talks Not Raids, giving the coalition's
demand for transparency and alternatives to raids a durable public campaign
site. The campaign concerned Multi-Agency Response to Community Hotspots
(M.A.R.C.H.), an NYPD-led inspection program. The Council later required
semiannual reports and 30 days' advance notice, with criminal-investigation and
serious health-or-safety exceptions. The Office of Nightlife reports that the City
replaced M.A.R.C.H. with direct engagement before enforcement in 2023.

## Do not say

- Jamie disbanded M.A.R.C.H.
- NYC Artist Coalition alone ended M.A.R.C.H.
- Jamie authored Local Law 220.
- Repository authorship proves sole authorship of coalition policy, copy, data,
  images, or design.
- CURE eliminates enforcement or guarantees an outcome for every venue.
