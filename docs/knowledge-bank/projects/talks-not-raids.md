# Talks Not Raids

Talks Not Raids was a coalition campaign seeking transparency, notice, and
more direct problem-solving around New York City's Multi-Agency Response to
Community Hotspots program. Its public website connected venue testimony,
reporting, a Council bill, a call script, sponsor tracking, coalition identity,
and campaign media.

## Jamie's role

The official February 11, 2019 Council transcript identifies Jamie as an NYC
Artist Coalition member. It records him asking for Talks Not Raids, public
MARCH information, and direct work between the Office of Nightlife, cultural
liaisons, and establishments. The approved portfolio record also supports his
contribution to the campaign's public web infrastructure.

Protected coalition records show an evidence workflow moving among public-
record research, hearing preparation, fact checking, web explanation, and
legislative advocacy. They do not assign every calculation, artifact, or line
of copy to Jamie.

## What became usable

- Experiences that were difficult to see as a citywide pattern gained a common
  campaign vocabulary and public route.
- A bill number, Council contact path, sponsor state, and call script made the
  requested action concrete.
- Public reporting and venue accounts were assembled beside the action rather
  than separated into an inaccessible research archive.
- Council testimony placed Jamie's requests and coalition affiliation in an
  official record.

## Outcomes and chronology

Intro 1156-A became Local Law 220 of 2019. The law required reporting on MARCH
operations and, with specified exceptions, advance notice and an opportunity
for establishments to provide relevant information. In 2023 the City announced
that an engagement-first CURE process replaced MARCH.

These are aligned institutional outcomes in the campaign's chronology. The
record does not establish that Jamie, NYC Artist Coalition, or Talks Not Raids
alone caused enactment or replacement. Nor does replacement establish that
every form of multi-agency nightlife enforcement ended.
