# Save NYC Spaces

Save NYC Spaces gathered cultural-space priorities around the creation of New
York City's Office of Nightlife. The campaign framed small, diverse spaces as
civic and cultural infrastructure and joined concerns that were often handled
separately: criminalization, regulatory navigation, displacement, and
commercial rent.

## Jamie's role

Jamie built the campaign website within NYC Artist Coalition's public identity
system and contributed his own public cultural-space framing. His broader
coalition work helped connect campaign pages, town halls, testimony, public
resources, and follow-through. The site also carries many collaborators' voices;
their contributions should remain attributed to them.

## What became usable

- a concise public agenda for the new Office of Nightlife;
- a campaign letter, action route, coalition list, and reporting context;
- named support needs including a path to legal compliance and trusted help;
- a bridge from the 2017 town hall to longer-running cultural-space advocacy.

The City created the Office of Nightlife in 2017. The sources support the
coalition's participation in the public process, while reserving legislative
sponsorship, enactment, and agency decisions to the officials and institutions
responsible for them.

## Present-grounded source return

The July 19, 2026 encounter returned to the live campaign site, the original
town-hall page, and the official record creating the Office of Nightlife. The
site's surviving structure makes clear that this was not a single-issue slogan:
it was a public requirements document for how an office could serve grassroots
cultural spaces.
