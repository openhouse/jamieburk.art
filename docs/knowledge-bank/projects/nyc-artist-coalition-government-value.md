# NYC Artist Coalition: Government Interface

## Current Defensible Record

Official City records show NYC Artist Coalition operating as a continuing
interface between informal cultural spaces and government in 2017-2019.

At the [May 19, 2017 Council budget hearing](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/View.ashx?GUID=68A2E207-53EA-43CA-A03E-F879A113464E&ID=5271559&M=F),
DCLA Commissioner Tom Finkelpearl named NYC Artist Coalition while describing
direct public feedback and common-cause collaboration. The
[DCLA commissioner page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dcla/about/message-from-the-commissioner.page)
and [CreateNYC plan](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dcla/downloads/pdf/cultureplan/createnyc-finalplan.pdf)
also treated the coalition as a result of public engagement. A
[City-hosted March 2017 coalition letter](https://createnyc.cityofnewyork.us/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/CreateNYC_Appendix-Sect5_NYC-Artist-Coalition-DIY-Community.pdf)
shows that participants continued from the January meeting into collective
recommendations about DCLA partnership with informal community-driven spaces.

Jamie appears in two recovered Council transcripts. At the
[September 14, 2017 Cabaret Law hearing](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/View.ashx?GUID=817A7573-F329-49C4-849A-347B6D00CBC5&ID=5444683&M=F),
he connected personal experience, cultural expression, fire-safety work,
compliance barriers, and trust; Rafael Espinal thanked him for his story and
advocacy. At the
[March 18, 2019 Small Business hearing](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/View.ashx?GUID=A217E78A-034D-4EE7-ACF4-F4A8DC1F9B16&ID=7230194&M=F),
Espinal acknowledged a multi-year working relationship with the coalition, and
Jamie proposed public datasets and online tools to help cultural spaces
navigate legality, safety, and compliance.

## Institutional Interpretation

The sources support a bounded analysis: the coalition made an informal cultural
constituency more legible and reachable to government. It translated lived
conditions into recommendations, testimony, participation systems, and
implementation feedback.

- **DCLA:** gained a continuing counterpart for direct feedback from spaces not
  always represented through funded-institution channels, plus a concrete
  example that public listening could produce durable civic capacity.
- **Council:** gained field knowledge, public testimony, constituency, and
  implementable recommendations connecting cultural-space experience to
  legislation and oversight.
- **Espinal:** benefited from sustained contact with a prepared constituency
  able to connect lived experience, operational detail, and public
  participation around nightlife policy.

These are source-backed interpretations, not direct statements that any agency,
legislative body, or official was dependent on Jamie or the coalition.

## Public-Use Boundary

Use **recognized**, **worked closely**, **supplied testimony**, **developed
recommendations**, and **created a continuing civic interface**. Do not convert
that evidence into claims that DCLA owned the coalition, Jamie authored the
relevant laws, the coalition alone caused an outcome, or government could not
have acted without it.

One explicit Finkelpearl Council reference has been recovered. A complete
Council transcript denominator remains open, so do not call it the only
instance.
