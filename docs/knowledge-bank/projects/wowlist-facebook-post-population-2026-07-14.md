## Answer First

Two terminal traversals of WOW List's current public Facebook
timeline recovered the same **53 distinct top-level post units**, with no
forward-only or reverse-only records. This is a complete accounting of the
current public timeline surface. It is not a native Meta export and not a claim
that WOW List created only 53 Facebook posts in its lifetime.

The corpus strengthens a specific understanding of the project. WOW List did
not use social media only to advertise its own software. Its Page repeatedly
routed people toward local community calendars, touring artists, small cultural
spaces, mutual-aid campaigns, civic-action calendars, and conversations about
how the platform itself should work. The surviving record shows a public
identity joining event discovery, community stewardship, and civic
participation.

## What The Posts Preserve

### Community calendars and local stewardship

Posts invited people to create accounts, add events, join weekly calendar
emails, and establish city or scene-specific calendars. Recoverable routes
include Phoenix, Los Angeles, Santa Barbara, Seattle, Chicago, San Francisco,
touring projects, and individual cultural programs. Community members described
WOW List as an alternative to Facebook event discovery and as a tool for
participating more fully in local life.

The account stated that members in nine cities introduced community calendars
and that one Los Angeles organizer added 41 upcoming DIY shows. The archive
directly supports that WOW List published these figures. This pass did not
independently audit the exact nine-city or 41-show counts, so they remain
account-stated scale evidence rather than promoted platform metrics.

### Cultural-space support and mutual aid

The Page amplified grant information, fundraising, post-fire recovery,
relocation needs, and public-support arguments for DIY and all-ages spaces.
Examples include Meow Wolf's DIY-space fund, The Trunk Space, The Silent Barn,
and a Southern California campaign for an all-ages, safer, sober,
community-driven arts and music space.

These routes show an information-support practice. Posting a route does not
establish that a reader donated, attended, joined, or adopted the platform.

### Event discovery as civic participation

WOW List routed readers to Women's March, Water Is Life, resistance, and other
civic-event calendars. It also amplified NYC Artist Coalition's Let NYC Dance
campaign, explained the Cabaret Law issue, and invited readers to contact the
New York City Council. This is evidence of outbound civic routing by WOW List.
This Page population does not establish reverse engagement by Council-member
accounts.

### Participatory product and community-design practice

One post invited members into a kitchen-to-kitchen video conversation about how
the site could improve. Two surviving post units preserve a member quotation
attributing to Jamie a community-first account of WOW List design. The
quotation is evidence that a member publicly attributed this idea to Jamie; it
is not an independently verified transcript, nor does it establish sole design
authorship.

Together, these records are useful evidence of an open participation structure:
the project invited community members to add events, establish local calendars,
teach one another, and help shape the service.
