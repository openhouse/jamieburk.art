# NYC Artist Coalition Facebook Post Archival Production

Date: 2026-07-14

## Question

What does the complete surviving NYC Artist Coalition Facebook Page-post
population establish about participation infrastructure, campaign continuity,
source routing, stakeholder addressing, visible response, and Jamie's role
without converting Page identity into individual authorship or social activity
into impact?

## Population Control

Two browser-based Page traversals used distinct scroll cadences across
824 combined scroll operations. They recovered the same **444 distinct
surviving Page-post identities**, spanning January 29, 2017, through September
15, 2021. The pass was accepted only after the exact identity sets matched and
42 and 41 terminal scroll controls produced no additional records.

Every recovered record received a public-safe disposition. The population
ledger retains only a stable ID, sequence, form, primary theme, classification
counts, and a visible-interaction boolean. Raw text, comments, names, the full
post-URL set, per-record metrics, media, relationship data, account state,
authentication material, and sensitive or stale action routes remain outside
the repository. Only the three public records selected for close reading below
are cited by URL.

This is 100 percent disposition coverage of the surviving July 2026 Page
surface. It is not a native Meta export, deletion history, or proof that no
historical record is missing.

## Separate Content Control

A separately recovered content-control comparison did not expose a historic
human-level publisher field and was not used as the denominator for the
2017-2021 public chronology.

All 444 timeline records displayed a Page-level action control for NYC Artist
Coalition. That identifies the publishing identity, not the human who created,
published, or wrote a particular post.

## Campaign And Participation Infrastructure

The corpus preserves continuity across:

- Let NYC Dance and Cabaret Law repeal;
- Save NYC Spaces and the Office of Nightlife public interface;
- Talks Not Raids and M.A.R.C.H. transparency;
- FairRentNYC and commercial-rent advocacy;
- recurring coalition meetings and public hearings; and
- COVID-19 relief, legal-information, and operational-resource routes.

This is a collective Page-level practice. The record does not assign every
post, meeting, campaign, event, quotation, or design decision to Jamie.

Three close-read public records show the range:

- A [Cabaret Law post](https://www.facebook.com/photo/?fbid=1945640715690307&set=a.544849644343446)
  tags Jamie and Council Member Rafael Espinal while connecting repeal, venue
  safety, and trust between cultural spaces and city government.
- A [Talks Not Raids post](https://www.facebook.com/photo/?fbid=2225985110989198&set=a.544849644343446)
  preserves Olympia Kazi's safety-and-transparency frame and routes readers to
  the campaign site. Olympia retains authorship and credit for her words.
- A [COVID-19 know-your-rights video](https://www.facebook.com/nycartc/videos/632085217644541/)
  routes cultural-space participants toward practical rent, tenant, and
  small-business legal information. It is historical, not current legal
  guidance or proof of service outcomes.

## Jamie's Role

Jamie's first-person account is accessioned precisely:

> Jamie remembers being the predominant person using the NYC Artist Coalition
> Facebook Page while also remembering that other coalition participants used
> it.

That memory is important and remains research-stage. The current interface
does not expose the human publisher for historic posts. A native Meta Page
export, administrator chronology, and collaborator proof notes are the next
evidence needed to quantify or promote the Page-publishing claim.

This pass does not use account activity to strengthen Jamie's individual role.
Existing public sources about Jamie's coalition safety and advocacy work remain
available elsewhere in the bank, with their own evidence and collective-credit
boundaries. The Facebook corpus contributes historical context and research
routes, not a shortcut around those boundaries.

## Posted Sources And Routes

The rendered corpus exposes 64 outbound-link occurrences resolving to 39 raw
unique URLs and 33 normalized public-safe routes. The routes include coalition
campaign sites, Council hearing and livestream pages, public-information tools,
event and cultural organizations, press, practical resources, fundraising
pages, and relief information. Two historical meeting-access or form
destinations remain represented without a public URL.

These enter the lifecycle as source-discovery or action-routing leads. A posted
URL is not automatic corroboration of its propositions and does not establish
authorship, readership, click-through, conversion, formal partnership, or
impact. Existing campaign-press and event records are reused rather than
duplicated when the same source is already in the bank.

Four routes are connected to source records. Existing New York Times and WNYC
Cabaret Law records are reused. Two new article records preserve additional
context:

- Rafael Espinal, Jr.,
  [A compromise for nightlife venues and their neighbors](https://www.cityandstateny.com/opinion/2018/09/a-compromise-for-nightlife-venues-and-their-neighbors/178085/),
  *City & State New York*, September 26, 2018. Espinal describes an Agent of
  Change proposal for soundproofing responsibility around new residential and
  nightlife construction. It is policy context, not evidence of enactment,
  Jamie's role, or coalition impact.
- The Seattle Times,
  [City announces $1.1 million and rent relief to support arts organizations in the coronavirus economic crisis](https://www.seattletimes.com/entertainment/city-announces-1-1-million-and-rent-relief-to-support-arts-organizations-in-the-coronavirus-economic-crisis/).
  The Page routed the article as comparative relief context, but it could not
  be accessed for close reading in this research environment. It remains a
  queued lead and supports no portfolio claim.

## Visible Response Boundary

On July 14, 2026, 389 of 444 records retained at least one visible reaction,
comment, or share. The currently visible counters sum to a floor of:

- 2,374 reactions;
- 212 comments; and
- 611 shares.

These are mutable interface observations. They may include attention to
reshared source material and do not identify unique people. They are not
historical analytics, reach, attendance, conversion, stakeholder endorsement,
causality, or impact.

The stronger mission-relevant finding is structural: over several years, the
Page repeatedly connected people, meetings, campaigns, institutions, sources,
and practical resources through a coherent collective identity.

## Anti-Claims

- Do not call this a native Meta export or deletion history.
- Do not say NYC Artist Coalition published only 444 Facebook posts.
- Do not say Jamie published or authored all 444 records.
- Do not infer a human publisher from the Page-level action control.
- Do not say Jamie was the sole Page administrator or sole author of the
  coalition's public voice.
- Do not transfer credit from Olympia Kazi, collaborators, event hosts,
  sources, campaigns, Council members, agencies, or cultural spaces.
- Do not convert tags, mentions, links, quotations, or reshares into verified
  stakeholder engagement or endorsement.
- Do not convert current counters into historical analytics, unique people,
  adoption, reach, attendance, conversion, policy influence, or impact.
- Do not infer policy causality from the social record.

## Lifecycle Decisions

- The 444-post population, form, theme, route, stakeholder, and response-floor
  claims remain corroborated reserve knowledge.
- Jamie's memory of predominant Page use remains research-stage, with no
  public projection.
- All mature claims remain deferred from the website pending audience-specific
  composition and human editorial review.
- A native Meta export and collaborator proof notes remain open research routes
  for deleted-post accounting, administrator chronology, and division of
  publishing labor.
