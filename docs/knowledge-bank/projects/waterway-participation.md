# Waterway Participation

## Current Defensible Record

Public reporting currently supports three parts of a longer practice:

- In 2006, *Good Times* reported that Jamie initiated *Open House*, a temporary
  communal-living experiment in a UCSC gallery that extended the participatory
  Shop Shows. The same account describes responsibility and decision-making as
  communal, so Jamie's initiating role should not become sole-leader language.
- In 2007, *The Pitch* and *The Kansas City Star* independently reported that
  Jamie originated the idea for a recycled-material river expedition connecting
  participatory art, Kansas City transportation history, and the city's
  relationship with the river. Public Facebook event records add dated traces
  of a July 9 raft-design discussion and a July 14 public sendoff. *The Star*
  reports a July 21 departure from the West Bottoms and places the three-person
  crew south of Baton Rouge by November 15. A later *Pitch* follow-up reports
  that the collective crew reached the Gulf of Mexico after four months.
- Charlotte Street's archive lists *Great Accommodations with Jamie Burkart -
  Imagining Lifestyles for Cities on the Water* for September 1 through October
  3, 2009.

The *Star* article also documents the operating form: an approximately
12-by-13-foot raft built from discarded building materials, civic refuse, and
plastic syrup drums; two bicycles connected to a paddlewheel; encounters and
invitations aboard along the route; a 51-day interruption near Vicksburg; and
legal, material, and social support that helped the crew resume the journey.
These are facts about the collective system and journey, not evidence that
Jamie personally designed or performed every component.

Together, the records support a durable professional throughline: Jamie creates
participatory structures through which people can examine how they inhabit a
place together. His contemporaneous comments to *The Star* frame the river as
civic, infrastructural, and cultural space and propose connection between the
West Bottoms and Delta communities. The sources do not establish every route,
participant, individual contribution, method, or measurable outcome across the
full body of water-based work.
