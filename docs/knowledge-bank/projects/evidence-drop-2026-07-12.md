## Source-backed findings

### Participatory art and shared space

A 2006 Good Times profile documents Jamie's Open House at UC Santa Cruz and
the earlier Shop Shows held with housemates from 2003 through 2005. The article
supports a long-running practice of inviting people to contribute, revise, and
live alongside art. It also requires collaborative credit and does not establish
a formal housing or social-service program.

Source:
[Good Times Santa Cruz](https://www.goodtimes.sc/archives/metro-santa-cruz/06.28.06/open-house-0626.html)

### Raft expedition and river-city work

The Pitch attributes the idea for an experiential raft expedition to Jamie and
describes a collaborative raft made from recycled materials. Charlotte Street's
2009 institutional record describes a four-month journey down the Missouri and
Mississippi until the water tasted salty, then documents Great Accommodations as
a participatory installation and public-program series spearheaded by Jamie.

The Charlotte Street record names Suzanne Hogan as Jamie's collaborator on
letter-based river-city outreach and describes hundreds of invitations for
people to contribute stories. The complete raft route, participant map, and
community-host record remain open.

Sources:
[The Pitch](https://www.thepitchkc.com/when-artists-turn-huck-finn/) and
[Charlotte Street](https://charlottestreet.org/event/great-accommodations-with-jamie-burkart-imagining-lifestyles-for-cities-on-the-water/)

### Cabaret Law organizing

Gothamist directly reports that Jamie organized fire-code study groups for DIY
venues and rallied at City Hall for repeal. The official Council hearing
transcript records Jamie identifying himself as a NYC Artist Coalition member,
asking the Council to repeal the law, and advocating for trust-based safety and
representation for lower-income communities.

These sources support Jamie's direct organizing and testimony. Repeal remains a
collective advocacy and legislative outcome.

Sources:
[Gothamist](https://gothamist.com/news/diy-venues-demand-repeal-of-widely-reviled-cabaret-law)
and the
[New York City Council transcript](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/View.ashx?GUID=41F1062B-FC32-4A12-846E-65CEB3BB052C&ID=5316935&M=F)

### Office of Nightlife and public input

NPR documents enactment of the Office of Nightlife in September 2017. The
Village Voice attributes the later Save NYC Spaces town hall to NYC Artist
Coalition and describes a gathering of about 100 people intended to connect
smaller performance spaces with city agencies and shape implementation of the
new office.

The coalition's public priorities document a desire for an office responsive
to diverse, neighborhood-scale spaces. The reviewed sources do not yet
establish Jamie's specific production responsibilities or prove that coalition
advocacy alone caused enactment.

Sources:
[NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/therecord/2017/09/20/552292586/with-its-no-dancing-law-verging-on-repeal-new-york-legitimizes-its-nightlife?renderPlatform=nprone_ios&unified=true),
[The Village Voice](https://www.villagevoice.com/awaiting-the-night-mayor/), and
[NYC Artist Coalition priorities](https://nycartc.com/priorities/)

### Talks Not Raids and MARCH

The Talks Not Raids project archive documents advocacy for transparency and
communication around raid-style MARCH operations. A later Council bill required
MARCH reporting and notice. In 2023, the Mayor's Office announced that MARCH
would be phased out in favor of an engagement-first approach.

The sequence is well supported. Jamie's individual role and the complete causal
pathway from campaign advocacy to legislation and phaseout remain research
questions.

Sources:
[Talks Not Raids](https://talksnotraids.com/),
[New York City Council Introduction 1156-2018](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=6A35ADA6-86E7-40B0-AD39-5B6E376FD23F&ID=3704342&Options=ID%7CText%7C&Search=1156),
and the
[Mayor's Office phaseout announcement](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2023/12/mayor-adams-launches-effort-enhance-nightlife-safety-strengthen-small-businesses-phasing)
