# KC Town Hall Phase One restoration, 2018-2019

Last reviewed: July 15, 2026

## Answer first

A protected 2019 KC Town Hall CCED proposal packet records Phase One cold-shell
work as completed in 2019. Its detailed scope includes roof deck repair,
insulation and TPO membrane, masonry repair, structural floor framing, water
service, basement egress, site safety, air-quality control, debris removal, and
related work.

Jamie states that he served as Phase One's general contractor in addition to
his other project duties. He describes hiring and coordinating historic brick
masonry, roofing, carpentry, welding, engineering, architecture, plumbing, and
related teams through daily work on site. The packet corroborates the
multidisciplinary scope and completion state but does not independently assign
the general-contractor title or responsibility map to Jamie.

Jamie also states that he created a four-by-six-inch neighborhood survey
handbill and backing data-collection system. The packet reproduces the survey,
names Oak Park Neighborhood Association and New Horizon Missionary Baptist
Church as process partners, and says survey results directly shaped the
proposal. A Council support letter included in the packet says the survey
influenced proposed retail uses.

## Phase One scope recorded in the packet

The packet's Finance 10-11 pages distinguish Phase One as a cold shell and mark
it completed in 2019. The detailed categories are:

- roof deck repair, energy-efficient insulation, and TPO membrane;
- masonry and brickwork repair;
- structural repairs to ceilings and floors;
- water-service connection;
- secure construction storage;
- trash and debris removal;
- tree removal for access and building protection;
- restoration of basement egress;
- transportation of materials;
- site safety and security;
- particulate and moisture control;
- tools, materials, and soft costs.

The packet's preceding local-industry page presents an active 2018-2019 timeline
for roof and facade work. It states that the project hired more than fifteen
local minority-owned contractors in 2018 and describes masonry tuckpointing as
a skills-training focus. These are project-prepared statements, not an
independent contractor census or employment audit.

## Participatory requirements process

The packet describes an ongoing neighborhood survey conducted with Oak Park
Neighborhood Association and New Horizon Missionary Baptist Church. The
handbill asks residents to select desired uses for the building, contribute an
additional idea, and optionally provide contact and participation information.

The packet says results directly shaped the proposal. A January 2019 Council
support letter included in the packet says the survey influenced proposed
retail uses. Those statements support a participatory requirements process;
they do not establish response count, sampling method, representativeness,
consensus, or adoption of every response.

No participant response, identity, contact field, or raw data is stored in this
repository.

## Site-based listening

Jamie describes his daily presence at the active restoration site as an
informal listening practice. As the long-abandoned building received visible
care, neighbors stopped to share histories, appreciation, and ideas for what
the place could become. This account adds situated qualitative context to the
formal survey system.

This is a first-person account, not a representative study. Do not reconstruct
or attribute quotations, identify participants, quantify sentiment, or convert
informal encounters into a neighborhood-consensus claim.
