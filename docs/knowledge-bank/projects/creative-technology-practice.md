# Creative Technology Practice

**Editorial status:** Reserve

**Period documented:** 2006-2016

This project record preserves independently documented creative-technology
work without displacing the portfolio's current technical project management,
product operations, and implementation argument.

## Historical Interpretive Context

A protected, unofficial UCSC narrative-evaluation copy supplies historical
context for how parts of this early practice were recognized in 2004-2006.
Margaret Morse's records support an editorial lens concerned with media
archaeology, embodiment, attention, time, and artistic practice. Warren Sack's
records support an editorial lens concerned with recursive social structure,
working prototypes, interface design, physical-digital interaction, multimodal
documentation, dialogue, and collective authorship.

The records are not public testimonials, current endorsements, or proof of
current proficiency. Raw text, grades, identifiers, correspondence, contact
details, and private locators remain protected. Current portfolio claims still
depend on the public project sources below. Those sources preserve viewer
participation in *Time Is Long*, NTER CHNG's software-and-architecture form,
its public many-to-many social information space, its shared three-person
credit, and the later Max/MSP work without assigning individual ownership where
the evidence does not.

## 2006: Time Is Long

[Cool Hunting](https://coolhunting.com/culture/bap-lab-part-1/) identifies
Jamie with *Time is Long*, an installation that records viewers and returns
their image approximately 20 minutes later through an extended VHS process.

**Supported:** artist attribution, viewer participation, and the time-delay
mechanism.

**Boundary:** the article does not provide complete engineering, fabrication,
exhibition, audience, or media-rights records.

## 2010-2011: NTER CHNG

[The Pitch](https://www.thepitchkc.com/ntr-chng/) describes NTER CHNG as equal
parts software application and architectural installation, with audience text
messages appearing on a digital wall in real time. The public
[project video](https://vimeo.com/21395655) credits Drew Bolton, Jamie Burkart,
and Garrett Fuselier with the interactive texting installation and separately
credits construction and performance collaborators.

The [archived project
site](https://web.archive.org/web/20110128193350/http://nterchng.com/) preserves
the same three-person maker credit. America: Now and Here's official archived
[Kansas City artist
page](https://web.archive.org/web/20110518071626/http://kansascity.americanowandhere.org/the-visual-artists/drew-bolton-jamie-burkart-and-garrett-fuselier/)
lists the collaborators as visual artists and documents NTER CHNG in the 2011
exhibition. The exhibition's first-party [I Text, Therefore I
Am](https://web.archive.org/web/20121017090512/http://americanowandhere.org/2011/06/i-text-therefore-i-am/)
describes visitors using the text-message input and projected display.

The [Nerman Museum exhibition
page](https://www.nermanmuseum.org/exhibitions/2011-05-11-kruger-barbara-america-now-here.html)
supplies institutional context for the Kansas City launch. It does not itself
name NTER CHNG, so it is not used as the direct inclusion source.

Two protected, contemporaneous-origin project documents add operational and
conceptual depth. An April 2011 installer plan records a coordinated workflow
across permissions, software queue behavior, hosting, display hardware,
architectural fabrication, wiring, networking, and gallery tuning. A second
project-text document preserves the shared three-person credit and describes
the interaction as turning private one-to-one text exchange into a public
many-to-many social information space. Their links and raw contents remain
protected.

**Supported:** collaborative maker credit, the software-and-architecture form,
real-time text participation, America: Now and Here exhibition inclusion,
observed visitor use, and a planned production system spanning software,
hosting, display hardware, fabrication, wiring, networking, and gallery tuning.

**Boundary:** the public records do not establish the detailed division of
software, design, construction, or production labor. This is not a solo-credit
claim. Observed use is not a quantified audience or impact claim. Archived
phone numbers and participant-submitted messages are excluded, and archive
access does not grant media republication rights. The installer is a plan, not
proof that every task was completed or that any one collaborator performed it.
The protected documents were modified in 2026 and are not treated as frozen
2011 revisions; their Drive links, private production details, and full text
are withheld.

## 2013: A Sorted Audio File

[Monthly Music Hackathon](https://monthlymusichackathon.org/post/44177616179/sortedaudio)
says Jamie made a Max/MSP program that divided audio into small segments and
sorted them according to a selected clip feature, demonstrated by ordering
song fragments by pitch.

**Supported:** maker attribution, Max/MSP implementation, and the documented
segment-and-sort behavior.

**Boundary:** the source documents a hack and demonstration, not production
deployment, commercial adoption, complete code history, or impact.

## Bounded Throughline

Independent public sources document Jamie's creative-technology work at four
intervals across a decade: time-delayed video, real-time text interaction,
Max/MSP audio transformation, and collaborative music visuals.

This is not proof of uninterrupted full-time practice. Each project retains
its own collaborators, context, authorship, and rights. The knowledge bank
holds the throughline in reserve until a future audience need makes it more
useful than the site's current composition.
