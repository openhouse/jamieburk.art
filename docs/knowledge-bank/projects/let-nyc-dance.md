# Let NYC Dance

Let NYC Dance translated the movement to repeal New York City's Cabaret Law
into a legible participation route. The surviving campaign site connected a
Council call script, bill and sponsor tracking, explanatory material, coalition
credit, public sharing, and a press collection rather than asking people to
infer how to act from a policy position alone.

## Jamie's role

Jamie built the campaign website as part of the public identity system he
established for NYC Artist Coalition. He also testified before the City Council
as a coalition member, urging full repeal and a trust-based approach to safety
for community spaces. A protected digital guide confirms a coordinated action
system, but it does not assign Jamie sole authorship of the guide or campaign.

## What became usable

- a direct call path and reusable script for constituents;
- a visible relationship between the policy ask, bill, sponsors, and next
  action;
- a shared campaign surface that coalition partners could use;
- a durable press and public-record trail after the Council repealed the law in
  2017.

The repeal was a collective and legislative outcome. The evidence supports
Jamie's website, testimony, safety-study, and coalition contributions; it does
not support saying that Jamie or NYC Artist Coalition accomplished repeal
alone.

## Present-grounded source return

The July 19, 2026 encounter returned to the live campaign site, where the call
script, action architecture, sponsor interface, coalition list, and repeal
update remain visible. It also checked the official hearing and repeal record
and the governed summary of the campaign guide. The present reading strengthens
the participation-system claim while retaining collective credit.
