# Let NYC Dance

Let NYC Dance was a multi-group campaign to repeal New York City's 1926
Cabaret Law. Its public surface connected policy explanation, a fact sheet,
maps, a Council sponsor tracker, calls to action, press, social sharing, and
coalition credit. The Council repealed the law in 2017 after a broad campaign
involving Council Member Rafael Espinal, cultural-space operators, artists,
advocacy groups, Council sponsors, the Council, and the Mayor.

## Jamie's Supported Role

Public reporting identifies Jamie as a founding NYC Artist Coalition member,
documents his Cabaret Law advocacy and hearing participation, and supports his
contribution to the coalition's public campaign websites. The defensible account
joins organizing and public-interface work without assigning every artifact,
message, strategy, or legislative result to him.

## What Became Usable

- public explanation connecting history to a current bill;
- a call script and Council contact route;
- an inspectable sponsor state;
- shared maps, media, fact-sheet, press, and coalition-credit surfaces; and
- an account of repeal that did not pretend every dance-policy issue was over.

## Boundary

Jamie contributed within a multi-group campaign that preceded repeal. Neither
Jamie nor NYC Artist Coalition alone repealed the law. Sponsor tracking does
not prove private endorsement, constituent conversion, or causality. Campaign
media require creator, rights, caption, and credit review before reuse.
