# Let NYC Dance

Let NYC Dance was a multi-group campaign to repeal New York City's 1926
Cabaret Law. Its public surface connected historical and policy explanation,
a fact sheet, a map, a Council sponsor tracker, calls to action, press, social
sharing, and coalition credit. The Council repealed the law in 2017 after a
broad campaign involving Council Member Rafael Espinal, cultural-space
operators, artists, advocacy groups, Council sponsors, the Council, and the
Mayor.

## Jamie's role

Public reporting identifies Jamie as a founding member of NYC Artist Coalition
and documents him organizing fire-code study groups and rallying outside City
Hall for full repeal. The portfolio's approved role record separately supports
his contribution to the coalition's public campaign websites, including Let
NYC Dance.

The strongest defensible account joins those two forms of work: Jamie helped
people understand and act on a difficult policy environment, while working
inside a coalition whose members and partner organizations held distinct
organizing, legislative, venue, communications, and community roles.

## What became usable

- A public explanation connected discriminatory history to a current bill.
- A call script and Council contact route turned concern into a specific next
  action.
- A sponsor tracker made legislative progress inspectable.
- Maps, media assets, a fact sheet, and press links helped a distributed
  coalition communicate from shared public material.
- The campaign site preserved repeal as a collective outcome and identified
  unresolved zoning work rather than presenting repeal as the end of every
  dance-policy constraint.

## Outcome and attribution

The completed governmental outcome was repeal of the Cabaret Law. Jamie's
documented actions and web-infrastructure contribution belong in that history,
but the Council vote does not measure his causal share. Credit must retain the
Let NYC Dance coalition, individual organizers, venue communities, Council
sponsors, the Council, and the Mayor.

This distinction makes the accomplishment stronger, not weaker: Jamie helped
build a public operating surface through which collective action could become
legible and repeatable.

## Boundaries

- Say Jamie contributed within the multi-group campaign that preceded repeal.
- Do not say Jamie or NYC Artist Coalition alone repealed the Cabaret Law.
- Do not turn a campaign sponsor tracker into proof of private endorsement,
  constituent conversion, or causality.
- Keep unresolved zoning work distinct from the repealed Cabaret Law.
- Do not republish campaign media without creator, rights, caption, and credit
  review.
