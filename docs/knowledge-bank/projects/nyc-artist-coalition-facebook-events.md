# NYC Artist Coalition Facebook events

Reviewed: 2026-07-15

## Public-safe conclusion

NYC Artist Coalition used recurring events as participatory advocacy
infrastructure. Meetings rotated through small cultural spaces, connected
artist and venue concerns to public agencies and City Hall, and sustained work
across safety, Cabaret Law repeal, nightlife governance, MARCH transparency,
commercial rent, anti-displacement, and relief.

The current Facebook archive exposes 33 coalition-listed past events from
January 2017 through January 2021. This dossier preserves the full population
that the live Past Events surface materialized, not a new public route or an
owner export.

## Population reconciliation

Jamie's authenticated browser session was used to open the NYC Artist
Coalition Facebook page, select Past Events, and scroll until four repeated
no-growth rounds established a stable terminal state.

- Facebook displayed a page-level count of 34 past events.
- Nine scroll rounds exposed 33 unique event cards.
- Every one of the 33 exposed detail pages was opened and recovered.
- One event represented by the displayed count did not materialize.
- That remainder is recorded as unresolved, not absent or deleted.

The committed public fixture is
`apps/www/src/data/knowledge-bank/fixtures/nycartc-facebook-events-full-population.json`.
It preserves event identities, dates, titles, venues, displayed organizer
relations, durations, aggregate response snapshots, mission topics, selected
outbound resources, and seven event-posted source articles.

The fixture excludes raw event descriptions, attendee identities, historical
emails and phone numbers, meeting IDs and passcodes, private working-document
links, authenticated-session state, and local research paths. A protected raw
capture remains available for audit and future source decomposition.

## Event system

Twenty-four of the 33 event cards displayed NYC Artist Coalition as an
organizer. Nine appeared as allied or cohosted listings. Detail-page organizer
lines sometimes collapse multiple partners as "and others," so those records
are not used to infer a complete cohost roster or coalition ownership.

The exposed chronology moves among cultural and community spaces including:

- Magick City;
- The Floasis;
- Market Hotel;
- Muchmore's;
- The City Reliquary;
- Shoestring Press;
- Chinatown Soup;
- Secret Project Robot;
- Friends and Lovers;
- Flowers for all Occasions; and
- Ode to Babel.

The venues rotated, but were not always different. Market Hotel and some other
settings recur. The archive also includes Department of Cultural Affairs and
Fire Department sessions, City Hall hearings and rallies, public-realm events,
an educational workshop, and a transition to virtual relief programming.

The 2017 sequence is especially legible. It moves from DIY-space safety and
coalition meetings through fire-safety and legal study sessions, a Market Hotel
town hall, Cabaret Law hearings, a Night Mayor town hall, and the Council
repeal event. Later records carry the practice into Office of Nightlife
accountability, MARCH transparency, commercial-rent advocacy, and COVID-era
relief.

This sequence is evidence of a collective operating method. It does not assign
every event to Jamie or establish sole causality for government outcomes.

## Public response snapshot

Thirty-two of the 33 recovered event pages retain displayed Facebook response
totals.

| Threshold | Events |
| --- | ---: |
| At least 100 responses | 19 |
| At least 500 responses | 7 |
| At least 1,000 responses | 3 |

Summing the displayed values as point estimates yields approximately 9,989
response actions. Three source values use rounded `K` notation.

These are historical Facebook RSVP actions. They are not unique people,
verified attendance, reach, impressions, conversion, endorsement, or impact.
The number must never be restated as "9,989 people attended."

Representative high-response records include:

The New Yorker, WNYC, Metro, Baffler, and Curbed records connect to sources

A posted link establishes source circulation and an event association. It does
not establish that Jamie authored the article, that every article assertion is
endorsed, or that an audience read the destination.

## Jamie's role

Jamie describes this event system as a major area of his contribution. In his
first-hand account, he connects lessons from developing WOW List with
legislative advocacy: recurring meetings in cultural spaces allowed the
coalition to listen, believe artists and event organizers, build relationships,
and carry situated concerns toward civic decision-making.

The current bank preserves that account as a public-safe, candidate role claim.
It is not yet active on the public website because the Facebook archive
corroborates the existence and form of the event system, not Jamie's individual
task ownership.

Promotion requires representative planning artifacts or collaborator
corroboration that distinguishes event-system design, production,
communications, facilitation, documentation, and partner roles. Every public
version must retain collective and cohost credit.

## Interpretive depth

Jamie interprets the recurring event practice as a kind of democracy lab:
believing cultural participants, gathering situated knowledge in the places
affected, translating between cultural and civic practices, and carrying
concerns toward public decision-making.

This is retained as an attributed interpretation rather than an empirical
outcome or a statement assigned to every coalition member. It can inform
future composition without displacing the concrete event and policy record.

## X crosswalk

Facebook events. The first crosswalk pass found events hosted by Sound Mind,
The Black Institute, DCLA, Dance Parade, LGBTQIA+ nightlife organizers,
anti-gentrification organizers, and others, plus unavailable and unresolved

Those are retained as promoted-event candidates. They are not added to the
33-event Facebook Past Events denominator unless a source independently shows
that they were page-owned or cohosted and explains their absence from the
current index.
