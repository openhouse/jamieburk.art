# KC Town Hall Public Record and Project Transition

## Public-safe finding

Official Kansas City records recover both Jamie's public proposal role and the
complete municipal funding decision:

1. The City's CCED Round Two proposal list names Jamie Burkart as KC Town
   Hall's developer/presenter for Proposal 16, with a request of $490,539.
2. Authenticated Resolution 190649 records that the CCED Sales Tax Board voted
   on July 16, 2019 to recommend $490,539 for KC Town Hall.
3. On September 26, 2019, the Council adopted Resolution 190649, accepting the
   recommendation and authorizing negotiation of a conditional funding
   agreement.
4. The same day, the Council passed Ordinance 190642. Its appropriation table
   lists $490,539 for KC Town Hall within the Central City Sales Tax-Projects
   account.
5. Ordinance 240317 records that KC Town Hall later withdrew, calls the funds
   unused, reduces the full $490,539 project account amount, and reappropriates
   the combined clawed-back funds in 2024.

Jamie's separate first-hand clarification adds that he transitioned the project
to a mission-aligned organization. This is public-safe professional context,
not a fact established by the municipal records.

## Claim decomposition

### Jamie's role

The public record supports a precise role claim: the City listed Jamie as
developer/presenter for the proposal. It does not make him the sole author,
owner, or cause of later governmental decisions.

### Government decision

The public record supports saying that the Council accepted the CCED Board's
recommendation and appropriated $490,539 for KC Town Hall. "Appropriated" is
more exact than "allocated" and stronger than recommendation alone.

### Project transition

Jamie's approved first-hand account supports saying that he transitioned the
project to a mission-aligned organization. The knowledge bank labels this as a
first-hand statement and does not render it as a government-record citation.
It does not identify the organization or establish the transition's legal
form, assets, obligations, funding, timing, or causal relationship to the
City's later withdrawal record.

### Later disposition

The public record does not support saying KC Town Hall received or spent the
money. The later Council ordinance identifies the full amount as unused,
records the project's withdrawal, and reappropriates the funds. Any public
mention of the 2019 appropriation should retain that ending.
