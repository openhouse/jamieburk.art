# WOW List, Sunday Dinner, and Call Script bridge

Last reviewed: July 15, 2026

## Purpose

This public-safe research note records what three different archival surfaces can
establish about Jamie Burkart's participatory infrastructure practice before and
during the emergence of NYC Artist Coalition. It stores aggregate findings and
evidence boundaries. It does not reproduce protected database rows, attendance
records, contact information, or authenticated account controls.

## Best-supported sequence

1. WOW List provided community-calendar infrastructure for following,
   publishing, and sharing events across many local cultural ecosystems.
2. Its production configuration included a civic-action surface at
   `popular.vote`.
3. Sunday Dinner sustained an in-person gathering practice with a long-running
   operating record.
4. In January 2017, Call Script's public event discussion invited people to help
   name an emerging advocacy group and to read, edit, and sign a letter to the
   Commissioner.
5. NYC Artist Coalition became the leading visible name in that recovered poll
   interface and appears with Call Script as an event host.

The project sequence is source-supported. Jamie identifies his practice as the
bridge among these systems. That individual facilitation and account-establishment
role remains mixed evidence until contemporaneous role records or collaborator
confirmation are recovered.

## Defensible claims

### WOW List scale

A protected July 2017 production snapshot supports rounded public language of
1,800-plus users, 16,000-plus posts/events, and roughly 35 city ecosystems. The
geography count is a thresholded count of operational labels, not a chapter map.

### Popular Vote surface

WOW List configured `popular.vote` as a custom domain for its Popular Vote
community surface. In the July 2017 snapshot, 933 distinct post/event records are
linked to that tag. The count is records, not people or completed civic actions.

### Sunday Dinner continuity record

The protected workbook tracks 345 numbered entries over more than nine years.
It is useful evidence for a sustained 300-plus gathering practice and for the
operational labor of continuity. It is not a clean attendance database. Its
person-level rows remain protected.

### Participatory coalition formation

The January 2017 Call Script discussion makes two mechanisms visible: public
group naming and collaborative letter drafting/sign-on. These records support a
claim about participatory organizing infrastructure during NYC Artist
Coalition's emergence. They do not prove that a poll formally founded the
coalition or that Jamie individually authored every shared-account post.

## Known, attributed, open, protected

**Known:** the aggregate production-snapshot counts; the `popular.vote` domain
mapping; the workbook's numbered-column count, date span, and mark coverage; the
public event, naming invitation, and collaborative letter prompt.

**Attributed to Jamie:** the interpretation that Call Script connected WOW
List's Popular Vote surface with Jamie's facilitation of NYC Artist Coalition's
emergence.

**Open:** historic Call Script account-creation records, the complete operator
and post-author map, collaborator confirmation, and a full coalition founding
responsibility map.

**Protected:** database rows, user and authentication data, geolocation,
community records, names, phones, email addresses, row-level attendance,
workbook formulas, authenticated account controls, and private archive locators.
