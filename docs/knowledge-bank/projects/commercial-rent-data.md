# Commercial Rent Data Product Scoping

**Public surface:** `/work/fair-rent-nyc`
**Primary claims:** `CLM-CRS-PRIVACY-PRESERVING-DATA-PILOT`,
`CLM-VACANCY-QUARTERLY-CORPUS`
**Status:** Eligible with boundary

## Defensible Claim

Jamie designed a privacy-preserving pilot data product for
geography-aggregated commercial vacancy and lease-cost indicators. The
specification defines:

- an aggregate indicators table;
- a coverage and suppression table;
- a plain-language methods note;
- minimum useful fields for geography, contributing count, occupied and vacant
  square footage, rent distributions, suppression, and release versioning;
- explicit exclusions for confidential filings, identifying records, raw lease
  material, and tenant information.

Jamie also assembled and organized a longitudinal working corpus of 81 unique
quarterly HUD-USPS business-vacancy snapshots spanning Q4 2005 through Q4
2025. The count removes four overlapping 2012 quarters across collection
bands.

## Why It Matters

This is data-product and implementation work, not merely policy writing. It
turns a fragmented opportunity into a bounded first release with user purpose,
requirements, privacy constraints, data-quality controls, and an adoption path.
The 20-year source corpus also shows the acquisition and provenance work needed
before longitudinal analysis becomes possible.

## Boundary

The reviewed artifacts are proposals. They do not establish City adoption,
agency approval, implementation, indicator publication, or Jamie's access to
confidential source data. The proposal builds on existing City data systems,
public analytical precedents, and collective public-interest work.

HUD and USPS originated the vacancy data. The corpus claim establishes Jamie's
assembly, organization, and coverage only; it does not establish data
authorship, completed validation or analysis, a production pipeline, or agency
adoption.

## Public Use

Use the claim to demonstrate requirements definition, privacy by design,
data-product scoping, and public-interest implementation. Do not imply that the
proposed product was shipped or adopted.
