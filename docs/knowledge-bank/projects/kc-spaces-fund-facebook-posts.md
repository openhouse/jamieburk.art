## Editorial conclusion

The surviving Page record documents how KC Spaces Fund made a COVID-era
mutual-aid effort usable in public: it routed applications and deadlines,
fundraising, funded-space spotlights, community resources, partner material,
the campaign site, and the GoFundMe through one recognizable project identity.

This is collective project evidence. It does not assign the posts, account, or
organizer role to Jamie.

Jamie's defensible individual claim remains behind-the-scenes digital
infrastructure. Protected local Git history supports his launch-period work on
the Ghost campaign site, reusable campaign theme, deployment path, and
GoFundMe display widget. Jamie separately recalls supporting the search for a
project name that was uniformly available across domains and social platforms;
that naming contribution remains a restricted research claim until a
collaborator or contemporaneous record corroborates it.

No public website revision is warranted from this pass. The current portfolio
already uses the stronger, safer line: Jamie supported KC Spaces Fund as
behind-the-scenes digital infrastructure. The Facebook archive deepens the
bank beneath that projection.

## Population method

The review traversed the Page timeline to repeated no-growth states three
times, using different scroll cadences. Within-traversal duplicates were
collapsed before comparing the passes.

| Control | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Deduplicated cards by traversal | 40 / 38 / 40 |
| Scroll iterations | 54 / 49 / 74 |
| Terminal no-addition checks | 18 / 18 / 24 |
| Stable media identities | 21 |
| Stable media-set match | Yes, across all three traversals |
| Final surviving-public denominator | 40 cards |

The middle, faster traversal omitted two non-media cards. The final slower pass
reproduced 40. The 21 durable media identities matched across all three passes.
The variance is preserved rather than hidden.

The result is complete only in the bounded sense that every unique card
surfaced by the currently accessible public Page received a disposition. It is
not a native Meta owner export, deletion history, or proof that the project
published only 40 posts. Deleted, private, unrendered, and owner-export-only
history may remain outside the surface.

Twenty records expose stable media routes. Twenty non-media records lack
durable public permalinks and retain positional identities. The public ledger
omits full post transcripts, contact details, personal commenter identities,
reaction identities, authenticated state, tokens, and private management data.

## Relief cycle

Taken together, the records preserve a public operating cycle:

1. explain who the fund was for and route potential applicants;
2. publish application timing and deadline reminders;
3. route donations and fundraising collaborations;
4. name funded-space spotlights;
5. share community resources and partner material; and
6. keep the campaign site and GoFundMe reachable through a consistent identity.

The campaign site's [application guidance](https://kcspacesfund.com/apply/)
describes emergency grants up to $500, rolling April-June 2020 review as
funding allowed, and priority for BIPOC, LGBTQIA+, Disabled, and Immigrant
communities. It names Caitlin Horsmon, Jordan Carr, Kendell Harbin, and Megan
Pobywajlo as organizers and Allied Media Projects as fiscal sponsor. That
credit stays attached.

## Public results and sources

The [public GoFundMe](https://www.gofundme.com/f/kcspacesfund) currently
displays **$9,590 raised against a $9,500 goal from 107 donations**. It
identifies Kendell Harbin for Allied Media Projects Inc. and retains the named
organizer list. The outcome belongs to the collective campaign; it is not an
individual fundraising claim for Jamie.

The [Kansas City Star](https://www.kansascity.com/news/coronavirus/article241807581.html)
independently listed KC Spaces Fund in an April 2020 guide to supporting Kansas
City artists during the coronavirus crisis. An
[Oddities Prints product page](https://www.odditiesprints.com/odd-shop/frank-norton-kaiju)
states that proceeds from a mutual-aid print partially benefit KC Spaces Fund
and KC Tenants, corroborating one fundraising collaboration routed by the Page.

Three posted-source leads remain incomplete: a historical Oddities mutual-aid
route, the Trans Women of Color Collective donation resource, and Do816's *The
Daily DoGood: Kansas City*. Their previews remain research leads until exact
canonical or archived destinations are recovered and close-read.
