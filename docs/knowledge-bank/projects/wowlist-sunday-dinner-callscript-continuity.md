# WOW List, Sunday Dinner, and Call Script continuity

Date: 2026-07-15

## Question

What can the surviving production database, a broad Sunday Dinner working
matrix, and the public Call Script record establish about Jamie's practice of
building participation infrastructure across software, recurring gatherings,
and civic organizing?

This pass treats the three sources as distinct layers before interpreting their
relationship:

- a protected production database documents product use and system scale;
- a protected working matrix documents recurring in-person operating
  continuity; and
- public Facebook records document a transition from event distribution into
  calls to representatives, a public-agency meeting, participatory group
  naming, collaborative letter-making, and a subsequent coalition meeting.

The website receives a concise projection. The deeper source distinctions,
data-quality limits, and anti-claims remain here in the bank.

## WOW List production audit

A fresh read-only parse reproduced selected table counts from the latest unique
production snapshot, dated July 22, 2017:

| Object | Rows |
| --- | ---: |
| Users | 1,846 |
| Posts/events | 16,142 |
| Tags/lists | 23,864 |
| Post-to-tag/list links | 45,562 |
| List follows | 28,837 |
| Saved/starred events | 20,927 |
| Going records | 2,965 |
| Google Calendar events | 15,915 |
| Post-to-calendar event links | 15,875 |

The geography join also reproduced 35 city-region-country keys with at least 50
posts, 48 with at least 25, 79 with at least 10, 133 with at least five, and 709
with at least one.

The public claim is therefore stronger and more exact than a rounded resume
memory: the platform's July 2017 production data supports 1,800+ users, 16,000+
posts/events, 23,000+ lists/tags, 28,000+ list follows, 20,000+ saved events,
and activity across roughly 35 city ecosystems at the conservative 50-post
threshold.

These are historical production counts. They are not current active users,
traffic, retention, revenue, official chapters, or proof that Jamie organized
each local scene. WOW List was shared work with Richard Caceres, local
organizers, users, and other contributors.

The database itself remains protected. It contains user, authentication,
calendar, location, and media information that has no place in a public
portfolio repository. The associated media archive is also incomplete relative
to the latest database, so this is not described as a complete product archive.

## Sunday Dinner aggregate audit

The supplied workbook is a private working record, not a public attendance
database. The review read its main matrix without copying any participant row,
name, contact detail, free text, invitation history, or individual attendance
history into the repository.

The aggregate structure is nevertheless meaningful:

- 346 event-labeled columns;
- numbering from 1 through 345;
- 341 unique event numbers;
- four missing sequence numbers;
- four duplicated numbers across nine columns;
- 342 event columns with at least one explicit affirmative mark; and
- 2,780 explicit affirmative marks.

This independently corroborates the existing public-safe claim of 300+
gatherings. It does not turn 342 columns into 342 independently audited events,
or 2,780 marks into 2,780 unique attendees. Duplicate numbering, missing
numbering, ambiguous marks, possible duplicate or administrative rows, and the
working nature of the sheet remain visible data-quality limits.

The appropriate public projection is simple: a protected operating record now
corroborates the 300-plus scale while every participant row remains private.
Public 100th and 200th event pages provide additional contemporaneous
milestones. Further maturation should seek public post-200 chronology, not
participant disclosure. This source does not establish the separate 20-plus
resident-artist claim.

## From popular.vote to Call Script

The existing full-population WOW List Facebook ledger contains a November 14,
2016 Page post routing to `popular.vote`, classified as civic-mobilization
infrastructure. At review time it displayed two likes, zero comments, and six
shares. Those are mutable interface signals, not reach, endorsement, or impact.

The [Call Script Page](https://www.facebook.com/callscript) currently describes
its purpose as calling representatives to make change and exposes
`popular.vote` as its public project link. The current profile field confirms a
project relationship; by itself it does not establish when the link was added
or who authored every historical post.

## The January 2017 DCLA meeting

On January 25, 2017, [Jamie's public post](https://www.facebook.com/jburkart/posts/pfbid02DvDYiJLK8ceJFAdhQYsYsyAVU3WWuZpbEASr8MFCKqBTJMhnNWrBjYHGQirZwa6al)
invited New Yorkers to pack a DCLA meeting in support of DIY spaces and
improve-dont-evict responses.

The public [January 27 event and discussion](https://www.facebook.com/events/388137698233507/?active_tab=discussion)
preserves several separate facts:

- the page displays NYC Artist Coalition and Call Script among the organizing
  identities and displays DCLA as a cohost;
- Facebook displayed 445 people responded during the July 2026 review;
- a Call Script post invited participants to choose a name the group could
  carry into ongoing creative-community advocacy;
- the displayed poll showed `NYC Artist Coalition` leading at 57 percent;
- the visible surface did not expose a complete vote count;
- Call Script self-reported that the event reached more than 10,000 people in
  24 hours and that more than 100 were going;
- that reach statement remains a contemporaneous Page claim rather than an
  independently audited metric; and
- another discussion post invited participants to read, edit, and sign a letter
  to the DCLA commissioner.

The [Call Script Page](https://www.facebook.com/callscript) then promoted the
[March 6, 2017 NYC Artist Coalition general meeting](https://www.facebook.com/events/1833265643557435/).
That creates a legible public chronology from civic calendar, to mobilization,
to agency meeting, to participatory identity formation and collaborative
action, to a recurring coalition meeting.

## Jamie's role and collective credit

Jamie remembers establishing Call Script and using its popular.vote connection
as part of his facilitation of early NYC Artist Coalition formation. The public
record now corroborates the sequence and Jamie's contemporaneous mobilization
more strongly than it allocates every task.

The defensible claim is therefore bounded:

> The combined record supports Jamie's account that Call Script became one
> bridge from WOW List's popular.vote calendar into early NYC Artist Coalition
> organizing.

This is meaningful individual contribution inside collective formation. It is
not a claim that Jamie single-handedly founded the coalition, authored every
Call Script or coalition post, created the group through one poll, produced
every element of the DCLA event, or caused subsequent policy outcomes.

The poll is especially valuable because it records method as well as identity:
the name was put to participants as something the group could carry together.
It is evidence of participatory identity formation, not a legal founding vote
or a complete account of the people and conversations through which the
coalition emerged.

## Traction boundary

The event's 445-response display and Call Script's contemporaneous
10,000-reached and 100-going statements document public mobilization signals.
They do not establish attendance, unique people, endorsement, mandate,
conversion, or policy impact. The knowledge bank preserves who made each
statement, when it appeared, and what the platform label meant.

## Website decision

Three projections are promoted:

1. The WOW List page now renders the exact July 2017 production-scale claim
   through the citation system instead of leaving it as unsupported prose.
2. The Sunday Dinner page now states that a protected record independently
   corroborates 300-plus gathering scale while keeping participant rows
   private.
3. The WOW List page now shows the bounded Call Script continuity into early NYC
   Artist Coalition organizing.

The full table counts, workbook quality notes, self-reported traction, and
research questions remain in the knowledge bank. The public pages receive the
smallest clear argument that materially strengthens Jamie's hiring story.
