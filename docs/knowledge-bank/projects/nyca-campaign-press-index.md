## Scope

This public-safe index preserves every press or reporting placement recovered
from four campaign websites Jamie confirms he built:

- Let NYC Dance: 21 placements.
- Talks Not Raids: 7 placements.
- Save NYC Spaces: 8 placements.
- FairRentNYC: 9 placements in the December 1, 2021 archive and 1 additional
  reporting item in the current reference library.

Together, the reviewed surfaces contain **46 campaign-to-article placements
representing 45 distinct articles**. The NPR article about Cabaret Law repeal
and the Office of Nightlife appears on both Let NYC Dance and Save NYC Spaces.

The count is an audit of surviving live and archived editorial surfaces, not a
claim that every item appeared simultaneously. A campaign press placement is
not publisher endorsement. It does not make Jamie the author of the reporting
or assign him individual credit for collective campaign or policy outcomes.

## Preservation And Use

- Forty-four of the 45 distinct articles have a surviving live, moved,
  access-restricted, or archived public source location in this pass. The
  SFGate placement survives only as campaign-index metadata; its publisher URL
  is dead and no Wayback capture was recovered.
- The campaign-listed Village Voice Cabaret Law URL was dead. A title-level
  search recovered the publisher's current canonical page, author, and date.
- Two Gothamist legacy URLs returned 404; title-level publisher searches
  recovered their current canonical pages.
- The current New York Times commercial-rent article returned an access
  restriction during automated verification. The publisher URL and campaign
  placement remain public metadata; no paywall was bypassed.
- Article records preserve title, publisher, URL, campaign relationship, and
  access state. Their bodies are not treated as fully decomposed evidence until
  a later claim-level close reading records what each source supports and does
  not support.
- Archive links are preferred where current pages are gone, blocked, or no
  longer preserve the relevant article.
- The canonical structured records live in
  `apps/www/src/data/knowledge-bank/campaignPress.ts`.

## Recovery Method

- Downloaded the three surviving campaign pages and the current FairRentNYC
  reference library on July 13, 2026.
- Downloaded the supplied December 1, 2021 FairRentNYC Wayback capture using
  the capture's `id_` representation so its original article URLs remained
  inspectable.
- Compared the recovered lists with independent corpus attempts on sibling
  `feature/evals-*` branches, then resolved title, URL, status, and count
  differences against the campaign surfaces.
- Preserved one source identity for the NPR article that appears in two
  campaigns, while retaining both placements.
- Kept the nine-item FairRentNYC archive and the one-item current reporting
  section as separate editorial states.

## Claim Boundary

The press corpus directly establishes the four public source trails and their
campaign-to-article relationships. Jamie states that he built those campaign
sites and press sections; that technical-authorship statement remains a memory
lead here and a separately governed careful proof elsewhere in the bank.

corroborate it campaign by campaign. The corpus does not establish that Jamie
wrote each campaign position, selected every article alone, received publisher
endorsement, or caused the collective legislative and policy outcomes discussed
in the reporting.

The corpus remains `no-public-projection`. The governed website-authorship proof
retains its existing approved-resume and Jamie-review basis while repository,
deployment, CMS, and collaborator corroboration remain open research tasks.
