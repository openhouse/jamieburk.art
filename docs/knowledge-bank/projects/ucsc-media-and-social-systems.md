# UCSC Media And Social Systems

## Public-Safe Finding

Protected UCSC narrative evaluations from 2004 and 2006 document two early
dimensions of Jamie Burkart's practice that remain visible in later work.

Margaret Morse's evaluations describe a practice grounded in media archaeology,
embodied and performative engagement with theory, installation, and attention.
They record Jamie bringing early home-video and video-synthesis equipment into
the classroom and realizing *Time is Long* as a gallery installation in New
York City. A protected 2014 recommendation further describes Jamie's warmth,
generosity, dedication, and successful execution of an ambitious installation,
without identifying that installation.

Warren Sack's evaluations describe an early recursive social-systems method.
They record an online-dictionary-source program; analysis of physical and
online social networks; an original application of structural-equivalence
reasoning to Flickr groups; a working image-similarity prototype with a
Max/MSP/Jitter interface; and a collaborative design that translated online
photo browsing into movement through projected physical rooms.

## Independent Public Context

Laura Mattingly's 2006 *Good Times* feature independently documents *Open
House*, a 10-day public experiment Jamie initiated and tended at UC Santa Cruz.
The project combined communal living, participatory art, collective
decision-making, and distributed documentation. The article also records Jamie
connecting the experience to social software and to seeing oneself represented
in relation to other people and things. It documents one complete revision
cycle: after Porter staff raised concerns, the group met, cleaned the space, and
marked a smaller operating boundary around technical equipment.

Together, these records support a bounded throughline:

> Jamie's present operating practice has an artistic and technical lineage. He
> studies relationships, makes structures people can inhabit, attends to what
> happens inside them, and turns observation into another round of design.

## What The Records Do Not Establish

- The source copy is not an official transcript.
- Historical faculty observations are not current blanket endorsements.
- Jamie did not invent structural equivalence as a field-wide concept; the
  record supports an original independent application of that reasoning.
- Classroom prototypes are not production deployments or evidence of sustained
  adoption.
- The physical photo-browsing installation design was collaborative.
- The protected recommendation does not identify which installation it
  describes.
- Participant work and collective decisions in *Open House* remain
  collectively credited.

## Publication Boundary

The public repository stores only typed source summaries, supported
propositions, limits, and projection decisions. Do not publish the student
identifier, grades, private correspondence, email addresses, telephone numbers,
postal addresses, raw academic-record text, protected locators, or screenshot.

The public website cites only the independently published *Open House* feature.
The faculty records remain protected corroboration and eval context.
