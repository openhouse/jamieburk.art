# Jamie Burkart Personal Social Archive Census

Date: 2026-07-14
Account: `@urbanhermit`
Method: authenticated, read-only archival production
Public treatment: aggregate accounting plus selected, close-read professional sources

## Research question

What mission-relevant sources, defensible claims, and stakeholder patterns can
be recovered from the complete surviving profile population of Jamie Burkart's
personal X account without turning a personal timeline into a public dossier?

## Privacy and editorial boundary

The full captured timeline is research material, not a portfolio artifact. The
public repository retains redacted ordered rows with coarse year and research
classifications so aggregate findings can be audited. This carries residual
reconstruction risk, but the rows are intentionally not individually linkable.
The repository does not retain full post text, exact dates, handles, status
identifiers, status URLs, media, personal relationship detail, historic contact
or location data, or per-record reaction metrics in the population ledgers.

Selected records enter the typed knowledge bank only after close reading and
only when they document a professional role, method, project, or useful research
lead. Ordinary life, relationships, health, politics, and third-party context
remain outside professional projection.

## Strong new source: Horse Lords

NPR Music's April 29, 2016 feature, [“Video: Horse Lords' Hypnotic
‘Truthers’ Will Blast Your Noodle”](https://www.npr.org/2016/04/29/476020413/video-horse-lords-hypnotic-truthers-will-blast-your-noodle),
identifies Jamie Burkart and M.C. Schmidt together as the makers of Horse Lords'
official “Truthers” video. NPR also reports Andrew Bernstein's explanation that
the visual construction uses simple materials, repetition, variation, text,
black and white, and color to mirror the band's musical construction.

Defensible claim:

> Jamie Burkart and M.C. Schmidt made Horse Lords' official 2016 “Truthers”
> video, translating the band's recursive musical structure through repetition,
> variation, text, and color; NPR Music featured the work.

Limits: the sources do not establish the division of labor, sole authorship, an
NPR commission, commercial performance, audience reach, or later use.
