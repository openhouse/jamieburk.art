# Waterways and participatory art

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-16

**Projection status:** Mature source-backed claims held for future composition.

## Defensible claims

- Jamie originated and helped organize a bicycle-powered paddle-wheel raft
  expedition beginning in Kansas City. The Star printed his name as James
  Burkart and named Libby Hendon and Laura Mattingly as the other core
  travelers. Contemporaneous reporting establishes a July 21, 2007 departure
  from the West Bottoms and a location south of Baton Rouge by November 15. A
  later institutional source establishes travel until salt water; the exact
  Gulf terminus remains an open chronology question.
- The expedition's documented mechanics included a three-week
  recycled-material build, a named three-person core crew, friends joining
  route segments, recurring provisioning and interaction stops, James
  Burkart's reported invitations to people met along the way, attributed
  steering and propulsion practices, route decisions and delays, a 51-day
  interruption, and community, legal, retrieval, and job-finding support for
  continuation.
- Jamie spearheaded *Great Accommodations* at Charlotte Street in 2009, using
  waterways as social infrastructure through participatory installations,
  public programs, community-story outreach, and working documentation.
- Jamie initiated *Open House*, a ten-day UCSC gallery experiment in communal
  living, public participation, shared decision-making, and multi-perspective
  documentation.
- Jamie and his housemates held open Shop Shows from 2003 to 2005, inviting
  visitors to contribute art, performance, scripts, and unfinished work.

## Source boundaries

The Pitch documents the raft's origin, recycled construction, and early journey
across Missouri. A November 15, 2007 front-page report by Darryl Levings in *The
Kansas City Star* adds the named three-person core crew, construction details,
route status south of Baton Rouge, friends joining route segments, reported
invitations to join, and a 51-day interruption-and-recovery sequence. The
article attributes its material description to Libby Hendon and names Paul
Kelly Loyacono for legal, retrieval, and job-finding assistance, specifically
substitute teaching, being a coffee barista, and labor with home renovators.
It reports 14 prior Coast Guard encounters separately from uncounted checks by
police, fire, sheriff, state conservation, and Army Corps authorities. It
credits Laura Mattingly with steering in one photographed moment, attributes the
crew's general propulsion practice and one Chain of Rocks account to Libby
Hendon, and attributes the Chain of Rocks approach to Mattingly. Charlotte
Street preserves a later first-person route account and institutional details
about *Great Accommodations*.

The Star report establishes its publication-date chronology, not the final
terminus or completed arrival at salt water. It records both an expectation
that salt would be encountered below New Orleans and later uncertainty about
whether that would happen by New Orleans; it also says the crew did not expect
to see the Gulf from the raft. Its moment-specific steering,
propulsion, and route accounts do not allocate all fabrication, navigation,
operation, or recovery labor among the crew and helpers, provide a complete
participant roster, or serve as a complete legal or safety record. The core
expedition remains collective work by Libby Hendon, Laura Mattingly, and Jamie
Burkart, with additional participants and communities along the route.

The newspaper survives here as public metadata and bounded observations. The
participant-held print PDF is copyrighted and remains outside the public
repository. A bounded exact-title search did not recover a live canonical
article URL; that negative result does not prove that no URL ever existed.
The newspaper's printed `James Burkart` is retained in source-level records;
the current portfolio uses `Jamie Burkart` as his public name.

## Held interpretation

The source-backed facts support a present-day hiring interpretation of the
expedition as an early example of Jamie making technical systems relational
through rapid prototyping, public encounter, adaptive logistics, and
place-based inquiry. This is analytical synthesis, not terminology used by
*The Kansas City Star*. It remains held for future composition and must retain
the source facts, collaborators, community contributions, and limitations that
make the interpretation responsible.

Good Times provides rich contemporaneous Open House and Shop Shows reporting.
Its participant and family details are not necessary for professional claims and
should not be reproduced without a separate editorial and consent decision.
