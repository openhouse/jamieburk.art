# Waterways and Participatory Art

## Project Thread

The recovered public record shows an early and sustained practice connecting
participatory program design, public space, documentation, software, movement,
and civic imagination.

## Defensible Claims

### Collaborative Raft Expedition

The Pitch reported in 2007 that Jamie conceived an experiential boat expedition
while investigating Kansas City's transportation history and lost relationship
with the Missouri River. The group crossed Missouri on a homemade raft built
from recycled materials.

Charlotte Street's later project history describes the earlier work as a
four-month, bicycle-powered paddle-wheel raft journey from Kansas City down the
Missouri and Mississippi until the water tasted salty. The strongest wording is
therefore a long collaborative river expedition toward the Gulf, without
asserting a precise final landing point that the recovered sources do not name.

Sources: `SRC-WATERWAYS-PITCH-2007-08-09`,
`SRC-WATERWAYS-CHARLOTTE-STREET-2009-09-01`.

### Great Accommodations

Charlotte Street identifies Great Accommodations as a project spearheaded by
Jamie. It treated the central rivers as a social network and invited people
across river cities to contribute stories and perspectives. Its forms included
correspondence, public programs, facilitation, interactive video, participatory
sculpture, computer programs, community projects, recycled materials, and
working documentation.

Source: `SRC-WATERWAYS-CHARLOTTE-STREET-2009-09-01`.

### Open House and Shop Shows

Good Times documented Open House as a ten-day experiment in communal living
inside a UCSC gallery. It also traced the project to Shop Shows held by Jamie
and housemates from 2003 to 2005, where visitors contributed artwork,
performance, scripts, food, interpretation, and activity. The article describes
working documentation as part of the participatory method.

Source: `SRC-OPEN-HOUSE-GOOD-TIMES-2006-06-28`.

## Chad-Lens Value

This thread shows Jamie designing conditions in which strangers could
participate, contribute knowledge, navigate ambiguity, and leave behind a
shared record. It expands the portfolio's account of technical and civic
operations by showing that the underlying practice began in participatory art,
hospitality, public programming, and documentation.
