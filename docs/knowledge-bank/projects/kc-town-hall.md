# KC Town Hall

## Phase One Restoration

A protected 2019 CCED proposal identifies Jamie Burkart and Julia Fredenburg as
KC Town Hall's founders and project managers. It presents a multi-trade team and
marks Phase One's cold-shell scope as completed in 2019. The itemized scope
includes roof deck repair, insulation and TPO membrane, historic masonry,
structural floor and ceiling framing, water service, access, site safety, air
quality, tools, materials, and other site operations.

Source: `SRC-KC-TOWN-HALL-CCED-PROPOSAL-2019`, PDF pages 2 and 11-12.

The proposal is a contemporaneous applicant-produced project record rather than
an independent completion certification. Completion refers to Phase One, not
Phase Two or the complete redevelopment. Julia Fredenburg and the specialist
teams retain their own credit.

## Construction Role

Jamie reports serving as Phase One's day-to-day general contractor: hiring and
coordinating masonry, roofing, carpentry, welding, engineering, architecture,
and plumbing teams and sequencing work from the basement through the roof. His
account includes arriving first with measured drawings and survey cards and
coordinating placement of the TPO membrane with restored parapet work and
ceramic caps. The proposal independently corroborates his project-manager title,
the multi-trade team, and the completed scope, but it does not use the
general-contractor title or establish licensing or permit status.

Source posture: `SRC-KC-TOWN-HALL-JAMIE-ACCOUNT-2026-07-15` with corroboration
from `SRC-KC-TOWN-HALL-CCED-PROPOSAL-2019`. Public projection remains on hold
pending the research tracked in `INQ-KC-TOWN-HALL-CONSTRUCTION-ROLE-2026`.

## Neighborhood Process

The proposal reproduces a neighborhood survey handbill, names Oak Park
Neighborhood Association and New Horizon Missionary Baptist Church as partners,
and says residents' responses directly shaped the proposal. Jamie reports
creating the 4-by-6-inch handbill and backing contact and response system and
using daily site presence to gather neighborhood histories, needs, and ideas
over time.

The project-level survey and its influence are confirmed. Jamie's individual
design and systems authorship remain first-person evidence pending further
corroboration. Resident identities, contacts, and responses remain protected.

## Official Record

Kansas City's Central City Economic Development Sales Tax Board minutes identify
Jamie as presenter of the KC Town-Hall proposal. The proposal described adaptive
reuse of an existing building for four retail spaces and three apartment units.
The minutes record a unanimous board recommendation to City Council for $490,539
in funding.

Source: `SRC-KC-TOWN-HALL-CCED-MINUTES-2019`.

## Council Action

On September 26, 2019, the Kansas City Council took two distinct actions:

- it adopted Second Committee Substitute for Resolution 190649, accepting the
  CCED Board's recommendation and authorizing negotiation of a funding agreement
  for an amount not to exceed $490,539; and
- it passed Committee Substitute for Ordinance 190642, appropriating $490,539
  to KC Town Hall in the Central City Sales Tax-Projects account.

Sources: `SRC-KC-TOWN-HALL-RESOLUTION-190649`,
`SRC-KC-TOWN-HALL-ORDINANCE-190642`.

## Later Disposition

A May 17, 2022 city update listed no funds disbursed and described the funding
agreement as under negotiation. In 2024, Ordinance 240317 recorded that KC Town
Hall had withdrawn; the City reduced the full $490,539 project account and
returned the unused appropriation to the Central City Sales Tax Fund.

Sources: `SRC-KC-TOWN-HALL-CCED-UPDATE-2022-05-17`,
`SRC-KC-TOWN-HALL-ORDINANCE-240317`.

## Defensible Claim

Jamie co-founded KC Town Hall and served as a project manager for a multi-trade
Phase One cold-shell restoration recorded as completed in 2019. The project
used a neighborhood survey to shape the next-phase proposal. Jamie later
presented that adaptive-reuse proposal; the board unanimously recommended
$490,539, and the Council accepted the recommendation and appropriated that
amount.

## Boundary

Council appropriation is not receipt, disbursement, expenditure, an executed
funding agreement, or project completion. The public record does not establish
Jamie's individual role in securing the Council actions. The later withdrawal
and reclamation must accompany any account of the funding outcome. Current
property status and private financial, legal, property, and stakeholder details
remain outside the public repository.

Do not translate Jamie's first-person general-contractor account into an
independently verified license or legal designation. Do not collapse Julia
Fredenburg, named trade professionals, neighborhood partners, residents, or
other collaborators into Jamie's role.
