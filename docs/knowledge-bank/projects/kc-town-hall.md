# KC Town Hall

KC Town Hall was a neighborhood-led effort to restore a long-abandoned historic
mixed-use building at 36th Street and Indiana Avenue in Kansas City. The work
joined physical restoration, resident listening, visual communication,
neighborhood operations, public funding processes, and a long-term vision for
affordable homes and small-business space.

## Phase One restoration

Phase One was completed in 2019 as a $189,629 cold-shell restoration. The
documented scope included roof-deck repair, insulation and TPO membrane,
historic masonry, floor framing, water connection, egress, safety, cleanup,
access, and related site work. It did not complete the later mixed-use buildout
or establish a permanent public opening.

Jamie states that he served as Phase One general contractor and daily site
coordinator. The project packet independently identifies him as founder and
project manager, names the architecture and trade team, and documents the
scope. Until an additional contract, permit, invoice set, or collaborator
statement is approved, the general-contractor title remains explicitly
first-hand. Jamie did not perform the licensed work of the masons, roofers,
architects, engineers, plumbers, welders, carpenters, or other professionals he
coordinated.

## Neighborhood process

The original packet reproduces a resident survey card asking what neighbors
wanted at the site. It names Oak Park Neighborhood Association and New Horizon
Missionary Baptist Church as process partners and states that ongoing survey
results directly shaped the proposal. Jamie states that he designed the card
and its backing data system.

The page is visually important evidence: resident invitation, neighborhood
photography, a legible choice architecture, and contact capture appear inside
the project narrative rather than as a later engagement claim. Raw respondent
identities and responses remain private, and no unsupported response total is
used.

Adjacent neighborhood work included the recurring Tired of Tires pickup and
design support for neighborhood initiatives. Those projects have their own
partners and should not be represented as KC Town Hall's work alone.

## Public funding chronology

The City record preserves distinct decisions:

1. the CCED Board recommended $490,539 in July 2019;
2. the Council accepted the recommendation and appropriated the amount in
   September 2019;
3. authorization was not receipt or expenditure; and
4. the project later withdrew, after which the City reappropriated the full
   unused amount in 2024.

The 2019 amount belongs to the later proposal and must not be attached to the
completed Phase One work. Jamie states separately that he transitioned the
project to a mission-aligned organization. Private reasons, the receiving
organization, and any legal transfer terms are intentionally not encoded here;
current sources do not connect that transition causally to the City's later
funding record.

See [Phase One detail](kc-town-hall-phase-one.md) and the [funding decision
chain](kc-town-hall-funding.md).

## Present-tense source return

- **Question brought to the source:** How can the Wiki show the integration of
  construction delivery and neighborhood listening without romanticizing the
  project or erasing its later limits?
- **Original material reopened:** the original 24-page 2019 CCED proposal
  packet in extracted and visually rendered form, together with the canonical
  City funding records and Jamie's bounded first-hand clarification.
- **Scope and completeness:** close reading of the neighborhood-process page,
  project narrative, maps, Phase One scope and budget pages, and support-letter
  context; not a review of private contracts, survey rows, financing, or every
  daily site record.
- **What changed in this reading:** the packet itself makes participation part
  of delivery. The resident survey is reproduced inside the proposal and said
  to shape it, while the maps locate the building among neighborhood anchors.
  Listening was an operating input, not decorative outreach appended later.
- **What did not change:** the packet does not independently confirm Jamie's
  general-contractor title, prove every proposed use was delivered, or turn the
  later City appropriation into received or spent funds.
- **Access boundary:** the packet and official legislation are authorized
  sources; private respondent data, contracts, financing, contact details, and
  the private circumstances of transition remain outside Git.
- **Next return:** seek a collaborator or trade-team account of Jamie's daily
  coordination role and a public-safe handoff record, while preserving the
  protected conclusion of the project.
