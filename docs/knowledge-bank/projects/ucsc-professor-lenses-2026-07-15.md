# UCSC professor lenses: public-safe source note

Date: 2026-07-15
Status: Evaluative controls integrated; protected records withheld
Public projection: One bounded through-line on `/about`

## Purpose

This note records the source and privacy boundaries for the Prof. Margaret Morse
and Prof. Warren Sack portfolio evals. The evals are not endorsements and do not
authorize publication of private praise. They ask whether the public portfolio
demonstrates two durable qualities of Jamie's practice through public-safe work.

## Public source

Laura Mattingly's 2006 *Good Times* article, [Open House](https://www.goodtimes.sc/archives/metro-santa-cruz/06.28.06/open-house-0626.html),
is the public source used for the new About-page lineage. It documents Jamie
initiating a ten-day UCSC gallery experiment that became collectively governed,
invited public participation, and used many-perspective documentation. The
knowledge bank already preserves this as `CLM-OPEN-HOUSE-PARTICIPATORY-GALLERY`
with collective-credit and participant-privacy boundaries.

A screenshot of a public-facing Margaret Morse recommendation was reviewed as a
research lead. It is not quoted or projected because this pass did not recover a
stable public URL, an archival copy with durable provenance, or a separate
editorial decision about testimonial use.

The separately governed Maven course-completion certificate already on the lab
page is an approved public artifact supplied by Jamie. It is not one of the
protected UCSC records and remains bounded as professional development rather
than instructor endorsement or proof of a production product.

## Protected sources

UCSC narrative evaluations by Margaret Morse and Warren Sack were reviewed as
protected educational records. They informed the questions the evals ask, but
the raw records are not reproduced. Their text, grades, student identifiers,
private archive paths, and related correspondence remain outside the repository
and public site.

The existing bounded record `CLM-SOCIAL-INFO-SPACES-PROTOTYPE` remains held. It
cannot be used to claim that Jamie invented structural equivalence, produced
peer-reviewed research, deployed a production system, solely authored collective
coursework, or has current proficiency in a historical toolset.

## Evaluative synthesis

The Margaret Morse lens asks whether the portfolio preserves one threshold where
artistic, civic, technical, and social practice remain connected. It looks for
experimentation, participation, attention, documentation, care, place,
hospitality, embodiment, and how people inhabit systems without romanticizing
vulnerability or displacing collective credit.

The Warren Sack lens asks whether a reader can reconstruct a recurring systems
sequence: observe relationships, model a system, prototype an interface, test it
through social or collective use, and document a handoff. It rewards evidence
across current projects, not abstract systems language or a historical tool list.

## Projection decision

The About page now connects the public Open House record to current and
historical work, then compares four public-safe examples through relationship,
interface and use, and learning and continuity. The copy names the recurring
method once, keeps Open House's governance collective, and publishes no faculty
testimonial or protected educational-record content.
