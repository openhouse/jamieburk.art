# Participatory Public Programs

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-12

This note preserves source-backed depth that is not currently required on the
public portfolio site.

## Open House

Good Times reported that Jamie initiated and tended Open House in 2006, a
ten-day experiment that made a university gallery into a communal living,
public art, and documentation space. Responsibility and decision-making were
described as communal. Participants created art, negotiated the space with
university staff, and generated photographs, video, and a shared diary.

The source supports Jamie's initiating and tending role. It does not make him
the sole author of participants' work or grant permission to republish images
of participants.

## Great Accommodations

Charlotte Street Foundation described Jamie as spearheading Great
Accommodations in 2009. The river-centered project combined mailed invitations,
public participation, immersive installation, interactive video, software,
community projects, facilitation, and public programs. Suzanne Hogan is named
as a collaborator in the correspondence work.

The source supports a strong public-engagement and program-design claim while
requiring collective credit and separate image-rights review.

## Raft expedition

The Pitch attributed the expedition idea to Jamie and connected it to Kansas
City transportation history and the Missouri River. Charlotte Street later
described a bicycle-powered paddlewheel raft journey down the Missouri and
Mississippi Rivers that lasted four months, until the water tasted salt.

The reviewed sources do not establish the exact final endpoint as the Gulf of
Mexico or provide a complete crew and route record. Those questions remain
open.

## Candidate longitudinal frame

Open House and Great Accommodations suggest a recurring practice of creating
participatory structures in which strangers enter an unfamiliar public
situation, develop agency together, and leave behind shared records. That is a
research hypothesis, not current site copy. It should be tested across later
cultural, civic, community, and knowledge-system projects before promotion.

## Photo-editor direction

Future selection work should look for sequences rather than isolated spectacle:

- Jamie facilitating or tending a participatory situation;
- participants visibly shaping the work;
- the operating artifacts of participation, including invitations, signs,
  diaries, maps, software, and documentation systems;
- river travel connected to resident encounters and public programs;
- captions, rights, consent, dates, collaborators, and vulnerable-person review.
