# Fair Rent NYC recent advocacy, July 13-August 12, 2026

The strongest supported account of this cycle has three layers. Jamie completed
a consequential but bounded pre-publication review of a campaign report. He
then made the cultural-space case for Commercial Rent Stabilization in public.
Protected records also document direct post-event follow-through with elected-
office staff, evidence transfer, and a proposed recurring alignment cadence.
Only the first two layers are ready for public-safe factual description; every
portfolio projection remains held for Jamie's approval.

## Report-review close reading

The tracked review did more than polish copy. It separated percentage points
from relative percentage change, distinguished reported rent from broader rent
claims, challenged non-comparable vacancy denominators, narrowed unsupported
causal and motive language, corrected bill and sponsor details, requested
reproducible source notes, and asked for more legible maps. The final report
adopted a meaningful subset of that work and expressly acknowledged Jamie's
review. It also retained some language the review had flagged, so the record
must preserve both contribution and editorial non-control.

## Public-speaking close reading

Jamie's delivered remarks place cultural space inside the campaign's economic
and administrative frame. The argument is not that culture is decorative; it
is that stages, studios, venues, restaurants, shops, and neighborhood rooms are
infrastructure in which cultural forms and relationships become possible. The
speech connects this material dependence to predictable lease rules, collective
credit, open public data, and a claim that displacement is shaped by policy.

The public event and delivered remarks support a speaking claim. The bounded
review did not recover a published Jamie quotation, completed interview,
broadcast placement, or indexed article. A request for footage and prepared
press language remain inputs, not publication outcomes.

## Correction and current-state gates

- The August 11 campaign caption says 84,000 businesses closed in Q2 2025;
  its second graphic and Council Resolution 496 say 8,400. Do not reuse 84,000.
  Recheck the originating NYCEDC source before closing the correction.
- The July 29 event page remains useful for event identity and purpose, not a
  complete speaker roster or verbatim remarks.
- Official records, not campaign recollection, control present legislative
  status. Recheck them before each public reuse.
- A proposed or scheduled coordination meeting is not a completed meeting.
