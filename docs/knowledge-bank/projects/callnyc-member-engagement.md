# CallNYC Council-member account engagement

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-12
**Status:** Public-safe research record
**Scope:** Public posts engaging with `@CallNYCapp` or referring residents to
CallNYC while the account holder was a sitting New York City Council member.

## Finding

Surviving public evidence identifies 11 substantive posts from accounts of 10
sitting NYC Council members engaging with CallNYC through quote-posts, direct
replies, amplification, acknowledgment, or a resident referral. Rosie Mendez's
account engaged twice.

This is account-level evidence of a public recognition and feedback loop. It is
not evidence that the New York City Council commissioned, adopted, partnered
with, or institutionally endorsed CallNYC.

## Account-level context

X reported 110 posts while failing to render the logged-out timeline, so the
review treated this as a retrieval limitation rather than evidence of an empty
account. An authenticated PDF preserved one populated page and six blank
continuation pages; exact pagination cursors across two public mirrors recovered
107 unique entries, leaving three unrecovered. No API credential was configured
or required for the core engagement claim.

The recovered `@CallNYCapp` corpus contained:

- 107 unique visible timeline entries against 110 posts reported by the
  profile;
- 70 CouncilStat-based recognition posts naming 26 Council-member handles;
- 45 recognition posts with at least one surviving engagement;
- 4 replies, 63 reposts, and 86 likes across those recognition posts in the
  current public mirror.

These counters are current observations, not contemporaneous analytics or
impact measures. They do not identify every liking or reposting account.

## Method

1. Rendered and inspected every page of an authenticated-browser PDF supplied
   by Jamie. The first page contained timeline material; six print-continuation
   pages were blank.
2. Confirmed that anonymous X reported 110 posts while rendering no
   logged-out timeline. This was treated as an access limitation, not an account
   history finding.
3. Carried exact pagination cursors across two public mirrors to recover the
   Posts and Posts & Replies timelines.
4. Deduplicated status IDs and parsed surviving counters by their rendered
   reply, repost, and like labels.
5. Searched public mentions, quote-posts, and CallNYC links for member-account
   activity outside the account timeline.
6. Kept sitting member accounts separate from institutional accounts, staff or
   later-member accounts, and CallNYC's own outbound tags.

## Boundaries

- Three reported timeline entries remain unrecovered.
- A member-account post does not prove that the elected member personally typed
  it; office staff may have operated the account.
- Tagging a Council member is outbound outreach, not engagement by that member.
- Surviving public engagement is not formal endorsement, adoption, partnership,
  or independent validation of every CallNYC ranking.
- Deleted, protected, removed, or unindexed activity may be absent.

## Approved projection

> CallNYC used CouncilStat data to create a public recognition and feedback loop
> with Council offices. Eleven surviving public posts document engagement by
> accounts of 10 then-sitting NYC Council members through interpretation,
> replies, amplification, acknowledgment, and a resident referral.

## Anti-claims

Do not say:

- The New York City Council endorsed CallNYC.
- Ten Council members formally endorsed CallNYC.
- Every tagged Council member engaged.
- The complete account history was recovered.
- Jamie personally conversed with every member.
