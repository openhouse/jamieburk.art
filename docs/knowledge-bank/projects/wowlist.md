# WOW List

WOW List was a community calendar co-built by Jamie Burkart and Richard Album.
It made it easy for people to publish events, follow interests, and receive
timely ways to participate. Its guiding proposition, “Being there changes
everything,” treated an event listing as an invitation into shared public life,
not merely a unit of content.

## Product and community practice

Participants described WOW List as a list of things to do, an inclusionary
calendar, a community-trust surface, and a noncompetitive structure in which
people could join and edit shared lists. The product connected publishing,
following, saved events, calendar distribution, email, city scenes, and direct
community support.

A July 2017 archive snapshot preserves substantial historical product activity
across at least 35 active city scenes. Those values are database rows and
relationships, not unique active users, attendance, current adoption, revenue,
or impact. The raw database and user records remain protected.

## Jamie's role

Jamie co-built the platform and helped steward its public operations. A full
review of the surviving Facebook publishing surface attributes all 50 rendered
post details to Jamie as publisher, with four additional table-only records.
That supports publishing stewardship on that Page, not sole authorship of every
quoted voice, every social account, or the project itself. Richard's co-builder
credit remains essential.

The social archive shows the account operating as a product surface: user
support, tutorials, event distribution, mission-relevant source curation, and
rapid civic coordination. Public-use signals are not measures of total adoption
or causal impact.

## From gatherings to civic facilitation

Sunday Dinner provided an embodied laboratory for recurring hospitality,
invitations, themes, attendance coordination, and relationships. WOW List gave
that practice a distributed technical form. The later `popular.vote` and Call
Script experiments adapted participation infrastructure toward civic
facilitation during the emergence of NYC Artist Coalition.

This continuity does not mean one product caused the coalition to form. It
shows a recurring design question: how can an invitation become presence, and
how can presence become shared agency without requiring centralized control?

See the detailed [WOW List, Sunday Dinner, and Call Script
dossier](wowlist-sunday-dinner-callscript.md), [Facebook publishing
record](wowlist-facebook-posts.md), and [event record](jamie-wowlist-facebook-events.md).

## Present-tense source return

- **Question brought to the source:** Was WOW List principally a calendar
  product, or was the underlying achievement a broader participation system?
- **Original material reopened:** the protected historical database archive,
  the original longitudinal Sunday Dinner coordination workbook through a
  structured read, the repaired “What is WOW List?” member-meeting transcript,
  and the public-safe aggregate audit.
- **Scope and completeness:** database and workbook structure plus bounded
  aggregate findings; no person-level rows, messages, contacts, or private
  participation history entered the Wiki.
- **What changed in this reading:** the workbook's invitation, response, theme,
  recurrence, and formula structure makes the operational continuity clearer.
  The project linked hospitality, memory, follow-up, and technical distribution,
  not event discovery alone.
- **What did not change:** the sources do not establish unique attendance,
  complete adoption, sole product authorship, or that later civic work was an
  inevitable result of WOW List.
- **Access boundary:** database and workbook originals are local protected
  artifacts; the member-meeting transcript is public-safe only in repaired,
  bounded form; personal contacts and participant rows remain excluded.
- **Next return:** recover collaborator-safe product history about division of
  labor, governance, and later handoff, with Richard's perspective and credit
  treated as first-class evidence rather than an annotation.

## Boundaries

- Always credit Jamie and Richard as co-builders.
- Treat archive counts as historical rows with stated definitions.
- Do not publish user, contact, invitation, or person-level attendance data.
- Do not infer attendance from event publication or social interaction.
- Keep current interface recovery distinct from deleted or private history.
