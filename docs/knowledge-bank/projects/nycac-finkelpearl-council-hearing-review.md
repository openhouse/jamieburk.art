# Finkelpearl and NYC Artist Coalition in Council transcripts

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-15
**Disposition:** Integrated as held institutional-capacity depth
**Public website projection:** None

## Question

Where, in recovered official New York City Council hearing transcripts, did
Department of Cultural Affairs Commissioner Tom Finkelpearl refer to NYC
Artist Coalition?

## Method

The bounded Council-side review began with the official Council committee
meeting dataset and selected the 74 Cultural Affairs committee records from
2014 through 2019. It recovered all 74 committee meeting pages, followed their
77 linked Legistar matter pages, and downloaded every attachment labeled
`Hearing Transcript` reached through that route.

The resulting set contained 132 searchable official transcript attachments
representing 91 unique PDF hashes. Forty-six attachments mentioned
Finkelpearl. Searches used `Finkelpearl` / `Finklepearl` and four Artist
Coalition name variants. Every coalition candidate was then read for speaker,
referent, and duplicate-route attribution.

The public-safe machine control is
[nycac-finkelpearl-council-transcript-review-2026-07.json](../data/nycac-finkelpearl-council-transcript-review-2026-07.json).

## Result

One substantive commissioner-attributed occurrence surfaced in the recovered
corpus. During the May 19, 2017 joint executive budget hearing, Finkelpearl
said:

> The NYC Artist Coalition formed after we hosted a meeting for DIY Arts
> Community this past January.

This sentence appears within an account of CreateNYC as a close reciprocal
relationship with the public, DCLA's wish to expand direct feedback, and the
power of bringing people together around a common cause. A byte-identical
Libraries-matter attachment preserves the same joint hearing; it is one
occurrence through two official routes, not two substantive references.

One other transcript contained both Finkelpearl's name and an Artist Coalition
variant. In the March 16, 2018 preliminary budget hearing, however, Jamie
Burkart was the speaker. Jamie identified as a coalition member and artist
safety advocate and described CreateNYC office hours as helping underground
artists reach safety services while helping the City understand artists'
lives. It is relevant evidence about reciprocity, but it is not another
Finkelpearl reference.

## Important referent boundary

The May 2017 transcript next discusses the Disability Arts NYC task force.
Finkelpearl's statement that "It has had a huge influence" refers to that
disability-arts task force, not NYC Artist Coalition. The evaluator rejects any
claim that transfers this phrase to the coalition.

## Institutional interpretation

The public record supports **institutional complementarity**:

- DCLA could convene, fund, plan, and open official feedback channels.
- NYC Artist Coalition could surface dispersed knowledge from grassroots and
  DIY spaces, organize trust and participation, translate that experience into
  recommendations and testimony, and sustain participation beyond one meeting.
- The Council could use hearings to receive that organized stakeholder record
  while retaining legislative authority and responsibility.
- Espinal's public nightlife-reform frame benefited from credible participants,
  venue-grounded safety analysis, and public forums connecting affected spaces
  with government.

Finkelpearl's selection of the coalition in a budget hearing made a recent,
concrete outcome of reciprocal public engagement legible to Council. This is a
defensible interpretation of the testimony's placement, not a claim about his
private motive, institutional dependence, or the coalition's causal share in
later laws.

## Limits

The result is complete for the declared committee-to-matter recovery route. It
does not cover every Council committee, written submission, video,
non-transcript attachment, or unavailable record. "No additional occurrence
surfaced" must not be rewritten as "no other reference ever existed."
