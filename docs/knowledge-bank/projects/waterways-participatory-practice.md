# Waterways, Participation, and Civic Imagination

**Knowledge-bank status:** Governed reserve claim; no current website projection

## Governed Claim

In 2007, Jamie conceived *Release Yourself onto the Water until it Tastes of
Salt*. The Kansas City Star documented Jamie, Libby Hendon, and Laura Mattingly
traveling from Kansas City's West Bottoms into Louisiana on a
reclaimed-material, bicycle-powered raft and passing the 1,000-mile marker.

The record supports a specific professional throughline: Jamie recognized an
overlooked relationship between city life and the river, initiated a
participatory premise, helped give it a material and operational form with
others, sustained the work across distance and disruption, and articulated a
public purpose for it.

## Confirmed 2007 Record

- The Kansas City Star published the story on its front page on November 15,
  2007, with the continuation on A4.
- The article attributes the expedition's initial idea to Jamie.
- It names Libby Hendon and Laura Mattingly as the other core crew members.
- The journey launched from Kansas City's West Bottoms on July 21.
- The article describes a roughly 12-by-13-foot raft made from reclaimed
  building material, civic refuse, and soda-manufacturing containers, with two
  bicycles linked to a paddlewheel.
- By publication, the crew was reported south of Baton Rouge and beyond the
  1,000-mile marker.
- Friends joined portions of the journey, and the record identifies material
  support from Vicksburg residents, hosts, legal assistance, and public
  agencies.
- Jamie presented the river as connective cultural space between Kansas City's
  West Bottoms and Delta communities, countering its treatment as only an
  invisible boundary or industrial corridor.

## Chad's Lens

**Actor:** Jamie initiated the expedition premise; Jamie, Libby Hendon, and
Laura Mattingly formed the named core crew, within a larger support ecology.

**Action:** The crew translated an artistic and civic proposition into a
material vessel, route, daily operating practice, public encounters, and an
extended collaborative journey.

**Artifact:** A reclaimed-material raft with bicycle-powered paddlewheel
propulsion, supported by launch events, documentation, public reporting, and
later exhibition interpretation.

**Useful end:** The work made the river available as lived, shared, and
connective cultural space rather than leaving it abstract, invisible, or
reduced to infrastructure.
