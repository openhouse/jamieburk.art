# NYC Artist Coalition and Nightlife Advocacy

## Current Defensible Record

Gothamist publicly identified Jamie with NYC Artist Coalition in June 2017 and
reported that he organized fire-code study groups for DIY venues after the Ghost
Ship fire. The article also documents his participation in a City Hall rally for
full repeal of New York City's Cabaret Law.

NPR later placed NYC Artist Coalition among the groups mobilizing under the Let
NYC Dance banner. This supports a clear account of Jamie's documented safety and
repeal advocacy within collective work. It does not, by itself, establish the
coalition's formation history, legislative authorship, or a single actor's
causation of repeal.

A Greene Hill Food Co-op interview now supplies a second, Jamie-specific public
record. Jamie describes working as part of NYC Artist Coalition, frames the work
as preserving community spaces and legalizing dance, and invites people into a
coalition town hall about the new Office of Nightlife. The coalition's event
page documents the October 11, 2017, Market Hotel gathering, its grassroots
cultural-space agenda, and participating City officials.

The coalition's public campaign pages show the operating form of the work. Let
NYC Dance combined a Council call script, bill link, district lookup, and public
licensing, citation, and zoning evidence. Talks Not Raids combined a public
report, partner credit, Council sign-on tracking, and direct constituent action
around Intro 1156.

Official records establish the outcome chronology without assigning sole
causation: Intro 1688 created the Office of Nightlife and Nightlife Advisory
Board in September 2017; Intro 1652-A became Local Law 214 and repealed cabaret
licensing in November 2017; and Intro 1156 became Local Law 220 in 2019,
requiring MARCH reporting and adding notice and response provisions. Gothamist
reported that the City dismantled MARCH in December 2023 while other interagency
inspections continued.

A public-safe aggregate review of surviving project records supports describing
Jamie as a founding-era organizer who built and stewarded coalition and campaign
web infrastructure, campaign memory, coordination materials, source maps,
public-data framing, policy communications, and follow-up systems. The formal
co-founder title remains held for independent formation-record or collaborator
corroboration.
