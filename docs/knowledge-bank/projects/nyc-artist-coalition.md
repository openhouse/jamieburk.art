# NYC Artist Coalition

This dossier collects public-safe sources, claims, inquiries, boundaries, and
future photo research concerning Jamie's coalition-building, civic systems,
public web infrastructure, and cultural-space advocacy.

The first lifecycle run established founding-role attribution and 2017 Cabaret
Law advocacy. A second ten-source research round develops the coalition's early
organizing, mutual-aid network, public town halls, affordability testimony,
campaign infrastructure, and a bounded legislative outcome while keeping
individual and collective causal claims separate.

## Confirmed In This Run

- Contemporaneous reporting identified Jamie as a founding member of NYC Artist Coalition. NPR used the variant name "NYC Arts Coalition"; Gothamist corroborates the coalition affiliation and current name.
- Gothamist documented Jamie organizing fire-code study groups for DIY venues and publicly calling for full Cabaret Law repeal.
- NPR documented NYC Artist Coalition mobilizing with other organizations under Let NYC Dance.
- A recovered Greene Hill Food Co-op Q&A documents Jamie and Julia inviting readers to a September 28 town hall for dialogue with the new Office of Nightlife. It establishes the invitation, not a completed event or its effects.
- NPR and the Council record establish the 2017 Office of Nightlife institutional context, but not Jamie's exact causal contribution.
- The Council record establishes the October 31 vote context for Introduction 1652-A, but this run does not treat the press release as a complete final-enactment or advocacy-causality record.
- Bedford + Bowery identified Jamie as an organizer by February 2017 and attributed to him a report that more than 100 people had joined the coalition's mutual-aid network. The count remains attributed, not independently audited.
- Mixmag documented Jamie speaking as a coalition member at a City Council Cabaret Law hearing.
- A coalition letter and contemporaneous reporting establish an October 11, 2017 Market Hotel town hall spearheaded by NYC Artist Coalition, with Jamie among coalition participants. They do not establish a numeric attendance total or Jamie's exact production share.
- The city's 2018-2021 nightlife report separately documents a March 2018 coalition town hall introducing the first nightlife director to broad stakeholders, but does not name Jamie's individual role.
- The official October 22, 2018 Council hearing transcript documents Jamie testifying as a coalition member, connecting cultural-space safety and survival to commercial affordability and supporting Intro 737.
- Talks Not Raids publicly connected an issue explainer, Council-support tracker, call script, coalition credits, and media kit to Intro 1156. Official Council records show that the measure later had nineteen sponsors and became Local Law 220 of 2019.
- The public coalition priorities and Save NYC Spaces surfaces preserve further Office of Nightlife, M.A.R.C.H., safety, affordability, partner-credit, and campaign-interface context for future composition.

## Projection Decision

The FairRentNYC case study now cites eight selected claims: founding and early
organizer roles, the attributed mutual-aid signup count, Cabaret Law advocacy,
the October town hall, affordability testimony, the Talks Not Raids campaign
surface, and the bounded Local Law 220 outcome. Office-creation causality,
March 2018 individual-role claims, Save NYC Spaces, and M.A.R.C.H. disbandment
remain unused context or open inquiries.
