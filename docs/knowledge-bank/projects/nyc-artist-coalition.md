# NYC Artist Coalition and Talks Not Raids

## Jamie's documented role

As a founding member of NYC Artist Coalition, Jamie built public-facing civic
systems and also appears in contemporaneous records doing direct advocacy:
organizing fire-code study groups, rallying for Cabaret Law repeal, representing
the coalition in public reporting and the Save NYC Spaces campaign, testifying
in 2018 about commercial affordability and cultural-space safety, and
testifying before the New York City Council for Talks Not Raids.

## Collective accomplishments

- NYC Artist Coalition participated in the broad Let NYC Dance coalition that
  won repeal of the Cabaret Law after decades of prior advocacy.
- Contemporary reporting described NYC Artist Coalition as instrumental in
  Office of Nightlife advocacy and as spearheading a town hall where small,
  diverse cultural-space stakeholders addressed elected officials.
- Save NYC Spaces preserved Jamie's public cultural-space argument while
  presenting NYC Artist Coalition inside a broad partner coalition.
- A 2018 Council transcript records Jamie advocating lease-renewal and
  commercial-rent protections as an NYC Artist Coalition member.
- Talks Not Raids organized public support for M.A.R.C.H. transparency. Intro
  1156 became Local Law 220 of 2019, requiring reporting and notice provisions.
- In 2023 New York City replaced M.A.R.C.H. with the engagement-first CURE
  process after years of collective effort.

## Boundaries

These are coalition and movement outcomes. The evidence does not support saying
that Jamie alone repealed the Cabaret Law, created the Office of Nightlife,
wrote or enacted Intro 1156, or caused the replacement of M.A.R.C.H. The exact
extent of Jamie's individual town-hall production role remains an open inquiry.
