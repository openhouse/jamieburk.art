# NYC Artist Coalition - Nightlife Policy and Public Programs

## Claim Structure

This dossier separates Jamie's documented actions, NYC Artist Coalition's
collective work, formal city outcomes, and still-open causal questions.

## Cabaret Law Repeal

The strongest direct evidence establishes that Jamie:

- organized fire-code study groups for DIY venues;
- rallied at City Hall for full Cabaret Law repeal;
- articulated the connection between licensing pressure and venue safety;
- testified before the New York City Council as a member of NYC Artist
  Coalition;
- called for repeal and for low-income communities to be represented in
  nightlife governance.

Bedford + Bowery later described NYC Artist Coalition as instrumental in
advocacy around the Office of Nightlife and Cabaret Law repeal. The Council's
Legistar record confirms that Introduction 1652 was approved and signed as
Local Law 214 in 2017.

This supports a strong contribution claim. It does not support sole causality,
sole movement leadership, or legal authorship.

## Office of Nightlife and Market Hotel Town Hall

Independent coverage says NYC Artist Coalition spearheaded and organized the
October 2017 Market Hotel town hall. Reports describe a packed gathering of
artists, venue operators, officials, and community members focused on what the
new Office of Nightlife should protect and prioritize. Bedford + Bowery names
Jamie and Olympia Kazi as NYC Artist Coalition participants who had spoken at
hearings or town halls.

The group-level event role and Jamie's participation are documented. Jamie's
complete individual production, drafting, facilitation, and follow-through
responsibilities require more evidence before stronger public wording.

## Talks Not Raids and MARCH

The Talks Not Raids campaign publicly advocated transparency, trust,
communication, and relationship-based safety work instead of disruptive MARCH
raids. In 2019, the city enacted MARCH reporting and advance-notice provisions.
In 2023, the city replaced MARCH with CURE, an engagement-first process that
emphasized communication and opportunities to correct problems before
enforcement. The CURE announcement transcript acknowledged NYC Artist
Coalition among nightlife advocates.

The chronology and thematic continuity are established. A complete causal
chain from campaign work to Local Law 220 and CURE is not yet established.
