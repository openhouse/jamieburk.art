# NYC Artist Coalition

NYC Artist Coalition formed in 2017 as artists, organizers, cultural-space
operators, and allied advocates built a collective response to conditions
facing small, diverse New York City spaces where culture is made. Public
sources identify Jamie as a founding member. The record supports co-founder,
systems, facilitation, testimony, and web-infrastructure contributions; it does
not support sole-founder or sole-causality language.

## Jamie's role

Jamie's strongest documented contribution is the operating layer that helped
people move between cultural and civic settings. He helped turn recurring
meetings, listening, and situated experience into public explanations, maps,
letters, campaign sites, calls to action, testimony, and follow-up. A protected
working guide identifies him as a co-writer of a participation-to-action method.
Public sources separately document his coalition-affiliated testimony and civic
data recommendations.

The coalition was collective. Olympia Kazi and many other organizers,
participants, venue operators, partner groups, public officials, agency staff,
and witnesses retain their own work and credit. The archive does not identify
the human author of every institutional social post or every line of every
campaign artifact.

## What the coalition accomplished

The public record supports a sequence of collective accomplishments and aligned
institutional outcomes:

- The coalition made informal cultural-space conditions more legible through
  convenings, recommendations, mapped policy constraints, public records, and
  testimony received by DCLA and the City Council.
- Jamie organized fire-code study groups and participated in the broad campaign
  preceding the Council's 2017 repeal of the Cabaret Law. The repeal belongs to
  a multi-group campaign, Council sponsors, advocates, the Council, and the
  Mayor, not to one person or organization.
- After the Office of Nightlife was established, the coalition delivered
  recommendations and pursued public accountability. Current sources do not
  establish that Jamie individually created the Office.
- Jamie testified for Talks Not Raids and MARCH transparency in 2019. The
  Council later enacted Local Law 220, requiring public MARCH reporting and,
  with exceptions, notice and an opportunity for establishments to respond.
- In 2023 the City announced that the engagement-first CURE process replaced
  MARCH. The chronology is important; it does not prove that Jamie or Talks Not
  Raids alone caused either institutional decision.

## How the coalition worked

Recurring gatherings at different small cultural spaces created a listening
surface. The format let issues emerge from the people living them, then gave
the coalition ways to carry those issues into study groups, public letters,
campaign identities, town halls, hearings, maps, datasets, and legislation.

That translation was reciprocal. DCLA and the Council retained formal authority
and institutional capacity. The coalition contributed situated knowledge,
relationships, public participation, and forms officials could receive. The
strong claim is civic usefulness, not private dependence by an official on
Jamie or the coalition.

## What the work is becoming

Current Fair Rent and cultural-space work continues the same practice around
commercial affordability, leases, insurance, public data, and the survival of
small spaces. It is emerging work. Plans, meetings, and proposals should remain
distinguishable from adopted policy, completed implementation, and coalition
decisions.

## Present-tense source return

- **Question brought to the source:** What did Jamie make possible inside the
  coalition, and what can be said without absorbing collective outcomes into an
  individual story?
- **Original material reopened:** the official March 18, 2019 City Council
  Small Business hearing transcript, including rendered printed pages 142-145,
  alongside canonical coalition claims and the protected shared-folder review.
- **Scope and completeness:** direct rereading of one official hearing sequence
  plus the already reconciled source cluster; not a new census of every
  coalition record or collaborator perspective.
- **What changed in this reading:** Jamie's testimony is clearest as product and
  requirements work. He translated operators' recurring compliance questions
  into proposed rows and columns for machine-readable permit and certificate
  data, while adjacent coalition testimony retained the broader affordability
  and tenant-protection frame.
- **What did not change:** the transcript does not establish adoption of the
  proposal, sole authorship of coalition policy, or individual causality for
  legislation, the Office of Nightlife, Cabaret repeal, or MARCH's replacement.
- **Access boundary:** the Council transcript is public; protected shared-folder
  artifacts remain represented only through approved opaque IDs, aggregate
  accounting, and bounded observations.
- **Next return:** seek attributable collaborator accounts of facilitation,
  event production, and artifact reuse, especially where current evidence
  establishes the collective method more strongly than Jamie's exact role.

## Boundaries

- Say co-founded or founding member, never sole founder.
- Distinguish Jamie's direct actions from collective campaign outcomes and
  governmental decisions.

  and unapproved media outside public Git.

  independently shown.
