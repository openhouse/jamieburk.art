# Content Safety

The site should be true enough to trust, safe enough to publish, clear enough
to refer, and fast enough to use for applications.

## Public-Safe

- Approved resume language.
- Public websites and public repositories.
- Aggregate metrics with careful contribution language.
- Public-safe summaries of private work.
- Representative diagrams that do not expose private records.
- Known / Open / Protected framing.

## Keep Offline

- Raw transcripts.
- Private coalition notes.
- Legal-review materials.
- Private correspondence.
- Stakeholder lists.
- Customer data.
- Internal analytics dashboards.
- Revenue detail beyond approved aggregate wording.
- Guest lists, attendance records, addresses, and raw community records.
- Unapproved photos, screenshots, quotes, or collaborator details.
- Credentials, keys, tokens, and non-example environment files.
- Private or proprietary font files.

## Copy Rules

- Name Jamie's role without overstating sole ownership.
- Explain what the work made usable.
- Translate specialized language for public-sector and HR readers.
- Use collective-credit language for collective work.
- Keep metrics near their guardrails.
- Move uncertainty into launch docs, not public pages.
