# River-centered public engagement

## What this is

This neighborhood joins Jamie's river expedition, public-history programming,
and Great Accommodations work without collapsing them into a single event.

## Human situation

Residents lived beside a connected river system whose transportation, cultural,
and civic histories were difficult to encounter as shared public experience.

## What Jamie did

Jamie originated and organized participatory routes, screenings, outreach, and
an expedition framework. Suzanne Hogan shares documented outreach credit, while
Libby Hendon and Laura Mattingly are named expedition companions.

## What became usable

Screenings, an exhibition, public contributions, and a completed collective
journey offered experiential ways to understand river cities and their history.

## Evidence and limits

Public reporting and program records support the broad work. They do not assign
every contribution, prove measured civic outcomes, or resolve every route and
landing detail.

## Open questions

The complete support network, exact division of labor, and final expedition
details remain bounded research questions.

## Public projection

Any public composition must retain collective credit and distinguish reported
route facts from interpretation.
