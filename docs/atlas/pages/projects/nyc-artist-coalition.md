# NYC Artist Coalition

## What this is

This page connects the coalition's safety work, cultural-space advocacy,
Cabaret Law and Office of Nightlife interfaces, public meetings, campaign
identities, and source-backed institutional memory.

## Human situation

Small and informal cultural spaces needed ways to share knowledge, understand
safety and policy, speak for themselves, and enter city decision-making without
their experience being flattened into a single institutional voice.

## What Jamie did

The bank documents Jamie as one organizer contributing participation,
communications, research, technical, and public-interface infrastructure. It
does not publish an unresolved founder claim or assign him every coalition act.

## What became usable

Working sessions, public tools, testimony routes, town halls, source packages,
campaign identities, and follow-through made collective knowledge more legible
to participants, journalists, Council members, and city agencies.

## Evidence and limits

Rafael Espinal and other Council members retained legislative authority;
Tom Finkelpearl and DCLA retained institutional authority; Olympia Kazi and other
coalition participants retain their testimony, representation, and organizing
credit. Dorothy Howard retains expert editing and publication-handoff credit.

## Open questions

The precise founding chronology, authorship of individual communications, and
causal share of any policy outcome remain bounded or research-stage questions.

## Public projection

Portfolio language may describe Jamie's documented contribution only alongside
the coalition's collective work and the distinct authority of public officials.
