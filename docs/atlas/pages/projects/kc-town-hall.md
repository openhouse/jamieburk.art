# KC Town-Hall

## What this is

KC Town-Hall is represented as a body of collective resident-facing operations,
neighborhood practice, and documented public process.

## Human situation

Residents and neighborhood groups needed problems to move from a public room
into a trackable operating loop without losing the people, place, or limits of
what had actually been promised.

## What Jamie did

The canonical record separates Jamie's documented operational and stewardship
work from collective delivery, first-party completion accounts, later project
transitions, and public funding history.

## What became usable

The work created participatory and follow-through structures that could carry
resident concerns into documented next actions.

## Evidence and limits

Official records, public project material, and archive research support bounded
claims. Julia Fredenburg shares documented building, neighborhood, and tire
workflow credit; Jimmy Fitzner publicly corroborated one hands-on collection
activity. Oak Park Neighborhood Association, participating residents, city
staff, tradespeople, service partners, and other collaborators retain their own
work. The bank also preserves unused-fund history, transition boundaries, and
the difference between a documented action and a claimed civic outcome.

## Open questions

Some long-term outcome and role questions remain research-routed rather than
resolved by rhetoric.

## Public projection

Atlas keeps deeper neighborhood and governance context available while the
portfolio uses only the smallest defensible story needed by its audience.
