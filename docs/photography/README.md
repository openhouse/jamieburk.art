# Photography Workstream

This workstream will develop a governed relationship between Jamie's private,
lifetime-scale photographic archive, the public-safe Knowledge Bank, and the
purpose-built portfolio.

The archive is a private source corpus. It is not part of this repository, a
public asset library, or a pool of automatically publishable images. This first
commit establishes the working boundary only. It does not inspect, copy,
modify, ingest, select, or publish any photograph or private metadata.

## Purposes

Photography may help us:

- remember projects, places, people, sequences, and material conditions;
- reconstruct timelines and recover research leads;
- examine how Jamie works through presence, making, facilitation, and care;
- prepare bounded briefs for human photo editors;
- corroborate or complicate claims using independent sources;
- compose precise visual arguments for a particular public audience;
- preserve images whose present value is not yet understood.

The portfolio will remain a selective composition. The Knowledge Bank may hold
more visual knowledge than the website displays.

## Distinctions

- Discovery is not selection.
- Selection is not safety clearance.
- Safety clearance is not rights or represented-person consent.
- Rights and consent do not determine editorial usefulness.
- Jamie's custody of a file does not by itself establish authorship or rights.
- A visible detail does not establish the full event, identity, role, date, or
  outcome around it.
- A photograph may generate a research lead without becoming public evidence.

## Private Working Layer

The private layer may retain source identities, exact locators, checksums,
technical metadata, contact sheets, annotations, duplicate and burst
relationships, safety holds, rights contacts, consent notes, and rejected
selections.

Exact filenames, private paths, EXIF and location data, face or people labels,
sensitive subjects, and detailed safety annotations must remain outside public
Git. The working layer should permit reproducible return to the same source
material without exposing protected locators.

## Public-Safe Layer

The public repository may eventually retain only what is needed to govern an
approved use:

- aggregate, non-sensitive descriptions of reviewed cohorts;
- opaque asset or source identifiers;
- bounded visual observations and explicit anti-claims;
- project, event, place, source, and claim relationships;
- photo-editor briefs naming the question and desired scene;
- held, deferred, approved-for-review, or approved-for-specific-use states;
- public citations and rights information already suitable for publication.

No public record should contain precise home locations, private travel or
health context, hidden participant histories, face labels, raw device metadata,
or enough filesystem detail to reconstruct a protected locator.

## Source-Return Protocol

1. Name the present editorial or research question.
2. Identify a bounded source cohort without changing the library.
3. Verify that candidate files are materially reachable and decodable.
4. Preserve source identity and duplicate or burst relationships.
5. Inspect the image itself; metadata and machine descriptions are navigation
   aids, not substitutes for looking.
6. Record only what is visibly supportable, with uncertainty and anti-claims.
7. Seek independent sources for identity, date, role, event, and outcome claims.
8. Review safety, authorship, rights, represented people, crop, caption, and
   context as separate questions.
9. Bind approval to an exact asset version and a specific public use.
10. Return new factual leads to the Knowledge Bank intake and review lifecycle.

## Publication Gate

A photograph remains held unless its exact source identity is stable, the file
has been visually reviewed, the proposed caption is evidence-bounded, safety
review passes, authorship and rights are understood, represented-person review
is complete where required, the crop and context are approved, and Jamie
approves that exact version for that exact surface.

An AI system may assist discovery, grouping, description, and evaluation. It
may not clear a safety hold, infer consent, or grant publication permission.

## First Research Pass

The first archive encounter should be read-only and bounded. It should map the
available libraries, material availability, formats, broad chronology, likely
project cohorts, duplicates and bursts, existing albums, and access failures.
It should produce aggregate orientation and librarian questions, not a public
inventory or premature image selection.

After that orientation, the team can define project-specific research questions
and design evaluation sets without treating the full archive as one undivided
search problem.
