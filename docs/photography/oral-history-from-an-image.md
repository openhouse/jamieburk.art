# Oral History from an Image

A photograph can return a person to a scene without supplying the scene's
history. Use this protocol when an editor shares one privately held image with
Jamie or another participant.

## Three Lanes

### 1. Visible observation

Describe only what can be seen. Do not infer a project, role, emotion,
relationship, date, place, or outcome from appearance or archive metadata.

### 2. First-person recollection

Invite the speaker to describe what they directly remember: where and when,
what was happening, who was present, what responsibility they carried, what
decisions or dependencies mattered, what language people used, and what the
frame leaves out.

Store the response as a dated, perspective-bound recollection. A transcript or
voice note remains private until reviewed. Recollection may open or qualify
research; it does not confirm an institutional or collective claim by itself.

### 3. Research and rights follow-up

Identify the original and adjacent frames in the private archive. Look for
project records that corroborate chronology, role, deliverables, and outcomes.
Resolve photographer, credit, depicted-person consent, and permission for the
exact proposed use.

## Publication Boundary

Selection for conversation is not selection for the portfolio. Keep the image,
private source coordinates, people associations, and unreviewed transcript
outside public Git. A public use requires its own governed asset, source,
caption, credit, permission, occurrence, and human approval records.

An oral-history inquiry should remain useful even when its eventual disposition
is `private`, `protected`, `not recovered`, or `no public use`.
