# Chad Lens

Do not make the reader work to find Jamie's agency, purpose, or impact.

## Editorial Standard

- Use action verbs.
- Put Jamie back into sentences where lists erase him.
- Explain each project's purpose: toward what end?
- Make technical and civic language legible to hiring readers and public-sector readers.
- Avoid jargon unless it is immediately explained.
- Avoid unexplained symbols where words are clearer.
- State what became usable.
- Preserve collective-work language: co-built, contributed to, helped structure,
  supported, stewarded.
- Avoid unsupported ownership claims.
- Do not reduce stewardship, facilitation, convening, coordination,
  documentation architecture, and analysis to merely tracking.
- Make named entities self-explaining.
- Support real employment opportunities in Technical Operations, Product
  Operations, Implementation, public-facing systems, and knowledge systems.

## Practical Test

For every page, ask:

- What did Jamie do?
- Who used it?
- What became easier, safer, clearer, or more durable?
- What is the strongest accurate claim?
- What would overclaim ownership, authority, policy impact, or certainty?

If the reader has to infer the answer, revise the copy.
