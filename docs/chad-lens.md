# Chad Lens: Professional Legibility

1. Minimize the reader's burden.
2. Put Jamie back into the sentence.
3. Use action verbs.
4. Answer "toward what end?"
5. Show what became usable.
6. Translate specialized language for hiring and public-sector readers.
7. Do not let tool lists make Jamie disappear.
8. Do not undersell stewardship as "tracking."
9. Use "helped," "supported," "contributed to," or "co-built" where work was
   collective.
10. Avoid unsupported ownership claims.
11. Do not publish unapproved contact info, metrics, screenshots, names,
   quotes, or private materials.

## Copy Examples

Weak:

> Tracking meetings, decisions, action items.

Better:

> Synthesizing meetings into decision records, action trackers, risk notes,
> open questions, and stakeholder follow-up.

Weak:

> Data.

Better:

> Open-data translation, dataset documentation, source maps, and public-data
> requirements.

Weak:

> Built and stewarded 30+ pages...

Safer:

> Helped build and steward shared campaign-memory infrastructure...

Weak:

> Local calendar maintainers.

Better:

> Local calendar organizers or community event-listing organizers.

## Emerging Work Language

Use "emerging work" when it removes shame from uncertainty. Then immediately
anchor it in concrete deliverables.

Preferred:

> Jamie helps teams surface the structure inside emerging work and turn it into
> usable systems: requirements, workflows, documentation, source maps, decision
> records, action tracking, public guidance, launch support, onboarding, and
> durable handoffs.

For stricter hiring contexts, define the situation:

> Emerging work often arrives before decisions, ownership, documentation, and
> handoffs are clear enough for a team to use.
