# Chad Lens

This portfolio is a professional self-representation surface. It should make Jamie legible as a Technical Project Manager - Product Operations & Implementation lead who creates operating structure for complex public-facing teams.

The site is not a total archive, a civic memoir, a private document browser, or a maximal proof dump. It should help a hiring reader understand what Jamie does, why it matters, and what became usable.

## Rules

1. Minimize the reader's burden.

   Do not require hiring readers to decode specialized language, coalition context, private history, or tool stacks before they understand the work.

2. Bring Jamie into view as the actor.

   Avoid tool lists or nouns where Jamie disappears. Use verbs: clarified, structured, coordinated, documented, synthesized, stewarded, built, co-built, implemented, supported, and maintained.

3. Ask "toward what end?"

   Every case study should answer why the work mattered and what changed for the team, public, organization, business, collaborator, or community.

4. Show what became usable.

   The recurring proof pattern is: unclear situation -> usable system. The system might be a workflow, decision record, source map, public guide, onboarding path, launch plan, campaign memory, catalog structure, or handoff.

5. Avoid tiny inflation.

   Prefer truthful verbs over stronger but less supportable ones. "Contributed to" can be stronger than an unsupported "caused." "Helped build" can be stronger than "led" when the work was collective.

6. Make specialized terms legible.

   If using terms like under-structured, maintainers, data, MVP, source-backed, or lightweight, translate them in plain language or replace them in reader-facing copy.

7. Target the opportunity.

   A true fact may still be distracting if it does not serve technical operations, product operations, implementation, public-interest technology, knowledge systems, documentation architecture, or source-backed team memory work.

## Practical Review Questions

- Does this sentence make Jamie easier to hire for the work he wants?
- Does it name what changed or only describe the topic?
- Does the verb match the evidence?
- Does the page protect private source material and collective credit?
- Can a reader understand the project without knowing Jamie's archive?
- Does the page make the work feel more usable, not merely more impressive?
