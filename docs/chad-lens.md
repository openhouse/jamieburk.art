# Chad Lens - Professional Self-Representation Guidelines

Core rule: make the unusual work institutionally legible without losing its
purpose, agency, or dignity.

Before shipping public copy, ask:

1. Is Jamie visible as the actor?
2. Does the sentence answer "toward what end?"
3. Is the language legible to a hiring manager, recruiter, HR reader, or
   public-sector reader?
4. Are specialized terms translated once?
5. Are acronyms spelled out or avoided?
6. Are symbols or arrows avoided where words would be clearer?
7. Are unfamiliar entities defined?
8. Does the copy protect collective credit and avoid overclaiming?
9. Does it avoid reducing stewardship, facilitation, convening, coordination, or
   analysis to mere "tracking"?
10. Does it make the work impressive without making the reader decode it?

Prefer:

- built
- co-built
- created
- stewarded
- synthesized
- structured
- translated
- coordinated
- documented
- maintained
- supported
- contributed to
- helped teams use

Use with care:

- under-structured
- lightweight
- tracking
- data
- AI-ready
- source-backed
- lab
- platform
- official

Avoid:

- single-handedly
- transformed everything
- official city service, unless true
- authoritative legal analysis
- solved
- owned entirely
- production SaaS, unless true
