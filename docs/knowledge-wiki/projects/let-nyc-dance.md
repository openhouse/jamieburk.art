# Let NYC Dance

Let NYC Dance was a multi-group campaign to repeal New York City's 1926
Cabaret Law. Its public surface connected historical and policy explanation, a
fact sheet, a map, Council sponsor state, calls to action, press, social
sharing, and coalition credit. The Council repealed the law in 2017 after a
broad campaign involving Council Member Rafael Espinal, cultural-space
operators, artists, advocacy groups, Council sponsors, the Council, and the
Mayor.

## Jamie's Supported Role

Public reporting identifies Jamie as a founding member of NYC Artist Coalition
and documents him organizing fire-code study groups and rallying for full
repeal. The governed portfolio record also supports his contribution to the
coalition's campaign websites. The strongest account joins those two forms of
work: helping people understand and act on a difficult policy environment while
working inside a coalition whose members held distinct organizing,
legislative, venue, communications, and community roles.

## What Became Usable

- a public explanation joining discriminatory history to a current bill;
- a call script and Council contact route;
- an inspectable sponsor state;
- shared maps, media, facts, and press context; and
- a campaign identity that preserved coalition credit.

## Outcome Boundary

The completed governmental outcome was repeal of the Cabaret Law. Jamie's
documented actions and web-infrastructure contribution belong in that history,
but they do not establish sole legislative causality. Do not say Jamie or NYC
Artist Coalition alone repealed the law, and do not treat repeal as the end of
every zoning or dance-policy constraint.
