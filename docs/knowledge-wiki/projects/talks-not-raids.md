# Talks Not Raids

Talks Not Raids sought transparency, notice, and more direct problem-solving
around New York City's Multi-Agency Response to Community Hotspots program. Its
public system connected venue testimony, reporting, a Council bill, a call
script, sponsor state, coalition identity, and campaign media.

## Jamie's Supported Role

The official February 11, 2019 Council transcript identifies Jamie as an NYC
Artist Coalition member and records him asking for Talks Not Raids, public
MARCH information, and direct work among the Office of Nightlife, cultural
liaisons, and establishments. The governed portfolio record also supports his
contribution to campaign web infrastructure. Protected records add an evidence
workflow but do not assign every calculation, artifact, or line of copy to
Jamie.

## What Became Usable

- a common campaign vocabulary for experiences that were difficult to see as a
  citywide pattern;
- a bill number, Council contact path, sponsor state, and call script;
- public reporting and venue accounts assembled beside the requested action;
  and
- Council testimony placing Jamie's requests and coalition affiliation in an
  official record.

## Outcome Boundary

Intro 1156-A became Local Law 220 of 2019. In 2023 the City announced that the
engagement-first CURE process replaced MARCH. These are aligned institutional
outcomes in the campaign chronology. The sequence does not establish sole
legislative causality or that Jamie or one campaign alone caused CURE. It does
not establish that every form of multi-agency nightlife enforcement ended.
Campaign-era numerical claims remain held until their data, denominators, and
transformations can be reproduced.
