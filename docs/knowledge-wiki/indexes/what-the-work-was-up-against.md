# What The Work Was Up Against

Naming pressure first prevents the portfolio from turning residents, artists,
workers, or collaborators into problems Jamie arrived to fix. The pressure is
often a failure of reception: a useful institution cannot yet hear, route,
remember, or act on knowledge already present.

| Project | Situation and pressure | Bounded response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CallNYC | Public constituent-services data was available but not organized as a resident-facing issue pathway. | An independent prototype translated categories into a more navigable contact route. |
| WOW List | Cultural information was distributed across organizers, inboxes, calendars, and social ties. | Jamie and Richard developed a community calendar with reusable publishing and distribution. |
| NYC Artist Coalition | Small cultural spaces encountered regulatory, affordability, safety, and representation pressures that were difficult to carry into formal civic processes. | A coalition practice linked meetings, campaign systems, testimony, public explanation, and follow-through. |
| KC Town Hall | A long-abandoned building needed coordinated physical care while neighborhood knowledge and future use remained unsettled. | Restoration operations and a lightweight listening process proceeded together. |
| Source-Backed Team Memory | Decisions and provenance disappear when records remain fragmented or only in the memories of people who were present. | A governed practice separates sources, claims, contradictions, protected absences, and publication decisions. |

These rows describe conditions and responses, not savior narratives, universal
community consensus, or proof that one intervention resolved the whole system.
