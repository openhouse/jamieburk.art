# Places Where The Work Became Real

## Orientation

Place is not background in Jamie's work. A gallery, river, kitchen, cultural
space, street, neighborhood route, or construction site changes who can enter,
what can be noticed, and what kind of structure becomes possible.

This is a retrieval map, not a claim that every place served the same purpose.

## Present Reading

### Galleries And Shared Domestic Space

Shop Shows and Open House tested invitation, shared responsibility, art and
life, institutional boundaries, and the conditions under which unfinished work
could be offered to other people.

### Waterways And Moving Public Space

The collaborative raft expedition and Great Accommodations treated rivers as
physical routes and possible social connections among cities. The sources
record public encounter and Jamie's interpretation, not consensus from every
river community.

### Kitchens And Recurring Tables

Sunday Dinner made repetition itself into infrastructure: invitations, themes,
attendance records, meals, and a recurring reason to gather. Protected ledgers
support aggregate continuity, not publication of person-level participation.

### Small Cultural Spaces

NYC Artist Coalition meetings and events moved among small cultural spaces.
The changing venues helped the coalition listen across different conditions
while creating a civic interface for artists, agencies, and elected officials.

### Streets And Neighborhood Routes

KC Town Hall's public operations and survey connected a building project to
resident input, recurring service information, and municipal process. The
record does not turn neighborhood testimony into Jamie's property or establish
his authorship of every interaction.

### Construction Sites

The 2019 municipal packet records a completed cold-shell phase at KC Town Hall.
The site connected sequencing, material constraints, restoration, public
benefit, and neighborhood imagination. Jamie's individual general-contractor
title remains a research question.

## Source Return

The [July 2026 editorial-depth source return](../research-runs/editorial-depth-source-return-2026.md)
revisited place-bearing public and protected records. The cross-project reading
supports situated learning while preserving distinct attribution, access,
safety, privacy, and consent boundaries for each place.

**Establishes:** a recurring practice of learning and building in the physical
and social settings where a problem, invitation, or public relationship was
actually present.

**Does not establish:** present-day access or safety, ownership of a place,
representative community consent, permission to identify participants, or a
single causal story connecting every site.

## Known / Open / Protected

- **Known:** selected public locations or place types, bounded project activity,
  and how specific settings shaped operating choices.
- **Open:** additional place records, participant and collaborator corrections,
  current conditions, failed invitations, and rights-clear visual evidence.
- **Protected:** private addresses, participant identities, access instructions,
  vulnerable circumstances, raw attendance, and unapproved photographs.

## Place And Person Boundary

No person becomes evidentiary texture because they appeared in a place where
Jamie worked. Name, quote, photograph, attendance, and personal-history use each
require their own source, relevance, privacy, rights, consent, and context
decision.

## Next Research

Build individual place records only when they improve retrieval and can retain
the specific access, safety, attribution, and consent boundary. Begin with
rights-clear public records, not private addresses or participant archives.
