## First-Person Draft

I keep returning to situations where people know something important before a
system knows how to hear it.

Sometimes the next useful thing is a room, a meal, a meeting, or a public
invitation. Sometimes it is a website, map, form, handbill, source trail,
workflow, or archive. I am interested in the moment when experience becomes
shared language and shared language becomes something people can use.

I believe structure can be a form of care. A clear record can save someone from
having to tell the whole story again. A recurring gathering can let trust grow
over time. A public identity can help people recognize one another and act
together. A boundary can protect the people who made the work possible.

The point is not to make every human situation orderly. It is to make the next
responsible action more possible without erasing ambiguity, conflict, pleasure,
or difference. I want documentation to preserve agency, not turn people into
material for somebody else's narrative.

What is at stake for me is whether the structures we build leave people more
able to enter, understand, contribute, correct, continue, and change the work.
That is the connection I see among art, public life, technical operations, and
memory. They are different practices, but they keep asking me the same
question: what can we make together that helps the best possibilities become
real?
