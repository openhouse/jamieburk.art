# CallNYC and Civic Hall, 2016

Public-safe research memo, reviewed July 11, 2026.

## Finding

The recovered record supports this wording:

> Jamie participated in a New York City Council constituent-services hackathon at Civic Hall on January 30, 2016. After the full CouncilStat dataset was released, he independently built CallNYC as a resident-facing interpretation of administrative case records.

The record does not support describing Jamie as an organizer or official representative, or CallNYC as an official Council product, commissioned project, hackathon submission, or winning entry.

## Recovered evidence

1. Civic Hall announced `1/30 1-3pm` for a New York City Council hackathon focused on constituent services: [original post](https://x.com/CivicHall/status/693124020917522433).
2. The attached Council-branded [promotional graphic](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/CZ5m-mAWwAA42td.png:large) reads `New York City Council Hackathon` and displays `labs.council.nyc`.
3. The New York City Council posted from Civic Hall on January 30 and identified the gathering as its first CouncilStat hackathon: [event-day post](https://x.com/NYCCouncil/status/693509031768506368).
4. The posts survive in a January 31 [Civic Hall Wayback capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20160131004601/http://civichall.org/events/page/2/) through an embedded social feed.
5. Miranda Neubauer's March 14, 2016 [Politico New York report](https://callnyc.org/data/media/Politico-Website-provides-new-information-about-council-members-focus.pdf) connects Jamie to the January event, the pre-release material, the subsequent full dataset release, and his independent development and iteration of CallNYC.
6. The public [CallNYC repository](https://github.com/openhouse/CallNYC) documents the surviving implementation.
7. A protected participant photograph records a breakout-table placard reading `Digital District - Help improve City Council District office operations` and supplies an approximately 2:10 p.m. image timestamp. The image has no public link and is not approved for display.

## Bounded reconstruction

The archival inquiry reviewed 4,630 Wayback captures across 1,240 URLs, grouped 296 event keys, and classified 215 successful event pages, 74 redirects, and 7 captured 404 responses.

The inquiry did not recover:

- a dedicated Civic Hall calendar listing or event-detail page;
- the complete registration form;
- a full agenda;
- a breakout roster or complete participant list;
- the facilitator of Digital District;
- the other breakout tracks;
- formal winners or a published project roster.

This is a record of what a bounded search did and did not recover. It is not proof that no dedicated event page ever existed.

## Required distinctions

### Embedded feed and calendar listing

The Wayback capture preserves the relevant posts inside Civic Hall's embedded social feed. It must not be described as a recovered Civic Hall calendar listing.

### Brand and formal title

The graphic directly supports the wording `New York City Council Hackathon`. Treat that as recovered event branding, not proof of a longer formal registration title.

### Breakout and event

Digital District is supported as a breakout-table title only. It was not established as the event's overall title.

### Image time and event hours

The participant photograph's approximately 2:10 p.m. timestamp documents the image. It is not the event start time. Civic Hall's public announcement supplies the 1-3 p.m. hours.

### Hackathon and independent follow-on

Jamie participated in the January event. The available sources place his independent CallNYC development after the full CouncilStat dataset release. They do not establish that CallNYC was commissioned, submitted to the hackathon, selected as a winner, or owned by the Council.

## Safe wording

- A New York City Council constituent-services hackathon at Civic Hall on January 30, 2016.
- Civic Hall announced the gathering for 1-3 p.m.; the Council described it on the event day as its first CouncilStat hackathon.
- A recovered promotional graphic branded the gathering `New York City Council Hackathon`.
- One documented breakout was titled `Digital District - Help improve City Council District office operations`.
- After the full CouncilStat dataset was released, Jamie independently developed CallNYC.

## Anti-claims

- Do not say Jamie organized, led, or officially represented the hackathon.
- Do not say Digital District was the event's official title.
- Do not say 2:10 p.m. was the event's start time.
- Do not say Jamie authored all participant recommendations.
- Do not say Jamie caused the CouncilStat release.
- Do not describe CallNYC as an official Council product.
- Do not describe CallNYC as commissioned, a formal hackathon submission, or a winning entry.
- Do not say Speaker Melissa Mark-Viverito attended.
- Do not treat CouncilStat totals as simple measures of office quality, effectiveness, specialization, or resident need.
- Do not claim measured improvements in case resolution, district-office efficiency, or constituent satisfaction.
