# Weekly Practice Review and Commitment Protocol

> **Proposal boundary**
>
> This RFC is `proposed`. It defines a bounded operating practice for review;
> it does not authorize implementation, surveillance, assignment of work to
> another person, publication, deployment, indexing, or an RFC stage change.
> Jamie Burkart remains the decision owner.

## Summary

Create a weekly review that turns a broad record of real work into a small,
explicit commitment slate. Preservation is not commitment: the Knowledge Wiki
may retain many worthy records while the next weekly cycle carries a default
maximum of three active outcome commitments. Each gathering names one governing
verb and ends with a closure receipt. Each next action has a voluntary owner,
an authority boundary, and an exit condition. Public records describe only the
general method and approved outcomes; relationship state, negotiations, private
communications, and protected interpretations remain in the private sidecar or
source vault.

The proposal combines three distinct operating capabilities: finishing and
selection, purposeful gathering, and distributed agency. Those capabilities
were sharpened through a user-requested fictionalized analytical exercise.
People whose public work informed the lenses are not authors, participants,
reviewers, endorsers, or decision-makers for this RFC.

## Motivation

The current system is unusually good at preserving context, opening useful
branches, and making ambiguous work legible. The same strength creates a
predictable risk: a preserved possibility can silently become an obligation,
every worthwhile conversation can become a project, and Jamie can become the
default owner of work whose authority properly belongs to a client,
collaborator, or collective.

The observed operating problem is not a lack of ideas or infrastructure. It is
the cost of too many concurrent worthy commitments, meetings without a single
decision purpose, and handoffs that leave ownership implied. That cost appears
as incomplete closure, unpaid bespoke scoping, branch proliferation, and a
mental model in which remembering work feels like promising to do it.

This RFC proposes a small control loop. It should make a week easier to judge by
what changed in the world, not by the volume of artifacts created. It should
also let a rich private record coexist with a narrow public surface without
turning privacy into forgetting or documentation into automatic publication.

## Goals

- Separate comprehensive preservation from selective commitment.
- Default to no more than three active outcome commitments in one weekly cycle.
- Keep lane balance visible without turning suggested lanes into quotas.
- Require one governing purpose for each planned gathering.
- End each gathering with a compact, inspectable closure receipt.
- Require voluntary ownership and explicit decision authority for next actions.
- Prevent a new commitment from entering a full slate without an explicit
  completion, removal, delegation, or substitution.
- Turn bespoke advice into paid work through an explicit boundary rather than
  continuing undefined unpaid scoping.
- Keep private relationships, negotiations, conflict, and entrusted material
  in permissioned custody.
- Permit public-safe learning about the method without exposing private
  provenance or implying another person's endorsement.
- Keep automation useful for inventory and consistency while reserving
  priorities, assignments, interpretation, and publication for people.

## Non-goals

- Do not create a productivity score, employee-monitoring system, time tracker,
  personal-surveillance dashboard, or measure of human worth.
- Do not require exactly three commitments or require one commitment from every
  suggested lane.
- Do not turn every preserved record, open branch, relationship, or idea into an
  active task.
- Do not let an agent choose Jamie's priorities, accept work, assign another
  person, or resolve collective authority.
- Do not infer consent, attendance, agreement, ownership, endorsement, or a
  deadline from a transcript, calendar event, message, or task list.
- Do not publish weekly private detail, raw transcripts, correspondence,
  negotiations, health or financial information, or private graph topology.
- Do not replace project-specific, collective, contractual, legal, or
  publication gates.
- Do not authorize changes to public website copy, a new route, deployment, or
  production indexing.

## Terminology

**Evidence window**
: The bounded dates and sources reviewed for a weekly reflection. It states what
  was observed, what was not available, and what remains inference.

**Preservation queue**
: Records, possibilities, branches, and ideas kept for future retrieval. An item
  can remain valuable without being scheduled. Preservation is not commitment.

**Active commitment**
: A voluntarily accepted, outcome-shaped obligation for the next weekly cycle
  with an owner, authority boundary, next evidence, and exit condition.

**Commitment slate**
: The complete set of active commitments for the weekly cycle. The default
  maximum is three.

**Suggested lane**
: A balance prompt, not a quota: public portfolio and product work;
  collective/civic work under the collective's authority; and paid practice or
  career conversion.

**Governing verb**
: The one primary purpose of a gathering: `understand`, `repair`, `advise`,
  `deliberate`, `decide`, or `commit`.

**Closure receipt**
: A short record of what changed, what remains unresolved, any voluntary
  owners, the public/private disposition, and the next evidence or review date.

**Agency map**
: A record separating people, interests, resources, decision authority,
  voluntary commitments, and unassigned needs.

**One-in, one-out substitution**
: The explicit disposition of one active commitment before another enters a
  full commitment slate.

## Detailed design

### 1. Open with an evidence window

The weekly review begins with a declared start time, end time, source inventory,
and coverage limits. Observed events remain distinct from interpretations. A
sent message is not acceptance; a meeting is not adoption; a draft is not an
agreement; preserved source material is not publication permission.

The review may use a private evidence packet to understand the week. A public
receipt must not disclose the packet's protected contents or locators.

### 2. Build two lists, not one

The preservation queue may be comprehensive. The commitment slate must be
selective. Moving an item into preservation therefore creates no deadline,
promise, or priority. Moving an item onto the commitment slate requires:

1. a one-sentence outcome that can be recognized when it exists;
2. a voluntary owner;
3. the decision authority relevant to that outcome;
4. the next observable evidence;
5. an exit condition; and
6. a privacy and publication disposition.

The default slate limit is three. Suggested lanes help reveal imbalance but are
not quotas. Jamie may carry fewer than three commitments. An emergency or
high-value exception requires a recorded override and an explicit account of
what will stop, move, or lose attention.

When the slate is full, admission follows a one-in, one-out rule. The outgoing
item must be marked `completed`, `delegated-with-opt-in`, `deferred`, `declined`,
or `archived` before the incoming item becomes active. Merely opening another
branch does not create capacity.

### 3. Give each gathering one purpose

Before a gathering, the host or authorized organizer selects exactly one
governing verb:

- `understand`: form a shared account of the situation;
- `repair`: address a rupture, error, or failed handoff;
- `advise`: offer bounded judgment without transferring decision authority;
- `deliberate`: compare options and tradeoffs without requiring a decision;
- `decide`: make a named decision held by the authorized decision-maker; or
- `commit`: invite voluntary owners to accept specified next actions.

Supporting activities may occur, but the invitation, agenda, and ending are
organized around the single verb. If the purpose changes during the gathering,
the closure receipt records the change instead of retroactively pretending the
original purpose was met.

### 4. End with a closure receipt

A gathering closes by recording:

- the governing verb and whether its purpose was met;
- what changed;
- what remains unresolved;
- decisions made and the authority under which they were made;
- voluntary owners and their accepted actions;
- unassigned needs, left visibly unassigned;
- the public, private-sidecar, or source-vault disposition; and
- the next evidence, review date, or explicit stopping condition.

No one is assigned work by inference. Silence, attendance, enthusiasm,
expertise, prior help, or mention in a transcript does not create ownership.

### 5. Review agency, not indispensability

For each active collective or client-facing item, the agency map asks:

- Who is affected?
- What interests are at stake?
- What resources are available?
- Who holds the relevant decision authority?
- Who has voluntarily accepted an action?
- What need remains unowned?
- Would this artifact increase others' ability to act, or make Jamie more
  indispensable?

An operating artifact passes this review when it improves shared orientation,
preserves choice, and makes responsibility clearer. It fails when it silently
centralizes authority or makes Jamie the permanent translator of every next
step.

### 6. Protect the paid-work boundary

A relationship-building conversation may remain exploratory. When the requested
work becomes bespoke analysis, research, document production, system design, or
implementation advice for another party, the next step must be one of:

- a paid bounded working session under written authorization;
- a separately defined project or employment process;
- a consciously donated contribution whose scope and recipient are explicit;
  or
- a stop.

Goodwill does not create an unlimited unpaid workstream. The weekly review may
surface this boundary, but it cannot infer another party's budget, intent, or
authority to engage Jamie.

### 7. Public and private records

The public repository may retain:

- this general method;
- synthetic or generic eval cases;
- aggregate process status without private denominators;
- approved public outcomes and artifacts; and
- public-safe reflections that do not reveal private provenance.

The private sidecar may retain governed relationship chronology, negotiation
state, private interpretations, participant-restricted records, decision
context, and source pointers. Raw account exports, credentials, unrestricted
recordings, and provider archives remain outside Git in the source-vault plane.

A private insight reaches the public surface only through a separate
publication packet that removes private provenance, verifies factual support,
preserves collective credit, and receives Jamie's approval. The public
repository never links back to private topology.

### 8. Automation boundary

Automation may inventory sources, identify missing fields, count active
commitments, detect a full slate, validate closure-receipt structure, flag a
public/private mismatch, and draft a review packet.

Automation may not choose priorities, assign people, infer opt-in, manufacture
deadlines, decide that a relationship has become commercial, resolve a
collective decision, grade Jamie's worth, expose private material, publish,
merge, deploy, or index. A generated recommendation remains advice to the human
decision owner.

### 9. Weekly review artifact

The proposed review artifact should fit within a 30-minute operator budget and
contain no more than one page of decision-facing material before appendices:

1. evidence-window receipt;
2. closed and carried work;
3. preservation-queue additions;
4. the next commitment slate;
5. gathering closure gaps;
6. agency and paid-work boundary flags; and
7. human decisions required.

The review does not require a letter grade. If a qualitative assessment is
useful, it must cite concrete observed outcomes and separate relational,
collective, commercial, and completion dimensions.

## Security and privacy

Weekly review concentrates context, making it a high-risk leakage surface. The
default detailed artifact belongs in the private sidecar. Public output must be
minimum necessary and may not contain private names, message bodies, transcript
text, relationship or negotiation state, protected locators, raw source hashes,
private graph topology, or an identity-revealing combination of aggregate
facts.

The protocol fails closed when placement is uncertain. Access is not consent,
evidence is not publication permission, and a private inference is not a public
claim. The protocol also rejects person-level productivity surveillance and
automated scoring of collaborators.

## Publication workflow

This RFC itself is public-safe. Adoption would not make detailed weekly reviews
public. A future public projection requires an exact candidate, source and claim
review, collective-credit review where relevant, privacy review, and Jamie's
explicit approval. Private-sidecar or source-vault locators must be absent from
the public candidate.

No named analytical lens is evidence of participation, review, endorsement,
availability, or authority. The role-play remains fictionalized editorial
input.

## Rollout plan

### Phase 0: review only

- Review this RFC and its deterministic adversarial cases.
- Decide whether a three-commitment default is appropriately bounded.
- Decide whether the six governing verbs cover real gathering purposes.
- Do not change current work solely because this proposal exists.

### Phase 1: two-week private canary

After explicit acceptance and implementation authorization, run two weekly
reviews in the private sidecar. Collect only process evidence: elapsed review
time, number of active commitments, explicit substitutions, closure-receipt
completion, and human correction notes. Do not publish the canary.

### Phase 2: human review

Jamie reviews whether the protocol reduced mental load, improved closure, and
distributed agency without suppressing necessary care. Any collaborator-facing
effect requires separate affected-party review.

### Phase 3: bounded implementation decision

Keep, revise, or close the RFC. A kept protocol may receive a small local tool
for validation, but must remain usable as plain Markdown and must not become a
surveillance dashboard.

Rollback is deletion or archival of generated review artifacts and cessation
of the practice. Source records remain under their existing custody and
retention rules.

## Decision gates

- Jamie accepts, revises, or closes the proposed default slate limit.
- Jamie accepts or revises the governing-verb vocabulary.
- Jamie authorizes any two-week private canary.
- Affected people opt in before receiving an assigned action.
- Collective decision owners authorize collective commitments.
- A client or authorized entity approves paid work in writing before bespoke
  work begins, unless Jamie explicitly records a bounded donation.
- Jamie approves any public projection from a weekly review.
- Separate approval governs implementation, merge, deployment, and indexing.

## Drawbacks

A numerical cap can become dogmatic, hide the difference between small and
large commitments, or encourage gaming. A closure receipt can become clerical
busywork. One governing verb may oversimplify a complex gathering. The protocol
may privilege easily stated outcomes over slow relational work. Private weekly
reviews also create concentrated sensitive records that require careful
custody.

These risks are why the slate limit is a default, the lanes are prompts rather
than quotas, relationship work may be named as an outcome, and the proposed
canary is private, time-bounded, and reversible.

## Alternatives

**Do nothing.** Preserve the current flexible practice. This avoids process
overhead but leaves preservation, commitment, and ownership easy to conflate.

**Use a conventional task manager.** This may improve reminders but does not
encode evidence state, authority, public/private placement, or collective
agency.

**Adopt a strict productivity system.** A rigid inbox/project taxonomy or time
metric could reduce ambiguity, but it would poorly represent relational,
collective, and private knowledge work and could become surveillance.

**Track every branch as an active project.** This makes Git the commitment
model. It is rejected because branches preserve possibility; they do not prove
priority, authority, or capacity.

**Make the review public by default.** This might demonstrate process, but it
would pressure private relationships and entrusted material into a performative
surface. Public method and approved outcomes are sufficient.

## Unresolved questions

- Is three the right default slate limit after two real weekly cycles?
- Should relationship stewardship be a suggested lane or remain an outcome
  type that may appear across lanes?
- Are `advise` and `deliberate` distinct enough in actual gatherings?
- What is the smallest closure receipt people will reliably complete?
- When should a consciously donated contribution require the same written scope
  as paid work?
- Which aggregate canary measures are useful without becoming productivity
  surveillance?
