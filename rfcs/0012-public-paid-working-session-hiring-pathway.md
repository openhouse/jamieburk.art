# Public Paid Working Session Hiring Pathway

> **Proposal boundary**
>
> This RFC is `proposed`. It recommends a public hiring pathway for review. It
> does not authorize website implementation, publication of the proposed price,
> a public contract, deployment, indexing, a claim of present availability, or
> an RFC stage change. Jamie Burkart remains the decision owner.

## Summary

Add, after explicit acceptance and copy approval, one small public entry offer:
a paid working session focused on a single useful outcome. The recommended
surface is a compact section on Contact, reached by at most one secondary link
elsewhere. The proposed $250 fixed fee covers up to 60 minutes of Jamie's total
effort, including agreed preparation, a working session or focused analysis,
and a short written recap. The session may stand alone. Any further work must be
scoped and authorized separately.

This is the smallest credible bridge between the portfolio's evidence and a
paid engagement. It is not an hourly-labor listing, a public rate card, a live
booking promise, a consulting marketplace, or the service agreement itself.
The public description must contain no private relationship history,
correspondence, transcript-derived context, negotiation state, private source
locator, or private graph topology.

## Motivation

The current Contact page offers a direct email address for roles, referrals,
consulting conversations, and collaboration. That is clear and honest, but it
leaves a prospective client to invent the commercial next step. A reader can
understand Jamie's work and still be unable to answer four practical questions:

1. What is the smallest useful thing I can engage Jamie to do?
2. What will I receive?
3. What does it cost?
4. Does starting small obligate either party to a larger engagement?

That ambiguity raises the response burden at the moment when the site should
make action easiest. It can also invite unpaid diagnostic work inside an
open-ended introductory conversation.

The opportunity is to make one compensated starting unit legible without
repositioning the portfolio as a freelance marketplace or weakening Jamie's
full-time role narrative. The public rationale is the reusable service design,
not any private event or relationship that helped reveal the gap.

Three public interaction patterns are useful but incomplete precedents:

- [GLG](https://glginsights.com/council-members/) makes compensated expert
  access legible and shows how a small consultation can precede larger work,
  but its platform and compliance model should not be copied into a personal
  portfolio.
- [Clarity](https://clarity.fm/how-it-works) makes a paid call and expert-set
  rate immediately understandable, but foregrounding time and a marketplace
  profile would understate Jamie's outcome-oriented practice.
- [Intro](https://intro.co/experts) demonstrates low-friction paid access to a
  named expert, but its booking-centered pattern could falsely imply live
  calendar availability and confirmed fit.

The proposed pathway keeps the useful lesson—make the compensated next step
plain—while preserving direct fit review, public/private separation, and a
portfolio-led identity.

## Goals

- Give a prospective client one understandable, compensated way to begin.
- Frame the purchase around a useful outcome rather than harvested time.
- Make preparation, working time, and a short recap part of one visible unit.
- Keep the offer small enough to stand alone without an implied upsell.
- State that additional work requires separate scope and authorization.
- Reduce inquiry burden to one problem or decision, one hoped-for outcome, and
  timing.
- Preserve the distinction among inquiry, fit, agreement, authorization,
  delivery, and follow-on work.
- Keep the full-time role pathway and project evidence primary on the site.
- Prevent private opportunity provenance from entering public copy, code,
  metadata, analytics, or graph relations.
- Give future implementation a deterministic evaluator and explicit human
  gates.

## Non-goals

- Do not implement or publish the offer while this RFC remains proposed.
- Do not publish a client-specific contract, legal template, negotiation, or
  private relationship context.
- Do not create a services catalog, public rate card, calendar integration,
  payment system, CRM, database, analytics product, or new public route.
- Do not promise current availability, immediate booking, client acceptance,
  or a guaranteed business result.
- Do not make a free discovery call the required entrance to paid work.
- Do not turn a one-session inquiry into automatic renewal, a retainer, or an
  open-ended authorization.
- Do not imply that a public RFC is an offer capable of acceptance or a signed
  agreement.
- Do not identify a private repository, record, source, person, company, or
  conversation as the origin of this proposal.
- Do not displace the site's primary positioning for product operations,
  implementation, and technical project-management roles.

## Terminology

**Paid working session:** One fixed-fee unit of focused professional work that
includes agreed preparation, a live session or focused analysis, and a short
written recap within a stated total-effort limit.

**Intended outcome:** The useful decision, diagnosis, map, plan, framing, or
next-step clarity that both parties agree the session should pursue. It is a
direction for the work, not a guaranteed external result.

**Total effort:** Jamie's combined preparation, session or analysis, and recap
time. The proposed unit caps this combined effort at 60 minutes; it does not
promise a 60-minute meeting plus unlimited preparation and follow-up.

**Inquiry:** A reader's expression of interest. It is not acceptance, a booking,
an authorization to begin, or evidence of signature authority.

**Fit check:** A short, non-substantive exchange to confirm that the problem,
scope, timing, conflicts, authority, and working format are suitable. It must
not become uncompensated consulting.

**Separate authorization:** A new written scope and approval for any work after
the initial unit. Silence, momentum, recurring meetings, or the existence of a
prior agreement cannot supply this authorization.

## Detailed design

### Recommended public information architecture

The complete offer belongs on the existing Contact page at
`/contact#working-session`. That is where a reader already expects action and
commercial contact information. It should appear as a distinct card after the
general contact introduction, without replacing paths for roles, referrals, or
collaboration.

One secondary invitation may point to that anchor from the most relevant
existing surface after editorial review. The preferred order is:

1. Contact contains the complete offer and inquiry instructions.
2. One existing About, Technical Operations, or lower-page homepage section may
   contain a quiet text link after the evidence, not before it.
3. Case studies remain evidence surfaces rather than carrying repeated sales
   modules.

The offer must not occupy the homepage hero. It does not need a new Services,
Consulting, Book, or Rates route. This keeps navigation stable, reduces the
impression of a marketplace, and lets the portfolio remain the argument for why
the session is worth buying.

### Recommended public copy specimen

The following is a review specimen, not approved website copy:

> **Start with one paid working session**
>
> Bring one decision, operating problem, knowledge-system question, or
> implementation knot. Before work begins, we'll agree on a useful outcome. A
> $250 fixed fee covers up to 60 minutes of total effort, including agreed
> preparation, the working session or focused analysis, and a short written
> recap. The session can stand alone. If more work would help, we'll scope and
> authorize it separately.
>
> **Propose a working session**

The phrase “paid working session” is preferred to “office hours,” which may
sound free or academic, and to “consulting hour,” which foregrounds time rather
than the work's purpose. “Propose” is preferred to “book” because the pathway
still requires mutual fit and does not establish calendar availability.

### Offer unit

The proposed commercial unit is:

| Element | Public description | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fee | $250 fixed fee | Proposed until Jamie explicitly approves display |
| Effort | Up to 60 minutes total | Preparation, session or analysis, and recap share the limit |
| Focus | One agreed useful outcome | No guaranteed external result |
| Deliverable | Short written recap | Format and depth fit inside the same unit |
| Continuation | Optional | New work needs separate scope and written authorization |
| Completion | Session and recap delivered | No automatic renewal or implied retainer |

The offer may support decisions, operating systems, implementation planning,
source-backed knowledge work, documentation, or another problem that fits
Jamie's public practice. The public copy should give examples without promising
every request is suitable.

### Inquiry design

The CTA should use the existing contact path and ask for no more than:

1. What decision or problem should the session address?
2. What would be a useful outcome?
3. What timing matters?

Name, email, and organizational context may arrive through the existing contact
mechanism. A public form is not required by this RFC. The first response may
clarify fit, conflicts, working format, legal party, and signature authority,
but it must avoid performing the substantive diagnosis for free.

### Engagement states

```text
public description
  -> reader inquiry
  -> mutual fit check
  -> written agreement with verified parties and authority
  -> separately authorized working session
  -> session or analysis plus short recap
  -> standalone close or newly scoped follow-on work
```

No state may be inferred from a later one. An inquiry is not a booking. A
booking is not a signed agreement. A signed agreement is not authorization for
unspecified future work. A completed session is not permission to publish the
client's identity, problem, deliverable, or relationship.

### Full-time and consulting pathways

The site should continue to welcome roles, referrals, and collaboration. The
working-session path adds a client engagement option; it does not redefine all
hiring as consulting. The Contact page should keep those choices together in a
short introduction so a full-time hiring reader is not made to pass through a
consulting funnel.

### Contract boundary

The public surface describes the service pattern only. A fitting agreement may
be shared privately after fit is confirmed. That agreement should identify the
actual legal parties and signatories, define payment and cancellation terms,
address confidentiality and intellectual property, and record the exact
authorization for the first unit. Those terms can vary by client and require
separate commercial and legal review.

No contract body or contract-document link belongs on the public page under
this RFC. Keeping the agreement downstream avoids presenting a general website
description as client-specific legal advice or an offer automatically capable
of acceptance.

### Evaluation and hill-climb

The deterministic evaluator checks the proposal before any reader study or
human decision. It denies:

- private provenance fields anywhere in a candidate;
- automatic follow-on work;
- “book now” or current-availability claims; and
- publication or linking of a public contract.

It holds candidates that replace the fixed-fee outcome with hourly-labor
framing, remove a required component, offer a free starting unit, move the
offer into a homepage hero or new route, or weaken the standalone boundary.

The baseline Contact surface scores 1 of 4 on commercial next-step clarity: it
has a direct contact path, but no smallest paid unit, price, contents, or
completion boundary. This RFC's review specimen scores 4 of 4 against the same
criterion and passes all deterministic scenarios. That improvement applies to
the proposal, not the live site. The live surface remains unchanged and cannot
inherit the proposal's score until implementation is separately authorized and
reviewed in a rendered candidate.

## Security and privacy

The RFC is public, but the origin of a product idea does not need to be public.
The public candidate must contain no private correspondence, transcript body,
relationship source, client negotiation, private repository locator, private
graph topology, contact data, or inference about a specific person's intent.

The evaluator checks forbidden field names recursively, but that cannot detect
every indirect identification. Human review must assess contextual clues,
business sensitivity, present relationship state, contractual duties,
collective credit, and whether examples could identify a private opportunity by
combination.

The live site must not log more inquiry data than its approved contact path
already requires. This RFC does not authorize analytics, scheduling, payment,
or customer-record infrastructure. If later implementation introduces any of
those systems, a separate review must define retention, access, consent,
security, and deletion behavior.

The public offer must not imply that Jamie is available at a particular time,
that an organization is a client, that a conversation will be confidential,
or that a result is guaranteed. Those facts can exist only after the relevant
parties agree them.

## Publication workflow

This RFC, its synthetic scenarios, and its machine-readable contract may be
reviewed in the public repository. They contain no private source body or
client-specific terms.

Publication of a live pathway requires, in order:

1. Jamie accepts this RFC or records a revised direction.
2. Jamie approves the exact public copy and whether the $250 price is displayed.
3. Commercial and legal review confirms that the public description and private
   agreement do not conflict.
4. An implementation pull request shows the rendered Contact section and any
   one secondary link at desktop and mobile sizes.
5. Deterministic, public-language, accessibility, route, and privacy checks pass
   against the exact candidate.
6. Jamie separately approves deployment and production indexing for the exact
   commit.

A merged proposed RFC does not satisfy any later gate. Automation may evaluate,
draft, and hold; it may not accept the RFC, approve the price, execute an
agreement, declare fit, deploy, or index the page.

## Rollout plan

### Phase 0: proposal review

- Review this RFC, its copy specimen, proposed price, and evaluation evidence.
- Seek legal and commercial review where appropriate.
- Record acceptance, revision, or closure through the RFC process.
- Make no visitor-facing change.

### Phase 1: private content prototype

- Draft the exact Contact card and one optional secondary-link placement.
- Test the three-prompt inquiry for comprehension and response burden.
- Confirm the private agreement's party, scope, payment, cancellation,
  confidentiality, intellectual-property, and authorization mechanics.
- Do not expose private provenance in the implementation candidate.

### Phase 2: public implementation candidate

- Implement only the accepted Contact card and approved secondary link.
- Do not add a new route, booking calendar, payment flow, or analytics system.
- Render and inspect desktop and mobile states, focus order, heading hierarchy,
  link purpose, and mail-client behavior.
- Run the full affected evaluation suite against the exact commit.

### Phase 3: staging observation

- Deploy to noindex staging only after explicit authorization.
- Ask representative hiring and client readers to state the offer, price,
  included work, next action, and continuation boundary.
- Revise if readers interpret the offer as free, hourly labor, a guaranteed
  result, an immediate booking, or an automatic retainer.

### Phase 4: production decision

- Jamie reviews the exact copy, displayed price, rendered candidate, and legal
  alignment.
- Deploy and index only after separate approval.
- Observe inquiry quality manually before proposing new data collection.

Rollback removes the working-session section and secondary link while retaining
the general Contact page. It does not delete this RFC or erase already executed
agreements.

## Decision gates

- **Direction:** Jamie decides whether the site should expose a paid entry unit.
- **Naming:** Jamie approves “paid working session” or a replacement.
- **Price:** Jamie approves, changes, or withholds the proposed $250 display.
- **Scope:** Jamie approves the 60-minute total-effort interpretation and recap.
- **Placement:** Jamie approves Contact as primary and at most one secondary
  link elsewhere.
- **Commercial and legal review:** The public description and private agreement
  are checked for consistency, party identity, authority, payment, cancellation,
  confidentiality, intellectual property, and applicable obligations.
- **Privacy:** A human reviewer confirms that no private opportunity provenance
  or identifying combination entered the public candidate.
- **Implementation:** Acceptance of the RFC precedes visitor-facing code.
- **Release:** Jamie separately approves staging, deployment, and production
  indexing for the exact candidate commit.

## Drawbacks

- A displayed price may anchor buyers before Jamie understands the problem or
  may be too low for complex organizations and procurement burden.
- A one-hour total-effort cap may feel less intuitive than a one-hour live call
  and requires careful explanation.
- A small offer can attract poorly fitted requests or create administrative
  overhead disproportionate to the fee.
- A consulting pathway may slightly complicate the site's primary full-time
  hiring narrative.
- Avoiding a calendar and payment flow preserves truthfulness but adds manual
  coordination.
- Outcome framing can be misread as an outcome guarantee unless the copy stays
  precise.
- A public price can become stale and requires explicit review when Jamie's
  practice, costs, or target clients change.

These costs are acceptable only if the pathway remains small, secondary,
direct, and easy to remove.

## Alternatives

### Keep the current general Contact page

This preserves simplicity and avoids commercial anchoring. It is not preferred
because the reader still has to invent the smallest paid next step and may ask
for substantive unpaid exploration.

### Offer a free introductory call

This is familiar but can recreate the uncompensated-hours problem. A
non-substantive fit check may still occur, but free diagnosis should not be the
product.

### Publish an hourly consulting rate

This is simple to calculate but frames Jamie as time for sale rather than as a
practitioner producing useful operating clarity. It also leaves preparation,
recap, and completion ambiguous.

### Create a full Services or Rates page

This could support several offers later, but it is too large for one entry unit
and would compete with the current portfolio and role narrative.

### Add instant scheduling and payment

This minimizes clicks but implies availability and fit, adds data and vendor
dependencies, and advances beyond the current V1 scope.

### Publish the reusable agreement

This could make terms inspectable, but it risks confusing a specimen with
client-specific legal terms and exposes more machinery than a first inquiry
needs. The agreement is better shared privately after fit.

### Hide the price and say “consulting available”

This avoids anchoring but preserves much of the current ambiguity. It remains a
reasonable fallback if Jamie is not ready to maintain a public fixed fee.

## Unresolved questions

- Is $250 the right public fixed fee for the target client mix and current
  administrative burden?
- Should the 60-minute limit cover total effort, as proposed, or should the
  offer promise a separate live-session duration with additional included
  preparation and recap?
- Which single secondary surface, if any, best reaches qualified readers without
  competing with the portfolio's primary story?
- Should the existing email link use a prefilled subject and three prompts, or
  should the prompts remain visible prose only?
- What cancellation, rescheduling, payment timing, confidentiality,
  intellectual-property, and expense terms should the private agreement use?
- Which kinds of requests are clearly outside the working session's fit?
- What review cadence keeps the price and availability language current?
- What evidence after staging would justify production publication rather than
  returning to the general Contact path?
