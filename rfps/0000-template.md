# RFP 0000: Proposal title

## Summary

Give a concise description of the proposed change and the decision requested.

## Motivation

Describe the problem, who experiences it, why present behavior is insufficient,
and why the problem merits a durable architectural or governance decision.

## Evidence and present state

Separate verified facts about the current system from interpretation. Cite only
public-safe sources and repository artifacts.

## Goals

- State the outcomes this proposal intends to produce.

## Non-goals and anti-claims

- State what the proposal will not build, prove, automate, publish, or permit.

## Stakeholders and human gates

Name affected roles, required reviewers, consent or rights questions, decision
ownership, and the judgments that cannot be delegated to an agent or eval.

## Detailed design

Describe components, interfaces, data flow, ownership, state transitions,
failure behavior, and operator experience in enough detail to evaluate the
proposal.

## Public, private, and protected boundaries

Define sensitivity classes, allowed dependencies, publication pathways,
retention, protected absence, and how accidental disclosure is prevented.

## Security and privacy

Describe the threat model, secret handling, access controls, logs, generated
artifacts, backups, deletion, recovery, and incident response.

## Collective credit and rights

Explain how the design preserves contributor credit, uncertainty, consent,
quotation rights, and claims that must remain bounded.

## Implementation plan

Break work into reversible phases. Begin with synthetic fixtures or a low-risk
vertical slice when protected data is involved.

## Evaluation and release criteria

Define deterministic checks, adversarial or mutation tests, qualitative review,
human approvals, operational evidence, rollback criteria, and stage exit gates.

## Migration, operations, and recovery

Describe compatibility, migration, observability, backups, restore testing,
ownership, maintenance, and the cost of long-term operation.

## Teaching and documentation

List the contributor, operator, and user learning materials required before the
proposal is ready for release.

## Drawbacks

Describe complexity, maintenance, social cost, new risks, and ways the proposal
could make the system worse.

## Alternatives considered

Record credible alternatives, including doing nothing, and explain their
tradeoffs without caricature.

## Unresolved questions

- List decisions that remain genuinely open.
