## What was unclear

KC Town Hall began with a long-vacant historic building whose structure, preservation needs, public value, and possible uses were still emerging. The work required specialist construction teams and a way for neighborhood histories, needs, and imagination to accumulate into decisions without exposing private legal, financial, or participant records.

## What became usable

Phase One produced a cold shell; the neighborhood process produced a survey and contact workflow; and the proposal translated construction progress, public purpose, and stakeholder context into material that collaborators and municipal reviewers could use.

## What I did

Jamie co-founded and project-managed KC Town Hall and served as general contractor for the 2018-2019 Phase One restoration. He coordinated specialist teams from basement to scaffolding to roof deck while carrying measured drawings, survey cards, construction dependencies, and neighborhood conversations into the same operating picture.

During the 2018-2019 Phase One cold-shell restoration, Jamie served as co-founder, project manager, and general contractor, coordinating specialist teams across a $189,629 proposal budget. The scope included roofing and TPO membrane work, historic masonry, floor framing, water service, access, site safety, debris removal, and the logistics that let one trade's work land correctly with the next.

This was coordinated collective work. Specialist contractors, consultants, collaborators, and neighbors carried distinct knowledge and labor; Jamie's role was to keep scope, sequence, people, field conditions, and public purpose connected.

## Neighborhood-led planning

Jamie created a four-by-six neighborhood survey handbill and backing contact and data-collection workflow so daily site conversations could inform a participatory vision for the space. The proposal reproduces the survey card and states that an ongoing survey with neighborhood partners directly shaped the proposal.

The record does not establish a representative sample, a complete response count, or implementation of every preference. Respondent identities, contacts, and raw survey data remain protected.
