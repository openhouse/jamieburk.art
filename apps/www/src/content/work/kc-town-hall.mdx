## Context

KC Town Hall LLC involved adaptive reuse planning for a long-vacant historic building. The work sat at the intersection of built environment, historic preservation, public benefit, funding context, stakeholder documentation, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

Redevelopment work carries many layers: property history, funding processes, public value, stakeholder needs, preservation constraints, and private legal or financial context that should not become public portfolio content.

## What became usable

Jamie helped turn years of field work, planning, neighborhood listening, construction coordination, and public-benefit documentation into a successful municipal funding request and a responsible transition to a mission-aligned organization.

## A resident service, end to end

<Claim
  claimId="CLM-KCTH-TIRED-OF-TIRES-SERVICE-DESIGN"
  projection="case-study"
  surface="/work/kc-town-hall"
  pageId="kc-town-hall"
  occurrenceId="tired-of-tires-service-design"
/>

The promise was simple for residents: request pickup or set out household tires
on a published date. Behind it sat a complete operating loop:

1. A 4×6 handbill and web page explained the service, eligibility, cadence, and
   ways to request pickup or volunteer.
2. Field pickup moved tires from neighborhood locations into a vehicle and
   trailer.
3. City coordination made a no-fee recycling handoff possible for the launch
   service.
4. A protected tracker recorded monthly counts and a formula-based estimate of
   avoided disposal fees.
5. A paired 4×6 survey carried neighborhood priorities back into planning for
   the building and its services.

beyond one meeting.
