## Context

KC Town Hall LLC involved adaptive reuse planning for a long-vacant building at 36th Street and Indiana Avenue in Kansas City. The work sat at the intersection of built environment, historic preservation, public benefit, funding context, stakeholder documentation, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

Redevelopment work carries many layers: property history, funding processes, public value, stakeholder needs, preservation constraints, and private legal or financial context that should not become public portfolio content.

## What became usable

Jamie helped shape redevelopment planning and public-benefit documentation so collaborators could describe the project, understand its civic value, and support funding and stakeholder conversations. Public records support a $490,539 Central City Economic Development Tax Board funding recommendation for the KC Town Hall project.

## What I did

The work included planning support, documentation, public-benefit framing, stakeholder context, and materials that helped make a complex built-environment effort easier to discuss and continue.

## Outcome / evidence

The public-safe claim is municipal-review support and a $490,539 public-funding recommendation, not final completion of the redevelopment. The project record also supports the planned program: four commercial spaces and three homes in a long-vacant building.

## Transferable proof

This is long-horizon implementation proof: ambiguous goals, many stakeholders, public/private boundaries, funding context, and documentation that has to be useful beyond one meeting.
