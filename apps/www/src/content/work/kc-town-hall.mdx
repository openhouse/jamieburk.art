## Context

KC Town Hall LLC involved adaptive reuse planning for a long-vacant historic building. The work sat at the intersection of built environment, historic preservation, public benefit, funding context, stakeholder documentation, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

Redevelopment work carries many layers: property history, funding processes, public value, stakeholder needs, preservation constraints, and private legal or financial context that should not become public portfolio content.

## What became usable

Jamie helped shape redevelopment planning and public-benefit documentation so collaborators could describe the project, understand its civic value, and support funding and stakeholder conversations.

## What I did

Jamie supported planning, documentation, public-benefit framing, stakeholder context, and materials that helped make a complex built-environment effort easier to discuss and continue.

## How people used it

Collaborators could use the documentation to explain the project context, discuss public value, orient around funding needs, and keep a long-horizon effort from depending on private memory.

## Outcome / evidence

The public-safe evidence is planning support, public-benefit documentation, historic-preservation context, and stakeholder materials that remain bounded because private legal, property, and finance-sensitive details are not published.

## What this proves

This is long-horizon implementation proof: ambiguous goals, many stakeholders, public/private boundaries, funding context, and documentation that has to be useful beyond one meeting.
