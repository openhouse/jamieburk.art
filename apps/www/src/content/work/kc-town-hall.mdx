## Context

I co-led planning and public-benefit documentation for the proposed adaptive reuse of a long-vacant building, presented the project in Kansas City's public funding process, and established a shared account that supported resident input and recurring neighborhood-service communication. The redevelopment did not proceed under the award; the planning record and later mission-aligned transition remain the durable result.

## Operating challenge

The observed consequence is a legible public sequence: neighborhood input informed a proposal, the proposal moved through Board recommendation and Council action, the award did not become a completed redevelopment, and the record remained usable through withdrawal and transition. The durable proof is the planning, public-benefit documentation, and coordination across that changing reality.

## Transferable proof

This is long-horizon implementation proof with an honest transition: ambiguous goals, many stakeholders, public/private boundaries, a public funding process, and documentation that remained legible when the project stopped proceeding under the award and moved to mission-aligned stewardship.
