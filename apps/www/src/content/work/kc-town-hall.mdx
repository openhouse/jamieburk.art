## Context

KC Town Hall LLC involved adaptive reuse planning for a long-vacant historic building. The work sat at the intersection of built environment, historic preservation, public benefit, funding context, stakeholder documentation, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

Redevelopment work carries many layers: property history, funding processes, public value, stakeholder needs, preservation constraints, and private legal or financial context that should not become public portfolio content.

## What became usable

Jamie helped shape redevelopment planning, public-benefit documentation, stakeholder context, and municipal-review support so collaborators could describe the project, understand its civic value, and support funding and stakeholder conversations.

The public process produced a concrete result. Jamie presented the mixed-use proposal in June 2019. On July 16, all four members of Kansas City's Central City Economic Development Sales Tax Board voted to recommend $490,539 to the City Council.

On September 26, 2019, the City Council adopted substituted Resolution 190649, accepting the Board recommendation and authorizing negotiation of a funding agreement. At the same meeting, the Council passed substituted Ordinance 190642, which appropriated $490,539 for KC Town Hall.

## What I did

The work included planning support, documentation, public-benefit framing, stakeholder context, and materials that helped make a complex built-environment effort easier to discuss and continue.

Independent reporting documented that KC Town Hall still lacked a final City contract in December 2021, more than two years after the Board recommendation. Jamie later transitioned stewardship of KC Town Hall to a mission-aligned organization.

The original CCED-backed redevelopment did not proceed under that award. City records later described KC Town Hall as withdrawn and reappropriated the unused $490,539 in 2024. Jamie's stewardship transition and the City's administrative withdrawal are related but distinct: public sources reviewed for this page establish the surrounding timeline, not the successor organization or transfer terms.

## Transferable proof

This is long-horizon implementation proof: ambiguous goals, many stakeholders, public/private boundaries, public review, a favorable recommendation and appropriation, a prolonged contract process, and a stewardship transition that still required a clear and accurate handoff record.
