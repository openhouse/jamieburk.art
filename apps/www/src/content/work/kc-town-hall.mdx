## Context

KC Town Hall LLC involved adaptive reuse planning for a long-vacant historic building. The work sat at the intersection of built environment, historic preservation, public benefit, funding context, stakeholder documentation, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

Redevelopment work carries many layers: property history, funding processes, public value, stakeholder needs, preservation constraints, and private legal or financial context that should not become public portfolio content.

## What became usable

Jamie helped translate a long-vacant historic building into a public-benefit redevelopment plan with adaptive reuse, commercial space, housing, funding documentation, and stakeholder coordination.

The hard public proof point is the funding record: Kansas City public records show a Central City Economic Development Tax Board recommendation to fund a KC Town Hall project at 36th Street and Indiana Avenue in the amount of $490,539.

## What I did

The work included planning support, public-benefit framing, stakeholder context, and documentation that helped make a complex built-environment effort legible enough to discuss, evaluate, and continue through a public funding process.

## Transferable proof

This is long-horizon implementation proof: ambiguous goals, many stakeholders, public/private boundaries, funding context, and documentation that has to be useful beyond one meeting. It belongs in the portfolio as a secondary proof point because it shows Jamie working inside real-world public process, not only digital product surfaces.
