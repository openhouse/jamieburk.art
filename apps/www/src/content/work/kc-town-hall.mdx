## Context

KC Town Hall LLC involved adaptive reuse planning for a long-vacant historic building in Kansas City. The work sat at the intersection of built environment, historic preservation, public benefit, municipal funding, resident participation, stakeholder documentation, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

Redevelopment work carries many layers: property history, funding processes, public value, stakeholder needs, preservation constraints, resident participation, and private legal or financial context that should not become public portfolio content.

## What became usable

Jamie helped shape redevelopment planning, public-benefit documentation, stakeholder context, and municipal-review support so collaborators could describe the project, understand its civic value, and support funding and stakeholder conversations.

The project's shared public identity also became practical neighborhood infrastructure: a place to invite resident input, coordinate a recurring service workflow, circulate civic resources, and keep public dialogue connected to the work.

## What I did

The work included planning support, documentation, public-benefit framing, stakeholder context, and materials that helped make a complex built-environment effort easier to discuss and continue.

The public account is project evidence, not a personal authorship log. Its records extend beyond Jamie's stated period of direct stewardship, so this portfolio does not assign him every post or later program outcome.
