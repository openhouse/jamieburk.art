## Context

Jamie Burkart and Julia Fredenburg co-founded KC Town Hall around the adaptive reuse of a long-vacant historic building at 36th Street and Indiana Avenue. The work joined physical restoration, historic preservation, neighborhood participation, public benefit, financing, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

The building needed coordinated cold-shell work across roof, masonry, structural framing, water, access, safety, and site operations. At the same time, the project needed a credible way to learn what neighbors wanted, translate that input into a future program, and move the next phase through municipal review without exposing private resident, property, legal, or financial records.

## What became usable

A protected 2019 proposal identifies Jamie and Julia as founders and project managers and records the multi-trade Phase One cold-shell scope as completed that year. It also documents a neighborhood survey conducted with Oak Park Neighborhood Association and New Horizon Missionary Baptist Church and says residents' responses directly shaped the Phase Two proposal.

That proposal then moved through a public decision trail: the CCED Board unanimously recommended $490,539, and the Kansas City Council accepted the recommendation and appropriated the amount. When Jamie concluded his active role, he transitioned project stewardship to an organization he regarded as mission-aligned. Separately, the municipal funding project later withdrew before disbursement, and the City reclaimed the unused appropriation in 2024.

## What I did

Jamie served as co-founder and project manager through Phase One, working across specialist trades, construction sequencing, public-benefit documentation, and the neighborhood process. He later presented the Phase Two proposal for municipal review and, when he concluded his active role, transitioned project stewardship to a mission-aligned organization. The protected archive preserves more detailed first-person accounts of his day-to-day general-contractor, survey-system, and stewardship-transition responsibilities; those exact role details remain bounded rather than being presented as independently verified.

## Transferable proof

This is implementation proof at building scale: coordinate specialized work, keep scope and sequence legible, turn informal neighborhood encounters into a usable participation system, hand off stewardship responsibly, and maintain an honest decision trail across recommendation, appropriation, non-disbursement, and withdrawal.
