KC Town Hall LLC involved adaptive reuse planning for a long-vacant historic building. The work sat at the intersection of built environment, historic preservation, public benefit, funding context, stakeholder documentation, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

Redevelopment work carries many layers: property history, funding processes, public value, stakeholder needs, preservation constraints, and private legal or financial context that should not become public portfolio content.

## What became usable

Jamie helped shape redevelopment planning, public-benefit documentation, stakeholder context, and municipal-review support so collaborators could describe the project, understand its civic value, and support funding and stakeholder conversations.

## What I did

The work included proposal presentation, planning support, documentation, public-benefit framing, stakeholder context, and materials that helped make a complex built-environment effort easier to discuss and continue. The City's developer/presenter designation does not erase collaborator contributions or turn later government decisions into Jamie's individual accomplishment.

The distinctions matter. A recommendation, Council acceptance, appropriation,
negotiated agreement, payment, and completed project are different states. The
record supports the first three, not receipt or expenditure, and it preserves
the later withdrawal and reappropriation rather than leaving the 2019 decision
without its ending.

## Transferable proof

This is long-horizon implementation proof: ambiguous goals, many stakeholders, public/private boundaries, funding context, and documentation that has to be useful beyond one meeting.
