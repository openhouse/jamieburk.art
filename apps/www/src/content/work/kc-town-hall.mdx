## Context

KC Town Hall LLC involved adaptive reuse planning for a long-vacant approximately 6,500 sq. ft. historic building into four commercial spaces and three homes. The work sat at the intersection of built environment, historic preservation, public benefit, funding context, stakeholder documentation, and long-horizon project management.

## What was unclear

Redevelopment work carries many layers: property history, funding processes, public value, stakeholder needs, preservation constraints, and private legal or financial context that should not become public portfolio content.

## What became usable

Jamie co-led redevelopment planning and public-benefit documentation so collaborators could describe the project, understand its civic value, and support funding and stakeholder conversations, including a $490,539 public funding recommendation.

## What I did

The work included planning support, documentation, public-benefit framing, stakeholder context, and materials that helped make a complex built-environment effort easier to discuss and continue. This page includes the approved public-record scale and funding facts while omitting private financial, legal, banking, property, and stakeholder details.

## Transferable proof

This is long-horizon implementation proof: ambiguous goals, many stakeholders, public/private boundaries, funding context, and documentation that has to be useful beyond one meeting.
