## Context

KC Spaces Fund was a collaborator-led mutual-aid campaign for Kansas City arts
and cultural spaces during the first months of the COVID-19 pandemic. Jamie
supported the effort behind the scenes through campaign-site implementation,
theme and fundraising-display work, domain and platform continuity, and support
for a project identity available across web and social channels.

## What was unclear

A fast-moving public campaign needed to make several paths legible at once:
support the fund, apply for assistance, join the coalition, find contact
guidance, and follow public updates. Those paths needed to feel like one
campaign without collapsing organizer, grantmaking, technical, and fiscal
responsibilities into one role.

## What I did

Jamie built and maintained the Ghost-based campaign web stack, customized a
reusable theme, implemented donation, application, signup, contact, and
fundraising-display affordances, and supported the selection of an available
cross-channel project identity.

Public organizer credit remains with the campaign's named organizers. Jamie did
not run the fundraiser, make grant decisions, serve as fiscal sponsor, publish
the collaborator-led Facebook Page, or act as the sole author of the project
name.

## What became usable

The campaign gained a coherent public surface connecting its action routes and
support explanation. The shared identity carried across the domain, campaign
site, fundraiser, and collaborator-led channels, making a rapid collective
response easier to recognize and navigate.

## Transferable proof

This is implementation proof: a time-sensitive, many-stakeholder effort became
a finished public website with clear action paths, reusable front-end patterns,
deployment continuity, and explicit role separation. Applicant, donor,
subscriber, payment, credential, and private campaign materials remain offline.
