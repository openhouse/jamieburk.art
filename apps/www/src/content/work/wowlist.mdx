## Context

WOWList.org was a co-built community-calendar and social-discovery platform organized around followable keyword communities. It used Python / Django and Ember.js to support event publishing, classification, discovery, saves, follows, email digests, calendar sync, and local scene distribution.

## What was unclear

Organizers needed low-overhead ways to share events without depending on one editorial gatekeeper or a single static calendar. Communities were often organized by interest, place, venue, scene, and informal networks rather than clean institutional categories.

## What became usable

The platform turned keywords into followable community surfaces. That gave organizers a way to publish and distribute events around the language their communities already used, while giving participants ways to follow interests, save events, mark themselves as going, and receive updates.

## What I did

Jamie co-built and supported the product model, event workflows, public-facing web system, email digest behavior, calendar synchronization, and low-cost deployment patterns. The work connected community organizing patterns to a maintainable technical platform.

## How people used it

Organizers could publish events through language their communities already followed, while audiences could discover gatherings through keyword-based community surfaces.

## Outcome / evidence

The public-safe proof is a historical Python / Django and Ember.js platform, followable keyword communities, organizer-facing workflows, and local archive analysis showing 1,846 users, 16,142 posts/events, 23,864 lists/tags, 28,837 list follows, 20,927 saved/starred events, and 35+ active city scenes by July 2017.

## What this proves

This is product-operations and public-web proof: community vocabulary, user workflows, platform logic, social event data, and distribution needs had to become a system that real organizers could use.
