## Context

Jamie Burkart and Richard Caceres co-founded and co-built WOWList.org, a community-calendar platform organized around followable keyword communities. It used Django, PostgreSQL / PostGIS, and Ember.js to support event distribution and discovery across DIY arts, music, and local-interest scenes.

## What was unclear

Organizers needed lightweight ways to share events without depending on one editorial gatekeeper or a single static calendar. Communities were often organized by interest, place, venue, scene, and informal networks rather than clean institutional categories.

## What became usable

The platform turned keywords into followable community surfaces. That gave organizers a way to publish and distribute events around the language their communities already used.

The evidence forms one connected product story: calendars made with Sunday Dinner clarified the problem; followable keyword communities made that need buildable; and public support and organizer materials helped people join, publish, and maintain local calendars across 35 city ecosystems.

### How the product model worked

1. Organizers wrote event information as collaboratively editable text.
2. Keywords connected that event to communities, places, and interests.
3. People followed lists to receive relevant events, including by weekly email.
4. Support exchanges explained how to join and publish; an independent organizer tutorial and local calendar routes helped others continue.

This is a reconstruction of the documented model, not an original interface capture or a formal usability study. The cited product record supports the workflows; it does not identify a particular support comment as the cause of a particular release. Product and implementation credit remains shared with Richard Caceres and the communities using the calendars.

## Transferable proof

This is product-operations and public-web proof: community vocabulary, user workflows, platform logic, and distribution needs had to become a system that real organizers could use.
