## Context

WOWList.org was a co-built community-calendar and social-discovery platform organized around followable keyword communities. It used Python / Django and Ember.js to support event distribution and discovery across DIY arts, music, and local-interest scenes.

## What was unclear

Organizers needed simple, low-burden ways to share events without depending on one editorial gatekeeper or a single static calendar. Communities were often organized by interest, place, venue, scene, and informal networks rather than clean institutional categories.

## What became usable

The platform turned keywords into followable community surfaces. That gave organizers a way to publish and distribute events around the language their communities already used.

The local archive supports stronger public-safe aggregate claims than a generic "multi-city" description. By the July 2017 database snapshot, WOWList had 1,846 users, 16,142 posts/events, 23,864 lists/tags, 28,837 list follows, 20,927 saved/starred events, 15,915 Google Calendar events, and geocoded activity supporting a conservative claim of 35+ active city scenes.

## What I did

Jamie co-built and supported the product model, event workflows, and public-facing web system. The work connected community organizing patterns to a maintainable technical platform: add/edit event flows, followable interests and lists, saved/starred events, going status, weekly digest emails, Google Calendar synchronization, media upload/thumbnail infrastructure, and geolocation-aware records.

The archive is useful because it proves real product scale. It is also sensitive. Raw database dumps, account/security records, private media, integration identifiers, and organizer records are not public portfolio material.

## Transferable proof

This is product-operations and public-web proof: community vocabulary, user workflows, platform logic, city-scene adoption, and distribution needs had to become a system that real organizers could use.
