## Context

WOWList.org was a co-built community-calendar platform organized around followable keyword communities. It used Python / Django and Ember.js to support event distribution and discovery across DIY arts, music, and local-interest scenes.

Public-safe aggregate archive analysis supports a stronger scale claim than a normal prototype would: by July 2017, the system had 1,800+ users, 16,000+ events/posts, 23,000+ lists/tags, 28,000+ list follows, 20,000+ saved or starred events, 15,000+ calendar-synced events, and 35+ active city or region scenes. Those are aggregate proof points only; raw user data and organizer records stay offline.

## What was unclear

Organizers needed lightweight ways to share events without depending on one editorial gatekeeper or a single static calendar. Communities were often organized by interest, place, venue, scene, and informal networks rather than clean institutional categories.

## What became usable

The platform turned keywords into followable community surfaces. That gave organizers a way to publish and distribute events around the language their communities already used.

It also made distribution mechanics usable: saved events, email updates, calendar sync, followable lists/tags, and organizer-facing publishing flows.

## What I did

Jamie co-built and supported the product model, event workflows, and public-facing web system. The work connected community organizing patterns to a maintainable technical platform.

The defensible claim is co-built, not solo-founded. Credit and adoption language should stay careful, and the site should never publish raw database records, private emails, user media, password hashes, organizer contact lists, or user-level examples without approval.

## Transferable proof

This is product-operations and public-web proof: community vocabulary, user workflows, platform logic, and distribution needs had to become a system that real organizers could use.
