## Context

WOWList.org was a co-built community-calendar platform organized around followable keyword communities. It used Python / Django and Ember.js to support event distribution and discovery across DIY arts, music, and local-interest scenes.

Local archive analysis supports the scale without exposing the underlying records: by July 2017, the system held 1,800+ users, 16,000+ posts/events, 23,000+ lists/tags, 28,000+ list follows, 20,000+ saved/starred events, and geocoded activity across 35+ active city scenes.

## What was unclear

Organizers needed low-overhead ways to share events without depending on one editorial gatekeeper or a single static calendar. Communities were often organized by interest, place, venue, scene, and informal networks rather than clean institutional categories.

## What became usable

The platform turned keywords into followable community surfaces. That gave organizers a way to publish and distribute events around the language their communities already used, with workflows for event publishing, follows, saved/starred events, going status, email digests, Google Calendar sync, media handling, and geolocation-aware event data.

## What I did

Jamie co-built and supported the product model, event workflows, public-facing web system, and distribution mechanics. The work connected community organizing patterns to a maintainable technical platform that could carry local vocabulary, city scenes, recurring events, and organizer habits.

## Transferable proof

This is product-operations and public-web proof: community vocabulary, user workflows, platform logic, and distribution needs had to become a system that real organizers could use.

The public page uses rounded aggregate metrics only. Raw user records, organizer contact data, emails, password hashes, social-auth data, Google Calendar identifiers, and private community records stay offline.
