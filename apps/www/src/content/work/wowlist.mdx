## Context

WOWList.org was a co-built community-calendar and social-discovery platform organized around followable keyword communities. It used Python / Django and Ember.js to support event publishing, list/tag discovery, follows, saved events, email digests, calendar sync, and discovery across DIY arts, music, and local-interest scenes.

## What was unclear

Organizers needed practical, low-overhead ways to share events without depending on one editorial gatekeeper or a single static calendar. Communities were often organized by interest, place, venue, scene, and informal networks rather than clean institutional categories.

## What became usable

The platform turned keywords into followable community surfaces. That gave local calendar organizers, community calendar editors, and event-list stewards a way to publish and distribute events around the language their communities already used.

Local archive analysis supports a stronger public-safe scale claim than the current page previously made: 1,800+ users, 16,000+ events/posts, 23,000+ lists/tags, 28,000+ list follows, 20,000+ saved/starred events, and 35+ active city scenes by July 2017. Those numbers are useful as scale evidence, but the raw archive is not public material because it includes private user, calendar, media, and account records.

## What I did

Jamie co-built and supported the product model, event workflows, information architecture, and public-facing web system. The work connected community organizing patterns to a maintainable technical platform that local organizers could actually use without forcing every scene into one official editorial calendar.

## Transferable proof

This is product-operations and public-web proof: community vocabulary, user workflows, platform logic, email/calendar distribution, geolocation, media handling, and privacy boundaries had to become a system that real organizers could use.
