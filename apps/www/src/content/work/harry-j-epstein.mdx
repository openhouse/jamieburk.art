## Context

Harry J. Epstein Company is an 80+ year-old industrial tool business with deep customer trust, a distinctive public voice, and a large amount of institutional knowledge living across people, inventory practices, product history, vendor context, and customer conversations.

The digital work was not simply a website refresh. It was a long-running implementation problem: how to let a legacy business participate in modern e-commerce without flattening the trust, specificity, humor, and hard-won product judgment that made the company valuable in the first place.

## What was unclear

The company had a strong public identity, but many pieces of the operating system were implicit. Product knowledge lived in experienced people. Inventory context was not always shaped for web display. Marketing, analytics, customer communication, content, and operations had to support each other instead of behaving like separate surfaces.

The practical question was how to make online growth possible while preserving the business's real-world voice. A generic e-commerce implementation would have missed the point. The system needed to be maintainable for people doing the work every day, understandable to customers, and respectful of internal details that should never become public content.

## What became usable

Customers gained a clearer way to find products, understand the company's point of view, and place orders. Internally, product knowledge, marketing, analytics, customer communication, and recurring operational questions could move through more coherent digital workflows.

## What I did

I helped maintain and improve the web and e-commerce presence, supported analytics and marketing work, structured content patterns, translated between technical and nontechnical needs, and turned recurring operational questions into more repeatable workflows.

That work included practical implementation details: clarifying what needed to be changed, shaping content and product information for customers, coordinating small releases, watching what analytics could and could not say, and keeping the public experience aligned with the company's distinctive voice.

## Outcome / evidence

The long-running work contributed to online growth, including the careful public claim that I contributed to a period of 2x revenue growth. It also joined customer-facing improvements to recurring operational practice, so releases, content, analytics, and customer communication could reinforce one another instead of behaving as separate projects.

## Transferable proof

The transferable pattern joins technical project management, product operations, and knowledge systems: work with ambiguous requirements and legacy systems, connect customer experience to internal practice, and translate institutional memory into maintainable public-facing structure.
