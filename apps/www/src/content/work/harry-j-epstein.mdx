## Context

Harry J. Epstein Company is an 80+ year-old industrial tool business with deep customer trust, a distinctive public voice, and a large amount of institutional knowledge living across people, inventory practices, product history, vendor context, and customer conversations.

The work was a long-running implementation problem: how to participate in modern e-commerce without flattening the specificity, humor, and hard-won product judgment that made the business valuable. Product knowledge, inventory context, marketing, analytics, customer communication, and operations had to support one another in a system people could maintain.

## What I did

Jamie helped maintain and improve the web and e-commerce presence, supported analytics and marketing work, structured content patterns, translated between technical and nontechnical needs, and helped turn recurring operational questions into more repeatable workflows.

The practical rhythm was to clarify a change, shape product information for customers, coordinate the release, examine what analytics could and could not say, and keep the public experience aligned with the company's voice.

## What became usable

Customers gained a more coherent path to products and orders. Internal stakeholders gained reusable patterns for product communication, marketing, e-commerce operations, analytics, and customer follow-up. Across the period, the company's revenue doubled; Jamie's web and operations work contributed to that growth alongside the wider team and business.

## Transferable proof

This is private-sector implementation proof: ambiguous requirements, nontechnical stakeholders, legacy systems, incremental releases, and the translation of institutional memory into a maintainable customer-facing system.
