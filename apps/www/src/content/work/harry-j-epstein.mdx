## Context

Harry J. Epstein Company is an 80+ year-old industrial tool business with deep customer trust, a distinctive public voice, and a large amount of institutional knowledge living across people, inventory practices, product history, vendor context, and customer conversations.

Across a long-running implementation relationship, I helped connect e-commerce, analytics, content, marketing, and recurring operations so customers could find and understand products while staff retained the company's trust, specificity, humor, and hard-won product judgment.

## What was unclear

Product knowledge lived in experienced people, inventory context was not always shaped for web display, and customer communication, content, analytics, and operations needed to reinforce one another. The system also had to keep internal business context private.

## What became usable

Customers gained a clearer way to find products, understand the company's point of view, and place orders. Internally, product knowledge, marketing, analytics, customer communication, and recurring operational questions could move through more coherent digital workflows.

## What I did

I clarified changes, shaped product information for customers, coordinated small releases, structured reusable content patterns, translated between technical and nontechnical needs, and treated analytics as evidence with limits rather than an answer by itself.

## Outcome / evidence

The long-running work contributed to online growth, including the careful public claim that I contributed to a period of 2x revenue growth. The durable result was a more coherent connection between the customer-facing store and the recurring work needed to maintain it.

## Transferable proof

The transferable pattern joins technical project management, product operations, and knowledge systems: work with ambiguous requirements and legacy systems, connect customer experience to internal practice, and translate institutional memory into maintainable public-facing structure.
