## Context

NYC Artist Coalition / FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, cultural-space advocacy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure: a way to keep decisions, sources, next steps, and public/private boundaries connected.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was both fragmentation and overexposure. The system had to support action without turning private strategy, legal-review context, stakeholder lists, or unapproved material into public content.

## What became usable

Collaborators gained a shared campaign record connecting meetings to decisions, sources, next steps, and public explanations while keeping protected context out of public channels.

## What I did

I designed the documentation architecture, converted meetings into running minutes, action trackers, and documented next steps, linked public claims to source context, and separated publishable material from questions requiring legal, policy, or collaborator review. On the public web side, my role was more direct: co-founding NYC Artist Coalition, building campaign websites, and creating public-facing information surfaces.
