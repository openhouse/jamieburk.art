## Context

I co-founded NYC Artist Coalition and built campaign websites for cultural-space advocacy. In the later FairRentNYC and Commercial Rent Stabilization workstream, I turned recurring coalition work into running minutes, action ownership, source maps, a legislative provenance redline, and a public-data pilot proposal. These artifacts helped collaborators act without exposing protected legal, strategy, or stakeholder context.

## What was unclear

Public materials, private notes, legal questions, city and state strategy, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context were distributed across people and files. The operating challenge was to connect them without flattening disagreement or publishing protected material.

## What became usable

Collaborators gained a shared campaign record connecting meetings to decisions, sources, next steps, and public explanations while keeping protected context out of public channels.

## What I did

I designed the documentation architecture, converted meetings into running minutes, action trackers, and documented next steps, linked public claims to source context, and separated publishable material from questions requiring legal, policy, or collaborator review. On the public web side, my role was more direct: co-founding NYC Artist Coalition, building campaign websites, and creating public-facing information surfaces.

## Outcome / evidence

The operating record gave collaborators a way to reconnect policy language, public data, current decisions, owners, and next steps without reconstructing the project after every meeting. The public-data handout carried the same discipline into a concrete, reviewable proposal.

## Transferable proof

The transferable pattern treats documentation as an operating system for stakeholder-heavy work: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and next actions can be reviewed and reused, with human judgment and explicit boundaries around what remains protected.
