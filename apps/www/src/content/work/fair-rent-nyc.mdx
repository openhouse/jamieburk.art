## Context

NYC Artist Coalition / FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

Jamie is a co-founding member of NYC Artist Coalition. In this work, his lane is not sole authorship of a movement. It is cultural-space fieldwork, coalition operations support, documentation systems, source mapping, action tracking, and public-safe campaign memory.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What became usable

Jamie helped build and steward a shared campaign-memory system: running minutes, action trackers, decision records, source maps, public-data framing, stakeholder follow-up notes, cultural-space fieldwork protocols, and public-safe explanations that could help collaborators continue the work.

The strongest proof is the 34-page running-minutes record and related campaign-memory infrastructure. Those materials helped separate known public material from open questions and protected context. They made it easier to see what had happened, what needed review, what could be explained publicly, and what should remain private.

## What I did

Jamie contributed to the documentation architecture, helped structure meeting memory, translated complex policy and public-data questions into usable materials, supported action tracking, and created source-backed context for collaborators.

The practical role has three lanes: NYC Artist Coalition cultural-space fieldwork, FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization infrastructure and archive support, and small-business / SBU bridge work. The work required careful language discipline. This page uses collective-work language on purpose: contributed to, helped structure, built and stewarded shared documentation, supported coordination, and translated complex questions into usable materials. It does not claim that Jamie led the movement, owned the bill, provided official legal analysis, or single-handedly created the campaign.

## How people used it

Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published.

For NYC Artist Coalition, the same boundary applies to cultural-space fieldwork. The work can surface patterns around affordability, lease pressure, insurance, venue survival, and public support needs without exposing private venue issues, landlord details, contact lists, or unapproved quotes.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is a body of campaign-memory infrastructure: running minutes, decision records, action trackers, source maps, legal/policy question logs, public-data framing, Comptroller-data memos, cultural-space follow-up protocols, and stakeholder next steps.

The broader coalition context matters. NYC Artist Coalition has contributed to public campaigns around Cabaret Law repeal / Let NYC Dance, Office of Nightlife and Save NYC Spaces, M.A.R.C.H. transparency / Talks Not Raids, COVID relief coordination, storefront vacancy and commercial-rent advocacy, and FairRentNYC. This page treats those as collective accomplishments, not individual trophies.

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal review materials, raw strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private emails, and unapproved quotes are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
