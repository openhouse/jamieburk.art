## Context

FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

NYC Artist Coalition is part of this story because small cultural spaces, venues, artist spaces, and informal community infrastructure experience commercial displacement as an operating condition, not an abstract policy topic. Jamie's lane is best described as cultural-space fieldwork plus coalition infrastructure: listening across that context, protecting sensitive details, and helping the work become usable inside broader Commercial Rent Stabilization coordination.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, meeting follow-ups, cultural-space field context, and sensitive coalition material.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal-review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What became usable

Jamie helped build and steward a shared campaign-memory system: running minutes, action trackers, decision records, source maps, public-data framing, consent-aware field follow-up, and public-safe explanations that could help collaborators continue the work.

The strongest proof is the shared campaign documentation infrastructure. Those pages helped separate known public material from open questions and protected context. They made it easier to see what had happened, what needed review, what could be explained publicly, and what should remain private.

## What I did

Jamie contributed to the documentation architecture, helped structure meeting memory, translated complex policy and public-data questions into usable materials, supported action tracking, and created source-backed context for collaborators.

Within NYC Artist Coalition, the role is not best described as symbolic membership or generic advocacy. It is operating work: cultural-space field follow-up, consent-aware handling of sensitive venue context, public/private boundary-setting, and translation between artist-space conditions and the policy/data/documentation work of Commercial Rent Stabilization.

The work required careful language discipline. ## How people used it

Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published.

## Outcome / evidence

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal-review materials, raw strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private emails, and unapproved quotes are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
