## Context

FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

In NYC Artist Coalition, Jamie's role is best described as co-founder and civic-systems / coalition-operations contributor. The practical work is the operating layer: public explainers, source maps, running minutes, action trackers, call scripts, QR / signup pathways, follow-up systems, provenance review, public-data framing, and public-safe materials that help collaborators remember, explain, and act.

The work supported commercial rent stabilization, storefront stability, cultural-space advocacy, and public-data framing. The purpose was not only to remember meetings. It was to help collaborators see what had been decided, what was blocked, what needed review, who needed to know, and what could safely become public-facing explanation.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal-review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What became usable

Jamie helped build and steward a shared campaign-memory system: meeting synthesis, action trackers, decision records, source maps, legal / policy questions, public-data opportunities, media assets, stakeholder follow-up notes, city / state strategy lanes, and public-safe explanations that could help collaborators continue the work.

The strongest proof is the 30+ pages of shared campaign-memory infrastructure. Those pages helped separate known public material from open questions and protected context. They made it easier to see what had happened, what needed review, what could be explained publicly, and what should remain private.

NYC Artist Coalition's public record is broader than one campaign. The coalition has helped make cultural-space displacement, criminalization, enforcement pressure, lease instability, vacant storefronts, and small-space affordability legible as civic issues. Public-facing work has included Cabaret Law repeal advocacy, Nightlife Mayor / Office of Nightlife input, M.A.R.C.H. transparency and Talks Not Raids materials, FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization materials, call scripts, public explainers, maps, reference libraries, and organizing documents.

## Current working lanes

The current work is best understood as a set of public-safe lanes rather than one finished product.

- Commercial Rent Stabilization / FairRentNYC: city and state strategy, bill-lineage review, public-safe campaign memory, small-business and cultural-space alignment, and careful collective-work framing.
- Public-data foundation: policy-neutral framing for aggregated commercial vacancy, occupancy, lease-cost, storefront, and related public indicators that could support future oversight without exposing confidential tenant or property records.
- Coalition memory and coordination: running minutes, action trackers, source maps, public/private boundaries, signup and follow-up protocols, story-consent handling, and review habits that reduce surprises.
- Cultural-space fieldwork: keeping venues, artist spaces, event organizers, DIY rooms, insurance, lease pressure, and rent shocks visible inside small-business and commercial-rent conversations.
- Nightlife enforcement accountability: maintaining awareness of CURE / M.A.R.C.H. style enforcement transparency as part of cultural-space safety and preservation.

## How people used it

Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is a body of campaign-memory infrastructure: meeting synthesis, decision records, action trackers, source maps, legal/policy question logs, public-data framing, media and stakeholder next steps, and reviewable open questions.

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal-review materials, raw strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private emails, and unapproved quotes are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
