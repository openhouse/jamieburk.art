## Context

FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

## NYC Artist Coalition lane

Jamie is a co-founding member of NYC Artist Coalition. In this work, his public-safe lane connects cultural-space field listening, Commercial Rent Stabilization coordination, public-data framing, and consent-aware follow-up.

NYC Artist Coalition's work is collective. The public arc includes cultural-space anti-criminalization, nightlife support, enforcement reform, and the longer fight to keep artist, venue, and neighborhood-serving commercial spaces from being displaced by rent shocks.

For the current Commercial Rent Stabilization work, Jamie helps maintain the connective tissue: shared memory, action tracking, source context, city/state alignment, cultural-space follow-up, and careful public/private boundaries. The goal is not to turn private venue experiences into public proof. The goal is to help collaborators act from what is known, protect what is sensitive, and keep the policy work legible.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, cultural-space field follow-up, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal-review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What became usable

Jamie helped build and steward a shared campaign-memory system: running minutes, action trackers, decision records, source maps, public-data framing, cultural-space field follow-up, and public-safe explanations that could help collaborators continue the work.

The strongest proof is the 34-page shared campaign-memory system. Those pages helped separate known public material from open questions and protected context. They made it easier to see what had happened, what needed review, what could be explained publicly, and what should remain private.

## What I did

Jamie contributed to the documentation architecture, helped structure meeting memory, translated complex policy and public-data questions into usable materials, supported action tracking, and created source-backed context for collaborators.

That included a policy-neutral public-data framing: how privacy-preserving, geography-aggregated vacancy and lease-cost indicators could help future public understanding without exposing raw confidential filings, tenant identities, or unit-level lease records.

The work required careful language discipline. This page uses collective-work language on purpose: contributed to, helped structure, built and stewarded shared documentation, supported coordination, and translated complex questions into usable materials. It does not claim that Jamie led the movement, owned the bill, provided official legal analysis, or single-handedly created the campaign.

## How people used it

Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is a body of campaign-memory infrastructure: running minutes, decision records, action trackers, source maps, legal/policy question logs, public-data framing, and stakeholder next steps.

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal-review materials, strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private emails, unapproved quotes, signups, and venue-specific lease or landlord details are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
