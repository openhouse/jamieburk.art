## Context

I co-founded NYC Artist Coalition and built campaign websites for cultural-space advocacy. In the later FairRentNYC and Commercial Rent Stabilization workstream, I turned recurring coalition work into running minutes, action ownership, source maps, a legislative provenance redline, and a public-data pilot proposal. These artifacts helped collaborators act without exposing protected legal, strategy, or stakeholder context.

## What was unclear

Public materials, private notes, legal questions, city and state strategy, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context were distributed across people and files. The operating challenge was to connect them without flattening disagreement or publishing protected material.

## What became usable

Collaborators gained a shared campaign record connecting meetings to decisions, sources, next steps, and public explanations while keeping protected context out of public channels. The strongest consequence I can support is operational: people could return to a maintained record instead of reconstructing the work after every meeting.

That role is not a claim to authorship of every event or policy outcome. Facebook responses are not attendance, and an event-level interface does not establish endorsement, a continuing partnership, or policy causality.

## What I did

I designed the documentation architecture, converted meetings into running minutes and documented next steps, linked public claims to source context, and separated publishable material from questions requiring legal, policy, or collaborator review. On the public web side, my role was more direct: co-founding NYC Artist Coalition, building campaign websites, and creating public-facing information surfaces.

The operating record kept policy language, public data, decisions, owners, and next steps connected. The public-data handout carried the same discipline into a concrete proposal that other people could review, question, and revise.

## Transferable proof

The transferable pattern treats documentation as an operating system for stakeholder-heavy work: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and next actions can be reviewed and reused, with human judgment and explicit boundaries around what remains protected.
