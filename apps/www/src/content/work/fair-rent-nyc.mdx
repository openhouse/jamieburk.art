## Context

NYC Artist Coalition / FairRentNYC work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

## Jamie's role

Jamie co-founded NYC Artist Coalition and works through NYC Artist Coalition / FairRentNYC as a civic systems, coalition operations, and policy communications lead.

In plain terms: Jamie turns cultural-space and storefront-stability advocacy into usable operating structure. That means shared records, public-safe explanations, source maps, call scripts, QR pathways, action trackers, meeting memory, decision context, policy/data question logs, and follow-up materials.

This is not a claim of sole movement leadership or legal authority. The public-safe role is narrower and stronger: Jamie helps collective work become legible, organized, careful, and actionable.

## What NYC Artist Coalition has helped make possible

NYC Artist Coalition's public history sits across cultural-space advocacy, anti-displacement work, enforcement transparency, nightlife support, and commercial rent stabilization. Public campaign surfaces include Let NYC Dance, Save NYC Spaces, Talks Not Raids, and Fair Rent NYC.

The careful claim is that NAC contributed to public advocacy ecosystems around Cabaret Law repeal, Office of Nightlife expectations, M.A.R.C.H. transparency, cultural-space support, and storefront-stability policy. Those are collective accomplishments. The portfolio should not convert them into solo authorship.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, cultural-space field context, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal-review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What became usable

Jamie built and stewarded 30+ pages of shared campaign-memory and coordination infrastructure: running minutes, action trackers, decision records, source maps, public-data framing, stakeholder follow-up notes, legal/policy question logs, and public-safe explanations that could help collaborators continue the work.

The strongest proof is the body of shared campaign-memory infrastructure and the legislative source-map work. Those pages helped separate known public material from open questions and protected context. They made it easier to see what had happened, what needed review, what could be explained publicly, and what should remain private.

The active work is Commercial Rent Stabilization and the operating structure around it. That includes city and state strategy lanes, source-backed legislative provenance, cultural-space follow-up, small-business partner alignment, and privacy-preserving public-data framing around commercial vacancy, occupancy, lease-cost conditions, Local Law 157 storefront data, and RPIE-derived aggregate indicators.

The public version is deliberately bounded: campaign memory, source mapping, public-data framing, coordination support, and careful civic communication. Private coalition notes, legal-review materials, raw transcripts, stakeholder lists, and internal strategy stay offline.

## How people used it

Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is 30+ pages of campaign-memory and coordination infrastructure: running minutes, decision records, action trackers, source maps, legal/policy question logs, public-data framing, provenance materials, and stakeholder next steps.

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal-review materials, raw strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private emails, and unapproved quotes are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
