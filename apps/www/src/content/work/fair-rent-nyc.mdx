## Context

NYC Artist Coalition, FairRentNYC, and Commercial Rent Stabilization work brings together cultural-space advocacy, storefront stability, public policy, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data. With many collaborators and mixed public/private sources, the operating challenge was to preserve decisions and momentum without exposing strategy, legal-review context, private stakeholder information, or unapproved quotes.

Here, documentation is infrastructure: the shared place where meetings, sources, open questions, decisions, and next steps remain usable over time.

## What I did

Jamie co-founded NYC Artist Coalition and built public campaign websites for the coalition, FairRentNYC, Talks Not Raids, and Let NYC Dance. He also helped structure meeting memory, action tracking, decision records, source maps, public-data framing, stakeholder follow-up, and policy-communications materials across the coalition's work.

The documentation architecture separated supported public material from open questions and protected context. Collaborators could orient after meetings, recover source context, track next steps, and prepare public explanations without treating private coordination as publishable content.

## What became usable

The work produced 30+ pages of shared campaign-memory infrastructure alongside maintained public campaign surfaces. Together, these systems supported coalition continuity, public communication, and reviewable source trails across complex civic work.
