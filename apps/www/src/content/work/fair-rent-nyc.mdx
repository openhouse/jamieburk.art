## Context

NYC Artist Coalition / FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, cultural-space advocacy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal-review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What became usable

Jamie created and stewarded a shared campaign-memory system: running minutes, action trackers, decision records, source maps, public-data framing, stakeholder follow-up notes, and public-safe explanations that could help collaborators continue the work.

The strongest proof is the system's architecture. It organized decisions, owners, action items, open questions, aligned city/state strategy lanes, and four consent levels for stories and participant context: Public, Anonymized, Confidential/internal only, and Needs follow-up. This made it easier to see what had happened, what needed review, what could be explained publicly, and what should remain private.

Jamie also authored a 90-day operating plan around six shared public goods: a clear front door, a recurring room, a shared public line, a stewarded story bank, an implementation-readiness packet, and a durable source-of-truth spine. The plan is evidence of operating design, not a claim that every proposed action was completed or adopted by every collaborator.

Jamie also co-founded NYC Artist Coalition and built public campaign web surfaces for cultural-space advocacy, including the coalition site, FairRentNYC, Talks Not Raids, and Let NYC Dance. That is a direct co-founder and web-infrastructure claim. It does not turn collective campaign accomplishments into a solo claim.

## What I did

Jamie contributed to the documentation architecture, structured meeting memory, translated complex policy and public-data questions into usable materials, supported action tracking, and created source-backed context for collaborators.

Two authored artifacts make that work concrete. A policy-neutral data memo proposed machine-readable aggregate rent and vacancy indicators for a future Commercial Rent Guidelines Board, with privacy and licensing safeguards and no request for proprietary microdata. An unofficial legislative provenance redline made visible how bill language moved across city, campaign, and state source layers. The memo was a proposal, not adopted government policy; the redline was a discussion tool, not legal advice or official legislative history.

## How people used it

Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is a body of campaign-memory infrastructure: a 90-day operating architecture, running minutes, decision records, action trackers, consent levels, source maps, an open-data proposal, an unofficial legislative provenance redline, legal/policy question logs, and stakeholder next steps.

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal-review materials, raw strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private emails, and unapproved quotes are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
