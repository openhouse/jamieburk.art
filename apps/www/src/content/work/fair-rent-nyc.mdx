## Context

FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

NYC Artist Coalition is part of the public history behind this work. The coalition's public record includes Let NYC Dance / Cabaret Law repeal work, Save NYC Spaces / Office of Nightlife advocacy, Talks Not Raids / M.A.R.C.H. transparency work, Fair Rent / commercial lease-protection advocacy, and COVID-era relief coordination. Jamie is a co-founding member of NYC Artist Coalition; this page focuses on his current contribution to the coalition's Fair Rent / Commercial Rent Stabilization work, not on sole ownership of the coalition or the movement.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal-review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What became usable

Jamie helped build and steward 30+ pages of shared campaign-memory and coordination infrastructure: running minutes, action trackers, decision records, source maps, legal/policy question logs, city and state strategy lanes, public-data framing, stakeholder follow-up notes, consent-aware story handling, and public-safe explanations that could help collaborators continue the work.

The strongest proof is the shared campaign-memory infrastructure. Those pages helped separate known public material from open questions and protected context. They made it easier to see what had happened, what needed review, what could be explained publicly, and what should remain private. They also helped keep Fair Rent NYC campaign history, NYC Artist Coalition cultural-space organizing, and Small Business United / Action Lab capacity visible without treating any one lane as the whole movement.

## How people used it

Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published. Current work includes city/state alignment, cultural-space affordability fieldwork, rent/lease/insurance follow-up, signup and data stewardship, and public-safe campaign continuity.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is a body of campaign-memory infrastructure: running minutes, decision records, action trackers, source maps, legal/policy question logs, city/state strategy lanes, public-data framing, stakeholder next steps, and consent levels that distinguish public, anonymized, confidential/internal, and needs-follow-up stories.

The record also supports a more specific provenance claim: Jamie created a legislative source map / redline to make Commercial Rent Stabilization bill language traceable across New York City Council Intro 93, Fair Rent NYC recommendations, Small Business Jobs Survival Act-derived provisions, and Albany Senate Bill S8319 revisions. That is source-memory and review support, not legal advice.

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal-review materials, raw strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private correspondence, and unapproved quotes are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
