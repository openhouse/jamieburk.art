## Context

FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

In that kind of work, public websites and documentation are not clerical aftercare. They are infrastructure. Without usable public pages, people outside the room cannot understand the ask or find the next step. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public websites, public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal-review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What became usable

Jamie built public campaign websites for NYC Artist Coalition advocacy and helped structure a shared campaign-memory system: running minutes, action trackers, decision records, source maps, public-data framing, stakeholder follow-up notes, and public-safe explanations that could help collaborators continue the work.

The strongest proof is the combination of public web infrastructure and 30+ pages of shared campaign-memory infrastructure. The public sites helped explain campaigns, calls to action, reference materials, and issue context. The shared memory pages helped separate known public material from open questions and protected context.

## How people used it

People outside the room could use the public sites to understand the issue, find calls to action, read reference material, and orient to campaign context. Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is a body of public web and campaign-memory infrastructure: campaign websites, public explainers, calls to action, reference materials, running minutes, decision records, action trackers, source maps, legal/policy question logs, public-data framing, and stakeholder next steps.

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal-review materials, raw strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private emails, and unapproved quotes are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
