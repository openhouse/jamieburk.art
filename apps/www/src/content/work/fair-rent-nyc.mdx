## Context

NYC Artist Coalition / FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization work sits inside a civic and coalition environment where public policy, cultural-space advocacy, storefront stability, arts communities, city and state strategy, legal questions, lived experience, and public data all meet.

In that kind of work, documentation is not clerical aftercare. It is infrastructure. Without a shared memory system, meetings fragment, source trails become hard to trust, action items disappear, private context leaks into places it should not, and collaborators lose the thread of what has already been clarified.

## What became usable

Jamie helped build and steward 30+ pages of shared campaign records. Collaborators could use them to find decisions, sources, open questions, and next steps; orient after meetings; prepare public explanations; and continue work without publishing private strategy or legal-review context.

Jamie also co-founded NYC Artist Coalition and built public campaign sites for cultural-space advocacy, including the coalition site, FairRentNYC, Talks Not Raids, and Let NYC Dance. That is a direct co-founder and web-infrastructure claim. Campaign accomplishments remain collective.

## What was unclear

The work involved many stakeholders and different kinds of knowledge: public materials, private notes, legal and policy questions, public-data framing, city/state strategy lanes, meeting follow-ups, and sensitive coalition context.

The risk was not only disorganization. The risk was overexposure. A civic documentation system has to help collaborators remember and act while still protecting private strategy, legal-review context, unapproved quotes, private stakeholder lists, and materials that should not become public.

## What I did

Jamie contributed to the documentation architecture, helped structure meeting memory, translated complex policy and public-data questions into usable materials, supported action tracking, and created source-backed context for collaborators.

The work required careful language discipline. This page uses collective-work language on purpose: contributed to, helped structure, built and stewarded shared documentation, supported coordination, and translated complex questions into usable materials. It does not claim that Jamie led the movement, owned the bill, provided official legal analysis, or single-handedly created the campaign.

On the public web side, Jamie's role was more direct: co-founding NYC Artist Coalition, building campaign websites, and creating public-facing information surfaces. The campaign work around those sites remains collective.

The broader NYC Artist Coalition work includes civic systems, coalition operations, and policy-communications materials for campaigns around Cabaret Law repeal, Office of Nightlife creation, nightlife enforcement reporting, Commercial Rent Stabilization, and storefront stability. Jamie's public speaking and testimony are documented individual contributions. The policy work and outcomes remain collective.

## How people used it

Collaborators could use the shared memory to orient after meetings, find source context, track next steps, identify unresolved legal or policy questions, and prepare more public-safe explanations of complex civic issues.

The system also helped manage the boundary between public communication and private coordination. That boundary is part of the work. A good civic documentation system should make it easier to share what can be shared and easier to protect what should not be published.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is a body of campaign-memory infrastructure: running minutes, decision records, action trackers, source maps, legal/policy question logs, public-data framing, and stakeholder next steps.

Because this is active and sensitive civic work, the evidence is described as a public-safe summary. Private notes, legal-review materials, raw strategy documents, private stakeholder lists, private emails, and unapproved quotes are intentionally omitted.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows how to create operating structure when goals are important, stakeholders are many, and the work cannot rely on memory alone.

It matters for product operations because it treats documentation as an operating system: a place where feedback, decisions, sources, risks, and adoption context can be gathered and reused.

It matters for knowledge systems and AI-readiness because it models a source-backed memory practice with human review, boundary-setting, and explicit Known / Open / Protected layers.
