## Context

Sunday Dinner and 196 Artists Residency were related but distinct forms of cultural infrastructure. Sunday Dinner depended on recurring invitations, hosting, and continuity; the residency depended on proposal review, onboarding, access, artist support, and bounded handoffs. Both depended on trust as much as logistics.

## What was unclear

The challenge was how to keep participation open, generous, and repeatable without exposing private community records or turning a living social practice into an archive that violated consent.

## What I did

For Sunday Dinner, the work included invitations, shared hosting rhythms, introductions, handoffs, space-making, and documentation practices. Jamie co-hosted this practice with Julia Fredenberg; the wider community made the gatherings meaningful.

For 196 Artists Residency, Jamie built proposal-review, scheduling, pre-arrival planning, space-configuration, independent-access, hospitality, documentation, and continuity practices that made a residency period usable without appropriating resident artists' work.

Hosting was part of the system, not support around it. Invitations, repetition, introductions, and handoffs made participation legible enough for people to enter and for the gathering to continue. The records support recurring structure, not individual attendance or participant outcomes.
