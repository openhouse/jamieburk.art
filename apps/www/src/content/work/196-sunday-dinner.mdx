## Context

196 Artists Residency / Sunday Dinner was cultural infrastructure: recurring gatherings, artist support, hosting, onboarding, facilitation, and continuity work that depended on trust as much as logistics.

## What was unclear

The challenge was how to keep participation open, generous, and repeatable without exposing private community records or turning a living social practice into an archive that violated consent.

## What became usable

Jamie created repeatable hosting, onboarding, facilitation, documentation, and continuity systems across 300+ gatherings and 20+ resident artists.

## What I did

Jamie created invitation rhythms, hosting practices, artist-support routines, handoffs, space-making practices, documentation habits, and quiet logistics that let recurring community work keep happening.

## How people used it

Participants and resident artists used the recurring structure to enter gatherings, understand expectations, receive support, and keep community work moving without relying on one-off explanations.

## Outcome / evidence

The public-safe proof is the duration and repeatability of the practice: long-running gatherings, resident-artist support, onboarding routines, facilitation, and continuity systems.
