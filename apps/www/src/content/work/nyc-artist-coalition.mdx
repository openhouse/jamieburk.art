## Why this work existed

Small cultural spaces carried essential knowledge about how New York City's
safety, licensing, enforcement, affordability, and cultural systems worked in
practice. That knowledge was distributed across artists, organizers, venues,
neighborhoods, and nights. It needed places to gather, public language, and
routes into civic decision-making.

Jamie co-founded NYC Artist Coalition and helped operate that interface. He
connected recurring participation, public identity, practical safety work,
campaign websites, testimony, and coalition memory so artists' lived
experience could become organized public action without claiming government
authority or individual ownership of collective outcomes.

## What became usable

- a recurring participation system rooted in cultural spaces;
- a durable public identity collaborators could use across years;
- practical routes to safety and compliance knowledge;
- public campaign sites, testimony, recommendations, and Council outreach;
- a continuing interface through which dispersed cultural-space experience
  could enter civic processes.

For technical project management and product operations, the proof is not that
Jamie held public authority. It is that he helped make a difficult,
stakeholder-heavy field easier to hear, organize, navigate, and continue.
