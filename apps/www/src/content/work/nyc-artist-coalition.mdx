## Context

NYC Artist Coalition formed around artist-led and community cultural spaces facing overlapping pressures: enforcement, safety, nightlife regulation, affordability, displacement, public misunderstanding, and the difficulty of asking for support without exposing vulnerable spaces.

The public work needed more than statements. It needed an identity, public campaign surfaces, issue explanations, calls to action, press context, supporter pathways, and enough structure for people outside the room to understand what was being asked.

## What was unclear

The campaigns involved many different publics: artists, venues, cultural workers, residents, elected officials, journalists, city agencies, small-business advocates, and partner organizations.

The hard part was making the work visible without making private coalition context too visible. A public campaign site had to explain the ask, cite public context, invite action, and preserve trust.

## What became usable

Jamie designed NYC Artist Coalition's visual identity and built public campaign websites for NYCArtC, Let NYC Dance, Save NYC Spaces, Talks Not Raids, and FairRentNYC.

Those sites made the work easier to navigate: what the issue was, what action supporters could take, which campaign or coalition was carrying the ask, what public sources supported the claim, and where the work stood.

## What I did

Jamie co-founded NYC Artist Coalition, designed its logo, and built public-facing campaign websites that translated cultural-space advocacy into usable public calls to action.

The claim here is intentionally specific. Jamie is credited for identity and campaign web infrastructure. Policy outcomes are described as collective campaign and coalition work, not as individual wins.

## How people used it

Supporters could read the public ask, contact elected officials, understand the coalition frame, find press or public references, and share campaign pages without needing access to private organizing context.

The same pattern carried across campaigns: make the issue understandable, make the next action clear, and protect the private material that should not become public.

## Outcome / evidence

The public evidence is the campaign infrastructure itself: NYC Artist Coalition, FairRentNYC, Talks Not Raids, Let NYC Dance, and Save NYC Spaces remain public web artifacts of the work.

## Transferable proof

This case matters for technical project management because it shows public-facing delivery under civic constraints: multiple stakeholders, live campaigns, shifting public asks, sensitive source boundaries, and the need for simple interfaces that people can act on quickly.

It matters for product operations because the output was not only a website. It was a repeatable campaign pattern: issue framing, source context, action path, progress signal, coalition credit, and public-safe limits.
