After the complete CouncilStat dataset was released, Jamie independently developed CallNYC.org as an archived resident-facing civic-data prototype. Politico New York covered the project in March 2016. <Cite page="callnyc" claimId="callnyc-independent-follow-on-project" />

CallNYC was an independent follow-on project, not an official Council product or current public-service directory.

## What was unclear

Constituent-services data carried civic signal, but residents needed a translation layer. What issue is this? Which office or district context might matter? What next step is reasonable? What should be treated as general guidance rather than official service direction?

The project also needed a strong disclaimer. CallNYC was not an official City Council service, not a current civic service, not legal guidance, and not a replacement for emergency or agency channels. It was a civic-tech prototype exploring how public data could be made more navigable.

## What became usable

The prototype organized open data into issue pathways, district context, and resident-facing guidance. It treated data translation as a product and information-architecture problem: a resident should not have to understand the whole dataset before seeing a possible next step.

The public-safe framing today is historical. The project shows civic-data translation, open-data product thinking, public interface design, and the ability to carry an event prompt into an independently developed working prototype without implying institutional sponsorship.

## What I did

Jamie independently built the prototype after the CouncilStat data release, shaped issue pathways, translated data into public-facing guidance patterns, and connected civic-data structure to a resident-readable interface.

The work required choosing what not to claim. A prototype can demonstrate a pattern without pretending to be an official service. The page therefore keeps the archived status visible and separates the Council's event from Jamie's later project.

## How people used it

The interface let a visitor explore how an issue might connect to district context, constituent-service categories, or possible civic next steps. It also gave civic-tech observers a concrete surface for discussing what more navigable public guidance could look like.

For product and operations teams, the transferable pattern is the same one visible elsewhere in Jamie's work: take a scattered or expert-facing source, map what a user needs to do, and create a pathway that is easier to enter.

## Outcome / evidence

CallNYC was a small but complete civic prototype: public data, information architecture, issue pathways, resident-facing guidance, an archived public interface, a public code repository, and independent press coverage.
