## Context

CallNYC.org was an archived civic-data prototype built after a New York City Council civic-data hackathon. The project explored how constituent-services open data could become clearer resident-facing guidance.

The public problem was familiar: data existed, but the path from a resident's issue to a useful next step was not always easy to see. A spreadsheet or dataset can be technically public and still be hard for a person to use.

## What was unclear

Constituent-services data carried civic signal, but residents needed a translation layer. What issue is this? Which office or district context might matter? What next step is reasonable? What should be treated as general guidance rather than official service direction?

The project also needed a strong disclaimer. CallNYC was not an official City Council service, not a current civic service, not legal guidance, and not a replacement for emergency or agency channels. It was a civic-tech prototype exploring how public data could be made more navigable.

## What became usable

The prototype organized open data into issue pathways, district context, and resident-facing guidance. It treated data translation as a product and information-architecture problem: the resident should not have to understand the whole dataset before seeing a next step.

The public-safe framing today is historical. The project shows civic-data translation, open-data product thinking, public interface design, and the ability to move from a hackathon context into a working prototype.

## What I did

Jamie built the prototype, shaped the issue pathways, translated data into public-facing guidance patterns, and connected civic-data structure to a resident-readable interface.

The work required choosing what not to claim. A prototype can demonstrate a pattern without pretending to be an official service. The page therefore keeps the archived status visible and avoids current-service language.

## How people used it

Residents and civic-tech observers could see how an issue might connect to district context, constituent-service categories, or possible civic next steps. The project also gave collaborators a concrete surface for discussing what useful public guidance could look like.

For product and operations teams, the transferable pattern is the same one visible elsewhere in Jamie's work: take a scattered or expert-facing source, map what a user needs to do, and create a pathway that is easier to enter.

## Outcome / evidence

The project was a historical civic-tech demo built from CouncilStat / constituent-services open data. It included public issue pages, resident-facing guidance, and civic-data interpretation after a New York City Council civic-data hackathon.

Politico New York covered CallNYC in 2016 as a website for exploring City Council constituent-services data and described Jamie's rationale for making civic casework data easier for residents to navigate.
