## What was unclear

Publishing administrative data did not automatically make it usable to residents. The records still needed a translation layer: What issue is this? Which office or district context might matter? What next step could a resident take? What could the totals responsibly support - and what could they not support?

The project also needed a clear boundary. CallNYC was not an official Council service, legal guidance, emergency guidance, or a substitute for current agency and district-office channels.

## What I did

Jamie recognized that the released data still needed a resident-facing product layer and built the independent prototype. He organized records into issue-oriented pathways, filtered records without borough data as one method of excluding apparent spam or out-of-city entries, optimized pages for public discovery and sharing, and kept the project's limits visible.<Cite pageId="callnyc" occurrenceId="work-method" />

## What became usable

CallNYC turned an expert-facing administrative dataset into a working public prototype with issue pages, district context, contact pathways, and language a resident could enter without first understanding the complete data structure.

The prototype did not settle every interpretive question. It made those questions concrete enough to inspect, discuss, and improve.

## Use and iteration

Politico New York documented Jamie's effort to make pages discoverable and shareable and his expansion of contact options after conversations with prospective users. That is evidence of a working iteration loop; it is not a claim of broad adoption or measured service outcomes.<Cite pageId="callnyc" occurrenceId="use-iteration" />

## Evidence and limits

The event record combines official and organizational social posts, independent journalism, the primary project repository, a protected participant photograph, and a bounded archival reconstruction. Together they support Jamie's participation and independent follow-on; no source establishes that he organized the event or built an official Council product.

The archival review did not recover a dedicated Civic Hall event page, full agenda, registration form, participant roster, breakout roster, facilitator list, or published winners. The recovered Wayback evidence comes from an embedded social feed, not a calendar listing. Absence from that bounded search is not proof that a page never existed.

CouncilStat totals also required interpretive restraint. They should not be treated as simple measures of office quality, effectiveness, specialization, or resident need.<Cite pageId="callnyc" occurrenceId="data-limits" />
