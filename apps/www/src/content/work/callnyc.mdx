## Context

In January 2016, Jamie participated in the New York City Council's first CouncilStat hackathon at Civic Hall, a workshop focused on constituent services and district-office operations.<Cite pageId="work.callnyc" citationId="citation.callnyc.hackathon.date-time" /><Cite pageId="work.callnyc" citationId="citation.callnyc.hackathon.councilstat" />

A private participant photograph documents a "Digital District" breakout focused on improving City Council district-office operations. That supports the breakout title and prompt, not a claim that "Digital District" was the event's official title or that the photograph timestamp was the event start time.<Cite pageId="work.callnyc" citationId="citation.callnyc.digital-district" />

After the full CouncilStat dataset was released, Jamie independently developed CallNYC.org as a resident-facing interpretation of the records, while documenting limitations created by inconsistent office recording practices.<Cite pageId="work.callnyc" citationId="citation.callnyc.follow-on" /><Cite pageId="work.callnyc" citationId="citation.callnyc.data-limitations" />

## What was unclear

CouncilStat data carried civic signal, but a public dataset was not yet a resident pathway. A person still needed to understand what an issue meant, which office or district context might matter, what next step was reasonable, and where the source data could mislead.

The project also needed a firm boundary. CallNYC could demonstrate a translation pattern without pretending to be official guidance, a live service, a legal or emergency channel, or a complete measure of district-office performance.

## What I did

Jamie built the independent prototype, organized released records into issue-oriented pathways, and designed pages for public discovery and sharing. The work translated an expert-facing dataset into a resident-readable interface while preserving visible limits around source quality and present-day use.

He also made source limits operational. CallNYC treated CouncilStat records as administrative records shaped by office practice, not as simple rankings of office quality, effectiveness, specialization, or resident need.<Cite pageId="work.callnyc" citationId="citation.callnyc.data-limitations" occurrence={2} />

## What became usable

The prototype turned administrative records into issue pages, district context, and possible next steps. It made the translation layer visible: residents did not need to understand the entire dataset before entering through a recognizable concern.

For product and operations teams, the transferable pattern is direct: map a difficult source to the decisions a person needs to make, build a bounded pathway, and keep the source limitations inside the product rather than in a separate disclaimer no one sees.

## Use and iteration

Contemporaneous reporting documents a small, fast feedback loop rather than broad adoption. Jamie optimized pages for search and sharing, then added more contact options after conversations with prospective users. The surviving repository keeps the implementation inspectable.<Cite pageId="work.callnyc" citationId="citation.callnyc.follow-on" occurrence={2} />

## Evidence and limits

- **Official organizational evidence:** Civic Hall and New York City Council posts support the January 30 date, announced hours, constituent-services focus, Civic Hall setting, and CouncilStat context.
- **Independent journalism and primary project evidence:** Politico New York supports the independent follow-on, build sequence, filtering method, and iteration; the public repository documents the surviving implementation.
- **Private participant evidence:** A participant photograph supports "Digital District" as one breakout-table title. It remains private and supplies neither an overall event title nor publication permission.
- **Reasoned reconstruction:** The recovered sources establish a bounded event and project sequence. They do not establish a dedicated Civic Hall listing, full agenda, breakout roster, participant list, facilitator, formal winners, or published project roster.
- **Data limits:** CouncilStat totals require context and do not independently measure service quality or resident outcomes.
