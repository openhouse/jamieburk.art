## Institutional context

On January 30, 2016, the New York City Council convened a constituent-services hackathon at Civic Hall. Civic Hall announced the session for 1-3 p.m., and the Council described it that day as its first CouncilStat hackathon.<Cite note="NOTE-CALL-EVENT-CONTEXT" occurrence="call-event-context-1" />

A New York City Council-branded promotional graphic used the wording "New York City Council Hackathon." That wording is treated here as recovered event branding, not as proof of a longer formal registration title.<Cite note="NOTE-CALL-PROMOTIONAL-BRANDING" occurrence="call-event-branding-1" />

After the complete constituent-services dataset became public, Jamie independently built CallNYC.org as a resident-facing interpretation of the records.<Cite note="NOTE-CALL-INDEPENDENT-FOLLOW-ON" occurrence="call-independent-follow-on-1" />

## What was unclear

Constituent-services data carried civic signal, but residents needed a translation layer. What issue is this? Which office or district context might matter? What next step is reasonable? What should be treated as general guidance rather than official service direction?

## What became usable

The prototype organized released data into issue pathways, district context, and resident-facing guidance. It treated data translation as a product and information-architecture problem: the resident should not have to understand the whole dataset before seeing a possible next step.

## What I did

Jamie independently built the prototype, shaped the issue pathways, translated data into public-facing guidance patterns, and connected civic-data structure to a resident-readable interface.

The work required choosing what not to claim. A prototype can demonstrate a pattern without pretending to be an official service.

## Data limits

CouncilStat records were administrative records, not a uniform performance-measurement system. Council staff cautioned that offices used the application differently and entered information with different frequency. Recorded case totals therefore required contextual interpretation.<Cite note="NOTE-CALL-DATA-LIMITATIONS" occurrence="call-data-limits-1" />

## Outcome / evidence

Politico New York covered CallNYC in March 2016 as a website for exploring City Council constituent-services data.<Cite note="NOTE-CALL-POLITICO" occurrence="call-politico-1" /> The public repository preserves the implementation history.<Cite note="NOTE-CALL-PUBLIC-REPOSITORY" occurrence="call-repository-1" />

## Transferable proof

This case matters for civic technology and public-facing tools because it shows open-data translation into a usable public interface.

It matters for product operations because the work moved from dataset to user pathway, with disclaimers and scope boundaries built into the surface.

It matters for implementation because it demonstrates a small but complete civic prototype: data source, information architecture, interface, and public-safe guidance.
