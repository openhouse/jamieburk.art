## What was unclear

The data carried civic signal, but a public dataset was not yet a resident pathway. A person still needed to understand what an issue meant, which office or district context might matter, what next step was reasonable, and where the source data could mislead.

## What I did

Jamie built the independent prototype, organized records into issue pathways, and translated administrative categories into public-facing guidance. A person could enter through a recognizable concern instead of first understanding the complete dataset. CallNYC was not commissioned by the Council, documented as a formal hackathon submission, or presented as an official City service.

## What became usable

The prototype organized constituent-services records into issue pages, district context, and possible next steps. It treated data translation as a product and information-architecture problem: map a difficult source to the decisions a person needs to make, build a clear pathway, and keep limitations inside the product.

## Outcome and evidence
