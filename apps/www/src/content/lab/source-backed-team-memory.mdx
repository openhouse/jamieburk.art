## Method

The Knowledge Wiki Graph is my current research and operating practice for turning a project's changing universe of materials into reviewable knowledge and audience-specific outputs. It grows from earlier Noting.us and Source-Backed Team Memory experiments. The system is active in how this portfolio is researched, evaluated, corrected, and composed; it is not presented as a client-deployed platform.

I do not hear this as a need for more notes. I hear it as a need for operating memory.

Operating memory helps a team understand what has been decided, why it was decided, what source material supports that understanding, what remains open, who holds which context, and what new collaborators need in order to join intelligently.

## Three Related Graphs

### Semantic graph

Projects, people, decisions, requirements, claims, corrections, and open questions describe what the work means now.

### Evidence graph

Sources, artifacts, observations, contradictions, and limitations show what supports, complicates, or corrects an interpretation.

### Source-custody graph / layer

Exact materials, versions, access conditions, rights, and retention rules record where authoritative material lives and when it may be inspected or reused.

Evaluations and human review govern movement between these structures. Passing a check does not grant permission to publish. A defensible claim may remain held from the portfolio because purpose, consent, credit, or audience is unresolved.

Each project repository remains useful on its own. Shared identity connects related records across the ecosystem without merging ownership, claims, permission, or publication authority.

## What It Makes Possible

A project can emerge from a living configuration of materials, sources, relationships, corrections, evaluation results, and human decisions. As that configuration changes, documentation, application materials, public claims, and portfolio pages can be updated together without pretending that every audience should see the same thing.

## What a First Engagement Tests

A first engagement begins with one approved source surface: for example, a meeting transcript, decision memo, onboarding document, or representative source prepared for the test. Together, we identify what is getting lost, what must remain protected, and who needs to review the result.

The first loop should:

- preserve useful ideas, decisions, open questions, onboarding context, and product reasoning without flattening them into a generic summary;
- connect the resulting memory to the source material from which it was derived;
- give the people who hold the context a practical way to review and correct the result;
- test whether new collaborators can get up to speed without repeatedly asking the team to reconstruct the past; and
- end with a clear continue, revise, or stop recommendation before anything larger is built.

No broad access to company systems is needed for this first test. The source can be non-sensitive, redacted, synthetic, or already approved for the participants. The format should fit the team: a document, a wiki page, a small Markdown structure, or another surface people already know how to use.

AI drafts. Humans review. The shared record remains inspectable and correctable.

## What Success Looks Like

The test succeeds when a team can recover why a decision was made, see what remains open, find the source behind a statement, and know who can correct it. It should reduce repeated explanation without creating a second bureaucracy. The result remains useful only while people can contest it, repair it, and decide what belongs in the shared record.

## What It Is Not

It is not a finished production SaaS, a chatbot, a surveillance system, a replacement for judgment, a legal, medical, or financial advice system, or a private archive browser. The point is not to automate trust. The point is to make context easier to review, correct, and transfer.

## Review Pattern

The pattern is Known / Open / Protected. Known material is public-safe or source-backed. Open material needs review, correction, or more context. Protected material is intentionally withheld because privacy, consent, law, safety, or client trust requires it.

Approved source -&gt; structured record -&gt; source-linked draft -&gt; ideas / decisions / open questions -&gt; trust / privacy check -&gt; accepted team memory.

AI drafts. Humans review. The shared record remains inspectable and correctable.

## Example Deliverables

- knowledge-friction map
- semantic, evidence, and source-custody maps
- source / workflow inventory
- decision-memory template
- meeting-memory template
- onboarding or "how we know what we know" starter page
- privacy, access, and retention notes
- 30-day continue / revise / stop recommendation

## Role Fit

The work connects technical project management, product operations, documentation architecture, AI-readiness, human review, and source-backed memory. It is visible as a lab method and as the research architecture behind this portfolio, not as the lead homepage case study.
