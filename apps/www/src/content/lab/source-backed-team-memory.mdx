## Method

Source-Backed Team Memory is a lab / proof-of-practice exploring how knowledge-heavy teams can preserve decision lineage, onboarding context, meeting synthesis, and human-reviewed AI-assisted workflows without turning private archives into an unsafe browsing surface.

The purpose is to help teams preserve ideas, decisions, open questions, source context, and onboarding knowledge so the work does not depend on repeated oral history or a few people's private memory.

## What It Is

It is an early method and consulting practice for teams that need source-backed operating memory: what happened, who decided what, which source supports a claim, what remains uncertain, and what should stay protected.

AI can help draft and organize. Humans remain responsible for review, correction, trust, privacy, and acceptance. The shared record remains inspectable and correctable.

## What It Is Not

It is not a finished production SaaS, a replacement for judgment, a legal or medical advice system, or a private archive browser. The point is not to automate trust. The point is to make context easier to review, correct, and transfer.

## Working Pattern

The pattern is Known / Open / Protected. Known material is public-safe or source-backed. Open material needs review, correction, or more context. Protected material is intentionally withheld because privacy, consent, law, safety, or client trust requires it.

The clearest current proof-of-practice is the FairRentNYC / Commercial Rent Stabilization memory work, where source trails, action items, public/internal boundaries, legal caution, and protected context all have to remain legible.

## Source-to-Memory Loop

A small, safe implementation starts with one approved source bundle, not a broad ingestion of private systems. The useful loop is:

1. Identify the source material, permission boundary, and intended audience.
2. Extract claims, decisions, action items, open questions, and sensitive context.
3. Draft source-linked meeting memory, decision records, onboarding notes, or handoff pages.
4. Run quality checks for source coverage, speaker attribution, decision-versus-discussion boundaries, open-question preservation, privacy scope, and human adoptability.
5. Have humans review, correct, accept, reject, or export the record.

That loop is deliberately small. It tests whether the practice helps the team remember, decide, onboard, and act before any larger workflow is considered.

## Evaluation Practice

The evaluation question is not whether AI produced a polished summary. The evaluation question is whether the system preserved what mattered, cited its sources, respected privacy, avoided false certainty, and left humans able to correct the record.

This connects to current AI evaluation practice: error analysis, annotation workflows, traces, failure modes, retrieval quality, and human-in-the-loop review. The method treats quality checks as part of documentation, not a separate technical afterthought.

## Role Fit

The work connects technical project management, product operations, documentation architecture, human review, AI-assisted documentation workflows, and source-backed memory. It is visible in V1 as a lab page, not as the lead homepage case study.
