## One-line summary

CallNYC.org explored how civic open data could be translated into clear resident-facing next-step guidance.

## Role

I framed and built the prototype as a public-facing civic tool, focusing on how a resident might move from a problem to a clearer next step.

## Context

Raw civic data can be informative without being immediately usable. A resident trying to solve a problem often needs plain language, orientation, and caveats more than a data table.

## What I did

- Framed the product problem around resident next steps.
- Translated civic-data concepts into public-facing language.
- Built a prototype structure that connected issues to guidance.
- Made the unofficial and archived status visible.

## What became usable

The prototype made a public-data idea legible as a resident experience. It also established the public-safety discipline needed for civic prototypes: do not imply official status where none exists.

## Tools and systems

Open-data review, civic service framing, web prototyping, plain-language guidance, route structure, and public disclaimers.

## Outcomes / impact

The project is best understood as a civic-data prototype and proof of translation skill, not as a current service.

## What this proves

This case shows the ability to turn public data into humane, careful product guidance while keeping public claims bounded.
