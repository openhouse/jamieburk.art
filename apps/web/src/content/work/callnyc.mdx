## One-line summary

CallNYC.org translated civic-data context into a public-facing prototype for residents looking for help and next steps.

## Role

Jamie built the product concept, public-facing guidance structure, and prototype framing around open data and resident needs.

## Context

Public data can be visible but still hard to use. Residents need plain-language routes into action, not only raw datasets or institutional language.

## What I did

- Interpreted constituent-services open data.
- Structured resident-facing help and next-step guidance.
- Built an archived prototype that connected data context to practical public copy.
- Used disclaimers to avoid implying an official government service.

## What became usable

The prototype made civic-data questions easier to understand as public guidance: what the source data suggested, where a resident might start, and what caveats mattered.

## Selected artifacts

Future public-safe artifacts can include archived screenshots, open-data source notes, information architecture diagrams, and guidance-copy examples.

## Tools and systems

Open data, public-facing web design, civic copywriting, prototype development, source review, and accessibility-aware information architecture.

## Outcomes / impact

This is best understood as product and civic-data proof: the ability to turn institutional source material into clearer public guidance.

## What this proves

Jamie can translate civic complexity into public-facing tools while keeping disclaimers visible and boundaries clear.
