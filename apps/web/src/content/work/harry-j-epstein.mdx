## One-line summary

Helped a legacy industrial business keep its public-facing and internal operating systems usable while e-commerce, analytics, marketing, content, and fulfillment expectations kept changing.

## Role

I worked across web operations, analytics, content, marketing support, workflow improvement, and practical documentation. The job was not only to make pages or campaigns, but to keep the business legible enough for people to act on.

## Context

The company had a deep history, specialized inventory, a public-facing e-commerce presence, and practical operations that could not pause while systems changed around them.

## What I did

- Supported public web and e-commerce improvements.
- Helped connect analytics, content, and marketing work to operational decisions.
- Improved repeatable workflows around product information, updates, and public communication.
- Created practical documentation and handoff knowledge where the work needed continuity.

## What became usable

The business had clearer web operations, better feedback loops, and more durable working knowledge. The public claim is intentionally narrow: this work contributed to revenue growth and modernization without overstating single-person causality.

## Tools and systems

Web publishing, e-commerce workflows, analytics, email and marketing systems, documentation, search behavior, product information, and day-to-day operational practices.

## Outcomes / impact

The work contributed to 2x revenue growth and supported the company through a long digital adaptation period. Private financial details and internal analytics remain protected.

## What this proves

This case shows long-term implementation judgment: translating messy business needs into durable operating structure while respecting the realities of a working company.
