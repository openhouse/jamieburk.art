## One-line summary

Long-running small-business digital transformation work for a legacy industrial supplier, focused on web systems, e-commerce operations, analytics-informed updates, content workflows, marketing support, and practical handoffs.

## Snapshot

- **Role:** Technical Project Manager & Web Systems Lead
- **Years:** 2012-present
- **Context:** 80+ year-old industrial tool and hardware business adapting legacy retail operations to digital channels
- **Tools / systems:** Public website, e-commerce workflows, analytics, content updates, marketing operations, documentation
- **Main outputs:** Maintainable web workflows, improved content operations, analytics-informed changes, public-safe digital presence
- **Transferable strengths:** Implementation, stakeholder translation, operations modernization, durable handoff
- **Status / caveat:** Public-safe summary only; private analytics and internal operations are protected

## Context

The work sat inside a real business with long memory, existing customer trust, inherited processes, and limited tolerance for shiny systems that would not hold up. The useful question was not just how to make a better website. It was how to help a legacy business keep its voice while making digital operations easier to maintain.

## What was unclear

Digital merchandising, content maintenance, analytics feedback, product updates, and operational follow-through were spread across practical day-to-day work. The system needed clearer ownership, simpler update paths, and better ways to connect public-facing changes with business outcomes.

## What I built / coordinated / documented

I helped structure web, e-commerce, marketing, analytics, content, and operations improvements over time. The work included turning recurring updates into repeatable workflows, using analytics to guide practical changes, protecting the company's established voice, and making handoffs concrete enough that the system could keep moving after individual implementation pushes.

## What became usable

The business gained clearer public-facing web systems, more maintainable content workflows, analytics-informed updates, and operational handoffs that supported ongoing digital work. The core professional proof is implementation under real constraints: improving a working business without pretending it was a blank slate.

## How people used it

Staff and collaborators could use the public site, content practices, and operational notes to keep customer-facing digital work moving. Customers encountered a more usable online path while the business retained the character that made it recognizable.

## Outcomes

- Contributed to 2x revenue growth through web, e-commerce, marketing, analytics, content, and operations improvements.
- Helped modernize digital workflows without erasing institutional voice.
- Supported long-running implementation rather than a one-time redesign.
