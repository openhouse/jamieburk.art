## Context

Harry J. Epstein Company is an 80+ year-old legacy industrial business with deep customer trust, product knowledge, and an established voice. The challenge was not simply to put a business online. It was to translate an inherited operating culture into web, e-commerce, analytics, marketing, content, and workflow practices that people could actually maintain.

## What I did

- Helped structure public-facing web and e-commerce work.
- Supported content, product, analytics, and marketing workflows.
- Created and maintained practical operating knowledge around the site.
- Helped preserve institutional voice while adapting to modern customer expectations.

## How people used it

The work supported customers trying to understand products, staff trying to manage changing digital operations, and future maintainers who needed a clearer path through the business's web and e-commerce systems.

## Outcome / evidence

The public-safe proof is continuity: a legacy business continued adapting to online commerce without losing its practical voice. Specific analytics, revenue, and internal operations remain protected until approved.

## Transferable proof

This case shows technical project management in a real small-business environment: requirements that changed, tools that needed care, workflows that had to fit the people using them, and documentation that mattered because the work kept moving.
