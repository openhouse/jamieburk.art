## Context

Fair Rent NYC and related NYC Artist Coalition work sat inside a public civic context: commercial rent pressure, cultural displacement, organizing, public communication, legal language, and coalition memory. The work needed to be legible without exposing private notes or overstating official authority.

## What I did

- Helped turn scattered civic context into clearer documentation.
- Supported source-aware public memory and handoff practices.
- Kept privacy boundaries visible around coalition work.
- Helped make the public argument easier to explain without publishing raw internal material.

## How people used it

The work helped collaborators, public readers, and future participants orient around what was known, what was open, and what needed protection. It created a better path from meeting memory to public-safe explanation.

## Outcome / evidence

The proof is in the structure: source layers, public-safe summaries, and a clearer relationship between civic material and the boundaries around it. This page does not publish private legal review, stakeholder lists, or internal notes.

## Transferable proof

This case is useful for roles involving civic technology, product operations, knowledge systems, documentation, and public-interest implementation because it shows how to work with sensitive context without flattening it into a generic public claim.
