## One-line summary

Jamie helped a legacy tool business keep public-facing e-commerce and day-to-day operations usable while the business moved through changing channels, customer expectations, and internal needs.

## Context

Harry J. Epstein Company is a long-running Kansas City tool business with a public catalog, online customers, in-person history, and a deep product knowledge base. The work sat at the intersection of web presence, customer service, catalog information, operations, and continuity.

## What was unclear

The business needed practical structure around product information, public-facing pages, customer paths, operating routines, and web changes. The challenge was not a single feature. It was keeping an older, knowledge-heavy business legible as more work moved through digital systems.

## What became usable

The work produced clearer web and catalog structures, more repeatable operating habits, and public-facing surfaces that made it easier for customers and staff to find, explain, update, and maintain the business.

## What I did

Jamie supported web updates, catalog organization, product information structure, operational documentation, customer-facing improvements, and handoff practices. The work required translating practical business knowledge into systems that people could keep using.

## How people used it

Customers used clearer public-facing surfaces to understand products and place orders. Staff used more organized information and workflows to answer questions, maintain pages, and keep operations moving.

## Outcome / evidence

The public-safe proof is a sustained contribution to business continuity and a reported 2x revenue growth contribution for the legacy e-commerce business.

## Transferable proof

This case shows Jamie's fit for technical project management and product operations: ambiguous needs, multiple stakeholders, legacy systems, customer-facing consequences, and the need for durable documentation.
