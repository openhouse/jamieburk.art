## One-line summary

CallNYC.org was a civic-data prototype that helped translate public representative information into a more direct action path.

## Context

The project came from a practical civic information problem: people often want to act, but the path from public data to the right contact action can be confusing.

## What was unclear

The public information existed, but the user path was not simple enough. The work needed to connect civic data, public context, and a clear web interface without implying official government status.

## What became usable

The prototype made civic contact information easier to approach and framed a repeatable pattern: public source, simplified interface, clear action, visible limits.

## What I did

Jamie structured the prototype, mapped the public information into a user-facing flow, and built the web surface around a practical civic action.

## How people used it

The intended user could move from public civic context to a clearer contact path, with the prototype acting as a bridge rather than an official service.

## Outcome / evidence

The case demonstrates public-facing civic-data work, careful labeling, and the ability to turn public information into a usable prototype.

## Transferable proof

This is proof for civic technology, implementation, and public-facing tools: the work had to be useful, limited, clear, and honest about its status.

## Current status

Archived prototype. Not an official or current City Council service.
