## One-line summary

A civic-data prototype that translated constituent-services open data into resident-facing issue pages and next-step guidance.

## Context

CallNYC was built after New York City Council's first civic-data hackathon. The prototype explored how CouncilStat and constituent-services open data could become easier for residents to understand and act on.

## What was unclear

- How raw civic-service data could become useful to a resident with a concrete issue.
- Which issue categories and next steps would make sense in a public-facing tool.
- How to keep the prototype clearly framed as unofficial and archived.

## What I did

- Built a public-facing civic-data prototype.
- Translated open data into resident-oriented issue categories and guidance.
- Created a structure for issue pages, public explanations, and next-step pathways.
- Maintained an explicit disclaimer that the project was not an official City Council service.

## What became usable

- A public prototype showing how civic data could become resident-facing guidance.
- A clearer data-to-service-design example for civic technology and public-facing tools.
- Archived proof of early open-data implementation work.

## Outcomes / impact

- Demonstrated a practical way to translate constituent-services data into public guidance.
- Created a visible civic-technology artifact connected to open-data experimentation.
- Received coverage from Politico New York.

## What this proves

Civic technology, open-data translation, service design, information architecture, public-facing tool building, and careful public disclaimers.

## Known / Open / Protected

Known: archived prototype, civic-data frame, issue-page guidance pattern, and public disclaimer.

Open: approved screenshots, public archive links, and the Politico New York link or PDF.

Protected: anything that could imply a current official government service or expose private resident information.
