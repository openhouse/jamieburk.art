## One-line summary
Helped an 80+ year-old legacy industrial business adapt to e-commerce through web, analytics, marketing, content, and operational workflow improvements.

## Context
The work sat inside a long-running industrial supply business with deep product knowledge, customer trust, and a public voice that did not map neatly onto generic e-commerce patterns.

## What was unclear?
A legacy business had public voice, customer trust, inventory knowledge, and operating habits that did not automatically translate into modern e-commerce workflows.

## What I structured
- Web and e-commerce improvements.
- Analytics and marketing workflows.
- Product content and maintenance rhythms.
- Translation between technical and nontechnical stakeholders.
- Durable operating patterns rather than one-off redesign work.

## What became usable
Improved web, e-commerce, analytics, marketing, content, and operational workflows supported online growth while preserving the company's distinctive institutional voice.

## How people used it
The work helped the business maintain a public-facing web presence, improve customer pathways, make better use of analytics, and keep operational knowledge connected to digital channels.

## Outcomes / evidence
- 2x revenue growth contribution, stated carefully as contribution rather than sole cause.
- Web, marketing, analytics, content, and operations improvements across a long-running business context.
- Stakeholder translation across technical and nontechnical work.

## What this proves
- Technical project management.
- Product operations.
- Implementation.
- Documentation.
- Public-facing web systems.
- Small-business operating memory.
