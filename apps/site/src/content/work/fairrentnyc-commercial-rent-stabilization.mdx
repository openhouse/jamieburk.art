## One-line summary
Built and stewarded shared campaign-memory and coordination infrastructure for Commercial Rent Stabilization and storefront-stability advocacy.

## Context
The work sat inside collective civic advocacy involving artists, small businesses, storefront stability concerns, public data, policy questions, and shifting city/state strategy lanes.

## What was unclear?
The work involved many stakeholders, public/private source materials, legal/policy questions, city/state strategy lanes, shifting meetings, and sensitive coalition context that could easily become fragmented or overexposed.

## What I structured
- Running minutes.
- Action trackers.
- Source maps.
- Legal and policy question logs.
- Public-data framing.
- Stakeholder follow-up notes.
- Public-safe explanations.
- Shared campaign memory.

## What became usable
Collaborators had a more inspectable record of meetings, open questions, sources, decision trails, follow-ups, and public-safe framing.

## How people used it
The materials helped collaborators remember what had been discussed, orient new participants, track actions, identify source-backed claims, and avoid exposing private or unapproved context.

## Outcomes / evidence
- 30+ pages of shared campaign-memory infrastructure.
- Running minutes and decision records.
- Source maps and public-data framing.
- City/state strategy lanes and legal/policy questions organized for collaborators.

## What this proves
- Technical project management in sensitive civic contexts.
- Product operations for collective work.
- Documentation architecture.
- Stakeholder coordination.
- Source-backed memory.
- Public-safe judgment.
