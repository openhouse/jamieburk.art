## One-line summary

Shared campaign-memory and coordination infrastructure for Commercial Rent Stabilization and storefront-stability advocacy.

## Context

The work involved civic, policy, legal, public-data, and stakeholder questions that needed to become usable without exposing private coalition context. The goal was not to create a heroic personal narrative; it was to help shared work remain findable, careful, and actionable.

## What was unclear

- Which public facts, policy questions, and data gaps mattered most.
- How to maintain a shared record across meetings, materials, and strategy lanes.
- How to separate public-safe materials from private notes and legal-review context.
- How to make storefront stability and commercial vacancy questions legible to different audiences.

## What I did

- Helped structure 30+ pages of shared campaign-memory infrastructure.
- Built and stewarded source maps, running minutes, decision records, action trackers, and public guidance pathways.
- Translated complex policy and data questions into usable materials.
- Supported coalition coordination while protecting private notes and sensitive stakeholder details.

## What became usable

- Shared documentation that helped participants understand what happened, what mattered, and what needed to happen next.
- Public-data framing around vacancy, occupancy, lease-cost questions, and storefront stability.
- Public handout and QR-pathway scaffolds that could point people toward safer, reviewed materials.

## Outcomes / impact

- Supported coalition coordination through source-backed records and clearer workstreams.
- Helped turn fragile civic context into shared campaign memory.
- Preserved privacy boundaries while making the public-interest work easier to explain.

## What this proves

Documentation architecture, coalition operations, stakeholder coordination, policy communications, open-data framing, public-safe writing, and careful civic implementation.

## Known / Open / Protected

Known: public-facing civic campaign frame, shared documentation practice, campaign-memory infrastructure, source mapping, and public-data framing.

Open: which public artifacts are approved for publication, which diagrams should be recreated, and what metrics are safe to include.

Protected: private notes, legal-review materials, raw strategy docs, stakeholder lists, contact lists, transcripts, and unapproved internal materials.
