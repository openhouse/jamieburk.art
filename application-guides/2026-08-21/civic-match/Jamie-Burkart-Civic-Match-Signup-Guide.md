## What Civic Match is good for

Civic Match is a free Work for America talent network for state and local
government. A maintained profile can support job-board discovery, saved roles,
human matching, and invitations from participating government employers. It is
different from NYC Jobs Open Data: it creates a candidate-facing relationship
and visibility surface rather than a machine-readable inventory.

### Private answer 1 — government impact

Visibility: Work for America team only; Civic Match says this answer will not
appear on the public profile. Maximum: 300 words.

```text
One evening at Shoestring Press, I crouched in the back garden with oil pastel and large sheets of card stock, trying to make a difficult coalition conversation visible. Venue operators, artists, nightlife advocates, and organizers had spent years living under New York City's 1926 Cabaret Law—a rule that could make ordinary dancing a licensing problem and leave cultural spaces carrying uncertainty that had little to do with safety.

I helped convene and facilitate that meeting. By the end, groups with different histories agreed to share LetNYCDance.com and one email list. That shared surface did not repeal a law by itself. It gave a coalition a clearer way to exchange information, invite participation, and speak together. Advocates, venues, artists, elected officials, and agency staff each carried different parts of the work.

When the City Council repealed the Cabaret Law in 2017, I saw what local government can do when public testimony, organizing, policy work, and legislative authority meet. A municipal rule had shaped who could gather, how a room could be used, and whether a venue could plan. Changing it altered the conditions of everyday cultural life.

The experience stays with me because government impact is not abstract. It arrives in a room: people can dance without an obsolete license standing between them and one another. The public task is to make that relationship between policy and lived experience visible early enough that residents can help shape the outcome.
```

### Private answer 2 — initiative and impact

Visibility: Work for America team only; Civic Match says this answer will not
appear on the public profile. Maximum: 300 words.

```text
In Kansas City's Oak Park neighborhood, a discarded tire was easy to notice and expensive to remove. Tires collected in vacant lots and along sidewalks. The immediate problem was rubber. Underneath it was a service gap: the City could recycle tires, but residents needed a practical route from the curb to that public system.

Through KC Town Hall, working with the Oak Park Neighborhood Association, Historic East Neighborhoods Coalition, residents, volunteers, and City staff, I helped design and operate Tired of Tires. I made the four-by-six-inch handbills and web page, established a recurring pickup rhythm, coordinated City recycling, and drove block by block through the ten-minute walk area around KC Town Hall. I helped older neighbors roll tires from backyards, caught tires as young people sent them down a driveway, loaded the trailer, and kept the project tracker.

The records helped us plan, but the contact mattered more. During pickups I also shared a neighborhood survey asking what services people wanted within walking distance. Conversations about tires opened conversations about food businesses, shared kitchens, storefronts, and what the building at 36th and Indiana might become.

I learned that a resident-facing service can be small, physical, and deeply systemic. Meet people at the curb, make the handoff reliable, keep the record, and the neighborhood begins to see that its care can take a usable form.
```
