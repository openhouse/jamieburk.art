# Cover letter — Benepass Product Operations Manager

## Letter

Dear Benepass Hiring Team,

An expensive product problem often first appears as a small operating question: a customer cannot find something, a support request repeats, or a team discovers too late that a launch has changed the work downstream. I am drawn to Product Operations because it creates a durable route from those signals to product decisions, launch readiness, adoption, and learning.

Through THICK ARTS, I have spent more than fourteen years translating customer and operating friction into requirements, priorities, sequenced releases, testing, support materials, runbooks, and maintainable systems. For Harry J. Epstein Company, I led incremental improvements across e-commerce, search, analytics, content, marketing, and operations that contributed to 2x revenue growth while respecting the language and practices of an 80-year-old business. The work was not one dramatic transformation. It was a sustained loop of noticing, testing, measuring, documenting, and deciding what the organization could responsibly absorb next.

I brought that same loop to WOWList, a natural-language community calendar I co-built with Richard Caceres and helped steward across roughly 35 city ecosystems. Organizer questions became product requirements, onboarding, weekly digests, reusable support, instrumentation, and maintenance practices. CallNYC similarly translated administrative data into 61 resident-facing issue pathways. My career has not followed a conventional SaaS Product Operations title sequence, but the operating problem is familiar: make customer and operations knowledge usable before a roadmap decision, make risk visible before launch, and make learning available after it.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how I could help Benepass connect Operations, Product, and Engineering through clear priorities, readiness decisions, useful runbooks, and measurable post-launch learning. I would bring careful translation across teams, practical respect for operational knowledge, and the habit of leaving behind a system another person can continue.

Thank you for your consideration,

Jamie Burkart
