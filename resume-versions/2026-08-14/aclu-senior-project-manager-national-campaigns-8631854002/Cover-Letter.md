# Cover letter — ACLU Senior Project Manager, National Campaigns

## Letter

Dear ACLU Hiring Committee,

A campaign is never only its public moment. It is also the meeting before the meeting, the person who needs a clear next step, the talking point whose authority must be understood, the risk surfaced early enough to change a plan, and the record that allows a coalition to remember what it decided. I am drawn to the Senior Project Manager, National Campaigns role because my work has centered on giving public purpose that kind of durable operating form.

As a co-founder and civic systems, coalition operations, and policy communications lead with NYC Artist Coalition / Fair Rent NYC, I help translate stakeholder listening, testimony, legislation, legal and policy questions, press work, and city and state strategy into shared plans, action trackers, source maps, scripts, public materials, ownership, and follow-through. Our current Commercial Rent Stabilization work includes more than 30 pages of governed campaign memory. I work directly with artists, small businesses, organizers, elected officials and staff, policy and legal collaborators, communications teams, venues, press, and participants while keeping confidentiality, decision authority, and collective credit explicit.

That practice is also technical. I built CallNYC, which translated New York City constituent-services data into 61 issue pathways and resident next steps. With Richard Caceres, I co-built and operated WOWList across roughly 35 city ecosystems, supporting organizers with shared publishing, onboarding, communication, and maintenance systems. These projects taught me that participation does not become power through visibility alone. People need a legible route from information to coordinated action—and a system that does not erase the local knowledge that made the action possible.

I would welcome the opportunity to help ACLU teams work from one clear plan while preserving affiliate and community leadership. I would be glad to discuss how I manage shifting priorities, public communication, risk, executive reporting, and campaign learning under pressure.

Thank you for your consideration,

Jamie Burkart
