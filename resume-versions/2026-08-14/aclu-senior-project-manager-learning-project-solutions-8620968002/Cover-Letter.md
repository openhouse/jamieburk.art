# Cover letter — ACLU Senior Project Manager, Learning and Project Solutions

## Letter

Dear ACLU Hiring Committee,

A project plan becomes useful at the moment someone who was not in the room can pick it up, understand what has been decided, and take the next responsible action. That is the kind of structure I have spent more than fourteen years building, and it is why I am drawn to the Senior Project Manager, Learning and Project Solutions role at the ACLU.

Through THICK ARTS, I lead technical and operating projects from discovery and business analysis through requirements, sequencing, implementation, launch, maintenance, and handoff. I coordinate clients, collaborators, designers, engineers, operations, communications, and leadership while keeping decisions, dependencies, risks, owners, and open questions visible. At KC Town Hall, that discipline crossed from documents into a 6,500-square-foot adaptive-reuse project: my collaborators and I coordinated design, vendors, public-benefit requirements, field work, and a successful proposal that secured a $490,539 public funding award. The work taught me that documentation is not what happens after implementation. It is one of the ways implementation remains usable.

My civic and product work has required the same translation across different kinds of expertise. CallNYC turned constituent-services data into 61 issue pathways that residents could navigate. NYC Artist Coalition / Fair Rent NYC turns testimony, policy research, legal questions, meetings, and public communication into shared plans and durable team memory. In each setting, the question is not only whether people care about the outcome. It is whether their care has been given a form that can survive handoffs, changing conditions, and institutional complexity.

I would welcome the opportunity to bring that implementation judgment, careful writing, process analysis, and humane approach to change into the ACLU’s cross-departmental work. I would be glad to discuss how I build a plan of record, surface risk early, support adoption, and turn post-mortem learning into stronger practice for the next project.

Thank you for your consideration,

Jamie Burkart
