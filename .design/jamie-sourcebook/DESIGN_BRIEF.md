# Design brief: Jamie Burkart Sourcebook pilot

## Purpose

Make a bounded set of Jamie's already-public original words directly
discoverable in GitHub and VS Code, with enough context to prevent a source from
being mistaken for endorsement, independent verification, or sole causation.

## Audiences and jobs

- **Researcher:** find the complete statement and controlling source quickly.
- **Hiring or public reader:** understand the specific public problem Jamie was
  addressing without first learning the archive's internal machinery.
- **Jamie or maintainer:** see population closure, hashes, permissions,
  anti-claims, dependencies, and stale output before merging a change.
- **Speaker or rights holder:** eventually inspect exact wording, attribution,
  context, and intended surface without receiving unrelated protected material.

## Content and interaction boundary

The first pilot contains three self-authored official-hearing statements already
present in the public repository. The Sourcebook adds metadata, indexes, a
publication packet, and checks. It adds no site route, search service, CMS,
private build dependency, third-party quotation, or public photograph.

## Success criteria

- One canonical statement body per record.
- Browse by year, project, theme, and source type in two ordinary link hops.
- Body, catalog, and export fingerprint drift fails closed.
- Active projection requires reviewed rights, attribution, and surface state.
- Mutations that convert contact, attendance, scheduling, repaired text, or a
  recommendation into stronger evidence fail.
- The Knowledge Wiki can join the pilot without duplicating the claim registry.

## Human gates

Rights, consent, attribution, collective credit, editorial selection,
deployment, and indexing remain separate decisions. See
[RFC 0004](../../rfcs/0004-jamie-burkart-sourcebook-and-knowledge-ecosystem.md).
