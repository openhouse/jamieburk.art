# Chad's Lens

Chad's lens evaluates whether Jamie's professional record is already composed
for a busy hiring reader. It does not ask the reader to infer the argument from
a list of artifacts, technologies, responsibilities, or admirable values.

## Required Moves

For each high-value claim or adjacent sentence pair, look for:

1. **Agency** - Jamie's specific action is explicit and arrives early.
2. **End** - the sentence names the problem, stakeholder need, or operating end
   the work served.
3. **Usable result** - it states what people could use, do, understand, or
   continue because of the work.
4. **Translation** - specialized language is translated into recognizable work
   and consequences; noun piles do not carry the argument alone.
5. **Credit** - collective work, contribution, causality, authority, and limits
   are described precisely.
6. **First-pass placement** - the composed claim appears in the summary or early
   reading path, not only after the reader has reconstructed it from the page.

## Required Surfaces

Inspect:

- the homepage;
- the resume page;
- Technical Operations & Implementation;
- at least three flagship case studies representing operating, civic, and
  community or technical work.

## Score Anchors

- **0** - the public rhetoric is absent, contradicted, or materially unsafe.
- **1** - most surfaces rely on titles, responsibilities, technologies, or
  artifact lists; Jamie's agency and the end served remain unclear.
- **2** - some composed claims exist, but important surfaces still require the
  reader to connect action, purpose, result, or credit across sections.
- **3** - the main path is clear and credible, but two or more high-value claims
  still arrive as noun piles, generic responsibilities, or separated evidence
  that a hiring reader must assemble.
- **4** - across every required surface, the strongest first-pass claims clearly
  connect Jamie's defensible action to the end served and a usable result in
  plain language, while collective credit and limits remain precise.

## Anti-Gaming Rules

- Do not count the words "built," "usable," "system," or "outcome" as proof by
  themselves.
- Do not require every sentence to use the same template.
- Do not turn collective accomplishments into solo claims.
- Do not convert contribution into sole causality.
- Do not expose private proof to make a sentence feel more concrete.
- Do not penalize a bounded omission when the positive public claim remains
  specific and useful.
