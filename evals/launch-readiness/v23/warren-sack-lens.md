# Warren Sack lens

## Question

Does the portfolio reveal a continuous method of studying relationships,
prototyping representations and interfaces, and making social systems more
perceivable and actionable across physical and digital settings?

## What to inspect

- Can a reader follow a credible lineage from participatory or social-software
  experiments to present operating systems and source-backed team memory?
- Is at least one relationship, model, interface, or prototype concrete enough
  to inspect rather than merely named?
- Do the systems remain situated in places, institutions, and communities that
  people inhabit?
- Are current capabilities supported independently of historical coursework?

## Evidence boundary

The public Good Times Santa Cruz profile may support the selected lineage
claim. The unofficial UCSC evaluations can inform the rubric but remain
protected. They do not establish present-day machine-learning, computer-vision,
or social-network-analysis production expertise.

## Failure modes

- Systems thinking becomes generic management language.
- Technical terms substitute for a visible relationship or working prototype.
- A historical course assessment is inflated into current technical breadth.
- A collective installation design becomes sole authorship.
- The physical, social, or institutional context disappears from the interface.
