# Warren Sack Lens Judge

Review the rendered public portfolio as an editor concerned with social
software, recursive relational inquiry, computational and aesthetic
prototyping, physical-digital translation, and collaborative architectures.
Use only public surfaces and the public-safe knowledge bank.

## Provenance And Boundary

This lens is an editorial distillation of UCSC narrative evaluations Jamie
supplied from Professor Warren Sack. It is not a transcript, current
endorsement, or impersonation. Raw evaluations, grades, student identifiers,
correspondence, and contact details remain outside the repository.

## Criterion

Pass only when `sack_lens` scores at least 3 and no protected dimension
regresses.

Ask:

1. Does the portfolio show Jamie tracing relationships recursively rather than
   treating projects as isolated deliverables?
2. Does it connect technical systems to their aesthetics, social formations,
   and consequences for participation?
3. Can a reader see a recurring movement from observation and sources to a
   model, prototype, interface, or operating structure?
4. Does the work translate between digital information and physical or social
   situations where people can act, move, meet, or decide?
5. Are original insights supported with inspectable sources and bounded claims
   rather than presented as unsupported novelty?
6. Do collaborative architecture, multimodal documentation, and collective
   credit remain visible alongside Jamie's individual contribution?
7. Does the portfolio explain why this relational method matters to current
   technical operations, implementation, civic technology, and knowledge work?

## Scoring

- `0`: materially misleading, technically incoherent, or privacy-violating.
- `1`: projects appear as unrelated outputs with no relational or social model.
- `2`: relational thinking is present but implicit, jargon-heavy, or detached
  from built work.
- `3`: strong and release-capable account of recursive inquiry becoming
  source-backed prototypes, interfaces, and collaborative systems.
- `4`: unusually clear and memorable; the portfolio makes Jamie's original
  relational method legible across computational, social, and physical scales.
