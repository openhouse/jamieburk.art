# WOW List Facebook post full-population run

Date: 2026-07-15

## Objective

Review every distinct post exposed by the authenticated WOW List Facebook page
feed, preserve a public-safe and replayable census, mature mission-relevant
sources and claims, and prevent project-account evidence from becoming
individual authorship, sole credit, or unsupported impact.

## Population reconciliation

Repeated lazy scrolling reached the same 40,095-pixel document height and
terminal post pair across seven consecutive checks. The resulting ledger
contains 57 distinct dated posts from April 25, 2015 through March 22, 2018:

- 22 posts in 2015;
- 27 posts in 2016;
- seven posts in 2017; and
- one post in 2018.

Facebook exposed no native total-post count. The population control is the
stable terminal crawl plus the reconciled 57-row ledger. Meta Business Suite's
Content surface labeled March 31, 2019 through July 14, 2026 as `Lifetime` and
returned no activity, but that window begins after the final post recovered
from the live feed. It cannot account for the 2015-2018 population.

This is complete capture-date live-feed accounting, not a native Meta export or
proof of complete lifetime history. Deleted, hidden, private, unpublished, or
no-longer-retained posts can remain outside the recovered population.

## Public-safe production

The fixture preserves dates, displayed source-card relationships, cleaned URL
routes, bounded mission and stakeholder tags, displayed interaction counts,
and content hashes. It excludes raw post bodies, comment text, interaction and
follower identities, contact details, tracking parameters, authenticated URLs,
cookies, session state, and local paths.

The 57 rows reconcile to:

- 24 shared-source-card rows and 33 page-originated rows;
- 55 distinct cleaned routes: 30 WOW List and 25 external;
- 17 product-onboarding or use rows;
- 16 cross-city organizer-infrastructure rows;
- three participatory product-governance rows;
- 12 venue-safety or survival rows; and
- six mutual-aid or civic-mobilization rows.

The stakeholder tags overlap. They describe displayed public source
relationships, not unique people, formal partnerships, post authorship, or a
social-graph census.
