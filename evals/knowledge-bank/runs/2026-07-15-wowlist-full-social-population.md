# WOW List full social-population archival production run

**Run date:** 2026-07-15

**Branch:** `feature/evals-H`

**Scope:** 100 percent of the profile-reported `@wowlist` population, every
posted URL, account-owned engagement, bounded external use, source roles,
knowledge-bank maturation, and selective portfolio projection.

## Objective

Account for the complete public profile population while preserving the
distinctions required for defensible use:

1. separate WOW List-authored posts, WOW List replies, and external posts
   reposted by the account;
2. inventory and resolve every posted short URL;
3. distinguish account-owned engagement from original-author engagement;
4. identify attributable public use without converting a bounded search into
   a platform-wide adoption claim;
5. mature mission-relevant evidence through the knowledge-bank lifecycle; and
6. project only the clearest employer-relevant claim to the public case study.

## Population reconciliation

Authenticated Posts and Replies crawls exposed overlapping but non-identical
subsets. Five status objects appeared in Replies but not Posts. Reconciliation
by public status ID produced the complete dated profile population:

| Measure | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Profile-reported population | 38 |
| Population dispositions | 38 |
| Recovered content objects | 38 |
| Not recovered | 0 |
| Ordinary WOW List-authored posts | 16 |
| WOW List replies | 6 |
| Reposted external posts | 16 |
| Distinct posted short URLs | 35 |

This is 100 percent profile-population recovery at review time. It is not a
platform export, complete product archive, or lifetime engagement census. The
public-safe manifest preserves structured metadata and bounded
classifications; raw post bodies and authenticated-session material remain
outside the repository.

## Findings

The population documents a historical operating system, not merely a launch:

- the first authored post credits Richard and Jamie and connects WOW List to
  calendars made at Sunday Dinner;
- support exchanges describe followed local lists, profiles, event publishing,
  multi-list distribution, and a weekly email;
- an independently published tutorial provides organizer-facing onboarding;
- event links move across local music, performance, neighborhood, and benefit
  contexts; and
- the account also routes civic and mutual-aid information, including Black
  Lives Matter demonstrations, post-election marches, Standing Rock support,
  Ghost Ship relief and vigils, and support for DIY spaces.

The social record corroborates historical workflows and mission. It does not
establish current availability, every product feature, or the aggregate scale
documented by separate product archives.

## Engagement and external use

The 22 account-authored or reply posts displayed two replies, 20 reposts, and
21 likes on the review date. All 10 such posts with displayed reposts were
audited. Current public repost lists exposed 16 account appearances from eight
distinct handles; four displayed reposts had no currently public identity.

The named public group included project collaborators, organizers, artists,
media participants, and the project-adjacent `@CallNYCapp` account. Two handles
remain in the manifest without a historical stakeholder-role inference; a
current biography is not used to backfill a 2016 role. Like identities were not
audited.

A bounded public search recovered eight independently authored posts linking
WOW List event or profile pages. A separate Santa Barbara DIY page directs
organizers to add events to its WOW List calendar, and Shelby Turner's public
tutorial independently documents organizer onboarding. These are attributable
use signals, not a complete adoption census, formal partnership claim, or
platform-wide usage total.

Metrics on the 16 external posts reposted by WOW List belong to their original
authors and are excluded from WOW List traction.

## Source-role review

Every closely read source received a bounded role:

- the Shelby Turner tutorial and Santa Barbara DIY page are independent
  product-use evidence;
- the Grasstronaut and Good Times articles are mission context, not WOW List
  coverage;
- the public trademark record supplies historical product-scope and date
  evidence, not operation or adoption; and

  independently confirm WOW List participation.

No protected source or authenticated-session artifact was sent to an external
model or committed to the repository.

## Lifecycle and projection

The pass added three intake records, eight observations, eight sources, one
held archive-depth claim, and one partially recovered inquiry. The machine
manifest, human-readable production report, source-role boundaries, and
anti-claims are all public-safe.

The public WOW List case study receives one concise projection: shared Sunday
Dinner provenance, historical organizer workflows, independent tutorial
evidence, and bounded local-calendar use. The full census, engagement ledger,
civic and mutual-aid routing, and source-role detail remain in the knowledge
bank as recomposable depth.

## Recursive hill climb

### Iteration 1: population completion

The prior social-media pass had recovered 37 of 38 profile-reported objects.
Cross-view status-ID reconciliation recovered the missing object and replaced
the incomplete count with a 38-of-38 census.

### Iteration 2: attribution and source roles

The archive was separated into account-authored posts, replies, and external
reposts. Independent product-use evidence was distinguished from mission,
convening, and historical product-scope context. Current biographies were
excluded from historical stakeholder inference.

### Iteration 3: lifecycle integrity

Initial records exposed two schema defects: an unsupported projection label
and an observation referencing an intake outside its module. The projection
was normalized to an archive note and the cross-intake reference was removed.
Canonical generation and lifecycle validation then passed.

### Iteration 4: anti-gaming and selective projection

The new hard criterion and mutation tests reject a dropped population row,
relationship-count drift, inherited original-author metrics, mission context
misrepresented as product use, duplicate external-adoption identities, and raw
post-body leakage. The website projection was kept narrower than the archive.

## Stopping decision

The deterministic criterion is met. Every profile-reported object and posted
URL has a disposition; account-owned and inherited engagement are separated;
external use remains bounded; source roles are explicit; shared-account
authorship remains unassigned; and no raw post body or private session material
entered the public repository.

Further research can expand external-use and collaborator-confirmation records.
The remaining release gate is independent review rather than another
deterministic content edit.
