**Run date:** 2026-07-15

**Branch:** `feature/evals-H`

**Scope:** Phase One field delivery, neighborhood survey practice, Tired of
Tires operations, Cleveland Avenue Unify to Beautify contributions, pro bono
neighborhood communications, source boundaries, and selective projection.

## Objective

Preserve Jamie's detailed first-person account without either losing the labor
or promoting it beyond the evidence. The pass distinguishes:

1. project facts supported by contemporaneous records;
2. participant-memory propositions retained for research;
3. protected raw material that must not enter the public repository;
4. collective and institutional actions that must not become sole credit; and
5. public claims that are already ready from deeper claims that remain held.

## Source production

The 24-page March 2019 CCED proposal bundle was close-read page by page. It
identifies Jamie Burkart and Julia Fredenburg as founders and project managers,
describes a multi-trade professional and construction team, defines the Phase
One cold-shell scope, reports substantial work by 2018, reproduces the compact
neighborhood survey, and says resident responses shaped the proposal.

The proposal does not call Jamie general contractor. Because it was assembled
in March 2019 while prose still described future completion, its table is not
later independent proof that Phase One was completed in 2019.

A protected site export and bounded archive notes corroborate the project-level
survey and the recurring KC Town Hall and Oak Park tire-pickup service. They do
not assign survey authorship, tire operations, or account authorship to Jamie.
One Cleveland Avenue source body did not materialize during review, so the
record says that rather than treating the source as nonexistent or recovered.

## Recursive hill climb

### Iteration 1: structured evidence lifecycle

The pass introduced the field-practice module, canonical integration, proof
coverage links, public-safe close-reading memo, research queue, and a fifteenth
hard criterion.

### Iteration 2: count-preserving overclaims

An independent holdout showed that count checks and editable approval hashes
could accept rewritten survey-authorship, tire-operations, Cleveland-credit,
capital-causation, and rendered-proof assertions. Claim-specific semantic gates
and regression tests were added.

### Iteration 3: lexical paraphrases and alternate surfaces

Fresh reviewers found pronoun substitutions and adjacent vocabulary such as
resident poll, response registry, scrap-tire removal, corridor beautification,
opening stabilization, initial build-out, finished cold shell, and resident
input. They also found routes through shared work data, technical-operations
copy, proof metadata, and case-study MDX.

The correction replaced reliance on a growing synonym list with code-level
review locks independent of editable approval manifests. The locks cover the
source module, canonical records, governed KC Town Hall knowledge bundle,
rendered proof projection, case-study MDX, and shared public surfaces.

### Iteration 4: public review-document closure

The municipal-archivist holdout then found that the public close-reading memo
itself was outside the lock boundary. The report became the seventh review-lock
surface, and the exact injected completion-and-authorship overclaim became a
regression test.

### Iteration 5: final independent review

The municipal archivist and skeptical hiring editor replayed every prior false
negative plus nearby variants. All mutations failed with the field-practice
criterion at 1 and `reviewLocksMatch=false`; the untouched candidate retained
all fifteen scores at 5.
