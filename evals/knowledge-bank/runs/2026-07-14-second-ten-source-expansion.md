# Second ten-source expansion and Talks Not Raids hill climb

Date: 2026-07-14

Branch: `feature/evals-H`

## Objective

Research and ingest ten sources not already represented by the Greene Hill
profile, the first ten-source expansion, or the deduplicated campaign press
archive. Use the resulting evidence to strengthen Jamie's public portfolio
without converting collective advocacy or government action into individual
causal credit.

1. New York City Council Cabaret Law oversight hearing transcript, June 19,

   May 19, 2017.
3. NYC Artist Coalition's City-preserved CreateNYC recommendations.
4. The final CreateNYC cultural plan.
5. NYC Government Publications Portal record for the first biannual M.A.R.C.H.
   operations report under Local Law 220.

8. New York City Council Int 1156-A / Local Law 220 of 2019.
9. NYC Office of Nightlife Annual Report 2022.
10. NYC Office of Nightlife Annual Report 2023-2024.

The cohort is disjoint by source ID and exact URL. Published-article records
also receive normalized title, URL-slug, and author-date identity checks so a
migrated article cannot silently re-enter the bank under a new source ID.

## Content result

The research strengthened the record in four ways:

- official testimony now directly documents Jamie's coalition affiliation,
  fire-safety walk-throughs, workshops, Fire Guard study groups, and Cabaret
  Law repeal testimony;
- City records document the coalition's formation, collective policy process,
  CreateNYC recommendations, and public dialogue;
- the Talks Not Raids inquiry now connects Jamie's campaign-site
  implementation to a bounded institutional chronology; and
- the public FairRentNYC case study now explains Jamie's usable contribution,
  defines M.A.R.C.H., preserves statutory exceptions, and distinguishes
  coalition advocacy, Council enactment, and Office of Nightlife action.

The public projection does not claim that Jamie enacted Local Law 220,
disbanded M.A.R.C.H., caused CURE, or solely authored coalition policy, copy,
data, or design.

## Recursive holdouts

The first skeptical holdout rejected the candidate. It found a migrated VICE
article counted twice, an unsupported back-projection of notice into the 2017
coalition agenda, omitted statutory exceptions, and a public paragraph that
made later policy chronology clearer than Jamie's contribution.

The iteration then:

- replaced the duplicate with the official Q1-Q2 2020 M.A.R.C.H. publication;
- added canonical-identity tests beyond source IDs;
- rewrote the public projection around Jamie's actor, action, purpose, and
  usable result;
- defined Multi-Agency Response to Community Hotspots as an NYPD-led inspection
  program;
- restored the criminal-investigation and serious health-or-safety exceptions;
  and
- kept later Council and Office of Nightlife actions explicitly separate from
  Jamie and coalition causation.

Two fresh holdouts accepted the corrected candidate:
