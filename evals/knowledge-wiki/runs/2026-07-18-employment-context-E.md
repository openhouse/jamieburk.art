# Knowledge Wiki employment-context hill climb - E

## Scope

This run implements the first opportunity-aware tranche from the July 18, 2026
employment-context and hiring-acceptance handoff. It treats branches A through E
as a donor library while preserving E's single canonical root and fail-closed
candidate governance.

## Donor synthesis

- A supplied task-oriented entry points.
- B supplied source-channel and operator continuity.
- C supplied role-readiness matrices and projection bindings.
- D supplied separate document, semantic, evidence, and publication concerns,
  plus metadata-only protected-source doctrine.
- E supplied the canonical compiler, exact-candidate controls, type-aware graph,
  public-safety mutations, retained dissent, and open human gates.

No parallel claim registry, lifecycle engine, projection registry, or public
Explorer was introduced.

## Baseline

The foundation candidate had 16 governed records and one broad Technical
Operations opportunity lens. It did not yet provide:

- six official-source Tier 1 opportunity records;
- stable role-requirement IDs;
- freshness and hard-screen contracts;
- confirmed, inferred, and unknown separation;
- career and source-channel coverage;
- title-blind opportunity discovery with negative controls;
- public-only reader evaluation separated from Wiki gap resolution;
- named public-context profiles with explicit simulation disclaimers.

## Iteration 1 - contract and graph integration

The first compile correctly rejected:

- two title collisions between official sources and opportunity records;
- one requirement pointing to an undeclared public route;
- one unsupported source-to-evaluation relation shape;
- seven records unreachable from the human root.

Repairs renamed source titles, declared the route, used a neutral relation, and
linked the employment index through ordinary Markdown navigation. The compiler
then reached all active governed records.

## Iteration 2 - evidence sufficiency

The first public evaluator counted any matching text as adequate. That would
have allowed generic implementation or analytics language to erase real Asana
and PermitFlow gaps.

The evaluator was tightened so a text match counts only when the role-context
record classifies the public signal as `visible-proven` or
`visible-qualified`. `visible-weak` remains a gap even when adjacent wording is
present.

The resulting deterministic dispositions are:

- OTI Technical Operations: ready for human review;
- Benepass Product Operations: ready for human review, with quantified
  prioritization still weak;
- CodePath Claude Corps: ready for human review;
- ACLU Product Discovery: evidence-gap review, especially the launched-product
  brief and do-not-build judgment;
- Asana AI Implementation: evidence-gap review for customer implementation,
  migration, and change-management authority;
- PermitFlow Product Operations: evidence-gap review for demonstrated product
  analytics fluency.

These are deterministic public-surface dispositions, not predictions or hiring
decisions.

## Iteration 3 - bounded public projection

The `/work/technical-operations` opening was shortened from a domain inventory
to one direct operating sentence. The page remains audience-focused rather than
OTI-branded, and no opportunity index or proof-bank Explorer was published.

## Mutation results

The 33-test Knowledge Wiki suite now demonstrates detection of:

- broken links and fragments;
- duplicate IDs and aliases;
- unknown or incompatible relation targets;
- private paths and protected-source leakage;
- unsafe pending projections and rights mutations;
- collective-to-sole and mention-to-endorsement mutations;

- stale live opportunities;
- removed public proof;
