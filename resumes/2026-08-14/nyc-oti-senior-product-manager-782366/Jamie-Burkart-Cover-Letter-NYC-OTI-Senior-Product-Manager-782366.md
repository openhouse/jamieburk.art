Dear NYC Office of Technology and Innovation Hiring Team,

A public dataset is only as useful as the next step it gives a resident. After New York City Council’s first CouncilStat hackathon, I built CallNYC.org as an independent civic prototype that translated constituent-services data into issue pages, district context, and practical guidance. I carried it from problem definition and information architecture through implementation and public release. The site was unofficial, but the product question was real: what information and structure would help a New Yorker understand an issue and act?

With CallNYC, I kept version one deliberately bounded: resident-facing issue pages and next-step guidance, not a claim to be an official City service. The boundary was part of the design. More information would not automatically make the service more usable; each element had to clarify an issue, add district context, or help a resident decide what to do next. That discipline shapes how I work now: participatory discovery, buildable briefs, explicit MVP boundaries, coordinated design and engineering, post-launch learning, and accessibility, privacy, performance, evidence, and maintainability as product requirements.

The work is digital, but it does not stop at the screen. Through KC Town Hall, I helped deliver Tired of Tires, a recurring free resident service connecting eligibility, form and phone intake, scheduled field collection, City recycling coordination, volunteer participation, and public result reporting. Through NYC Artist Coalition and FairRentNYC, I turn stakeholder listening, public testimony, policy questions, and coalition priorities into public interfaces, decision records, communications, and sustained government-facing follow-through. These are collective efforts; my contribution is to make the relationships, decisions, and next actions durable enough for others to use.

PIT Crew’s mandate—to understand a public problem, build and test a useful service, launch it well, learn from New Yorkers, and leave the partner agency stronger—is the work I want to do inside City government. I want to help City services give a New Yorker one clearer next step while leaving the partner agency able to learn, improve, and carry the work forward. I would welcome a structured conversation or practical exercise about one resident-service problem, the version-one boundary I would choose, the evidence I would seek, and the outcome that should determine what happens next.
