Dear NYC Office of Technology and Innovation Hiring Team,

A project can be moving and still leave everyone carrying a different version of its state. The risk may live in one conversation, the decision in another document, and the next action in the memory of the person who stayed latest. What appears to be a coordination problem is often an absent operating structure.

Building that structure has been the center of my work for more than fourteen years. Through Thick Arts LLC and civic, cultural, and small-business projects, I turn ambiguous work into plans, ownership, delivery tracking, risk and dependency maps, decision records, onboarding materials, runbooks, status reporting, release practices, and retrospectives. I translate among technical and nontechnical collaborators, surface what remains unknown, and add enough process to make the work visible without turning the process into the work.

At NYC Artist Coalition and FairRentNYC, I built and stewarded more than thirty pages of shared campaign memory connecting minutes, decisions, actions, legal and policy questions, media assets, and city and state strategy while keeping private records offline. At KC Town Hall, I co-led planning, vendors, municipal compliance, and field dependencies for a complex adaptive-reuse project and helped deliver a recurring resident service with intake, scheduled collection, City handoff, volunteers, and public reporting. At WOW List, I helped a distributed maintainer network operate a public platform across approximately thirty-five city ecosystems. In each case, documentation was not the work after the work. It was one way the work remained available to others.

For a future OTI role with this operating pattern, I would bring candid reporting, technical fluency, calm follow-through, and a commitment to leaving the team less dependent on any one person. I would welcome a practical discussion of how to create one trusted view of delivery, risk, staffing, and decisions—and how to improve it after the team learns what it actually needs.

Warmly,

Jamie Burkart
